# TCPA Topic 09 — National DNC Registry, Internal DNC List, and Telephone Solicitation  
  
**Statute:** 47 U.S.C. § 227(c)  
**Regulations:** 47 C.F.R. § 64.1200(c), (d), (e), (f); 16 C.F.R. Part 310 (FTC Telemarketing Sales Rule, cross-referenced)  
**Key FCC orders:** 2003 TCPA Order, 18 FCC Rcd. 14014; 2023 Robotext/DNC Order, FCC 23-107 (codifying DNC protections for text messages); 2015 Omnibus Order, 30 FCC Rcd. 7961  
**Last updated:** May 16, 2026  
  
---  
  
## 1. Overview  
  
Section 227(c) of the TCPA regulates **telephone solicitations** to **residential telephone subscribers** and authorized the FCC to create the **National Do Not Call Registry**. Two separate mechanisms govern most modern DNC litigation:  
  
1. The **National DNC Registry** (47 C.F.R. § 64.1200(c)) — prohibits telephone solicitations to numbers registered with the FTC's DNC database.  
2. The **Internal DNC List** (47 C.F.R. § 64.1200(d)) — requires every entity making telephone solicitations to maintain its own opt-out list, with written policies, training, and procedures.  
  
Both create a private right of action under § 227(c)(5).  
  
---  
  
## 2. National DNC Registry  
  
### 2.1 Statutory authority  
  
Section 227(c)(1)–(3) directed the FCC to establish a system to enable consumers to limit telephone solicitations. The FCC adopted regulations and partnered with the FTC, which maintains the **donotcall.gov** registry. The FTC's Telemarketing Sales Rule (16 C.F.R. Part 310) created a parallel federal regime.  
  
### 2.2 The prohibition — 47 C.F.R. § 64.1200(c)  
  
> "No person or entity shall initiate any telephone solicitation to … a residential telephone subscriber who has registered his or her telephone number on the national do-not-call registry of persons who do not wish to receive telephone solicitations that is maintained by the Federal Government."  
  
47 C.F.R. § 64.1200(c)(2).  
  
### 2.3 Registration mechanics  
  
- Consumers register via donotcall.gov, by phone (1-888-382-1222), or via certain consumer apps.  
- Registration is **permanent** (no longer time-limited; original 5-year cap was eliminated in 2008).  
- Telemarketers have **31 days** after registration to stop calls. 47 C.F.R. § 64.1200(c)(2)(i).  
  
### 2.4 Application to cell phones  
  
The FCC has stated that wireless subscribers may register their numbers on the DNC registry. See FCC, *Wireless Phones and the National Do-Not-Call List* (consumer guide). DNC restrictions have long applied to wireless phones; the question whether the DNC prohibition under § 64.1200(c) applies independently to mobile numbers has produced some debate, but the modern view (codified by the FCC) is that **§ 64.1200(c)'s prohibition reaches both residential landlines and wireless numbers used for residential purposes**.  
  
The FCC's 2023 Robotext Order (FCC 23-107) **expressly codified that text messages are calls** subject to the DNC registry and that the DNC restrictions apply to wireless numbers. [VERIFY exact rule language and effective date for codification.]  
  
Note: TCPA protections under § 227(b)(1)(A)(iii) apply to **all** cellular numbers regardless of DNC registration. The DNC registry adds a parallel protection against live-agent telephone solicitations.  
  
### 2.5 Exemptions  
  
DNC restrictions do **not** apply to:  
  
- **Tax-exempt nonprofit organizations** — exempted by statute and rule. 47 C.F.R. § 64.1200(f)(15) definition of "telephone solicitation" excludes calls by tax-exempt 501(c)(3) entities calling on their own behalf.  
- **Calls with the prior express invitation or permission** of the subscriber, in writing.  
- **Calls to subscribers with an established business relationship (EBR)**, unless the subscriber has asked the seller not to call. 47 C.F.R. § 64.1200(f)(5). EBR duration: 18 months after purchase or transaction; 3 months after an inquiry.  
- **Calls by or on behalf of a person with whom the subscriber has a personal relationship** — i.e., true personal-call exception. Rare in litigation.  
- **Calls made for emergency purposes.**  
- **Calls to debt collectors and others making non-solicitation calls** — DNC only restricts **telephone solicitations**, which excludes calls "to any person with that person's prior express invitation or permission" and calls to those with EBR. A pure debt-collection call (no marketing of new services) is not a telephone solicitation.  
  
### 2.6 "Telephone solicitation" — § 227(a)(4)  
  
> "the initiation of a telephone call or message for the purpose of encouraging the purchase or rental of, or investment in, property, goods, or services, which is transmitted to any person …"  
  
47 U.S.C. § 227(a)(4). Excludes: emergency calls, calls with prior express invitation/permission, EBR calls, personal-relationship calls, and tax-exempt-nonprofit calls.  
  
### 2.7 Common defenses and plaintiff responses  
  
- **EBR defense.** Defendant claims prior business relationship. Plaintiff must show no transaction within 18 months or inquiry within 3 months, or show the consumer asked to stop calls.  
- **Number-not-residential defense.** Defendant argues the called number is a business line, not a residence. Increasingly difficult given cellular ubiquity; *Chennette v. Porch.com, Inc.*, 50 F.4th 1217 (9th Cir. 2022) — held that residential cell-phone numbers, even when used by self-employed plaintiffs, may qualify as residential for DNC purposes.  
- **Single-call rule.** Section 227(c)(5) requires "more than one telephone call within any 12-month period." A single isolated call does not give rise to a § 227(c)(5) claim. Often litigated.

## 3. Internal Do-Not-Call List — 47 C.F.R. § 64.1200(d)  
  
### 3.1 Coverage  
  
Every person or entity making "any call for telemarketing purposes" to a residential subscriber must comply with § 64.1200(d) **regardless of whether the consumer is on the National DNC Registry**. This is a separate, parallel obligation.  
  
### 3.2 Required procedures — § 64.1200(d)(1)–(7)  
  
A telemarketer must:  
  
1. **Maintain a written policy**, available upon demand, for honoring DNC requests.  
2. **Train personnel** engaged in telemarketing in the existence and use of the DNC list.  
3. **Record opt-out requests** at the time made — recording the name, if provided, and telephone number, and placing the request on the company's DNC list within a reasonable time, not to exceed 30 days.  
4. **Honor opt-out requests for at least 5 years.**  
5. **Affiliated persons or entities.** Where requested, opt-out applies to affiliated entities only if the consumer would reasonably expect the affiliation.  
6. **Identify the caller** — name of the individual caller, name of person or entity on whose behalf calling, telephone number/address at which the entity may be contacted.  
7. **Maintain in good faith** with the operation, training, and supervision necessary to comply.  
  
### 3.3 Private right of action under § 64.1200(d)  
  
A long-running circuit question — whether § 64.1200(d) is enforceable through the private right of action in § 227(c)(5) — has now mostly settled in favor of plaintiffs. The 2023 Robotext Order and the great weight of circuit authority hold that § 64.1200(d) is a regulation prescribed under § 227(c) and so is privately enforceable. See *Charvat v. NMP, LLC*, 656 F.3d 440 (6th Cir. 2011) (recognizing private enforcement); *Cordoba v. DIRECTV, LLC*, 942 F.3d 1259, 1265 (11th Cir. 2019) (assuming private enforceability); 2023 FCC Robotext Order ¶¶ X–Y [VERIFY paragraph].  
  
Some defense-side cases continue to argue that § 64.1200(d) was promulgated only under § 227(d) (technical/procedural standards), not § 227(c) (consumer protection). This argument has been rejected by most courts.  
  
### 3.4 Common § 64.1200(d) violations  
  
- No written policy at all.  
- Written policy exists but is not provided on demand.  
- No training of telemarketing personnel.  
- Failure to maintain internal DNC list.  
- Failure to relay opt-out across business units (collections placed it on collections list but not on marketing list).  
- Caller-identification disclosure missing.

## 4. Telephone solicitation rules (general)  
  
### 4.1 Hour restrictions — § 64.1200(c)(1)  
  
No telephone solicitation may be made to a residential subscriber **before 8:00 a.m. or after 9:00 p.m.** local time at the called party's location.  
  
### 4.2 Caller identification — § 64.1200(d)(4) and TRACED Act  
  
The caller must, at the start of the call, identify:  
  
- The individual caller's name (or company name if individual).  
- The name of the entity on whose behalf the call is being made.  
- A telephone number or address at which the entity may be contacted.  
  
The 2019 TRACED Act and STIR/SHAKEN framework reinforce caller-ID accuracy obligations. See Topic 10.  
  
### 4.3 Disclosure during call  
  
The caller must promptly disclose, in the case of a prerecorded message, the identity of the calling entity and a contact number, and provide opt-out instructions during the message. 47 C.F.R. § 64.1200(b)(1)–(3).  
  
### 4.4 Stop-calling-list rights  
  
Consumers may demand to be placed on the entity's internal DNC list at any time, and the entity must honor that request for a minimum of 5 years (§ 64.1200(d)(3), (d)(6)). Failure to do so is independently actionable.

## 5. § 227(c)(5) — Private right of action  
  
> "A person who has received more than one telephone call within any 12-month period by or on behalf of the same entity in violation of the regulations prescribed under this subsection may, if otherwise permitted by the laws or rules of court of a State bring in an appropriate court of that State —  
> (A) an action based on a violation of the regulations prescribed under this subsection to enjoin such violation,  
> (B) an action to recover for actual monetary loss from such a violation, or to receive up to $500 in damages for each such violation, whichever is greater, or  
> (C) both such actions."  
  
47 U.S.C. § 227(c)(5).  
  
### 5.1 Damages  
  
- **$500 per violation** (statutory).  
- **$1,500 per violation** for willful or knowing violations (treble).  
- **Actual damages** if greater.  
- **Injunctive relief**.  
  
### 5.2 "More than one call in 12 months"  
  
A § 227(c)(5) claim requires at least **two calls within 12 months** by or on behalf of the same entity in violation of § 227(c) regulations. A single call is not actionable under § 227(c)(5), though it may still be actionable under § 227(b) if the technology and consent rules of that subsection are violated.  
  
### 5.3 Stacking with § 227(b)  
  
The same call may violate both § 227(b)(1)(A) (ATDS to cell number without consent) **and** § 227(c) (DNC registry violation). Most courts allow stacking: $500 under each provision, for $1,000 per call (or $3,000 for willful/knowing). See *Charvat v. NMP, LLC*, 656 F.3d 440 (6th Cir. 2011) (stacking permitted across different TCPA provisions).  
  
### 5.4 Vicarious liability — "on behalf of"  
  
A seller may be liable for its agent's telemarketing under federal common-law agency principles, including apparent authority and ratification. *Campbell-Ewald v. Gomez*, 768 F.3d 871 (9th Cir. 2014), aff'd on other grounds, 577 U.S. 153 (2016); FCC 2013 DISH Network Declaratory Ruling, 28 FCC Rcd. 6574. Confirmed in jury verdicts including *Krakauer v. Dish Network*, 925 F.3d 643 (4th Cir. 2019) (affirming $61 million class verdict).

## 6. Selected DNC cases  
  
- ***Krakauer v. Dish Network, LLC***, 925 F.3d 643 (4th Cir. 2019) — affirmed class verdict against DISH for DNC violations by retailer; vicarious liability and willfulness instructions upheld; $61.34 million awarded.  
- ***Charvat v. NMP, LLC***, 656 F.3d 440 (6th Cir. 2011) — multiple violations per call permitted; § 64.1200(d) privately enforceable.  
- ***Chennette v. Porch.com, Inc.***, 50 F.4th 1217 (9th Cir. 2022) — residential cell numbers; standing; DNC protection extended to cell users.  
- ***Cordoba v. DIRECTV, LLC***, 942 F.3d 1259 (11th Cir. 2019) — class certification issues in DNC case; standing of class members who actually placed numbers on DNC.  
- ***Stoops v. Wells Fargo Bank, N.A.***, 197 F. Supp. 3d 782 (W.D. Pa. 2016) — professional plaintiff lacked standing where she purchased phones specifically to receive TCPA calls; cited but not widely followed.  
- ***Drew v. Lexington Consumer Advocacy, LLC***, 2016 WL 1559717 (N.D. Cal. 2016) — § 64.1200(d) violations; private enforcement.

## 7. Internal DNC list — litigation focus  
  
Modern plaintiff strategy frequently centers on § 64.1200(d) because:  
  
- The technology-driven defenses available under § 227(b) (post-*Duguid* ATDS narrowing) do not apply to § 64.1200(d) — DNC violations don't require ATDS.  
- A live-agent telemarketing call to a residential subscriber, made by a company without a written DNC policy, is actionable.  
- Discovery routinely targets the company's written DNC policy, training records, internal opt-out list maintenance, and procedures for processing opt-outs across business units.  
  
Defendants often produce a written policy in litigation; plaintiffs then attack its operation — was it followed? Were employees trained? Was the policy provided to the plaintiff on demand?

## 8. Litigation checklist for § 227(c) cases  
  
1. **Identify the number's status** — registered on national DNC registry? When?  
2. **Count the calls** — more than one in 12 months from the same entity?  
3. **Telephone solicitation analysis** — was the call "telemarketing" within § 227(a)(4)?  
4. **EBR analysis** — was there a transaction (18 mo.) or inquiry (3 mo.)?  
5. **Hour restrictions** — calls before 8 a.m. or after 9 p.m.?  
6. **§ 64.1200(d) violations** — written policy? training? opt-out honored?  
7. **Caller identification** — were required disclosures made?  
8. **Vicarious liability** — was the call made "on behalf of" a seller? Agency analysis.  
9. **Stacking with § 227(b)** — was ATDS or prerecorded voice used?  
10. **Damages: $500 per violation; $1,500 willful; stack across provisions.**
