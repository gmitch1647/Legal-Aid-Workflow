# TCPA Discovery — Initial Disclosures (Rule 26(a)(1))

**Primary authority:** Fed. R. Civ. P. 26(a)(1); 47 U.S.C. § 227(b), (c); 47 C.F.R. § 64.1200; *In re DISH Network, LLC*, 28 FCC Rcd 6574 (2013)

---

## A. Strategic context

Rule 26(a)(1) initial disclosures in a TCPA case are deceptively important. Many TCPA cases settle before any written discovery moves; in those cases, the only meaningful pre-mediation exchange of information is the Rule 26(a)(1) disclosure plus a Rule 26(f) ESI protocol.

Three things drive TCPA initial disclosures:

1. **The chain of parties.** Plaintiff must identify every entity in the call chain whose conduct is at issue.
2. **The damages-per-call arithmetic.** Damages are mechanical: number of calls x $500 (or $1,500 if willful).
3. **Vicarious liability.** Disclosures should identify documents and witnesses relevant to actual authority, apparent authority, and ratification under *DISH Network*.

---

## B. Witness disclosures — Rule 26(a)(1)(A)(i)

### B.1 Plaintiff-side witness disclosures
1. **Plaintiff** — recipient of the calls; subscriber/owner of the called number; revocation events.
2. **Household members** present during calls.
3. **Telephone carrier records custodian**.
4. **Any consumer of the called number who can testify to lack of consent**.

### B.2 Defendant-side witness disclosures
1. **Dialer system administrator**.
2. **Compliance officer / TCPA compliance lead**.
3. **Marketing or campaign manager**.
4. **Consent capture vendor representative**.
5. **Telemarketing or call-center floor supervisor**.
6. **Records custodian** for the call recording system.
7. **If a seller** — vendor management / vendor compliance officer.
8. **In-house IT** for the dialer database.

---

## C. Document disclosures — Rule 26(a)(1)(A)(ii)

### C.1 Plaintiff-side
1. Telephone bills / carrier records showing the calls.
2. Screen captures of caller ID.
3. Voicemail recordings.
4. Text messages (database export with metadata).
5. Plaintiff's notes / call log.
6. Revocation evidence.
7. DNC registration confirmation from donotcall.gov.
8. Plaintiff's purchase history with the seller.

### C.2 Defendant-side
1. **Call detail records (CDRs)** — native format with metadata.
2. **Consent records** — web-form submission, TrustedForm/Jornaya certificate, audio, double-opt-in.
3. **Internal DNC list policies and procedures** — 47 C.F.R. § 64.1200(d)(1)–(3).
4. **National DNC scrubbing records**.
5. **Dialer configuration files**.
6. **Dialer-vendor service agreement**.
7. **Marketing/Telemarketing Services Agreement (MSA)**.
8. **Scripts and approved campaign materials**.
9. **Prerecorded message audio files**.
10. **Lead-source documentation**.
11. **Prior consumer complaints and litigation involving the same campaign**.

## D. Damages computation — Rule 26(a)(1)(A)(iii)

### D.1 Individual case computation template

> **§ 227(b)(1)(A) — autodialed/prerecorded calls to cell phone:**
> N calls x $500 per call = $X
>
> **§ 227(c)(5) — DNC violations:**
> N calls x $500 per call = $Y
>
> **Willfulness — treble:**
> Up to $1,500 per call per subsection.
>
> **Maximum statutory exposure (individual):** $1,500 x N x 2 (subsections) = $Z.
>
> **Injunctive relief** sought under § 227(b)(3) / § 227(c)(5).

### D.2 Class case computation template

> Estimated class size: ~[N] persons.
> Estimated calls per class member: ~[X].
> Per-call statutory damages: $500 (or $1,500 if willful).
> **Aggregate range:** $[low] – $[high], plus injunctive relief.

---

## E. Insurance — Rule 26(a)(1)(A)(iv)

TCPA insurance coverage is notoriously contested. Many CGL policies exclude "violation of statutes." Plaintiff should request the full policy and any reservation of rights letters.

---

## F. Common defense objections and counter-positions

| Defense position | Plaintiff response |
|---|---|
| "Initial disclosures should be limited to plaintiff's individual calls." | Rule 26(a)(1) covers documents supporting pleaded claims including class claims. |
| "The dialer vendor is a third party; we don't have its documents." | Defendant has access to its own account configuration on the vendor's platform. |
| "Consent records for the class are not within Rule 26(a)(1)." | Class consent records are relevant to both liability and Rule 23 predominance. |
