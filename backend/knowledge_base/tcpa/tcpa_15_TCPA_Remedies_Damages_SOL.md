---  
  
## A. Private right of action — 47 U.S.C. § 227(b)(3)  
  
The TCPA's principal damages provision, § 227(b)(3), provides a private right of action for violations of § 227(b) (autodialed/prerecorded calls to cell phones, prerecorded residential calls, and junk faxes — implemented at 47 C.F.R. § 64.1200(a)).  
  
### A.1 Statutory damages — $500 per violation  
  
A prevailing plaintiff is entitled to *the greater of* actual monetary loss OR $500 in damages for each violation. 47 U.S.C. § 227(b)(3)(B). In practice, plaintiffs almost universally elect the $500 statutory floor — actual damages are typically trivial, and the burden of proving actual loss is rarely worth bearing.  
  
### A.2 Treble damages — § 227(b)(3) flush language  
  
If the court finds that the defendant willfully or knowingly violated the statute, the court may, in its discretion, increase the amount of the award to an amount equal to not more than 3 times the amount available under § 227(b)(3)(B) — i.e., up to $1,500 per willful or knowing violation. 47 U.S.C. § 227(b)(3).  
  
**"Willful or knowing"** — under the Communications Act definition at 47 U.S.C. § 312(f)(1), willful means the conscious and deliberate commission or omission of the act, regardless of intent to violate the law. The defendant need not know the conduct violates the TCPA — knowledge of the act itself is sufficient. *Harris v. World Fin. Network Nat'l Bank*, 867 F. Supp. 2d 888 (E.D. Mich. 2012); accord *Texas v. American Blastfax, Inc.*, 164 F. Supp. 2d 892 (W.D. Tex. 2001).  
  
Treble damages are discretionary, not mandatory. Many courts treble routinely upon a willfulness finding; some require evidence of bad faith.  
  
### A.3 Per-call / per-text / per-fax computation  
  
Each unlawful call, text, or fax = a separate violation. A two-call sequence in violation of (a)(1) yields $1,000 in baseline damages; if both calls are willful, up to $3,000. Combined with class certification, this drives the eight- and nine-figure settlements characteristic of TCPA litigation.  
  
Aggregating provisions: violations of multiple subsections (e.g., (b)(1)(A) cell-phone ATDS plus (c)(2) DNC) can yield separate awards under each, but circuits split on cumulation. *Charvat v. NMP, LLC*, 656 F.3d 440 (6th Cir. 2011), permits cumulation; some district courts decline.  
  
### A.4 Actual damages alternative  
  
Section 227(b)(3)(B) provides "actual monetary loss" or $500, whichever is greater. Plaintiffs may plead actual damages — typically for productivity loss, distress, or out-of-pocket charges — but the rarity of larger actual losses makes statutory damages the standard choice.

## B. DNC violations — 47 U.S.C. § 227(c)(5)  
  
Section 227(c)(5) provides a private right of action for "any person who has received more than one telephone call within any 12-month period by or on behalf of the same entity in violation of the regulations prescribed under this subsection" — i.e., violations of § 64.1200(c) (federal DNC) and (d) (internal DNC).  
  
### B.1 Damages — $500 / $1,500 per call  
  
Identical to § 227(b)(3): the greater of actual monetary loss or $500 per call; court may treble for willful/knowing violations.  
  
### B.2 Affirmative defense — § 227(c)(5)(C) and § 64.1200(c)(5)  
  
The defendant may invoke an affirmative defense by showing it has established and implemented, with due care, reasonable practices and procedures to effectively prevent telephone solicitations in violation of the regulations. The required showing includes:  
- Written DNC procedures;  
- Training of personnel;  
- Recording / disclosure of consumer requests;  
- Maintenance of an internal DNC list;  
- Use of a process to scrub the federal DNC database; and  
- That the specific violation resulted from error despite the procedures.  
  
In practice the defense is rarely successful — courts hold that one or two errors over many calls do not negate liability unless the defendant proves all six elements were rigorously implemented.  
  
### B.3 The "two-call" threshold  
  
A plaintiff must have received more than one call within a 12-month period from the same entity to assert a § 227(c)(5) DNC claim. This threshold is procedural; standing is a separate question (see § E).  
  
---  
  
## C. Junk fax remedies — 47 U.S.C. § 227(b)(3)  
  
Junk faxes are within § 227(b)(3): $500 per unsolicited fax, treble for willfulness. Federal Register publication of the Junk Fax Prevention Act of 2005 codified the EBR exception and opt-out notice rules now in § 64.1200(a)(4).  
  
**Notable fax cases.** *Imhoff Investment LLC v. Alfoccino Inc.*, 792 F.3d 627 (6th Cir. 2015) (standing for one unsolicited fax); *American Copper & Brass v. Lake City Indus. Prods.*, 757 F.3d 540 (6th Cir. 2014) (class certification appropriate).  
  
---  
  
## D. Class actions in TCPA  
  
### D.1 Why TCPA class actions dominate  
  
Per-call $500 statutory damages multiplied by class sizes routinely in the hundreds of thousands produce some of the largest aggregate exposures in consumer-protection law:  
- **Capital One** — $75.5 million settlement (2014, N.D. Ill.) — class of ~21 million.  
- **Dish Network / Krakauer** — $61 million judgment, affirmed *Krakauer v. Dish Network LLC*, 925 F.3d 643 (4th Cir. 2019).  
- **Facebook (Brickman)** — $25 million settlement (N.D. Cal. 2020).  
- **Bank of America** — $32 million (2014).  
- **Caribbean Cruise Line** — $76 million.  
- Numerous other eight-figure settlements.  
  
### D.2 Numerosity, commonality, and ascertainability  
  
Numerosity is almost never an obstacle — call records readily produce class sizes in the tens of thousands or more. *Krakauer* held that telephone numbers on the DNC registry plus the defendant's own call records are sufficient to ascertain class members.  
  
Commonality / predominance is sometimes contested where consent is individualized — *Gene & Gene LLC v. BioPay LLC*, 541 F.3d 318 (5th Cir. 2008), denied certification on consent issues; *Krakauer* upheld certification where individualized issues were manageable.  
  
### D.3 *Krakauer v. Dish Network LLC*  
  
925 F.3d 643 (4th Cir. 2019). The Fourth Circuit affirmed:  
- Class certification (about 18,000 class members);  
- Jury verdict of $400 per call x 51,119 calls = $20.5 million;  
- Trial court trebled to $61 million on finding of willfulness;  
- Held DNC provisions are a "model of clarity";  
- Held *TransUnion v. Ramirez* does not destroy standing for class members who received calls on numbers actually registered with the DNC.  
  
### D.4 Settlement-class issues post-*Frank v. Gaos*  
  
*Frank v. Gaos*, 139 S. Ct. 1041 (2019) (per curiam), remanded for standing analysis under *Spokeo* / *TransUnion*. TCPA settlement classes now routinely face heightened scrutiny:  
- District courts examine whether every class member has Article III standing;  
- Cy pres-only settlements are disfavored;  
- *Drazen v. Pinto* (11th Cir. 2023 en banc) clarified standing for single-text TCPA classes;  
- Settlement structures that include zero-call class members are increasingly attacked.

## E. Standing post-*TransUnion v. Ramirez*  
  
*TransUnion LLC v. Ramirez*, 594 U.S. 413 (2021), holds Article III standing requires a concrete injury "with a close historical or common-law analogue."  
  
### E.1 The standing analogues for TCPA  
  
Courts almost uniformly find TCPA harms analogous to:  
- **Intrusion upon seclusion** — the common-law tort of invasion of privacy by intrusion;  
- **Public/private nuisance** — repeated unwanted intrusions;  
- **Trespass to chattels** (for fax cases — paper, ink, line tying).  
  
### E.2 Single-text / single-call standing  
  
**Pre-*Drazen*: *Salcedo v. Hanna*, 936 F.3d 1162 (11th Cir. 2019)** — Single unwanted text held insufficient for Article III standing in the Eleventh Circuit. This created a circuit split.  
  
**Post-*Drazen v. Pinto*, 74 F.4th 1336 (11th Cir. 2023) (en banc)** — Overruled *Salcedo*. Held that a single unwanted text confers concrete injury for Article III standing, aligning with the Second, Third, Fourth, Fifth, Sixth, Seventh, Ninth, and Tenth Circuits.  
  
**Other circuits affirming single-call/single-text standing:**  
- *Susinno v. Work Out World, Inc.*, 862 F.3d 346 (3d Cir. 2017);  
- *Van Patten v. Vertical Fitness Grp., LLC*, 847 F.3d 1037 (9th Cir. 2017);  
- *Melito v. Experian Marketing Solutions, Inc.*, 923 F.3d 85 (2d Cir. 2019).  
  
### E.3 Internal-DNC standing — *Cordoba*  
  
*Cordoba v. DIRECTV, LLC*, 942 F.3d 1259 (11th Cir. 2019). The Eleventh Circuit held that the absence of an internal-DNC list (a (d)(1) violation) was a procedural violation; class members who never asked to be on the list lacked standing because they suffered no harm from the procedural failure. Standing for (d) claims requires showing that the procedural failure caused the unwanted call.  
  
### E.4 Junk-fax standing  
  
*Florence Endocrine Clinic, P.L.L.C. v. Arriva Med., LLC*, 858 F.3d 1362 (11th Cir. 2017); *Imhoff Investment LLC v. Alfoccino Inc.*, 792 F.3d 627 (6th Cir. 2015) — Unsolicited faxes create concrete harm (paper, ink, line, productivity loss); standing satisfied.

## F. Statute of limitations — 4 years  
  
The TCPA contains no express statute of limitations. The federal "catch-all" four-year limitations period in **28 U.S.C. § 1658(a)** applies. *Sawyer v. Atlas Heating & Sheet Metal Works, Inc.*, 642 F.3d 560 (7th Cir. 2011); accord *Giovanniello v. ALM Media, LLC*, 726 F.3d 106 (2d Cir. 2013).  
  
### F.1 Accrual  
  
The cause of action accrues when the violation occurs — typically when each unlawful call/text/fax is received. Each call is a separate violation; the limitations period runs separately for each.  
  
### F.2 Discovery rule  
  
Some circuits have applied a discovery rule to junk-fax claims (where the recipient may not realize the fax was unsolicited until later) — but most TCPA violations involve calls the plaintiff perceives immediately. *Stoll v. JPMorgan Chase Bank, N.A.*, 2020 WL 6587076 (M.D. Fla. Nov. 10, 2020).  
  
### F.3 FCC enforcement SOL  
  
Under the TRACED Act amendments, the FCC's administrative-enforcement SOL is 4 years for intentional violations and 1 year for non-intentional violations. 47 U.S.C. § 503(b)(6). The private-action SOL (28 U.S.C. § 1658) is unaffected.

## G. Federal jurisdiction — *Mims v. Arrow Financial Services*  
  
*Mims v. Arrow Fin. Servs., LLC*, 565 U.S. 368 (2012). The Supreme Court held:  
- Federal courts have concurrent federal-question jurisdiction over TCPA private actions under 28 U.S.C. § 1331,  
- The statutory authorization for state-court suits in § 227(b)(3) and § 227(c)(5) ("may, if otherwise permitted by the laws or rules of court of a State, bring") is permissive, not exclusive,  
- TCPA private suits are removable to federal court under 28 U.S.C. § 1441.  
  
**Consequence.** Most TCPA class actions are filed in or removed to federal court. State-court filings remain common in California, Florida, and other states with favorable plaintiff procedures.

## H. State law analogues  
  
### H.1 Florida Telephone Solicitation Act (FTSA)  
  
Fla. Stat. § 501.059. Substantially amended 2021 to add a private right of action with $500 per call statutory damages, treble for willfulness, and an expansive "automated system" definition broader than post-*Duguid* ATDS. After significant litigation explosion, the legislature narrowed it in 2023 (HB 761) — adding a pre-suit notice / 15-day cure period and tightening the "automated system" definition.  
  
### H.2 California — Consumer Legal Remedies Act and Bus. & Prof. Code § 17200  
  
California also has stand-alone telephone solicitation rules in Cal. Bus. & Prof. Code §§ 17590–17597. Civil Code § 1770(a)(22)(A) covers misleading telemarketing.  
  
### H.3 Washington CEMA  
  
Wash. Rev. Code § 19.190 (Commercial Electronic Mail Act) covers unsolicited commercial text messages with private right of action and $500 statutory damages.  
  
### H.4 Texas Business and Commerce Code §§ 302, 304  
  
Texas's mini-TCPA framework regulates telephone solicitations and contains a private right of action.  
  
### H.5 Other state analogues  
  
Maryland (Md. Code, Com. Law §§ 14-3201–14-3204); Massachusetts (M.G.L. ch. 159C); New York (Gen. Bus. Law § 399-p); Oklahoma (Okla. Stat. tit. 15, § 775A.1 et seq. — the "Oklahoma Telephone Solicitation Act," modeled on Florida).

## I. Attorney's fees  
  
### I.1 No statutory fee-shifting under federal TCPA  
  
The TCPA does NOT have a fee-shifting provision. Section 227(b)(3) and (c)(5) authorize injunctive and damages relief but say nothing about attorney's fees. *Charvat v. Echostar Satellite, LLC*, 676 F. Supp. 2d 668 (S.D. Ohio 2009).  
  
### I.2 Common-fund / lodestar in class actions  
  
In class actions, plaintiffs' counsel recover fees from the common settlement fund under *Boeing Co. v. Van Gemert*, 444 U.S. 472 (1980), typically using a percentage-of-fund (often 25–33%) or lodestar method with multiplier. Fees on TCPA class settlements are routine and substantial.  
  
### I.3 State-law fees  
  
Several state-law TCPA analogues do shift fees:  
- FTSA pre-2023 had attorney-fees; the 2023 amendments removed them prospectively;  
- Some state UDAP statutes shift fees and can be plead alongside TCPA.  
  
### I.4 Pro se / individual plaintiff actions  
  
Individual plaintiffs proceeding pro se or with limited counsel cannot recover fees under the federal TCPA. Practical implication: most non-class TCPA cases are leveraged for $500–$3,000 per-call statutory damages plus pre-judgment interest, with no fee recovery.

## J. Defendant remedies and affirmative defenses  
  
### J.1 No bona fide error defense  
  
Unlike the FDCPA (15 U.S.C. § 1692k(c)), the TCPA does NOT contain a bona-fide-error defense for unintentional violations.  
  
### J.2 The (c)(5)(C) / § 64.1200(c)(5) compliance defense  
  
Only available for § 227(c) (DNC) claims. Requires the six-element showing detailed in § B.2 above. Rarely successful.  
  
### J.3 Reassigned-number safe harbor  
  
47 C.F.R. § 64.1200(m). Available only to callers who queried the FCC Reassigned Numbers Database immediately before placing the call and received a "no" return. Documentation is essential.  
  
### J.4 Express consent defense  
  
The defendant may show prior express consent (for non-marketing autodialed/prerecorded cell calls) or prior express written consent (for marketing). Consent is an affirmative defense the defendant must plead and prove. *Van Patten v. Vertical Fitness Grp., LLC*, 847 F.3d 1037 (9th Cir. 2017).  
  
### J.5 Revocation defense  
  
Post-2024 Revocation Order, a defendant may show that no revocation was received, or that revocation was processed within 10 business days. Pre-2025 calls are evaluated under common-law revocation principles.  
  
### J.6 *Duguid* defense  
  
For (a)(1) ATDS-only claims (no prerecorded/artificial voice), the defendant may show the dialing system did not use random or sequential number generation. *Facebook v. Duguid*, 141 S. Ct. 1163 (2021). This defense is unavailable where the call used prerecorded or AI-generated voice.  
  
### J.7 Vicarious liability defenses  
  
Sellers facing third-party telemarketer claims may defend by showing no agency relationship under *In re Dish Network*, 28 FCC Rcd. 6574 (2013): no actual authority, no apparent authority, no ratification.  
  
### J.8 Constitutional / *Barr v. AAPC* implications  
  
*Barr v. American Ass'n of Political Consultants, Inc.*, 591 U.S. 610 (2020), held the government-debt exception to § 227(b)(1)(A)(iii) was an unconstitutional content-based speech restriction, but severed the exception rather than striking the entire statute. Pre-severance government-debt callers cannot claim retroactive immunity (*Lindenbaum v. Realgy, LLC*, 13 F.4th 524 (6th Cir. 2021)).

## K. Damages calculation worksheet (illustrative)  
  
For a TCPA case with 150 calls to a single plaintiff over 6 months, all to a cell phone using prerecorded voice with no consent and post-revocation:  
  
- 150 calls x $500 = $75,000 baseline (§ 227(b)(3))  
- All willful (continued after STOP) → court may treble: 150 x $1,500 = $225,000  
- If 75 calls also violated DNC under (c)(2): 75 x $500 = $37,500 additional under § 227(c)(5)  
- Treble those willful DNC violations: 75 x $1,500 = $112,500  
- Total maximum: ~$337,500 plus injunctive relief.  
  
In a class action of 100,000 members each receiving 10 unlawful calls: 100,000 x 10 x $500 = $500,000,000 baseline statutory exposure. This drives the rapid settlement of most certified TCPA classes.

## Practical / litigation notes  
  
1. **Plead each violation separately.** Statutory damages stack per call/text/fax and across subsections. Plead (a)(1), (a)(2), (c)(2), (d)(1)–(6) separately to maximize exposure and avoid issue preclusion.  
  
2. **Demand willfulness for trebling.** Allege facts supporting willfulness — continued calls after STOP, scripted "do not call" override, knowledge of the consumer's DNC registration.  
  
3. **Standing pleading.** Post-*TransUnion*, plead concrete harm: time wasted, interruption, fee charged, battery drain, intrusion upon seclusion. Avoid pleading only procedural violations (especially for (d) claims after *Cordoba*).  
  
4. **SOL is 4 years per call.** No tolling for ignorance of legal rules. File quickly; older calls may be time-barred.  
  
5. **Mini-TCPA leverage.** Where federal TCPA fails (e.g., no ATDS post-*Duguid*), the FTSA or analogous state statute may capture the conduct with broader definitions. Watch the FTSA's 2023 cure-period and the per-state procedural overlays.  
  
6. **No fee shifting.** Plaintiff lawyers in non-class cases must price representation against $500/$1,500 per-call recoveries — typically meaning contingency-only with damages aggregation.  
  
7. **Defense pre-suit posture.** Defendants benefit from rigorous PEWC documentation, RND queries, "STOP" reply automation, internal DNC training records, and STIR/SHAKEN compliance. These reduce exposure but do not constitute a global affirmative defense.
