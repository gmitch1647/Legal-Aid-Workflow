## A. Foundational orders  
  
### A.1 1992 FCC Order implementing the TCPA  
  
**Citation.** *Rules and Regulations Implementing the Telephone Consumer Protection Act of 1991*, Report and Order, 7 FCC Rcd. 8752 (1992).  
**Year.** 1992.  
**Summary.** The original Order codified the TCPA in 47 C.F.R. § 64.1200 and established:  
- The original ATDS definition,  
- The cell-phone autodialed/prerecorded prohibition,  
- The first internal-DNC framework (declining to create a federal DNC registry at that time),  
- Time-of-day restrictions (8 a.m.–9 p.m. local time),  
- The original junk-fax rules.  
**Why it matters.** The 1992 Order remains foundational. Most subsequent rule amendments are framed as modifications to its structures (especially (a), (c), (d)).  
  
### A.2 2003 FCC Order — establishing the DNC framework  
  
**Citation.** *Rules and Regulations Implementing the TCPA*, Report and Order, 18 FCC Rcd. 14014 (2003) (CG Docket No. 02-278).  
**Year.** 2003 (effective Oct. 1, 2003 for most provisions).  
**Summary.**  
- Created the joint FCC/FTC National Do-Not-Call Registry framework,  
- Held wireless subscribers are "residential subscribers" for DNC purposes,  
- Confirmed text messages count as "calls" for § 227 purposes,  
- Defined EBR and the 18-month/3-month parameters,  
- Required telemarketers to honor DNC requests within a reasonable time (later capped at 30 days),  
- Established the affirmative defense in (c)(5).  
**Why it matters.** This is the single most important FCC rulemaking. Most modern TCPA litigation is keyed to the structures the 2003 Order built.  
  
### A.3 2008 FCC Declaratory Ruling — phone number on application = prior express consent  
  
**Citation.** *In re Rules and Regulations Implementing the TCPA*, Declaratory Ruling, 23 FCC Rcd. 559 (2008).  
**Year.** 2008.  
**Summary.** Held that consumers who knowingly provide a cell-phone number to a creditor in connection with an existing debt have given "prior express consent" to be contacted at that number regarding the debt — a critical ruling that opened the door to debt-collection robocalls absent prior written consent. This was construed narrowly: consent extends only to calls "regarding" the debt, not unrelated marketing.  
**Why it matters.** A primary defense for debt collectors. Plaintiffs respond by showing the call subject matter exceeded the scope of consent, or that consent was revoked.

## B. 2012 FCC TCPA Order — prior express written consent for marketing  
  
**Citation.** *In re Rules and Regulations Implementing the TCPA*, Report and Order, 27 FCC Rcd. 1830 (2012) (FCC 12-21).  
**Year.** Adopted February 15, 2012; effective October 16, 2013.  
**Summary.**  
- Created a new tier of consent: "prior express written consent" (PEWC) under § 64.1200(f)(8) (now (f)(9)),  
- PEWC is now required for any telemarketing/advertising call/text to a cell phone using ATDS or prerecorded voice, and for any prerecorded telemarketing call to a residential line,  
- Eliminated the EBR exception for prerecorded telemarketing calls to residential lines,  
- PEWC must be a signed agreement clearly and conspicuously disclosing that the consumer will receive autodialed/prerecorded marketing calls, including the specific cell-phone number; cannot be a condition of purchase. E-SIGN signatures qualify.  
**Why it matters.** Created the leading source of modern wireless TCPA litigation. Plaintiffs frequently challenge whether the PEWC disclosures were clear, conspicuous, and unambiguous; whether the seller is specifically identified; and whether consent was a condition of purchase.

## C. 2015 FCC Omnibus TCPA Order  
  
**Citation.** *In re Rules and Regulations Implementing the TCPA*, Declaratory Ruling and Order, 30 FCC Rcd. 7961 (2015) (FCC 15-72).  
**Year.** July 10, 2015.  
**Summary.** Wide-ranging interpretive ruling:  
- Expansive interpretation of "ATDS" — any equipment with the future capacity to generate and dial random/sequential numbers,  
- Reaffirmed text messages are calls,  
- Held that a single revocation by any "reasonable means" suffices and that called parties may revoke at any time and by any method,  
- Adopted a one-call safe harbor for reassigned numbers based on "reasonable reliance" on prior consent,  
- Identified the "called party" as the current subscriber.  
**Why it matters.** This Order triggered a wave of class actions. It was largely overruled by:  
- *ACA Int'l v. FCC*, 885 F.3d 687 (D.C. Cir. 2018), vacating the expansive ATDS interpretation and the one-call reassigned-number safe harbor, and  
- *Facebook v. Duguid*, 141 S. Ct. 1163 (2021), adopting a narrow ATDS definition.  
  
The 2015 Order's revocation analysis was incorporated and superseded by the 2024 Revocation Order.

## D. 2018 *ACA International v. FCC* — D.C. Circuit  
  
**Citation.** *ACA Int'l v. FCC*, 885 F.3d 687 (D.C. Cir. 2018).  
**Year.** 2018.  
**Summary.** The D.C. Circuit set aside two key parts of the 2015 Order:  
- The expansive ATDS interpretation (vacated as arbitrary and capricious),  
- The one-call reassigned-number safe harbor (also vacated).  
**Why it matters.** Forced the FCC to start over on ATDS and reassigned-number issues. ATDS analysis sat in limbo from 2018 until *Duguid* (2021). The reassigned-number issue was eventually addressed by the FCC's 2018 Reassigned Numbers Database Order (below).

## E. 2018 FCC Reassigned Numbers Database Order  
  
**Citation.** *Advanced Methods to Target and Eliminate Unlawful Robocalls*, Second Report and Order, 33 FCC Rcd. 12024 (2018) (FCC 18-177); codified in 47 C.F.R. § 64.1200(m).  
**Year.** December 12, 2018; database operational November 1, 2021.  
**Summary.**  
- Established a single, comprehensive Reassigned Numbers Database (RND) administered by Somos,  
- Service providers must report permanent disconnections,  
- Created a safe harbor against TCPA liability for callers who query the database and rely on a "no" return immediately before placing a call,  
- Safe harbor does not protect callers who do not query the database, who rely on stale queries, or who receive a "yes" or "no data" return.  
**Why it matters.** The RND is the primary means for callers to manage post-*ACA International* reassigned-number risk. Plaintiffs increasingly assert TCPA claims on numbers reassigned to them; the RND safe harbor is the principal defense, but only if database use is documented.

## F. 2023 FCC One-to-One Consent Order  
  
**Citation.** *Targeting and Eliminating Unlawful Text Messages, Rules and Regulations Implementing the TCPA*, Second Report and Order, FCC 23-107 (Dec. 13, 2023).  
**Year.** December 2023; rules originally set to be effective January 27, 2025; partially stayed.  
**Summary.** Amended (f)(9) to require:  
- Consent to be obtained for *one* seller at a time (not a generalized consent to a list of partner companies),  
- Calls/texts must be "logically and topically associated" with the website/interaction giving rise to consent.  
**Disposition.** Vacated by the Eleventh Circuit in *Insurance Marketing Coalition Ltd. v. FCC*, 127 F.4th 303 (11th Cir. Jan. 24, 2025). The court held the FCC exceeded its statutory authority — the TCPA requires "prior express consent," not "prior express consent plus."  
**FCC follow-up.** On July 14, 2025, the FCC issued an Order halting the proposed (f)(9) amendment and reverting to the pre-2023, common-law definition of prior express consent.  
**Lead-generation industry implications.** Comparison-shopping and lead-gen websites had restructured their consent flows during 2024 in anticipation of the rule. Post-vacatur, multi-seller "buddy" consent flows are again permissible under the federal rule, though plaintiffs continue to challenge them under the common-law "express consent" standard.

## G. 2024 FCC Revocation of Consent Order  
  
**Citation.** *In re Rules and Regulations Implementing the TCPA*, Report and Order, FCC 24-24 (Feb. 16, 2024); codified at 47 C.F.R. § 64.1200(a)(10).  
**Year.** February 2024; partial effective date April 11, 2025; remainder waived until April 11, 2026.  
**Summary.**  
- "STOP," "QUIT," "END," "REVOKE," "OPT OUT," "CANCEL," and "UNSUBSCRIBE" are presumed effective revocations when sent in reply,  
- Any reasonable expression of revocation suffices,  
- Callers must process revocations within a maximum of 10 business days,  
- A revocation in response to one type of robocall/robotext applies to *all* future robocalls and robotexts from that caller on unrelated topics — this is the "company-wide" rule,  
- One confirmation message is permissible within 5 minutes; further confirmations are barred.  
**Implementation timeline (as of May 2026).**  
- The 10-business-day rule and the "any reasonable manner" rule became effective April 11, 2025,  
- The cross-topic "company-wide" rule is waived until April 11, 2026 (CGB DA 25-312, April 7, 2025).  
**Why it matters.** Most TCPA plaintiff complaints filed in 2025–26 are post-revocation cases. Defendants must produce evidence that the consumer's "STOP" or other revocation was processed within 10 business days; failure exposes per-call statutory damages.

## H. 2024 FCC AI Voice Declaratory Ruling  
  
**Citation.** *In re Implications of Artificial Intelligence Technologies on Protecting Consumers from Unwanted Robocalls*, Declaratory Ruling, FCC 24-17 (Feb. 8, 2024).  
**Year.** February 2024 (effective immediately).  
**Summary.**  
- AI-generated voices — whether wholly synthetic or cloned from a real person's voice — are "artificial voice" within § 227(b)(1)(A) and § 64.1200(a)(1),  
- All AI-voice calls to cell phones therefore require prior express consent (and PEWC for marketing),  
- AI-voice calls to residential lines for telemarketing require PEWC,  
- Callers must also satisfy (b) identification and disclosure rules.  
**Why it matters.** The ruling immediately criminalized voice-cloning robocall scams (e.g., fake-Biden New Hampshire primary calls). It also brought legitimate AI-voice marketing (e.g., AI customer service callbacks) within the PEWC regime. Civil litigation under this theory has accelerated rapidly.

## I. TRACED Act of 2019 and STIR/SHAKEN  
  
**Citation.** Pallone-Thune Telephone Robocall Abuse Criminal Enforcement and Deterrence Act ("TRACED Act"), Pub. L. 116-105, 133 Stat. 3274 (Dec. 30, 2019).  
**Year.** 2019.  
**Summary.**  
- Required FCC to mandate STIR/SHAKEN caller-ID authentication framework on IP networks,  
- Increased TCPA enforcement penalties (up to $10,000 per intentional violation by FCC),  
- Extended statute of limitations for FCC enforcement actions to four years (intentional) / one year (non-intentional),  
- Required the FCC to reassess all (a)(3) exemptions, leading to the December 2020 TRACED Order (FCC 20-186) and codification at § 64.1200(a)(9),  
- Required FCC to establish a registry of authorized providers and a Robocall Mitigation Database,  
- Mandated annual reporting and DOJ coordination.  
**STIR/SHAKEN implementation.**  
- *Report and Order on STIR/SHAKEN*, FCC 20-42 (Mar. 31, 2020), required large carriers to implement by June 30, 2021,  
- Small / rural carrier deadline extended to June 30, 2023; later shortened to June 30, 2022 for many smaller providers (FCC 21-122),  
- Codified at 47 C.F.R. §§ 64.6300–64.6308.  
**Why it matters.** The TRACED Act is the principal post-TCPA enforcement statute. Providers that fail to implement STIR/SHAKEN and file robocall mitigation plans face removal from the Robocall Mitigation Database, which effectively cuts them off from the U.S. telephone network.

## J. *In re Dish Network* — vicarious liability framework  
  
**Citation.** *In re DISH Network, LLC*, Declaratory Ruling, 28 FCC Rcd. 6574 (2013) (FCC 13-54).  
**Year.** May 9, 2013.  
**Summary.** Held that while a "seller" does not "initiate" a call placed by a third-party telemarketer, a seller may be held vicariously liable under federal common-law agency principles for TCPA violations committed by a telemarketer acting on its behalf. The Order recognizes three theories:  
- **Actual / formal agency.** Seller controls the manner and means of the telemarketer's work.  
- **Apparent authority.** Seller manifests to third parties that the telemarketer is its agent (e.g., access to seller's customer database, use of trade name).  
- **Ratification.** Seller knows of the unauthorized conduct and accepts the benefits.  
**Subsequent adoption.** The Ninth Circuit adopted the framework in *Gomez v. Campbell-Ewald*, 768 F.3d 871 (9th Cir. 2014), aff'd on other grounds 577 U.S. 153 (2016). The Fourth Circuit applied it in *Krakauer v. Dish Network*, 925 F.3d 643 (4th Cir. 2019).  
**Why it matters.** Sellers cannot insulate themselves from TCPA liability simply by hiring third-party telemarketers. This is the principal liability theory in modern lead-generation cases.

## K. CFPB / FTC interaction and parallel frameworks  
  
### K.1 FTC Telemarketing Sales Rule (TSR)  
  
**Citation.** 16 C.F.R. § 310 (TSR), implementing the Telemarketing and Consumer Fraud and Abuse Prevention Act, 15 U.S.C. §§ 6101–6108.  
**Summary.** Parallel framework covering:  
- Federal Do-Not-Call (jointly administered with FCC),  
- Required disclosures,  
- Prerecorded telemarketing call prohibitions (16 C.F.R. § 310.4(b)(1)(v)),  
- Abandoned-call rate,  
- Caller-ID transmission,  
- $50,591 civil penalties (as of 2025 adjustment) per violation; no private right of action.  
**Overlap with TCPA.** Many TCPA-actionable calls are simultaneously TSR-actionable. FTC enforcement complements private TCPA actions. Consumers cannot sue under the TSR directly but may benefit from FTC orders and settlements.  
  
### K.2 CFPB Debt Collection Rule (Regulation F)  
  
**Citation.** 12 C.F.R. § 1006 (Reg. F), implementing the FDCPA.  
**Summary.** Limits debt collectors' communications, including a "7-in-7" call cap. CFPB and FTC coordinate with FCC on debt-collection robocalls.  
**TCPA overlap.** A single debt-collection call may simultaneously violate TCPA (a)(1) (cell-phone autodialed), FDCPA § 1692d(5) (harassment by repeated calls), and Reg. F. Cumulative damages apply unless statutes preclude.  
  
### K.3 Cross-enforcement coordination  
  
The FCC, FTC, CFPB, and DOJ jointly maintain the Industry Traceback Group (ITG), which traces illegal robocalls back through providers. The 2024 Robocall Response Team brings agencies together to coordinate enforcement.

## L. Healthcare exemption — *Cohen* and FDA-related issues  
  
### L.1 The healthcare exemption  
  
**Source.** § 64.1200(a)(2) (HIPAA-covered exemption); § 64.1200(a)(3)(v) (residential).  
**Coverage.** Calls delivering a "health-care message" made by, or on behalf of, a "covered entity" or "business associate" (as defined in HIPAA Privacy Rule, 45 C.F.R. § 160.103), to a wireless number provided by the patient, fall outside the PEWC requirement of (a)(2) — though they remain subject to other TCPA requirements (e.g., the called party retains revocation rights).  
  
### L.2 *Cohen v. Apple, Inc.* and FDA-product issues  
  
**Citation lineage.** Multiple FCC declaratory rulings and consent decrees have addressed pharmacy refill-reminder calls; e.g., *Anda, Inc. Petition for Expedited Declaratory Ruling*, 29 FCC Rcd. 13998 (2014). Pharmacy refill-reminder calls and prescription notifications fall within (a)(2)/(a)(3)(v) exemptions if they meet HIPAA-business-associate criteria. Marketing of health-related products (as opposed to existing-patient communications) is NOT within the exemption — *Latner v. Mt. Sinai Health System*, 879 F.3d 52 (2d Cir. 2018), distinguished between marketing and patient-care communications.  
  
### L.3 *Aderhold v. car2go N.A.*  
  
**Citation.** No. C13-489RAJ, 2014 WL 794802 (W.D. Wash. Feb. 27, 2014); 668 F. App'x 795 (9th Cir. 2016).  
**Why mentioned.** Illustrative of how courts have analyzed whether a one-time confirmation text (not health-care related) requires PEWC.

## Practical / litigation notes  
  
1. **Cite the controlling order.** TCPA pleadings should cite the specific FCC order interpreting the relevant subsection — courts give Hobbs Act-protected FCC orders binding effect under *PDR Network LLC v. Carlton & Harris Chiropractic*, 588 U.S. 1 (2019).  
  
2. **Track waivers and stays.** The 2024 Revocation Rule has a layered effective-date schedule; the 2023 One-to-One rule was vacated. Confirm current rule text on eCFR before pleading.  
  
3. **AI voice opens new theories.** Post-2024, plaintiffs increasingly plead "artificial voice" under (a)(1) rather than ATDS — avoiding the *Duguid* hurdle. The AI Voice Declaratory Ruling is the key citation.  
  
4. **Vicarious liability is plead distinctly.** Under *Dish Network*, plead actual agency, apparent authority, AND ratification separately when targeting sellers for telemarketer conduct.  
  
5. **STIR/SHAKEN evidence.** Plaintiffs increasingly use call-authentication data (or its absence) to defeat ATDS / spoofing defenses. Subpoena the originating provider's robocall mitigation database certification.
