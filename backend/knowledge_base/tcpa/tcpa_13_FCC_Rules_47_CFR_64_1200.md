47 C.F.R. § 64.1200 — captioned "Delivery restrictions" — is the FCC's principal codification of the TCPA. Subpart L of Part 64 also contains parallel rules for fax broadcasters (§ 64.1200), caller-identification (§ 64.1601), and the National Do-Not-Call (DNC) registry (§§ 64.1200(c)–(d)). This file walks each subsection in detail.  
  
Sources cited: eCFR Title 47, Part 64, Subpart L (https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-47/chapter-I/subchapter-B/part-64/subpart-L); Federal Register publications of each amendment; FCC orders cited at the relevant subsection.  
  
---  
  
## (a) Restrictions on use of telephone equipment  
  
Subsection (a) is the heart of the regulation. It implements 47 U.S.C. § 227(b) and prohibits various categories of automated and prerecorded calls absent the requisite consent.  
  
### § 64.1200(a)(1) — ATDS / artificial / prerecorded calls to specified services  
  
**Plain English.** No person may initiate any call (other than for emergency purposes or with prior express consent) using an automatic telephone dialing system (ATDS) or an artificial or prerecorded voice to:  
  
- (i) Any emergency telephone line, including 911 and any emergency line of a hospital, medical physician/service office, health-care facility, poison control center, or fire/law enforcement agency;  
- (ii) The telephone line of any guest room or patient room of a hospital, health-care facility, elderly home, or similar establishment;  
- (iii) Any telephone number assigned to a paging service, cellular telephone service, specialized mobile radio service, or other radio common carrier service, or any service for which the called party is charged for the call. This is the cell-phone provision.  
  
**Key practical implications.**  
- The prohibition applies to "calls" — which the FCC has long held includes SMS and MMS text messages. *Satterfield v. Simon & Schuster*, 569 F.3d 946 (9th Cir. 2009); FCC 2003 Order ¶ 165.  
- The "called party" is generally the current subscriber/customary user — not the intended recipient. *ACA Int'l v. FCC*, 885 F.3d 687 (D.C. Cir. 2018), set aside the FCC's contrary 2015 interpretation; the FCC's 2018 reassigned-numbers-database order (§ 64.1200(m)) supplies the safe harbor.  
- After *Facebook, Inc. v. Duguid*, 141 S. Ct. 1163 (2021), "ATDS" requires use of a random or sequential number generator to store or produce the number. Artificial/prerecorded-voice calls remain restricted regardless of ATDS status.  
  
**Recent amendments.** The 2024 AI-Voice Declaratory Ruling (FCC 24-17) confirmed that AI-generated voices constitute "artificial voice" under § 227(b)(1)(A) and § 64.1200(a)(1) — bringing voice-cloned and synthesized calls within the cell-phone prohibition.  
  
### § 64.1200(a)(2) — Prior express written consent for marketing autodialed / prerecorded calls to wireless  
  
**Plain English.** Telemarketing calls using an ATDS or artificial/prerecorded voice to cell phones require prior express written consent (PEWC) — not just prior express consent.  
  
**Definition of PEWC** is in (f)(9): a signed written agreement, in clear and conspicuous terms, identifying the specific seller, authorizing calls or texts using an ATDS or prerecorded/artificial voice, including the cell number, with a disclosure that consent is not a condition of purchase. E-SIGN signatures qualify.  
  
**Recent amendments.**  
- The FCC's December 2023 "One-to-One Consent" Order (FCC 23-107) attempted to redefine PEWC at (f)(9) to require single-seller consent that is "logically and topically related" to the website where given.  
- The Eleventh Circuit vacated that amendment in *Insurance Marketing Coalition Ltd. v. FCC*, 127 F.4th 303 (11th Cir. Jan. 24, 2025), holding the FCC lacked authority to add restrictions beyond "prior express consent" itself.  
- The FCC issued an Order on July 14, 2025 reverting (f)(9) to the pre-2023 definition.  
  
### § 64.1200(a)(3) — Prior express written consent for residential prerecorded telemarketing  
  
**Plain English.** No person may initiate any telephone call to any residential line using an artificial or prerecorded voice to deliver a message without the prior express written consent of the called party, unless the call:  
- (i) Is made for emergency purposes;  
- (ii) Is not made for a commercial purpose;  
- (iii) Is made for a commercial purpose but does not include or introduce an advertisement or constitute telemarketing;  
- (iv) Is made by or on behalf of a tax-exempt nonprofit;  
- (v) Delivers a HIPAA-covered health-care message; or  
- (vi)–(vii) Falls within other narrow exemptions tied to Congressional / FCC findings under § 227(b)(2)(B).  
  
**TRACED Act effect.** Under § 8 of the TRACED Act (Pub. L. 116-105, 133 Stat. 3274), the FCC re-evaluated and added numerical / frequency caps and opt-out requirements to these residential exemptions in its December 2020 Order (FCC 20-186), now reflected in (a)(3)(i)–(v).  
  
### § 64.1200(a)(4) — Junk fax restrictions  
  
**Plain English.** No person may use any telephone facsimile machine, computer, or other device to send an unsolicited advertisement to a telephone fax machine, unless:  
- (i) The unsolicited advertisement is from a sender with an established business relationship (EBR) with the recipient;  
- (ii) The sender obtained the fax number through voluntary communication or from a directory/site/advertisement to which the recipient agreed to make the number available; and  
- (iii) The advertisement contains a compliant opt-out notice on the first page.  
  
**Opt-out notice requirements** are detailed in (a)(4)(iii)(A)–(E): clear and conspicuous; located on the first page; statement that the recipient may make a do-not-fax request; cost-free mechanisms (e.g., toll-free number, website, email).  
  
**EBR exception.** Junk-fax EBR is defined more broadly than the (f)(5) EBR for voice calls — it survives indefinitely until a do-not-fax request is honored.  
  
**Solicited faxes.** In *Bais Yaakov of Spring Valley v. FCC*, 852 F.3d 1078 (D.C. Cir. 2017), the D.C. Circuit set aside the FCC's rule requiring opt-out notices on solicited (consented-to) faxes. The Commission acknowledged the change in its 2019 Amerifactors ruling.  
  
### § 64.1200(a)(5) — Online-fax exemption  
  
The FCC has clarified that faxes sent to online-fax services that are received as email or stored on a computer (rather than to a stand-alone fax machine) are not "telephone facsimile machine" deliveries — and therefore not actionable under (a)(4). See *Amerifactors Financial Group LLC Declaratory Ruling*, 34 FCC Rcd. 11950 (CGB 2019).  
  
### § 64.1200(a)(7) — Calls to emergency lines  
  
Tracks (a)(1)(i): no ATDS or prerecorded calls to 911 or specified emergency lines absent an emergency purpose or prior express consent. There is no telemarketing carve-out.  
  
### § 64.1200(a)(9) — Exempted call categories with conditions (post-TRACED)  
  
The 2020 TRACED Order added (a)(9), codifying numerical and opt-out conditions on the previously open-ended exemptions in (a)(3):  
- Cap on number of calls per period (e.g., three prerecorded artificial-voice calls per 30-day period for non-commercial / non-advertising / tax-exempt categories);  
- Mandatory automated opt-out mechanism in every prerecorded call;  
- Identification of caller / responsible party.  
  
Failure to comply with the numerical or opt-out conditions strips the exemption and exposes the caller to TCPA liability.  
  
### § 64.1200(a)(10) — Revocation of consent  
  
Added by the FCC's February 2024 Revocation Order (FCC 24-24):  
- Callers must honor a revocation in any reasonable manner — including reply "STOP" or any other reasonable word/phrase;  
- The default presumption is that a "STOP" reply revokes consent for *that* type of robocall/robotext from the caller;  
- Companies must process revocations within 10 business days;  
- The expansion to "all future calls and texts on unrelated matters" was originally to take effect April 11, 2025 but was waived through April 11, 2026 (CGB DA 25-312, April 7, 2025).

## (b) Required call disclosures  
  
Section 64.1200(b) requires every artificial or prerecorded voice telephone message:  
  
- (b)(1) At the beginning of the message, identify the business, individual, or other entity initiating the call;  
- (b)(2) During or after the message, state clearly the telephone number (other than the autodialer or prerecorded-message originator's number, and not a 900 or premium number) of such business/entity at which the called party may contact the calling party;  
- (b)(3) In every case where the call is to a residential line, contain an automated, interactive voice- and/or key-press-activated opt-out mechanism allowing the called party to immediately make a do-not-call request.  
  
**Practical implications.** The (b)(3) opt-out must be announced at the outset, accessible throughout the call, and add the called party to the seller's internal DNC list automatically. The absence of any single (b) element is per-call actionable.

## (c) National Do-Not-Call (DNC) Registry  
  
Section 64.1200(c) prohibits telephone solicitations to:  
- (c)(1) Residential telephone subscribers between 9 p.m. and 8 a.m. local time at the called party's location;  
- (c)(2) Any residential telephone subscriber who has registered the number on the national DNC registry maintained by the FTC.  
  
**Coverage extension to cell phones.** The FCC has long held wireless subscribers are residential subscribers for DNC purposes (FCC 2003 Order ¶¶ 33–36). The "Robotext" Order (FCC 23-107) confirmed text messages are within the DNC prohibition.  
  
**Exceptions to (c)(2):**  
- (c)(2)(i) Prior express invitation or permission, evidenced by a signed, written agreement;  
- (c)(2)(ii) Established business relationship (subject to subscriber's request not to be called, which terminates the EBR);  
- (c)(2)(iii) Tax-exempt nonprofit organizations.  
  
**Subscription period.** Originally five years; the Do-Not-Call Improvement Act of 2007 (Pub. L. 110-187) made registrations permanent. The FCC and FTC formally adopted this in their 2008 amendments, and post-2024 the registry continues to be permanent unless the subscriber requests removal.  
  
**Affirmative defense in (c)(5).** A seller is not liable for a (c)(2) violation if it can demonstrate: written DNC procedures; training of personnel; recording and disclosure of names of consumers who request not to be called; an internal DNC list; using a process to scrub the federal DNC database; and that the violation was the result of error. This defense parallels (d) and is rarely successful at trial.

## (d) Internal DNC list and procedures  
  
Section 64.1200(d) imposes minimum standards on any "person or entity" initiating a call for telemarketing purposes:  
  
- **(d)(1) Written policy.** A written policy, available on demand, governing the maintenance of an internal do-not-call list.  
- **(d)(2) Training.** Personnel engaged in telemarketing must be informed and trained in the existence and use of the internal DNC list.  
- **(d)(3) Recording, disclosure.** A do-not-call request must be honored within a reasonable time, not to exceed 30 days from the date of the request. The internal DNC entry must persist.  
- **(d)(4) Identification of caller.** Each call must identify the seller's name; if applicable the seller's contact info; and a telephone number or address for opt-out requests.  
- **(d)(5) Affiliated persons or entities.** Requests apply only to the entity making the call and any "subsidiary" or affiliate where the consumer would reasonably expect linkage.  
- **(d)(6) Maintenance of DNC list.** Internal DNC entries must be retained for a minimum of five years.  
- **(d)(7) Tax-exempt nonprofit organizations.** Required to comply with the internal DNC list rules (a 2003 change).  
  
**Hour restrictions.** Section 64.1200(c)(1) imposes the 8 a.m.–9 p.m. local-time restriction on telephone solicitations to residential subscribers; (d) extends related procedural duties.  
  
**Standing for internal-DNC violations.** *Cordoba v. DIRECTV*, 942 F.3d 1259 (11th Cir. 2019), held that a (d) violation (absence of an internal DNC list) is a procedural-process violation that confers Article III standing only when paired with concrete injury (unwanted calls).  
  
---  
  
## (e) Tax-exempt nonprofit exception  
  
Section 64.1200(e) carves tax-exempt nonprofit organizations partly out of (c) and (d) telemarketing prohibitions — they are not subject to the federal DNC list, but they must comply with internal-DNC and time-of-day rules. This exception tracks the statutory carve-out in 47 U.S.C. § 227(a)(4) (excluding tax-exempt nonprofits from "telephone solicitation").

## (f) Definitions  
  
The 16 definitions in § 64.1200(f) are the structural foundation of TCPA compliance analysis.  
  
- **(f)(1) Advertisement.** Any material advertising the commercial availability or quality of any property, goods, or services.  
- **(f)(2) Automatic telephone dialing system (ATDS).** Equipment with the capacity to store or produce telephone numbers to be called using a random or sequential number generator, and to dial such numbers. Post-*Duguid* (2021), the random/sequential generator capacity is essential.  
- **(f)(3) Clear and conspicuous.** A notice that would be apparent to the reasonable consumer, separate from any other terms.  
- **(f)(4) Emergency purposes.** Calls made necessary in any situation affecting the health and safety of consumers.  
- **(f)(5) Established business relationship.** A prior or existing relationship formed by a voluntary two-way communication based on a purchase or transaction within 18 months (or inquiry/application within 3 months), which has not been terminated by either party.  
- **(f)(6) Facsimile broadcaster** / **(f)(7) Fax broadcaster.** Entities that transmit faxes on behalf of others.  
- **(f)(8) Personal relationship.** Family member, friend, or acquaintance of the telemarketer.  
- **(f)(9) Prior express written consent (PEWC).** A signed, written agreement bearing the signature of the called party, clearly and conspicuously disclosing that the called party authorizes the seller to deliver telemarketing calls/texts using an ATDS or artificial/prerecorded voice, including the specific number to which the consent applies; not required as a condition of any purchase. E-SIGN satisfies. (Post-July 2025 Order: reverted to pre-2023 common-law definition.)  
- **(f)(10) Seller.** The person or entity on whose behalf a telephone call or message is initiated for the purpose of encouraging the purchase of property or services.  
- **(f)(11) Sender** (fax context).** The person or entity on whose behalf a fax advertisement is sent.  
- **(f)(12) Telephone facsimile machine.** Equipment which has the capacity to transcribe text/images from an electronic signal received over a regular telephone line to paper.  
- **(f)(13) Telemarketer.** Person or entity that initiates a telephone call or message for the purpose of encouraging purchase.  
- **(f)(14) Telemarketing.** Initiation of a telephone call or message for the purpose of encouraging purchase of property/goods/services.  
- **(f)(15) Telephone solicitation.** Initiation of a call/message to encourage purchase, transmitted to any person, except calls or messages: (i) to a person with prior express invitation or permission; (ii) to a person with whom the caller has an EBR; or (iii) by a tax-exempt nonprofit.  
- **(f)(16) Text message.** A message consisting of text, images, sounds, or other information transmitted via SMS/MMS to or from a wireless number. The FCC's 2023 Robotext Order clarified that "text message" includes RCS messages but not in-app messaging.

## (g) Caller identification requirements (post-TRACED Act)  
  
Section 64.1200(g)(1)–(2) sets out caller-ID requirements specific to prerecorded artificial-voice messages and is reinforced by separate STIR/SHAKEN rules at 47 C.F.R. §§ 64.6300–64.6308.  
  
- Telephone solicitors must transmit caller-ID information identifying the responsible telemarketer.  
- Blocking of caller ID is prohibited for any telephone solicitation.  
- The TRACED Act expanded FCC enforcement authority and ordered providers to implement STIR/SHAKEN by June 30, 2021 (large carriers) and June 30, 2023 (smaller/rural; later moved up to June 30, 2022 for many smaller providers).  
  
**Robocall mitigation database.** All voice service providers must file a certification with the FCC describing their robocall mitigation programs.

## Practical / litigation notes  
  
1. Every TCPA pleading should plead the precise (a) subsection violated. (a)(1) (cell-phone autodialed/prerecorded), (a)(2) (cell-phone marketing without PEWC), (a)(3) (residential prerecorded marketing without PEWC), (a)(4) (junk fax), (c)(2) (federal DNC), and (d)(1)–(6) (internal DNC) state distinct claims with potentially distinct standing posture.  
  
2. Pleading both (c) and (d) violations is common — *Charvat v. NMP, LLC*, 656 F.3d 440 (6th Cir. 2011), holds that (c) and (d) violations are independently recoverable and *can* yield separate $500 awards for the same call. *But see* circuit splits.  
  
3. The 2024 revocation rule overlay: once "STOP" or any reasonable revocation is sent, every subsequent call is independently actionable. Plaintiffs frequently allege revocation by oral statement, written request, or "STOP" text; defendants must show compliance within 10 business days.  
  
4. Post-*Duguid*, defendants frequently obtain dismissal of (a)(1) ATDS claims if there is no allegation of random-or-sequential generation. Plaintiffs should plead artificial/prerecorded voice (or AI voice) instead, where applicable, since those theories do not require ATDS.  
  
5. The FCC's reassigned-numbers database safe harbor at (m) is narrow — applies only to calls made after a "no" return on the most recent database query.
