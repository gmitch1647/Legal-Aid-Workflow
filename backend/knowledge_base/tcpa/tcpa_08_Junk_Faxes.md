# TCPA Topic 08 — Junk Fax Prevention Act / Unsolicited Fax Advertisements  
  
**Statute:** 47 U.S.C. § 227(a)(5), (b)(1)(C), (b)(2)(D)–(G), (b)(3); Junk Fax Prevention Act of 2005, Pub. L. No. 109-21 (JFPA)  
**Regulations:** 47 C.F.R. § 64.1200(a)(4), (f)(1), (f)(5)  
**Key FCC orders / cases:** 2006 Junk Fax Order, 21 FCC Rcd. 3787 (2006); *Bais Yaakov of Spring Valley v. FCC*, 852 F.3d 1078 (D.C. Cir. 2017); *Amerifactors* Declaratory Ruling, 34 FCC Rcd. 11950 (CGB 2019), aff'd, 36 FCC Rcd. 6090 (2021)  
**Last updated:** May 16, 2026  
  
---  
  
## 1. Overview  
  
The TCPA's junk-fax provisions prohibit sending "unsolicited advertisements" by telephone facsimile machine. Originally enacted as part of the 1991 TCPA, the fax provisions were substantially amended by the **Junk Fax Prevention Act of 2005** (JFPA), which added the **established business relationship (EBR) exception** for fax advertising and codified the **opt-out notice requirement** for both unsolicited and (until 2017) solicited fax ads.  
  
Although fax litigation has declined as fax usage has declined, fax cases remain an active TCPA-class-action category, particularly involving healthcare, insurance, and B2B marketing senders.  
  
---  
  
## 2. § 227(b)(1)(C) — The prohibition  
  
> "It shall be unlawful for any person within the United States, or any person outside the United States if the recipient is within the United States … to use any telephone facsimile machine, computer, or other device to send, to a telephone facsimile machine, an unsolicited advertisement, unless —  
>  
> (i) the unsolicited advertisement is from a sender with an established business relationship with the recipient;  
> (ii) the sender obtained the number of the telephone facsimile machine through —  
> (I) the voluntary communication of such number, within the context of such established business relationship, from the recipient; or  
> (II) a directory, advertisement, or site on the Internet to which the recipient voluntarily agreed to make available its facsimile number for public distribution; and  
> (iii) the unsolicited advertisement contains a notice meeting the requirements under paragraph (2)(D) …"  
  
47 U.S.C. § 227(b)(1)(C).  
  
The prohibition applies to any person sending faxes to U.S. recipients, including foreign senders.  
  
---  
  
## 3. "Unsolicited advertisement" — § 227(a)(5)  
  
> "any material advertising the commercial availability or quality of any property, goods, or services which is transmitted to any person without that person's prior express invitation or permission, in writing or otherwise."  
  
47 U.S.C. § 227(a)(5); 47 C.F.R. § 64.1200(f)(15).  
  
Key applications:  
  
- **"Property, goods, or services"** is read broadly — covers product samples, free seminars, free trials, free reports, etc., where the underlying purpose is commercial promotion. ***Sandusky Wellness Ctr., LLC v. Medco Health Solutions***, 788 F.3d 218 (6th Cir. 2015) — formulary updates sent to physicians were **not** advertisements where their primary purpose was informational; commercial benefit was incidental. The "primary purpose" test is now widely applied.  
- **Free seminars** advertising paid services are ads. *Physicians Healthsource, Inc. v. Boehringer Ingelheim Pharma., Inc.*, 847 F.3d 92 (2d Cir. 2017).  
- **Pricing updates and product availability notices** to customers may not be ads if part of an existing business relationship and transactional. Compare *Sandusky*, supra.  
- **"Without prior express invitation or permission."** Permission can be express oral, written, or electronic; courts require evidence that the recipient affirmatively agreed to receive faxes — mere publication of a fax number in a directory is not enough.

## 4. Permissible fax advertising — EBR + opt-out elements  
  
Under § 227(b)(1)(C), an unsolicited fax advertisement is **lawful** only if all three conditions are met:  
  
1. **EBR with the recipient** — defined at 47 C.F.R. § 64.1200(f)(6) as a prior or existing relationship formed by a voluntary two-way communication between sender and recipient based on the recipient's purchase, transaction, or inquiry within a defined time period.  
2. **Number obtained voluntarily** — directly from recipient or from a directory where the recipient agreed to public listing.  
3. **Compliant opt-out notice** on the first page of the fax.  
  
If any condition is missing, the fax is unlawful even if the sender has a long-standing EBR.  
  
---  
  
## 5. Opt-out notice requirements — 47 C.F.R. § 64.1200(a)(4)(iii)  
  
The opt-out notice must:  
  
- Appear on the **first page** of the fax.  
- Be **clear and conspicuous**.  
- State that the recipient may make a request to the sender not to send any future unsolicited advertisements.  
- State that failure to comply, within the shortest reasonable time (no more than 30 days), with a recipient's opt-out request is unlawful.  
- Provide a **cost-free mechanism** for sending an opt-out request (e.g., toll-free phone number, fax number, website, email).  
- Permit the recipient to opt out at any time on any day of the week.  
  
Failure to include any required element renders the fax non-compliant — each fax is a separate $500 violation under § 227(b)(3).

## 6. The *Bais Yaakov* solicited-fax controversy  
  
### 6.1 The FCC's 2006 Order  
  
In the 2006 Junk Fax Order, the FCC required opt-out notices on **both** unsolicited **and solicited** fax advertisements. The Commission reasoned that the recipient's prior consent could be revoked, so an opt-out mechanism should be available even for solicited faxes.  
  
### 6.2 ***Bais Yaakov of Spring Valley v. FCC***, 852 F.3d 1078 (D.C. Cir. 2017)  
  
A split panel of the D.C. Circuit struck down the Solicited Fax Rule. Then-Judge Kavanaugh, writing for the majority, held that the TCPA "draws a line between unsolicited and solicited fax advertisements" — § 227(b)(1)(C)'s opt-out-notice requirement applies by its plain terms only to **unsolicited** ads. The FCC lacked authority to extend the requirement to solicited faxes.  
  
### 6.3 Aftermath  
  
- The FCC formally rescinded the Solicited Fax Rule in 2019.  
- Several class actions premised on missing opt-out notices on solicited faxes were dismissed post-*Bais Yaakov*.  
- The Second Circuit, in *Gorss Motels, Inc. v. Safemark Sys., LP*, 931 F.3d 1094 (11th Cir. 2019), and other circuits, have applied *Bais Yaakov* uniformly.  
- Solicited faxes (those sent with prior express invitation or permission) need **not** include an opt-out notice. They may include one voluntarily.  
  
### 6.4 Cert denied  
  
The Supreme Court denied certiorari in 2018, leaving *Bais Yaakov* as the controlling rule.

## 7. Fax-as-email loophole — *Amerifactors* and online fax services  
  
### 7.1 The issue  
  
Modern "efax" and online fax services receive faxes as PDFs by email rather than printing them on paper. The question: is a transmission to such a service a "fax" within the TCPA?  
  
### 7.2 *Amerifactors* Declaratory Ruling  
  
In *Amerifactors Financial Group, LLC*, 34 FCC Rcd. 11950 (Consumer & Governmental Affairs Bureau 2019), the Bureau ruled that an **online fax service that receives faxes via email** is **not** a "telephone facsimile machine" within § 227(a)(3), and so transmissions to such services are not actionable under § 227(b)(1)(C).  
  
The full Commission affirmed and clarified in *In re Amerifactors Financial Group*, 36 FCC Rcd. 6090 (2021).  
  
Practical effect: many recipients now receive faxes through cloud-based services, and many cases turn on whether the recipient's number was routed to a traditional fax machine or to an efax service. Discovery on the receiving telephony architecture is now essential.  
  
### 7.3 Distinguished — traditional fax machines  
  
The exception does **not** apply to traditional standalone or multifunction fax machines that print or store fax images directly. Those remain "telephone facsimile machines."

## 8. Damages and remedies — § 227(b)(3)  
  
- **$500 per fax** in statutory damages, or actual damages, whichever is greater.  
- **Treble damages** ($1,500 per fax) for willful or knowing violations.  
- **Injunctive relief** also available.  
- Each fax is a separate violation. Class actions involving thousands of faxes can produce multi-million-dollar exposure.  
  
### 8.1 Statute of limitations  
  
Four years under 28 U.S.C. § 1658. *Sawyer v. Atlas Heating & Sheet Metal Works, Inc.*, 642 F.3d 560 (7th Cir. 2011).  
  
### 8.2 Cy pres and class settlements  
  
Fax class settlements have produced significant cy pres distributions. Courts have approved settlements ranging from a few cents per class member to face-value $500 per fax.

## 9. Major fax cases (additional)  
  
- ***Holtzman v. Turza***, 728 F.3d 682 (7th Cir. 2013) — fax sent on behalf of CPA was an ad; "sender" liable; $4.2 million class judgment.  
- ***Sandusky Wellness Ctr., LLC v. ASD Specialty Healthcare, Inc.***, 863 F.3d 460 (6th Cir. 2017) — opt-out notice missing required elements; per-fax violation.  
- ***Spine & Sports Chiropractic, Inc. v. ZirMed, Inc.***, 715 F. App'x 422 (6th Cir. 2017) — applying *Bais Yaakov* to dismiss solicited-fax class.  
- ***Career Counseling, Inc. v. AmeriFactors Fin. Grp., LLC***, 91 F.4th 202 (4th Cir. 2024) — declining Hobbs Act preclusion challenge to *Amerifactors* ruling [VERIFY exact holding].  
- ***Imhoff Inv., L.L.C. v. Alfoccino, Inc.***, 792 F.3d 627 (6th Cir. 2015) — sender liability includes "on whose behalf" a fax was sent.

## 10. The "sender" — who is liable  
  
47 C.F.R. § 64.1200(f)(11) defines **"sender"** as "the person or entity on whose behalf a facsimile unsolicited advertisement is sent or whose goods or services are advertised or promoted in the unsolicited advertisement."  
  
Liability flows to the entity whose goods/services are advertised, not merely the fax broadcaster. *Imhoff*, 792 F.3d at 635. This forecloses the common defense that the sender was merely "a marketing vendor."

## 11. Class-action history  
  
Junk-fax cases drove much of the TCPA class-action wave of 2005–2018 because:  
  
- Each fax = a $500 violation, multiplied across thousands of recipients.  
- Class certification questions (commonality, predominance) were typically straightforward when the same fax campaign was sent to many recipients.  
- TCPA fax cases were resilient to *Spokeo* standing challenges.  
  
The market has cooled post-*Bais Yaakov* and *Amerifactors*, but live disputes continue, especially over:  
  
- Whether a particular fax meets the "primary purpose" test for an ad (*Sandusky*).  
- Whether a recipient gave "prior express invitation or permission" (consent).  
- Whether opt-out notices on **unsolicited** ads meet all required elements.  
- Whether the recipient's system constitutes a "telephone facsimile machine" or a *Amerifactors*-style online service.  
  
---  
  
## 12. Litigation checklist for fax cases  
  
1. **Was the transmission a "fax" under the statute?** Examine the receiving system; was it a traditional fax machine or an online service? Discovery should target telephony architecture.  
2. **Was the fax an "advertisement"?** Apply *Sandusky* primary-purpose test.  
3. **Solicited or unsolicited?** If solicited (with prior express invitation or permission), claims are limited post-*Bais Yaakov* to consent/revocation issues.  
4. **EBR analysis** for unsolicited ads. Does the sender have an EBR? Was the number obtained properly?  
5. **Opt-out notice analysis.** Every element of 47 C.F.R. § 64.1200(a)(4)(iii) must be present.  
6. **Sender identification.** Identify all "senders" — both the broadcaster and the advertiser.  
7. **Count faxes** for damages and class size.  
8. **Statute of limitations** — four years.
