# TCPA Topic 07 — § 227(b) Restrictions on Calls  
  
**Statute:** 47 U.S.C. § 227(b)  
**Regulations:** 47 C.F.R. § 64.1200(a)(1)–(3), (a)(9), (b)  
**Key FCC orders:** 2003 TCPA Order (18 FCC Rcd. 14014); 2012 TCPA Order (27 FCC Rcd. 1830) (prior express written consent for marketing); 2015 Omnibus Order (30 FCC Rcd. 7961); In re Implications of AI Technologies, FCC 24-17 (Feb. 8, 2024) (AI Voice Declaratory Ruling)  
**Last updated:** May 16, 2026  
  
---  
  
## 1. Overview  
  
Section 227(b) is the core operative restriction of the TCPA. It prohibits, with narrow exceptions, the use of:  
  
- An **automatic telephone dialing system (ATDS)** to call certain numbers;  
- An **artificial or prerecorded voice** to call certain numbers;  
- An unsolicited **fax advertisement** (covered separately in Topic 08);  
- Calls or texts to certain protected lines (emergency, healthcare, paging, etc.).  
  
Violations carry a private right of action under § 227(b)(3) with statutory damages of $500 per violation, treble damages ($1,500) for willful or knowing violations.  
  
The provision has two principal subdivisions of interest: **§ 227(b)(1)(A)** (cellular and other specified service lines, with ATDS or artificial/prerecorded voice) and **§ 227(b)(1)(B)** (residential lines, prerecorded voice).  
  
---  
  
## 2. § 227(b)(1)(A) — ATDS / artificial / prerecorded voice to specified lines  
  
It is unlawful for any person within the United States, or any person outside the United States if the recipient is within the United States —  
  
> "to make any call (other than a call made for emergency purposes or made with the prior express consent of the called party) using any automatic telephone dialing system or an artificial or prerecorded voice —  
>  
> (i) to any emergency telephone line (including any 911 line and any emergency line of a hospital, medical physician or service office, health care facility, poison control center, or fire protection or law enforcement agency);  
>  
> (ii) to the telephone line of any guest room or patient room of a hospital, health care facility, elderly home, or similar establishment; or  
>  
> (iii) to any telephone number assigned to a paging service, cellular telephone service, specialized mobile radio service, or other radio common carrier service, or any service for which the called party is charged for the call …"  
  
47 U.S.C. § 227(b)(1)(A).  
  
### 2.1 Subsection (A)(i) — Emergency lines  
  
Calls using ATDS or artificial/prerecorded voice to 911, hospital, poison control, or law-enforcement emergency lines are flatly prohibited (subject to the "emergency purpose" exception and prior express consent). Litigation is rare; mostly an FCC enforcement provision.  
  
### 2.2 Subsection (A)(ii) — Patient/guest room lines  
  
Hospital patient lines, hotel guest-room lines, and elderly-home rooms are protected. Used in occasional litigation against health-care marketers calling patient rooms.  
  
### 2.3 Subsection (A)(iii) — Cellular and paging numbers  
  
This is the heart of TCPA practice. It restricts ATDS and artificial/prerecorded-voice calls to **any** cellular number, paging number, or specialized mobile radio number, regardless of whether the number is on the DNC registry and regardless of whether the call is marketing or transactional.  
  
Key points:  
  
- **"Call" includes text messages.** *Campbell-Ewald Co. v. Gomez*, 577 U.S. 153 (2016) (text messages are calls under TCPA); *Satterfield v. Simon & Schuster*, 569 F.3d 946 (9th Cir. 2009).  
- **Consent required is "prior express consent"** for non-marketing; **"prior express written consent"** is required if the call constitutes telemarketing under 47 C.F.R. § 64.1200(a)(2). See Topic 05.  
- **Reassigned numbers.** ***Soppet v. Enhanced Recovery Co.***, 679 F.3d 637 (7th Cir. 2012) (Easterbrook, J.): the "called party" is the **current subscriber** at the time of the call, not the consenting party who once held the number. A debt collector who calls a reassigned cellular number violates § 227(b)(1)(A)(iii) unless the new subscriber consented. The FCC's Reassigned Numbers Database was created in 2018 to give callers a safe harbor for documented database checks (47 C.F.R. § 64.1200(a)(4)(iv)).  
- **ATDS definition.** Narrowed by ***Facebook, Inc. v. Duguid***, 592 U.S. 395 (2021): an ATDS must use a random or sequential number generator to store or produce numbers. See Topic 03.  
  
### 2.4 Subsection (A)(iv) — Charged-for calls  
  
Captures calls to numbers where the recipient is charged for the call. Largely overlaps with (A)(iii) for cellular service.

## 3. § 227(b)(1)(B) — Prerecorded calls to residential lines  
  
> "to initiate any telephone call to any residential telephone line using an artificial or prerecorded voice to deliver a message without the prior express consent of the called party, unless the call is initiated for emergency purposes, is made solely pursuant to the collection of a debt owed to or guaranteed by the United States, or is exempted by rule or order by the Commission under paragraph (2)(B) …"  
  
47 U.S.C. § 227(b)(1)(B).  
  
### 3.1 Coverage  
  
Applies to **residential** lines using an **artificial or prerecorded voice** message. ATDS is not required — the prohibition turns on the use of a prerecorded or artificial voice. Live-agent calls to residential numbers are governed by § 227(c) (DNC) rather than § 227(b)(1)(B).  
  
### 3.2 Exemptions (rule-based)  
  
FCC has promulgated exemptions under § 227(b)(2)(B) at 47 C.F.R. § 64.1200(a)(3):  
  
- **Non-commercial calls** to a residence.  
- **Commercial calls that do not constitute telemarketing** (e.g., transactional/informational).  
- **Tax-exempt nonprofit calls.**  
- **HIPAA-related healthcare calls** under specified conditions.  
  
Each exemption has numerical limits (typically 3 calls per 30 days), opt-out requirements within the message, and disclosure requirements. See In re Rules and Regs. Implementing the TCPA, 35 FCC Rcd. 14091 (2020).  
  
### 3.3 2024 AI-Voice Declaratory Ruling  
  
On February 8, 2024, the FCC adopted a Declaratory Ruling (FCC 24-17) holding that **AI-generated voices are "artificial voice"** within § 227(b)(1)(A) and (B). The ruling took effect immediately.  
  
Implications:  
  
- Voice-cloning robocalls (such as the January 2024 fake "Biden" robocall to New Hampshire voters) violate the TCPA absent prior express consent.  
- Any generative-AI voice — including TTS, voice synthesis, and voice cloning — is treated as "artificial voice."  
- Compliance requires (1) prior express (or written, for marketing) consent; (2) caller identification; (3) opt-out mechanisms during prerecorded messages (per § 64.1200(b)).  
- The Ruling does not alter the live-agent exemption; a live person using a normal voice is not "artificial."  
  
The Ruling expressly applies to political robocalls and is being enforced through FCC investigations and state-AG actions (e.g., the New Hampshire prosecution of Lingo Telecom/Steve Kramer for the fake Biden robocalls).

## 4. § 227(b)(2) — FCC implementing regulations  
  
Section 227(b)(2) authorizes the FCC to issue regulations implementing § 227(b). The primary regulations are at **47 C.F.R. § 64.1200**.  
  
Key provisions:  
  
- **§ 64.1200(a)(1)** — Mirrors § 227(b)(1)(A) on ATDS/prerecorded-voice calls.  
- **§ 64.1200(a)(2)** — Requires "prior express **written** consent" for any telemarketing call to a cellular number using ATDS or prerecorded voice (the EBR exemption was eliminated for marketing calls in 2012; effective Oct. 16, 2013).  
- **§ 64.1200(a)(3)** — Prior express written consent required for telemarketing prerecorded calls to residential lines.  
- **§ 64.1200(a)(4)** — Junk fax restrictions (see Topic 08).  
- **§ 64.1200(a)(5)–(8)** — Healthcare exemption details; package-delivery exemption.  
- **§ 64.1200(a)(9)** — Specific exemptions for non-marketing informational calls.  
- **§ 64.1200(a)(10)** — Revocation rules (see Topic 06).  
- **§ 64.1200(b)** — Required disclosures in prerecorded calls: identity of the caller, contact number, and opt-out mechanism during the message.  
- **§ 64.1200(d)** — Internal DNC requirements (see Topic 09).  
  
### 4.1 Healthcare exemption  
  
47 C.F.R. § 64.1200(a)(2) (cellular calls) provides a limited healthcare exemption for HIPAA-covered entities and business associates. Calls must (a) deliver a healthcare message; (b) not include marketing; (c) be limited in frequency; and (d) include opt-out mechanisms. The exemption was clarified in the 2015 Omnibus Order, ¶¶ 138–148.  
  
### 4.2 Package-delivery exemption  
  
In 2015 the FCC granted a limited exemption for package-delivery notifications to cellular numbers (one-time notification with specific information). 47 C.F.R. § 64.1200(a)(9)(vi). The exemption has tight content and frequency caps.  
  
### 4.3 Government-debt exception (struck down)  
  
The TCPA was amended in 2015 to exempt calls made "solely to collect a debt owed to or guaranteed by the United States." The Supreme Court struck this exemption as a content-based restriction in violation of the First Amendment in ***Barr v. American Association of Political Consultants, Inc.***, 591 U.S. 610 (2020). The remedy was severance — the government-debt exception was excised, but the rest of § 227(b)(1)(A)(iii) survived.

## 5. § 227(b)(3) — Private right of action  
  
> "A person or entity may, if otherwise permitted by the laws or rules of court of a State, bring in an appropriate court of that State —  
> (A) an action based on a violation of this subsection or the regulations prescribed under this subsection to enjoin such violation,  
> (B) an action to recover for actual monetary loss from such a violation, or to receive $500 in damages for each such violation, whichever is greater, or  
> (C) both such actions. If the court finds that the defendant willfully or knowingly violated this subsection or the regulations prescribed under this subsection, the court may, in its discretion, increase the amount of the award to an amount equal to not more than 3 times the amount available under subparagraph (B)."  
  
47 U.S.C. § 227(b)(3).  
  
### 5.1 Per-call statutory damages  
  
Each call (or text) is a separate violation. $500 per call; $1,500 per call if willful or knowing.  
  
### 5.2 "Willful or knowing"  
  
Most circuits hold that "willful or knowing" requires only that the caller voluntarily made the call — not that the caller knew it was unlawful. See, e.g., *Krakauer v. Dish Network*, 925 F.3d 643, 662 (4th Cir. 2019); *Roylance v. ALG Real Estate*, 2015 WL 1522244 (N.D. Cal. 2015). A minority view requires actual knowledge of TCPA noncompliance.  
  
### 5.3 Federal-question jurisdiction  
  
The Supreme Court resolved an earlier circuit split and held that federal district courts have **federal-question jurisdiction** over private TCPA suits in ***Mims v. Arrow Financial Services, LLC***, 565 U.S. 368 (2012).  
  
### 5.4 Standing  
  
Concrete injury satisfied by unwanted calls and texts. The Court's standing decisions in *Spokeo, Inc. v. Robins*, 578 U.S. 330 (2016), and *TransUnion LLC v. Ramirez*, 594 U.S. 413 (2021), have produced significant TCPA-standing litigation, but most circuits hold that even a single unwanted call/text is a concrete injury akin to common-law nuisance/intrusion. See *Gadelhak v. AT&T Servs.*, 950 F.3d 458, 462–63 (7th Cir. 2020); *Susinno v. Work Out World Inc.*, 862 F.3d 346 (3d Cir. 2017).  
  
### 5.5 Reassigned-number damages — *Soppet*  
  
Following *Soppet*, plaintiffs in reassigned-number cases routinely collect $500 per call. Defendants who fail to scrub against the Reassigned Numbers Database lose the regulatory safe harbor. See 47 C.F.R. § 64.1200(a)(4)(iv).

## 6. Distinguishing the consent categories  
  
Practitioners must keep three consent categories straight:  
  
| Call type | Recipient | Required consent | Source |  
|---|---|---|---|  
| Marketing (any tech) | Cellular w/ ATDS or AV/PR voice | Prior express **written** consent | 47 C.F.R. § 64.1200(a)(2) |  
| Marketing | Residential prerecorded | Prior express **written** consent | 47 C.F.R. § 64.1200(a)(3) |  
| Informational/transactional | Cellular w/ ATDS or AV/PR voice | Prior express consent | 47 U.S.C. § 227(b)(1)(A)(iii) |  
| Informational/transactional | Residential live agent | None required by § 227(b); may need DNC compliance | § 227(c) |  
| Emergency | Any | None required | § 227(b)(1)(A) preamble |  
  
"Telemarketing" = "the initiation of a telephone call or message for the purpose of encouraging the purchase or rental of, or investment in, property, goods, or services." 47 C.F.R. § 64.1200(f)(13).

## 7. Key cases (selected, by issue)  
  
- **ATDS scope:** *Facebook, Inc. v. Duguid*, 592 U.S. 395 (2021) — random/sequential generator required.  
- **Reassigned numbers:** *Soppet v. Enhanced Recovery Co.*, 679 F.3d 637 (7th Cir. 2012); *ACA Int'l v. FCC*, 885 F.3d 687 (D.C. Cir. 2018) (vacating FCC's one-call safe harbor; FCC later established reassigned-numbers database).  
- **Texts are calls:** *Campbell-Ewald v. Gomez*, 577 U.S. 153 (2016); *Satterfield v. Simon & Schuster*, 569 F.3d 946 (9th Cir. 2009).  
- **Government-debt exception:** *Barr v. AAPC*, 591 U.S. 610 (2020).  
- **Federal jurisdiction:** *Mims v. Arrow Fin. Servs.*, 565 U.S. 368 (2012).  
- **Willfulness:** *Krakauer v. Dish Network*, 925 F.3d 643 (4th Cir. 2019).  
- **Cellular ATDS calls — strict liability standard:** *Murphy v. DCI Biologicals Orlando*, 797 F.3d 1302 (11th Cir. 2015) (adopting 7th Cir. approach).

## 8. Litigation checklist for § 227(b) cases  
  
1. Identify the **technology** used (ATDS? Prerecorded? AI voice?). Discovery on dialer specs, software, and call records.  
2. Identify the **line type**: cellular, residential landline, or specialized (hospital, emergency).  
3. Identify the **call purpose**: marketing vs. transactional. Determines consent standard.  
4. Establish (or rebut) **consent** — prior express written consent for marketing, prior express consent otherwise.  
5. Establish (or rebut) **revocation** — see Topic 06.  
6. **Count calls** for damages: $500 per call, $1,500 if willful.  
7. Evaluate **class certification** (cellular ATDS cases are common class vehicles; *Krakauer* — class verdict affirmed).  
8. Preserve evidence: voicemails, call logs, screenshots of texts, account records.
