# 47 C.F.R. § 64.1200 — FCC Rules Implementing the TCPA

**Topic:** FCC's Implementing Rules for the Telephone Consumer Protection Act
**Primary Statute:** 47 U.S.C. § 227 (TCPA)
**Primary Regulation:** 47 C.F.R. § 64.1200 (FCC TCPA Rules)
**Regulatory Authority:** Federal Communications Commission
**Key Amendments:** 2012 TCPA Order (prior express written consent); 2015 Omnibus Order; 2020 Broadnet Order; 2023 One-to-One Consent Order; 2024 Reassigned Numbers Database rules
**Last Updated:** May 2026

---

## Overview and Regulatory Structure

47 C.F.R. § 64.1200 contains the FCC's rules implementing the Telephone Consumer Protection Act of 1991 (47 U.S.C. § 227). These rules govern the use of automatic telephone dialing systems (ATDS), artificial and prerecorded voice messages, telephone solicitation, the National Do-Not-Call Registry, and internal do-not-call lists. The FCC has primary rulemaking authority under TCPA § 227(b)(2) and § 227(c)(3), while private enforcement occurs under § 227(b)(3) (private right of action for $500 per violation, trebled to $1,500 for knowing/willful violations).

The FCC's TCPA rules are critically important for consumer litigation because the TCPA's private right of action expressly encompasses violations of the FCC's regulations. Under 47 U.S.C. § 227(b)(3), a person may bring an action "to recover for actual monetary loss from such a violation, or to receive $500 in damages for each such violation, whichever is greater." The statute further provides that the court "may, in its discretion, increase the amount of the award to an amount equal to not more than 3 times the amount available" if the violation was willful or knowing.

**Key regulatory subsections:**
- § 64.1200(a) — Restrictions on telephone equipment use (ATDS, prerecorded)
- § 64.1200(b) — Identification requirements for prerecorded calls
- § 64.1200(c) — Telephone solicitation restrictions (Do-Not-Call rules)
- § 64.1200(d) — Technical and procedural standards for DNC compliance
- § 64.1200(e) — [Reserved]
- § 64.1200(f) — Definitions

## § 64.1200(a) — Restrictions on Use of Telephone Equipment

### Core Prohibitions

No person may:

**(1) Initiate any telephone call (other than a call made for emergency purposes or with prior express consent of the called party) using an automatic telephone dialing system or an artificial or prerecorded voice:**

**(i) To any emergency telephone line** (including 911, hospital emergency, physician, health care facility, poison control center, fire protection or law enforcement agency)

**(ii) To the telephone line of any guest room or patient room of a hospital, health care facility, elderly home, or similar establishment**

**(iii) To any telephone number assigned to a paging service, cellular telephone service, specialized mobile radio service, or other radio common carrier service, or any service for which the called party is charged for the call, unless such call is made solely to collect a debt owed to or guaranteed by the United States**

This is the provision most commonly invoked in TCPA litigation. It prohibits calls to cell phones using an ATDS or prerecorded/artificial voice without prior express consent (or prior express written consent for telemarketing calls, per the 2012 amendment).

**(2) Initiate any telephone call to any residential telephone line using an artificial or prerecorded voice to deliver a message without the prior express consent of the called party,** unless the call:
- Is made for emergency purposes
- Is not made for a commercial purpose
- Is made for a commercial purpose but does not include an advertisement or constitute telemarketing
- Is made by or on behalf of a tax-exempt nonprofit organization
- Delivers a "health care" message under HIPAA

**(3) [Pre-2012 prior express consent standard — superseded by (a)(1) for telemarketing calls]**

### Prior Express Consent vs. Prior Express Written Consent

The FCC's rules distinguish between two consent standards:

**Prior Express Consent (non-telemarketing calls to cell phones):**
- Required for ATDS/prerecorded calls to cell phones that are NOT telemarketing
- May be oral or written
- Providing a cell phone number in connection with a transaction constitutes prior express consent for calls related to that transaction
- Example: Giving your cell number on a credit application constitutes consent for calls about that specific credit account (but not for marketing other products)

**Prior Express Written Consent (PEWC) (telemarketing calls to cell phones):**
- Required since October 16, 2013 for ALL telemarketing/advertising calls to cell phones using ATDS or prerecorded voice
- Must be in a signed written agreement (including electronic signatures under E-SIGN)
- Must include the telephone number to which calls may be placed
- Must clearly authorize the specific caller to deliver telemarketing messages using ATDS or prerecorded voice
- Cannot be a condition of purchase (cannot be buried in mandatory terms)

### The ATDS Definition — Post-Facebook v. Duguid

The definition of "automatic telephone dialing system" was dramatically narrowed by the Supreme Court in *Facebook, Inc. v. Duguid*, 592 U.S. 395 (2021). The Court held that an ATDS must have the capacity to:
1. Store a telephone number **using a random or sequential number generator**; OR
2. Produce a telephone number **using a random or sequential number generator**

**Pre-Duguid FCC interpretation (now overruled):** The FCC's 2015 Omnibus Order had broadly defined ATDS to include any equipment with the "capacity" to dial numbers from a stored list automatically, even without a random/sequential number generator. This expansive definition was vacated by the D.C. Circuit in *ACA International v. FCC*, 885 F.3d 687 (D.C. Cir. 2018) and definitively rejected by *Duguid*.

**Post-Duguid regulatory landscape:**
- A system that merely dials numbers from a stored customer list (without random/sequential generation) is NOT an ATDS
- Predictive dialers that dial from pre-loaded lists are likely NOT ATDS (though circuit courts are still developing this)
- The "capacity" question now focuses narrowly on random/sequential number generation capability
- Plaintiffs must allege that the specific equipment used had the capacity to store or produce numbers using a random or sequential number generator

**Impact on debt collection TCPA claims:** Post-*Duguid*, many debt collector dialing systems (which dial from stored account-holder numbers) may not qualify as ATDS. However, claims based on **prerecorded/artificial voice** messages remain fully viable regardless of ATDS status, as the prerecorded-voice prohibition is independent of the ATDS prohibition.

**Pleading strategy post-Duguid:** Plaintiff's counsel in NDGA should:
1. Plead prerecorded/artificial voice claims as the primary theory (not dependent on ATDS)
2. Allege ATDS only where factual basis exists for random/sequential generation capability
3. Plead revocation of consent as an independent theory
4. Assert Do-Not-Call violations under § 64.1200(c)/(d) which do not require ATDS

## § 64.1200(b) — Caller Identification for Prerecorded Messages

All artificial or prerecorded voice telephone messages must:

1. **Identify the caller** at the beginning of the message — must state clearly the identity of the business, individual, or other entity initiating the call
2. **During or after the message**, state clearly the telephone number or address of the caller (this number may not be a 900 number or any other number for which charges exceed local or long-distance transmission charges)

**Litigation relevance:** Failure to identify is independently actionable. Debt collectors who use prerecorded messages without proper identification violate both § 64.1200(b) and FDCPA § 1692d(6) (placing calls without meaningful disclosure of identity). This creates parallel claims under both statutes.

## § 64.1200(c) — Do-Not-Call Requirements

### National Do-Not-Call Registry

No person or entity may initiate any telephone solicitation to a residential telephone subscriber who has registered on the National Do-Not-Call Registry maintained by the FTC, unless:

1. **Established business relationship (EBR) exception:** The caller has an established business relationship with the subscriber (but this exception was narrowed — see § 64.1200(f)(5))
2. **Prior express invitation or permission:** Written or oral
3. **Tax-exempt nonprofit exception:** The call is made by or on behalf of a tax-exempt nonprofit organization

### Internal Do-Not-Call Lists (§ 64.1200(d))

**Every person or entity making telephone solicitations** must maintain a company-specific do-not-call list. This is separate from and in addition to the national registry.

Requirements:
- Must maintain a list of persons who request not to receive telephone solicitations from that entity
- Must honor requests within a reasonable time (not to exceed 30 days)
- Requests remain effective indefinitely (until consumer revokes)
- A single request covers all affiliated entities sharing the internal list

**Critical for debt collection:** Even if a debt collection call is not "telephone solicitation" (and thus the National DNC Registry does not apply), a consumer's direct request to stop calling creates enforceable rights under the internal DNC provision. Furthermore, the Eleventh Circuit has held that a consumer's revocation of consent (distinct from DNC) is effective immediately for ATDS/prerecorded calls.

## § 64.1200(d) — Technical and Procedural Standards

Entities making telephone solicitations must institute procedures to comply with DNC requirements:

1. **Written policy:** Must maintain a written policy for maintaining its do-not-call list
2. **Training:** Must train personnel engaged in telephone solicitation on DNC procedures
3. **Recording:** Must record DNC requests and maintain records for the regulatory period
4. **Identification:** Must provide the caller's name, the name of the person or entity on whose behalf the call is made, and a telephone number or address at which the caller may be contacted
5. **Time restrictions:** May not call before 8:00 a.m. or after 9:00 p.m. local time
6. **List maintenance:** Must purchase access to the National Do-Not-Call Registry data at required intervals (every 31 days for organizations making more than 3 solicitations per year)

### Safe Harbor (§ 64.1200(c)(2))

A caller will not be liable for DNC violations if it demonstrates:
1. The call was made in error
2. As part of the caller's routine business, it maintains and follows written procedures to comply with DNC requirements
3. It has an established business relationship with the subscriber OR has obtained prior express permission
4. It maintains records demonstrating compliance

**This safe harbor is strictly construed.** The caller bears the burden of proving each element. In practice, it requires:
- Written DNC policies
- Regular scrubbing against the national registry
- Employee training documentation
- Error rate tracking

## § 64.1200(f) — Definitions

### Key Definitions for Litigation

**(1) "Automatic telephone dialing system" (ATDS):** Equipment which has the capacity to store or produce telephone numbers to be called using a random or sequential number generator, and to dial such numbers. (Post-*Duguid*, this definition must be read to require the random/sequential generation element.)

**(4) "Established business relationship" (EBR):**
- **Transaction-based:** A prior or existing relationship formed by a voluntary two-way communication between a person or entity and a residential subscriber, with or without an exchange of consideration, on the basis of the subscriber's purchase or transaction within the 18 months immediately preceding the date of the telephone call
- **Inquiry-based:** A relationship based on the subscriber's inquiry or application within the 3 months immediately preceding the call

**Important limitation:** The EBR exception does NOT exempt a caller from the internal DNC list requirement. If a consumer with an existing business relationship asks to be placed on the internal DNC list, that request must be honored regardless of the EBR.

**(9) "Prior express consent":**
- For non-telemarketing calls: The called party's consent to be called at a particular number. Providing a phone number in connection with a commercial transaction constitutes consent to receive calls at that number related to the transaction.
- The consent must be given to the specific caller or its agents (not transferable without clear evidence of assignment)

**(10) "Prior express written consent" (PEWC):**
An agreement in writing that:
- Bears the signature of the called party (including electronic signature)
- Clearly authorizes the caller to deliver or cause to be delivered advertisements or telemarketing messages using an ATDS or artificial/prerecorded voice
- Includes the telephone number to which the signatory authorizes such calls
- Is not required as a condition of purchasing any good or service

**(12) "Telemarketing":** The initiation of a telephone call or message for the purpose of encouraging the purchase or rental of, or investment in, property, goods, or services, which is transmitted to any person.

**(14) "Telephone solicitation":** The initiation of a telephone call or message for the purpose of encouraging the purchase or rental of, or investment in, property, goods, or services. Essentially synonymous with "telemarketing" for DNC purposes.

## The 7-Calls-in-7-Days Safe Harbor/Presumption

While Regulation F (12 C.F.R. § 1006.14(b)) establishes the 7-in-7 presumption for FDCPA harassment claims, the FCC's TCPA rules do not contain an identical numerical safe harbor. However, the two frameworks interact:

**For debt collection calls that are NOT telemarketing:**
- The FDCPA/Reg F 7-in-7 rule applies directly
- The TCPA's harassment analysis (for purposes of revocation of consent or DNC violations) may incorporate the Reg F standard as evidence of what constitutes reasonable calling frequency

**For telemarketing calls:**
- The FCC has not established a specific call-frequency cap
- But excessive calling after DNC request or consent revocation creates per-call TCPA damages ($500-$1,500 per call)
- Courts have found as few as 1-2 calls after revocation sufficient to establish liability

**Practical convergence:** In debt collection cases involving both FDCPA and TCPA claims, the Reg F 7-in-7 standard effectively caps calling frequency for both theories. Exceeding 7 calls in 7 days creates:
- Presumptive FDCPA/Reg F harassment (§ 1006.14(b))
- Evidence supporting TCPA claims if consent was previously revoked or calls use prerecorded voice

## Post-Duguid ATDS Definition — Implications for Litigation

### What Still Constitutes an ATDS After Duguid

1. **Random-number generators** (classic "war dialers" that generate and dial random numbers)
2. **Sequential-number generators** (equipment that dials numbers in sequence, e.g., 555-0001, 555-0002, etc.)
3. **Equipment with latent capacity** to store or produce numbers using random/sequential generation, even if not presently using that function (though this is contested in lower courts)

### What Likely Does NOT Constitute an ATDS After Duguid

1. **Predictive dialers** that merely dial from a pre-loaded list of known customer numbers
2. **Power dialers** that automatically dial the next number in a list (without random/sequential generation)
3. **Click-to-call** systems where a human selects each number
4. **CRM-integrated dialers** that pull numbers from account databases

### Circuit Split on "Present Capacity" vs. "Potential Capacity"

Post-*Duguid*, courts disagree on whether the equipment must presently use a random/sequential number generator, or merely have the latent capacity to do so:
- **Narrow view (present functionality):** Equipment must actually generate numbers randomly/sequentially as configured — majority trend post-*Duguid*
- **Broader view (potential capacity):** Equipment that could be modified to add random/sequential generation qualifies — minority view

The Eleventh Circuit has not definitively resolved this split, but NDGA district courts have generally followed the narrow/present-functionality approach.

### Litigation Strategy Post-Duguid

For plaintiffs in NDGA:
1. **Lead with prerecorded voice claims** — not affected by *Duguid* and often easier to prove
2. **Plead consent revocation** — applies regardless of ATDS status for calls after revocation
3. **Assert DNC violations** — no ATDS element required
4. **Discovery on equipment** — request technical documentation, system specifications, and configuration to establish random/sequential capability if ATDS claim is viable
5. **Human intervention defense** — challenge the defendant's claim that a human was involved in initiating each call

## One-to-One Consent Rule and Its Vacatur

### The FCC's 2023 One-to-One Consent Order

In December 2023, the FCC issued a declaratory ruling and order (FCC 23-107) tightening the definition of "prior express written consent" for TCPA purposes:

**Key requirements of the order:**
1. **One-to-one consent:** Consent must be given to a single, identified seller — consent to one entity does not constitute consent to its marketing partners, lead generators, or comparison-shopping websites
2. **Logically related:** The call/text must be logically and topically related to the interaction that generated the consent
3. **Lead generator restrictions:** Multi-seller comparison-shopping websites could no longer obtain blanket consent for all participating sellers through a single checkbox
4. **Effective date:** Originally January 27, 2025

### Eleventh Circuit Vacatur — Insurance Marketing Coalition v. FCC

In *Insurance Marketing Coalition v. FCC*, No. 24-10277 (11th Cir. 2024), the Eleventh Circuit vacated the FCC's one-to-one consent order. The court held:

1. The FCC exceeded its statutory authority by redefining "prior express consent" in a manner not supported by the TCPA's text
2. The order was arbitrary and capricious because it failed to adequately consider the reliance interests of businesses that had structured lead-generation practices around the prior interpretation
3. The "logically and topically related" requirement was impermissibly vague

**Current status (as of May 2026):** The one-to-one consent order is vacated and unenforceable. The prior consent framework remains:
- PEWC may be obtained by or on behalf of a specific caller
- Third-party consent (given to a lead generator for identified downstream callers) remains valid if the consent clearly identifies the parties authorized to call
- Multi-seller comparison websites may still obtain consent for multiple identified sellers through a single disclosure, provided the consumer is clearly informed

**Litigation implications in NDGA:**
- Defendants may argue that consent obtained through lead generators is valid under pre-2023 standards
- Plaintiffs should challenge the specificity and clarity of any third-party consent — was the defendant specifically identified in the consent disclosure?
- The vacatur does NOT affect the basic requirement that PEWC must identify the authorized caller and the phone number

## How to Plead FCC Rule Violations in TCPA Complaints

### Statutory Framework for FCC Rule Violations

Under 47 U.S.C. § 227(b)(3), a private right of action exists for violations of § 227(b) or "the regulations prescribed under" § 227(b). This expressly incorporates § 64.1200 violations into the private right of action.

### Sample Complaint Allegations

**Prerecorded voice to cell phone (§ 64.1200(a)(1)(iii)):**
> "Defendant initiated telephone calls to Plaintiff's cellular telephone number [number] using an artificial or prerecorded voice, without Plaintiff's prior express consent, in violation of 47 C.F.R. § 64.1200(a)(1)(iii) and 47 U.S.C. § 227(b)(1)(A)(iii). [Number] of such calls were placed between [date] and [date], each constituting a separate violation carrying statutory damages of $500 per call."

**ATDS to cell phone (§ 64.1200(a)(1)(iii)):**
> "Defendant initiated telephone calls to Plaintiff's cellular telephone number using equipment that constitutes an automatic telephone dialing system as defined in 47 U.S.C. § 227(a)(1) and 47 C.F.R. § 64.1200(f)(1), specifically [describe equipment's random/sequential generation capability], without Plaintiff's prior express consent, in violation of 47 C.F.R. § 64.1200(a)(1)(iii) and 47 U.S.C. § 227(b)(1)(A)(iii)."

**Revocation of consent:**
> "On or about [date], Plaintiff clearly and unequivocally revoked any prior express consent by [describe revocation — written notice, oral statement during call, text message]. Notwithstanding Plaintiff's revocation, Defendant continued to place [number] calls to Plaintiff's cellular telephone using [ATDS/prerecorded voice], each in violation of 47 C.F.R. § 64.1200(a)(1)(iii)."

**Do-Not-Call violation (§ 64.1200(c)):**
> "Plaintiff's residential telephone number has been registered on the National Do-Not-Call Registry since [date]. Defendant initiated telephone solicitations to Plaintiff's number on [dates], in violation of 47 C.F.R. § 64.1200(c)(2) and 47 U.S.C. § 227(c). Defendant does not qualify for any exception under § 64.1200(c)(2)(i)–(iv)."

**Internal DNC violation (§ 64.1200(d)):**
> "On [date], Plaintiff specifically requested that Defendant place Plaintiff's telephone number on Defendant's internal do-not-call list. Defendant failed to honor Plaintiff's request and continued telephone solicitations on [dates], in violation of 47 C.F.R. § 64.1200(d) and 47 U.S.C. § 227(c)."

**Failure to identify (§ 64.1200(b)):**
> "Defendant initiated artificial/prerecorded voice telephone calls to Plaintiff that failed to identify the caller at the beginning of the message and/or failed to provide a telephone number or address during or after the message, in violation of 47 C.F.R. § 64.1200(b)(1)–(2)."

## Key FCC Declaratory Rulings Relevant to Litigation

### 2012 TCPA Order (CG Docket No. 02-278, FCC 12-21)

- Established prior express written consent requirement for telemarketing robocalls to cell phones (effective Oct. 16, 2013)
- Eliminated the established business relationship exception for prerecorded calls to residential lines
- Required all prerecorded telemarketing calls to include an automated opt-out mechanism

### 2015 Omnibus Declaratory Ruling (CG Docket No. 02-278, FCC 15-72)

- Broadly defined ATDS (later vacated by *ACA International* and overruled by *Duguid*)
- Clarified that consumers may revoke consent through any reasonable means (oral, written, any method clearly expressing revocation)
- Confirmed that a single call violates the TCPA if made without consent using prohibited equipment
- Addressed reassigned numbers (later modified by the Reassigned Numbers Database order)

### 2020 Broadnet Declaratory Ruling (CG Docket No. 18-152)

- Addressed government-contractor liability under the TCPA
- Confirmed that "on behalf of" callers (third-party vendors placing calls for a principal) are subject to TCPA rules
- Established that the principal is vicariously liable for the vendor's TCPA violations under federal common law agency principles

### 2023 Consent Revocation Order (CG Docket No. 02-278, FCC 23-107)

- Confirmed that consumers may revoke consent through any reasonable means at any time
- Codified that callers must honor revocation within a "reasonable time" (the FCC declined to set a specific number of days but suggested promptness)
- Held that text messages (including "STOP" replies) constitute effective revocation

### 2024 Reassigned Numbers Database Order

- Established the FCC's Reassigned Numbers Database (RND)
- Callers who check the database before calling and receive a "not reassigned" response have a safe harbor
- Callers who do not check the database may not use "reasonable reliance" on old consent as a defense to calls to reassigned numbers
- Database became available November 2024

### CFPB-FCC Coordination

The CFPB and FCC coordinate on debt collection communications issues. Key intersection points:
- Regulation F's call frequency limits (7-in-7) and the TCPA's consent/revocation framework
- Regulation F's limited-content message safe harbor and the TCPA's prerecorded-voice prohibition (a limited-content message using prerecorded voice still requires TCPA consent for cell phone delivery)
- Electronic communication opt-outs under both frameworks

## Damages Calculation

### Statutory Damages

| Violation Type | Per-Violation Amount | Trebled (Willful/Knowing) |
|---|---|---|
| ATDS/prerecorded to cell phone | $500 | $1,500 |
| Prerecorded to residential line | $500 | $1,500 |
| DNC violation (national registry) | $500 | $1,500 |
| Internal DNC violation | $500 | $1,500 |

### Willfulness Standard

For treble damages, the plaintiff must show the violation was "willful or knowing." Courts in the Eleventh Circuit have held this means:
- The caller knew or should have known its conduct violated the TCPA
- Continuing to call after revocation of consent is per se willful
- Calling without any consent verification procedures demonstrates willfulness
- Failure to maintain required DNC records supports willfulness

### Aggregation

Each call or text constitutes a separate violation. A debt collector making 50 calls after consent revocation faces potential liability of $25,000 (at $500/call) to $75,000 (trebled). In class actions, aggregate damages can reach millions.
