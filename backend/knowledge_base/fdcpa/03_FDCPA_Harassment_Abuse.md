# FDCPA Harassment or Abuse (15 U.S.C. § 1692d)

## The Prohibition

A debt collector may not engage in any conduct the natural consequence of which is to harass, oppress, or abuse any person in connection with the collection of a debt.

## Specifically Prohibited Conduct — § 1692d(1)-(6)

### § 1692d(1) — Threats of Violence or Criminal Means
Use or threat of use of violence or other criminal means to harm the physical person, reputation, or property of any person.

**Examples:**
- "We're going to send someone to your house"
- "You'll be sorry if you don't pay"
- Threatening to damage consumer's credit (if framed as retaliation rather than legitimate reporting)
- Threatening arrest or criminal prosecution for a civil debt

### § 1692d(2) — Obscene or Profane Language
Use of obscene or profane language or language the natural consequence of which is to abuse the hearer or reader.

**Examples:**
- Cursing at the consumer
- Using racial slurs or epithets
- Sexually degrading language
- Calling the consumer names ("deadbeat," "loser," etc.)

### § 1692d(3) — Publication of Deadbeat Lists
Publication of a list of consumers who allegedly refuse to pay debts (except reporting to a CRA or persons meeting § 1681b requirements).

**Note:** Posting a consumer's name/debt publicly on social media, a website, or a physical bulletin board would violate this.

### § 1692d(4) — Advertising Debt for Sale to Coerce
Advertisement of any debt to coerce payment.

### § 1692d(5) — Telephone Harassment
Causing a telephone to ring or engaging any person in telephone conversation repeatedly or continuously with intent to annoy, abuse, or harass any person at the called number.

**Key elements:**
- "Repeatedly or continuously" — no specific number threshold in the statute
- "Intent to annoy, abuse, or harass" — but intent can be inferred from volume/pattern
- Regulation F creates a presumption of violation at 7+ calls in 7 days

**Litigation tips:**
- Obtain phone records showing call frequency, duration, time of day
- Pattern of calls immediately after consumer hangs up = strong evidence of intent
- Calling from multiple numbers to circumvent blocking = harassment
- Letting phone ring many times without leaving a message, repeatedly = harassment

### § 1692d(6) — Failure to Identify
Placement of telephone calls without meaningful disclosure of the caller's identity.

**Requirements:**
- Must disclose: who is calling (company name) and that the call is from a debt collector
- Failure to identify when consumer answers = violation
- Regulation F: limited-content messages (voicemails) need not include mini-Miranda, but live conversations must

---

## The Catch-All — § 1692d (opening clause)

The enumerated list is NOT exhaustive. ANY conduct whose "natural consequence" is to harass, oppress, or abuse violates § 1692d, whether or not it fits neatly into subsections (1)-(6).

**Additional conduct courts have found harassing:**
- Calling consumer's elderly/ill family members
- Calling dozens of times per day
- Calling at work repeatedly after being told to stop
- Simulating urgency ("respond within 24 hours or else")
- Continuing to call after cease-and-desist letter
- Using predictive dialers that hang up when consumer answers (abandoned calls)

---

## Regulation F Call Frequency Safe Harbor and Presumptions

**Presumption of violation (§ 1006.14(b)(2)):**
- 7+ telephone calls within 7 consecutive days to a person in connection with a particular debt
- Calling within 7 days after a telephone conversation about the debt

**Presumption of compliance (§ 1006.14(b)(3)):**
- 7 or fewer calls in 7 days AND no call within 7 days of a conversation

**Important notes:**
- Presumptions are rebuttable — a collector CAN be found harassing with fewer than 7 calls if the circumstances show intent to harass
- Text messages are NOT telephone calls for purposes of the frequency presumption (but may still constitute harassment under the catch-all)
- Voicemails count as telephone calls for frequency purposes
- Unanswered calls count

## Proving § 1692d in Litigation

**Evidence to gather:**
- Complete phone records (incoming calls from collector's numbers)
- Voicemail recordings (tone, language, frequency)
- Call logs from collector's system (obtainable in discovery)
- Notes from consumer about when calls occurred and what was said
- Witness testimony (family members who heard calls/messages)
- Collector's dialer records showing call attempts vs. connections

**Damages:**
- Emotional distress from harassment (anxiety, sleep disruption, fear)
- Lost wages if calls at work caused employment issues
- Medical treatment for stress-related conditions
- Statutory damages up to $1,000
- Attorney fees under § 1692k(a)(3)
