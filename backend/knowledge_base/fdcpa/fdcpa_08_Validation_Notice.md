\#\# 1. Why § 1692g matters  
  
Along with § 1692e, § 1692g is the most-litigated FDCPA section. It creates the consumer's right to validate a debt before paying it, and it imposes precise content, timing, and post-dispute conduct requirements on the collector. In 2021, the CFPB's Regulation F overhaul (12 C.F.R. § 1006.34) layered detailed content and form requirements on top of the statute, including a safe-harbor model form (Appendix B). The two regimes operate together: a collector must comply with § 1692g and § 1006.34, and compliance with § 1006.34 does not automatically defeat a § 1692g claim. \*McGlinchey Stafford\*, "NJ District Court Underscores Limits of 'Safe Harbor' Protection Under Regulation F."  
  
\#\# 2. Statutory framework — § 1692g  
  
\#\#\# 2.1 § 1692g(a) — Required disclosures within five days of initial communication  
  
\> "Within five days after the initial communication with a consumer in connection with the collection of any debt, a debt collector shall, unless the following information is contained in the initial communication or the consumer has paid the debt, send the consumer a written notice containing —  
\> (1) the amount of the debt;  
\> (2) the name of the creditor to whom the debt is owed;  
\> (3) a statement that unless the consumer, within thirty days after receipt of the notice, disputes the validity of the debt, or any portion thereof, the debt will be assumed to be valid by the debt collector;  
\> (4) a statement that if the consumer notifies the debt collector in writing within the thirty-day period that the debt, or any portion thereof, is disputed, the debt collector will obtain verification of the debt or a copy of a judgment against the consumer and a copy of such verification or judgment will be mailed to the consumer by the debt collector; and  
\> (5) a statement that, upon the consumer's written request within the thirty-day period, the debt collector will provide the consumer with the name and address of the original creditor, if different from the current creditor."  
  
Five elements, all mandatory. The notice may be embedded in the initial communication or sent within five days afterward.  
  
\#\#\# 2.2 § 1692g(b) — Effect of timely dispute  
  
\> "If the consumer notifies the debt collector in writing within the thirty-day period described in subsection (a) that the debt, or any portion thereof, is disputed, or that the consumer requests the name and address of the original creditor, the debt collector shall cease collection of the debt, or any disputed portion thereof, until the debt collector obtains verification of the debt or a copy of a judgment, or the name and address of the original creditor, and a copy of such verification or judgment, or name and address of the original creditor, is mailed to the consumer by the debt collector."  
  
Three key effects:  
\- \*\*Cease collection.\*\* All collection activity must stop, not just the specific dispute response. Continuing demand letters, calls, or suit filings during the cease-collection period are independent FDCPA violations. \*Smith v. Transworld Sys., Inc.\*, 953 F.2d 1025 (6th Cir. 1992).  
\- \*\*Limited verification duty.\*\* The collector's only affirmative duty is to \*\*obtain verification from the creditor\*\* and mail it to the consumer. There is no statutory duty to conduct an independent investigation or to evaluate the consumer's evidence. \*Chaudhry v. Gallerizzo\*, 174 F.3d 394 (4th Cir. 1999) ("Verification of a debt ... only requires the collector to confirm in writing that the amount being demanded is what the creditor is claiming is owed."); \*Clark v. Capital Credit & Collection Servs., Inc.\*, 460 F.3d 1162 (9th Cir. 2006).  
\- \*\*Restart on mailing of verification.\*\* Once the verification (or judgment or original-creditor information) is mailed, collection may resume. The collector must mail, not merely prepare, the verification.  
  
Reg F § 1006.38 supplements § 1692g(b) by codifying the collector's obligations on receipt of a dispute or original-creditor request.  
  
\#\#\# 2.3 § 1692g(c) — Failure to dispute is not admission  
  
\> "The failure of a consumer to dispute the validity of a debt under this section may not be construed by any court as an admission of liability by the consumer."  
  
A defensive provision: the collector cannot use the consumer's silence during the 30-day window as evidence in a later collection suit.  
  
\#\#\# 2.4 § 1692g(d) — Legal pleadings excluded from "initial communication"  
  
\> "A communication in the form of a formal pleading in a civil action shall not be treated as an initial communication for purposes of subsection (a)."  
  
Added by the 2006 amendment. The filing of a complaint does not trigger the five-day validation-notice clock. The collector must, however, send the validation notice within five days of any subsequent non-pleading communication (e.g., a settlement letter, a phone call, a demand outside the litigation).  
  
\#\#\# 2.5 § 1692g(e) — Safe harbor for compliant notices  
  
\> "The sending or delivery of any form or notice which does not relate to the collection of a debt and is expressly required by \[the IRC, Title V of GLB, or any provision of federal or state law relating to notice of data security breach or privacy\], or any regulation prescribed under any such provision of law, shall not be treated as an initial communication in connection with debt collection for purposes of this section."  
  
Added in 2010. Protects collectors from inadvertently triggering the validation clock by sending statutorily required tax forms, GLBA privacy notices, or breach notifications.  
  
\#\# 3. The overshadowing and inconsistency doctrines  
  
Even a facially compliant validation notice can violate § 1692g if other content "overshadows" or is "inconsistent with" the validation rights. The doctrine has two flavors:  
  
\#\#\# 3.1 Overshadowing  
  
A demand or other language that draws the consumer's attention away from the validation rights — typically by emphasizing urgency, prompt payment, or adverse consequences within the 30-day window — violates § 1692g. \*Russell v. Equifax A.R.S.\*, 74 F.3d 30 (2d Cir. 1996); \*Graziano v. Harrison\*, 950 F.2d 107 (3d Cir. 1991); \*Bartlett v. Heibl\*, 128 F.3d 497 (7th Cir. 1997) (Posner, J.) (proposing safe-harbor language reconciling demand and validation rights). Typical overshadowing tactics:  
\- Bold "PAY NOW" or "URGENT" stamps on the same page as the validation notice.  
\- Demands for payment "within 10 days" placed prominently near the 30-day validation language.  
\- Disclaimers minimizing the validation right ("This does not affect your obligation to pay.").  
  
\#\#\# 3.2 Inconsistency  
  
Language that contradicts the validation rights — e.g., telling the consumer the dispute must be in writing within 10 days, or that disputes must be telephonic — violates § 1692g. The validation right is for \*\*written\*\* dispute within \*\*30 days\*\*; collectors cannot shorten the period or change the form.  
  
The 1996 amendment added § 1692g(b)'s explicit reference to written disputes, which had been litigated as to whether oral disputes triggered the cease-collection requirement. The statute now clearly requires that disputes for § 1692g(b) purposes be in writing; oral disputes still suffice for § 1692e(8) credit-reporting purposes.  
  
\#\#\# 3.3 The 30-day window and collection activity  
  
Collection activities "that do not otherwise violate this subchapter may continue during the 30-day period" (§ 1692g(b)) \*\*unless\*\* the consumer has disputed in writing. The collector may send demand letters, make calls, and otherwise pursue payment within the 30 days — provided nothing overshadows the validation rights. A common compliance technique is to send the initial demand and validation notice together, then send no further collection communication for 30 days.  
  
\#\# 4. Reg F § 1006.34 — the 2021 overhaul  
  
\#\#\# 4.1 Trigger and timing  
  
§ 1006.34(a) requires the validation information to be provided either:  
\- In the initial communication, as defined in § 1006.34(b)(2); or  
\- Within five days of that initial communication.  
  
The "initial communication" definition expressly excludes the "limited-content message" defined in § 1006.2(j) (see § 5 below).  
  
\#\#\# 4.2 § 1006.34(c) — Content requirements  
  
The validation information consists of:  
  
\*\*(1) Debt-collector communication disclosure\*\* (the § 1692e(11) mini-Miranda equivalent).  
  
\*\*(2) Information about the debt:\*\*  
\- (i) Debt collector's name and a mailing address at which the collector accepts disputes and requests for original-creditor information.  
\- (ii) Consumer's name and mailing address.  
\- (iii) If the debt is for a consumer financial product or service, the name of the creditor to whom the debt was owed on the \*\*itemization date\*\*.  
\- (iv) The account number associated with the debt on the itemization date (or a truncated version).  
\- (v) The name of the creditor to whom the debt is currently owed.  
\- (vi) The itemization date.  
\- (vii) The amount of the debt on the itemization date.  
\- (viii) An itemization of interest, fees, payments, and credits applied to the debt from the itemization date through the date of the validation notice.  
\- (ix) The current amount of the debt as of the date of the notice.  
  
\*\*(3) Information about consumer protections:\*\*  
\- (i) The end date of the 30-day validation period — disclosed as a specific calendar date.  
\- (ii) A statement that if the consumer notifies the collector in writing before the end date that the debt is disputed, the collector must cease collection until the dispute is verified.  
\- (iii) A statement that the consumer may request the original creditor's name and address.  
\- (iv) A statement that the communication is from a debt collector.  
  
\*\*(4) Consumer-response information\*\* — prompts/checkboxes for disputing, requesting information, or paying.  
  
\*\*(5) Optional information\*\* — limited to certain enumerated items (e.g., payment disclosures, electronic-communication links).  
  
\#\#\# 4.3 § 1006.34(b)(3) — Itemization date alternatives  
  
The collector may choose one of five reference dates as the "itemization date":  
\- (A) Last statement date — date of the last periodic statement or written account statement or invoice provided to the consumer by the original creditor.  
\- (B) Charge-off date — date of charge-off of the debt.  
\- (C) Last payment date — date of the consumer's last payment that was applied to the debt.  
\- (D) Transaction date — date of the transaction giving rise to the debt.  
\- (E) Judgment date — date of the most recent judgment, if any.  
  
The collector must use the same itemization date consistently for each debt across communications. Inconsistency between letters (e.g., charge-off date in the validation notice and last-payment date in a subsequent letter) is itself a potential § 1006.34 violation and an FDCPA § 1692e/§ 1692g claim.  
  
\#\#\# 4.4 § 1006.34(d) — Safe-harbor model validation notice  
  
A debt collector using the model validation notice in \*\*Appendix B (Form B-1)\*\* is deemed to comply with § 1006.34's content and format requirements. The safe harbor applies to:  
\- Content (the items listed in § 1006.34(c)).  
\- Format (the visual presentation in Form B-1).  
  
\*\*Crucial limit:\*\* The safe harbor is \*\*only\*\* for Reg F § 1006.34 compliance, not for FDCPA § 1692g compliance generally. Form B-1 is presumptively compliant with the regulatory content list but does not immunize against:  
\- Overshadowing or inconsistency claims arising from additional content the collector adds.  
\- Errors in the variable fields (e.g., wrong itemization-date amount).  
\- Claims under §§ 1692e, 1692f.  
  
Appendix B also contains:  
\- \*\*Form B-2\*\* — Spanish-language model.  
\- \*\*Form B-3\*\* — itemization table alternative.  
  
\#\#\# 4.5 § 1006.34(e) — Electronic delivery; form  
  
The validation notice may be provided:  
\- In writing by mail to the consumer's last known address;  
\- In writing by an electronic means consistent with § 1006.6(d) (which requires E-SIGN-compliant consent); or  
\- Orally, but only if the collector provides the written notice within five days after the oral communication.  
  
If the collector provides the notice electronically, the notice must be "in a form that the consumer may keep" — i.e., printable or downloadable — under § 1006.42(b).  
  
\#\#\# 4.6 § 1006.42 — Sending required disclosures  
  
Specifies the methods by which the collector may transmit any required disclosure:  
\- By mail to the last known address;  
\- By email to an address obtained directly from the consumer or that meets the "permission from the prior debt collector or creditor" procedures in § 1006.42(c);  
\- By text message under § 1006.42(d) procedures.  
  
Improper-channel claims are now common — particularly emails to a work address or to an address the consumer never affirmatively provided.  
  
\#\# 5. § 1006.2(j) — limited-content message exemption  
  
A "limited-content message" is \*\*not a "communication"\*\* under Reg F and therefore does not trigger:  
\- § 1692e(11)'s mini-Miranda;  
\- § 1006.34's initial-communication clock; or  
\- § 1692c's third-party communication prohibition (so long as the message complies with the strict content limits).  
  
A limited-content message may contain only:  
\- The consumer's name.  
\- A request that the consumer reply to the message.  
\- The name(s) of one or more natural persons whom the consumer can contact to reply.  
\- A telephone number(s) the consumer can use to reply.  
\- A generic statement that the message relates to an account.  
\- Suggested response dates and times.  
\- A statement, if the message is left as a voicemail, that the message is from a debt collector if the collector wishes to include it (optional).  
  
Adding any content beyond the enumerated list converts the message back into a "communication" that triggers the full FDCPA disclosure regime.

\#\# 7. Litigation themes  
  
\#\#\# 7.1 Timing — the five-day rule  
  
The validation notice must be sent within five days \*\*of the initial communication\*\*, not within five days of the consumer's receipt. The "initial communication" is the first communication "in connection with the collection of any debt" (§ 1692a(2)) other than a formal pleading or limited-content message. If the initial communication itself contains the validation information, no separate five-day notice is required.  
  
Common claims:  
\- Late notice (sent six or more days after the initial call/letter).  
\- Notice purportedly sent but not actually mailed — discovery often turns on print/mail logs.  
\- Notice sent to wrong address (and the collector knew or should have known the address was bad).  
  
\#\#\# 7.2 Form — written vs. oral; e-communication consent  
  
The notice must be in writing (§ 1006.34(e)). Oral communication of the validation information satisfies the mini-Miranda (§ 1692e(11)) but does not satisfy § 1692g/§ 1006.34. Electronic delivery requires E-SIGN-compliant consent under § 1006.6(d) and a "form that the consumer may keep" under § 1006.42(b).  
  
\#\#\# 7.3 Content sufficiency  
  
Pleading checklist for content claims:  
\- Each required item in § 1006.34(c) — present, accurate, consistent?  
\- Itemization arithmetic — does the chain "Amount on itemization date + interest + fees − payments/credits = current amount" actually balance?  
\- End-of-dispute date — present as a calendar date, not just "30 days from receipt"?  
\- Mini-Miranda — present and not overshadowed?  
\- Debt collector name — true name (§ 1692e(14))?  
\- Creditor name — accurate as of itemization date AND current?  
  
\#\#\# 7.4 Pleadings exception  
  
A "formal pleading in a civil action" (§ 1692g(d)) does not trigger the five-day clock. The exception is narrow:  
\- A complaint is a pleading and qualifies.  
\- A summons accompanying a complaint qualifies as part of the pleading service.  
\- A pre-litigation demand letter does \*\*not\*\* qualify.  
\- Post-judgment collection communications (e.g., garnishment notices, satisfaction-of-judgment letters) generally trigger the validation notice unless they themselves are formal pleadings.  
  
When a collector communicates outside the pleading (e.g., a settlement letter delivered with the summons but separate from the complaint), the validation notice obligation revives.  
  
\#\#\# 7.5 Validation-dispute responses — limited duty  
  
On a timely written dispute, the collector must (a) cease collection and (b) obtain verification from the creditor and mail it to the consumer. The verification duty is \*\*not\*\* an independent investigation duty:  
  
\- \*Chaudhry v. Gallerizzo\*, 174 F.3d 394 (4th Cir. 1999): verification means confirming with the creditor that the demanded amount is what the creditor is claiming is owed.  
\- \*Clark v. Capital Credit & Collection Servs., Inc.\*, 460 F.3d 1162 (9th Cir. 2006): the verification need not include itemized billing statements or contract evidence; a writing from the creditor confirming the amount suffices.  
\- \*Haddad v. Alexander, Zelmanski, Danner & Fioritto, PLLC\*, 758 F.3d 777 (6th Cir. 2014): when the dispute concerns a specific item (e.g., a fee), the verification must address the disputed portion.  
  
\*\*Reg F § 1006.38\*\* codifies the duty: on receipt of a dispute, the collector must either (a) provide a verification response or (b) cease collection of the disputed portion. The rule does \*\*not\*\* import the FCRA's "reasonable investigation" standard.  
  
\#\#\# 7.6 Continuing-violation vs. single-violation  
  
Courts split on whether each post-dispute collection communication is a separate § 1692g(b) violation (favoring stacked statutory damages under § 1692k) or a single continuing violation. The Second, Seventh, and Ninth Circuits generally treat them as a single violation for purposes of the $1,000 statutory-damage cap, but each communication may be a separate basis for actual damages. \*Wright v. Finance Service of Norwalk, Inc.\*, 22 F.3d 647 (6th Cir. 1994).  
  
\#\# 8. Practical notes  
  
\- \*\*Always demand production of the actual notice\*\* and the mail/print log. Many cases turn on whether the notice was sent at all and to which address.  
\- \*\*Test the itemization math\*\* in Reg F validation notices. Mismatches are a common ground for § 1006.34 and § 1692e(2)(A) claims.  
\- \*\*Catalog every collection communication\*\* in the 30 days after a dispute is sent. Each separate touch may support an actual-damages award and stacking under multiple subsections.  
\- \*\*Demand evidence of E-SIGN consent\*\* for any electronic notice. The collector bears the burden under § 1006.6(d).  
\- \*\*Limited-content messages\*\* are an underused defense for collectors and an overlooked basis for plaintiff claims when collectors exceed the § 1006.2(j) content limits. Always check the precise content of any voicemail or short message against the § 1006.2(j) list.  
\- \*\*Pleadings exception.\*\* Distinguish carefully between the complaint itself and any cover letter, settlement demand, or stipulation served with it. The latter often triggers the validation notice.
