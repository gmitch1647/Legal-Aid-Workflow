# FDCPA Communication Rules (15 U.S.C. §§ 1692b, 1692c)

## § 1692b — Acquisition of Location Information

When communicating with third parties to acquire location information about a consumer, the debt collector:

1. **Must identify themselves** and state they are confirming/correcting location information
2. **Must NOT state** that the consumer owes any debt
3. **Must NOT communicate** with any such person more than once unless requested or reasonably necessary
4. **Must NOT communicate by post card**
5. **Must NOT use any language or symbol on envelope/correspondence** that indicates they are in the debt collection business
6. **After learning consumer is represented by attorney** — must communicate only with the attorney (unless attorney fails to respond within reasonable time)

**Violations to look for:**
- Telling a relative/neighbor/employer that the consumer owes money
- Calling the same third party multiple times
- Using company name on envelope that reveals debt collection purpose
- Contacting consumer's workplace after learning they have an attorney

## § 1692c — Communication in Connection with Debt Collection

### § 1692c(a) — Communication with the Consumer

**Prohibited times (without prior consent or court permission):**
- Before 8:00 a.m. local time of the consumer
- After 9:00 p.m. local time of the consumer

**Prohibited places:**
- At the consumer's place of employment if the collector knows or has reason to know the employer prohibits such communications

**If consumer is represented by attorney:**
- Collector must communicate with the attorney only (unless attorney fails to respond within reasonable time or consents to direct contact)

### § 1692c(b) — Communication with Third Parties

**General rule:** Without prior consent of the consumer or express permission of a court, a debt collector may NOT communicate with ANY person other than:
- The consumer
- The consumer's attorney
- A consumer reporting agency (if permitted by law)
- The creditor
- The creditor's attorney
- The debt collector's attorney

**This means:** Telling a consumer's family members, neighbors, friends, employer, or coworkers about the debt is a per se violation.

**Exception:** § 1692b (location information) communications are permitted but must follow strict rules above.

### § 1692c(c) — Ceasing Communication

**If the consumer notifies the debt collector IN WRITING that:**
1. The consumer refuses to pay the debt, OR
2. The consumer wishes the debt collector to cease further communication

**Then the debt collector must cease ALL communication EXCEPT:**
- To advise the consumer that the collector's further efforts are being terminated
- To notify the consumer that the collector or creditor may invoke specified remedies ordinarily invoked
- To notify the consumer that the collector or creditor intends to invoke a specified remedy

**Key practice points:**
- Must be in writing (oral cease-and-desist is NOT sufficient under the statute, but Regulation F expands this)
- Each communication after written cease is a separate violation
- The three exceptions are narrow — cannot use them as pretextual excuses for continued collection
- Filing a lawsuit IS invoking a "specified remedy" and is permitted after cease letter
- Reporting to a CRA after cease letter — circuit split on whether this is a "communication" that must cease

## Regulation F Additions (12 C.F.R. § 1006.6)

Regulation F (effective November 30, 2021) expanded communication rules:

**Call frequency limits (§ 1006.14(b)):**
- Presumption of harassment: more than 7 telephone calls within 7 consecutive days per debt
- After a telephone conversation: must wait at least 7 days before calling again about that debt
- These are per-debt limits, not per-consumer limits (multiple debts = multiple allowances)

**Electronic communications (§ 1006.6(d)):**
- Emails and text messages are permitted IF:
  - Consumer has given prior consent or provided the email/phone for the account
  - Message includes reasonable and simple opt-out mechanism
  - Collector honors opt-out within reasonable time (must be no more than 5 days)
- Must NOT require payment to opt out
- Must NOT charge for opt-out

**Limited-content messages (§ 1006.2(j)):**
- Voicemails containing ONLY: business name (that does NOT indicate debt collection), request to reply, name of person to contact, and telephone number — are NOT "communications" under the FDCPA
- This safe harbor allows collectors to leave voicemails without triggering § 1692e or mini-Miranda requirements

## Common § 1692c Violations in Practice

1. **Calling before 8am or after 9pm** — per se violation, no damages threshold needed
2. **Calling workplace after "stop calling here" instruction** — violation even without written cease letter (collector had "reason to know" employer prohibits)
3. **Disclosing debt to third party** — telling a family member the consumer owes money
4. **Continuing collection after written cease** — each call/letter is a separate violation
5. **Contacting consumer directly when represented by known attorney**
6. **Excessive calling** — under Regulation F, more than 7 calls in 7 days creates presumption of § 1692d harassment
7. **Leaving detailed voicemails** that disclose debt information where others might hear — potential third-party disclosure violation
