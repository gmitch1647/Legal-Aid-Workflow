# FDCPA Corpus File 12 — Regulation F (12 C.F.R. Part 1006) Summary  
  
**Topic:** CFPB's Regulation F implementing the Fair Debt Collection Practices Act  
**Primary Statute:** 15 U.S.C. §§ 1692–1692p (FDCPA)  
**Primary Regulation:** 12 C.F.R. Part 1006 (Reg F)  
**Effective Date of Final Rule:** November 30, 2021 (Debt Collection Rule, 85 Fed. Reg. 76734 (Nov. 30, 2020); 86 Fed. Reg. 5766 (Jan. 19, 2021))  
**Last Updated:** May 16, 2026  
  
---  
  
## Overview  
  
Regulation F, codified at 12 C.F.R. Part 1006, is the Consumer Financial Protection Bureau's implementing regulation for the Fair Debt Collection Practices Act. Authority was transferred from the Federal Trade Commission to the CFPB by the Dodd-Frank Wall Street Reform and Consumer Protection Act of 2010, Public Law 111-203. Reg F was finalized in two parts (the "October 2020 Final Rule" addressing communications and prohibitions and the "December 2020 Final Rule" addressing disclosures and time-barred-debt litigation prohibitions), both with a unified effective date of November 30, 2021. Source: Federal Register, "Debt Collection Practices (Regulation F)," 86 Fed. Reg. 5766 (Jan. 19, 2021); CFPB, "Debt Collection Rule (Regulation F)" compilation page.  
  
Reg F is structured in three subparts plus appendices:  
- **Subpart A — General** (§§ 1006.1 – 1006.3)  
- **Subpart B — Rules for FDCPA Debt Collectors** (§§ 1006.6 – 1006.38; with § 1006.34 governing validation notices)  
- **Subpart C — Miscellaneous** (§§ 1006.42 – 1006.108)  
- **Appendix A** — Spanish-language model disclosures  
- **Appendix B** — Model validation notice forms (B-1, B-2, B-3)  
- **Supplement I** — Official Interpretations (functions like the Reg's "official commentary")  
  
Reg F applies only to "debt collectors" as defined in the FDCPA, i.e., persons regularly collecting debts owed or asserted owed to another and persons in any business the principal purpose of which is debt collection (15 U.S.C. § 1692a(6); 12 C.F.R. § 1006.2(i)). First-party creditors collecting their own debts in their own name are generally outside Reg F (subject to state law and the CFPB's separate UDAAP authority).

## Subpart A — General (§§ 1006.1 to 1006.3)  
  
### § 1006.1 — Authority, purpose, and coverage  
Reg F is issued under sections 814 and 817 of the FDCPA (15 U.S.C. §§ 1692l, 1692o), as transferred to the CFPB by Dodd-Frank. The purpose is to prescribe federal rules governing the activities of debt collectors, to eliminate abusive collection practices, to ensure that those debt collectors who refrain from abuses are not competitively disadvantaged, and to promote consistent state action to protect consumers. Coverage tracks the FDCPA: it applies to "debt collectors" as defined in § 1006.2(i) when collecting "debt" as defined in § 1006.2(h) (i.e., obligations arising out of a consumer transaction primarily for personal, family, or household purposes).  
  
### § 1006.2 — Definitions  
Section 1006.2 imports most FDCPA definitions and adds several Reg F-specific terms central to electronic communications.  
  
Key definitions:  
- **"Attempt to communicate"** (§ 1006.2(b)) — any act to initiate a communication or other contact about a debt with any person through any medium, including by soliciting a response from such person. An attempt to communicate includes leaving a limited-content message.  
- **"Communicate" / "Communication"** (§ 1006.2(d)) — the conveying of information regarding a debt directly or indirectly to any person through any medium. "Any medium" includes oral, written, electronic, or other medium.  
- **"Electronic communication"** — a communication via email, text, or other electronic medium. The term incorporates the broader "any medium" language but is the trigger for the electronic-communication consent and opt-out provisions in § 1006.6(d).  
- **"Limited-content message"** (§ 1006.2(j)) — a voicemail message for a consumer that contains only specific enumerated content. Required elements: (i) a business name for the debt collector that does not indicate it is in the debt collection business; (ii) a request that the consumer reply to the message; (iii) the name(s) of one or more natural persons whom the consumer can contact; and (iv) a telephone number to reply. Optional content: a generic statement that the message relates to "an account," a generic salutation, and dates/times for return calls. A limited-content message that meets the definition is treated as not being a "communication" for FDCPA third-party disclosure purposes, but IS an "attempt to communicate."  
- **"Debt collector"** (§ 1006.2(i)) — tracks 15 U.S.C. § 1692a(6); excludes officers/employees of a creditor, government collectors, attorneys collecting on behalf of clients (subject to the regular-collection test), persons collecting fiduciary debts, etc.  
- **"Consumer"** (§ 1006.2(e)) — natural person obligated or allegedly obligated to pay any debt; for purposes of § 1006.6, also includes the consumer's spouse, parent (if minor), guardian, executor, or administrator.  
  
### § 1006.3 — Reserved (note: Reg F does not currently use this section).  
  
### Relation to state laws  
While Subpart C addresses state-law relation more fully (§ 1006.104), Subpart A's general coverage section confirms that Reg F does not preempt state laws that provide greater consumer protection, tracking FDCPA § 1692n.

## Subpart B — Rules for FDCPA Debt Collectors (§§ 1006.6 to 1006.38)  
  
### § 1006.6 — Communications in connection with debt collection  
This is the most operationally important section. It implements FDCPA §§ 1692c and adds Reg F's electronic-communications framework.  
  
**Time and place (§ 1006.6(b)):** Tracks § 1692c(a)(1). Inconvenient times are presumed to be before 8:00 a.m. or after 9:00 p.m. local time at the consumer's location.  
  
**Place of employment (§ 1006.6(b)(3)):** A debt collector must not communicate with a consumer at the consumer's place of employment if the debt collector knows or has reason to know the employer prohibits such communications.  
  
**Third-party communications (§ 1006.6(d)):** Tracks § 1692c(b); a debt collector generally may not communicate with third parties about the debt without consumer consent or court permission.  
  
**Electronic communications and opt-out (§ 1006.6(d) and (e)):** Reg F authorizes email and text-message communications, subject to procedural safeguards:  
- A debt collector who emails the consumer must use an address obtained under one of three permissible procedures: (i) provided directly by the consumer (not subject to opt-out); (ii) obtained from the creditor with notice and an opportunity to opt out (the "Bedrock Procedures" / "B Procedures"); or (iii) obtained from a prior debt collector after similar notice (the "B Procedures" carry-forward).  
- For text messages, the debt collector must verify the number is not a reassigned wireless number using a covered database (or other reasonable procedures).  
- Every electronic communication must include a clear and conspicuous statement describing a reasonable and simple opt-out method.  
- The debt collector cannot require a fee or any information other than opt-out preferences and the address/number to be opted out.  
- Once a consumer opts out of a particular email or number, the debt collector cannot use that channel for ANY communication, including required disclosures.  
  
**Voicemail / limited-content messages:** A limited-content message is the safe-harbor mechanism for leaving a voicemail without violating the third-party disclosure rule (because it is not a "communication" under § 1006.2(d)). A standard voicemail revealing the debt or the collector's identity may constitute an unlawful third-party disclosure if overheard.  
  
### § 1006.10 — Acquisition of location information  
Implements § 1692b. A debt collector contacting third parties (employers, neighbors, relatives) to find the consumer's address, phone, or workplace may not (i) state that the consumer owes any debt; (ii) communicate more than once unless requested or reasonably necessary to correct false or incomplete information; (iii) use postcards; (iv) use language or symbols indicating debt collection on envelopes; or (v) communicate after the debt collector knows the consumer is represented by counsel (subject to standard counsel-unavailability exceptions).  
  
### § 1006.14 — Harassing, oppressive, or abusive conduct  
Implements § 1692d. The general rule: a debt collector must not engage in any conduct the natural consequence of which is to harass, oppress, or abuse any person in connection with the collection of a debt. Enumerated examples include violence threats, obscene language, publishing nonpayment lists, anonymous calls, and calling at unusual times.  
  
**Telephone call frequency — the "7-in-7" rule (§ 1006.14(b)):** Reg F introduces explicit, quantitative call frequency presumptions:  
- **Presumption of violation:** A debt collector is presumed to violate § 1006.14(b) if it places telephone calls to a person in connection with the collection of a particular debt more than seven (7) times within any seven (7) consecutive-day period, OR within seven (7) days after engaging in a telephone conversation with the person about the particular debt.  
- **Presumption of compliance:** A debt collector is presumed to comply if it stays at or below the 7-in-7 thresholds.  
- **Per-debt, not per-consumer:** The count is by "particular debt," so collectors with multiple accounts for the same consumer may face complex tracking.  
- **Calls vs. conversations:** "Telephone call" includes attempted calls, completed calls, and voicemail-only calls; the second prong is triggered only by a "telephone conversation" (i.e., actual two-way contact).  
- Limited-content messages count as telephone calls for the 7-in-7 count.  
  
The presumption is rebuttable in both directions, but in practice the 7-in-7 ceilings function as a near-bright-line cap.  
  
### § 1006.18 — False, deceptive, or misleading representations  
Implements § 1692e. Prohibits any false, deceptive, or misleading representation or means in connection with debt collection. Specific prohibitions include:  
- False representations as to character, amount, or legal status of the debt (§ 1006.18(b)).  
- Threats to take action that cannot legally be taken or that the debt collector does not intend to take.  
- Communicating false credit information; failing to communicate that a disputed debt is disputed.  
- False government affiliation.  
- False statements that nonpayment will lead to arrest, garnishment, or seizure unless lawful AND intended.  
- Use of false business names.  
- Furnishing deceptive forms (§ 1006.18(e)).  
  
Reg F requires the "mini-Miranda" — a disclosure in the initial communication that the debt collector is attempting to collect a debt and that any information obtained will be used for that purpose. If the initial communication is oral, the disclosure must be repeated in the initial written communication.  
  
### § 1006.22 — Unfair or unconscionable means  
Implements § 1692f. Prohibits unfair or unconscionable means to collect any debt. Specific prohibitions include:  
- Collection of any amount not expressly authorized by the agreement creating the debt or permitted by law (§ 1006.22(b)). This is the basis for the CFPB's pay-to-pay fee advisory opinion.  
- Postdated check abuses.  
- Causing charges (e.g., long-distance, collect calls) without disclosure.  
- Non-judicial repossession threats without right.  
- Envelope and mail restrictions: a debt collector must not use any language or symbol on the envelope, other than the debt collector's non-revealing name and address (§ 1006.22(f)(1)).  
- **Workplace email prohibition (§ 1006.22(f)(3)):** A debt collector must not communicate with a consumer using an email address the debt collector knows is provided by the consumer's employer, unless the consumer provided that address directly or used it to communicate with the debt collector about the debt.  
- **Social-media restriction (§ 1006.22(f)(4)):** A debt collector must not communicate via social media if the communication is viewable by the general public or the consumer's social-media contacts. Private messaging within a platform is permitted subject to other Reg F rules.  
  
### § 1006.26 — Collection of time-barred debts  
Implements an interpretation of § 1692e and § 1692f directed at "out-of-statute" debt.  
- **Core prohibition:** A debt collector must not bring or threaten to bring a legal action against a consumer to collect a time-barred debt.  
- **Strict liability:** The CFPB has affirmed by advisory opinion (May 2023) that this prohibition applies without regard to the debt collector's knowledge of the time-barred status.  
- **"Time-barred debt"** means a debt for which the applicable statute of limitations has expired.  
- Reg F's preamble noted that disclosures regarding revival of time-barred debt (e.g., partial payment reviving the SOL in some states) were proposed but not finalized; further rulemaking on time-barred-debt disclosures was reserved.  
  
### § 1006.30 — Other prohibited practices  
- **§ 1006.30(a) — Prohibition on "passive collections":** A debt collector must not furnish information about a debt to a consumer reporting agency before either (i) speaking to the consumer about the debt in person or by telephone or (ii) sending the consumer a written or electronic communication about the debt and waiting a reasonable period (presumptively 14 consecutive days) without receiving a notice of undeliverability. This directly targets the practice of "parking" debt on consumer credit reports to coerce payment.  
- **§ 1006.30(b) — Sale, transfer, or placement of certain debts:** A debt collector must not sell, transfer for consideration, or place for collection a debt that the debt collector knows or should know has been paid, settled, discharged in bankruptcy, or is subject to a pending identity-theft report (with limited exceptions, e.g., transfer to the creditor or for resolution of disputes).  
  
### § 1006.34 — Notice for validation of debts ("Validation Information")  
Implements § 1692g and substantially restructures the validation notice.  
  
**Timing (§ 1006.34(a)):** Validation information must be provided in the initial communication or in writing within five days after the initial communication, except where the consumer has paid in full.  
  
**Content (§ 1006.34(c)):** Required validation information includes:  
1. Debt collector communication disclosure (the "mini-Miranda").  
2. Information about the debt:  
 - Debt collector's name and mailing address.  
 - Consumer's name and mailing address.  
 - If from a creditor with periodic statements: the most recent periodic statement and the amount; the itemization date; the amount of the debt on the itemization date; an itemization of interest, fees, payments, and credits since the itemization date; and the current amount.  
 - Otherwise: similar itemization keyed to a defined "itemization date" (last payment date, charge-off date, transaction date, judgment date, or last statement date).  
 - The name of the creditor to whom the debt is owed.  
3. Consumer protection information — the validation period dates (start and end), and statements regarding the consumer's rights to dispute, request original-creditor information, and the consequences of doing so.  
4. Consumer-response information — a tear-off / response form with prompts for typical disputes (e.g., "I want to dispute the debt because I think: ___ This is not my debt; ___ The amount is wrong; ___ Other (please describe).").  
  
**Format (§ 1006.34(d)):** The validation information must be clear and conspicuous and segregated; certain content must appear "on the front of the first page."  
  
**Safe harbor:** A debt collector that uses **Model Form B-1** (or that complies with its content/format and remains substantially similar) is deemed to comply with the content and format requirements.  
  
**Model Forms in Appendix B:**  
- **Form B-1** — primary model validation notice with itemization table.  
- **Form B-2** — model validation notice for residential mortgage debt with a most-recent periodic statement.  
- **Form B-3** — model validation notice tailored for debts purchased in the prior 12 months / specific debt-buyer contexts.  
  
### § 1006.38 — Disputes and requests for original-creditor information  
Implements § 1692g(b). If a consumer disputes the debt or any portion or requests the original creditor's name and address during the validation period (30 days from receipt of validation information), the debt collector must cease collection until it mails the consumer verification of the debt or the original-creditor information.  
  
- **Duplicative dispute exception:** A debt collector need not respond to a duplicative dispute (one substantially the same as a prior dispute already responded to), but must notify the consumer that the dispute is duplicative and provide a copy of the prior response.  
- **Effect of dispute on credit reporting:** A debt that has been disputed must be reported as disputed under FCRA § 1681s-2 and § 1006.18(c)(2).

## Subpart C — Miscellaneous (§§ 1006.42 to 1006.108)  
  
### § 1006.42 — Sending required disclosures  
A debt collector who sends a required disclosure (validation notice, opt-out notice, etc.) in writing or electronically must:  
1. Send it in a manner reasonably expected to provide actual notice; AND  
2. Send it in a form the consumer may keep and access later.  
  
Electronic delivery must comply with **E-SIGN Act § 101(c)** consent and disclosure requirements (15 U.S.C. § 7001(c)).  
  
A debt collector may deliver a disclosure electronically via a hyperlink only if it complies with specific "secure messaging" or hyperlink-safe-harbor procedures. Notices of undeliverability mean the disclosure was not sent in a manner reasonably expected to provide actual notice.  
  
If a consumer opts out of communications to a particular email or number under § 1006.6(e), the debt collector cannot use that channel even for required disclosures.  
  
### § 1006.100 — Record retention  
A debt collector must retain evidence of compliance with Reg F for a period of three years after the debt collector's last collection activity on a debt. For recorded telephone calls (if recorded), retention is required only for as long as the recording is maintained. Records of opt-out elections must be retained.  
  
### § 1006.104 — Relation to state laws  
Reg F does not annul, alter, affect, or exempt any person from complying with state laws relating to debt collection, except to the extent state law is inconsistent with the FDCPA and Reg F, and then only to the extent of the inconsistency. State laws that provide greater consumer protection are NOT inconsistent. Mirrors FDCPA § 1692n.  
  
### § 1006.108 — Severability  
Standard severability provision: if any provision of Reg F or its application is held invalid, the remainder is not affected.

## Appendices  
  
### Appendix A — Spanish-language disclosures  
Provides translated versions of the model disclosures (validation notice; in-language opt-out language). Debt collectors who communicate with consumers in Spanish may rely on the Spanish translations to provide validation information in Spanish.  
  
### Appendix B — Model forms (B-1, B-2, B-3)  
- **Form B-1** is the principal validation notice safe-harbor form.  
- **Form B-2** is the variant for mortgage debt with a most-recent periodic statement.  
- **Form B-3** is the variant for certain debt-buyer scenarios.  
  
Use of a model form (or substantial compliance with it) creates a safe harbor for the validation-notice content and format requirements. Reg F allows specified variations and optional content additions (e.g., translations into other languages, time-barred debt disclosures under state law, Spanish-language version on a separate page).  
  
### Supplement I — Official Interpretations  
Functions as Reg F's official commentary. Provides interpretive guidance on each substantive section. Courts give significant deference to Supplement I as the CFPB's authoritative interpretation of its own regulation under *Kisor v. Wilkie*, 588 U.S. 558 (2019), and the *Auer/Bowles* doctrine.

## Recurring CFPB Supervisory and Enforcement Themes  
  
The CFPB's annual Supervisory Highlights and FDCPA Annual Reports to Congress (most recently the November 2025 FDCPA Annual Report and Summer 2024 Supervisory Highlights, Issue 34) identify recurring Reg F implementation failures by supervised debt collectors:  
  
1. **Validation notice deficiencies (§ 1006.34):** Missing itemization tables; incorrect itemization dates; failure to send the validation notice within five days; missing consumer-response options; placement of required content on incorrect pages.  
2. **Electronic communication opt-out failures (§ 1006.6(e)):** Opt-out mechanisms requiring fees or extra information; failure to honor opt-outs across all collector channels; continuing to send required disclosures to opted-out addresses.  
3. **Call frequency overages (§ 1006.14(b)):** Inadequate per-debt call counters that aggregate at the consumer rather than debt level; failure to apply the 7-day cooldown after a conversation.  
4. **Passive collection / parking (§ 1006.30(a)):** Furnishing tradelines to CRAs without first attempting the required pre-furnishing communication or without observing the 14-day reasonable period.  
5. **Time-barred suits and threats (§ 1006.26):** Filing collection lawsuits past the state SOL, including in jurisdictions where revival law is uncertain.  
6. **Pay-to-pay / convenience fees (§ 1006.22(b)):** Collecting fees not authorized by the underlying agreement or by law (see CFPB Advisory Opinion, July 5, 2022).  
7. **Bedrock email procedures (§ 1006.6(d)(4)):** Failure to satisfy any of the three permissible procedures for using an email address; failure to verify reassigned-number status for text messages.

## Major Implementation Issues  
  
### Electronic-communication consent and the "Bedrock Procedures"  
The most operationally complex aspect of Reg F is the email/text framework. Collectors must (i) source the address through a permissible procedure; (ii) include opt-out language in every electronic message; (iii) maintain per-address opt-out registries; and (iv) ensure that opt-out election covers required disclosures. Many collectors continue to use legacy emails of unknown provenance, exposing them to Reg F liability and FDCPA § 1692c/§ 1692f claims.  
  
### Opt-out mechanics  
Acceptable opt-out methods must be both "reasonable" and "simple." A reply-with-STOP method, a clearly labeled hyperlink, or a dedicated phone line are typically acceptable. Methods that require account login, captcha, or providing additional identifying information (beyond opt-out preferences and the address/number) violate § 1006.6(e)(2).  
  
### Call-frequency calculation  
Three nuances dominate disputes:  
- **Per-debt aggregation:** Multiple debts for the same consumer permit multiple call streams, but the collector must track at the debt level. Software that aggregates at the consumer level risks under-counting violations.  
- **Rolling 7-day window:** Calls are counted on a rolling basis; a strict per-week reset is non-compliant.  
- **Voicemail-only calls:** Limited-content messages and unanswered calls count as "telephone calls" toward the cap.  
  
### Validation notice — itemization date selection  
The "itemization date" governs the start of the itemization table. Reg F permits five permissible dates: last payment, charge-off, transaction, judgment, or last statement date. The choice has substantive consequences for the calculation of interest and fees and for the consumer's ability to recognize the debt. CFPB supervisory findings frequently target inconsistent or incorrect itemization-date selections.  
  
### Interaction with the underlying statute  
Reg F is *implementing* — it does not displace the FDCPA but operationalizes it. Many Reg F sections track their statutory counterparts almost verbatim (e.g., § 1006.10 / § 1692b). Where Reg F adds quantitative or procedural detail (e.g., 7-in-7, Bedrock procedures, itemization tables, passive-collection bar), it functions as the operative rule. A violation of Reg F is also a violation of the FDCPA under 15 U.S.C. § 1692l(d) and is enforceable through the FDCPA's private right of action under § 1692k.
