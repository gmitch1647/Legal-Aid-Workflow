# FDCPA Complete Definitions — 15 U.S.C. § 1692a

Every defined term in the Fair Debt Collection Practices Act. These definitions control the scope of the entire statute.

---

## § 1692a(1) — "Commission"

The Federal Trade Commission. (Note: Enforcement authority has since transferred to the CFPB for most purposes under Dodd-Frank.)

---

## § 1692a(2) — "Communication"

The conveying of information regarding a debt directly or indirectly to any person through any medium.

**Scope:** Extremely broad. Encompasses:
- Telephone calls (including voicemails)
- Letters and written correspondence
- Text messages and SMS
- Emails
- Faxes
- In-person conversations
- Social media messages
- Credit reporting (in some contexts — circuit split)
- Indirect communications through intermediaries
- Automated/pre-recorded messages

**Practice note:** A communication need not be received to be actionable. A voicemail left on a machine the consumer never checks is still a "communication." A letter sent to an old address is still a "communication" when sent.

---

## § 1692a(3) — "Consumer"

Any natural person obligated or allegedly obligated to pay any debt.

**Key points:**
- Natural persons only — corporations, partnerships, LLCs cannot be "consumers"
- Includes persons ALLEGEDLY obligated — wrong-person collection creates standing
- The consumer does not need to actually owe the debt
- Deceased persons' estates may be consumers in certain contexts
- A guarantor or co-signer is a consumer
- An authorized user on a credit card is generally NOT a consumer (not obligated to pay)

---

## § 1692a(4) — "Creditor"

Any person who offers or extends credit creating a debt or to whom a debt is owed, but such term does not include any person to the extent that he receives an assignment or transfer of a debt in default solely for the purpose of facilitating collection of such debt for another.

**Key distinctions:**
- Original creditor = NOT subject to FDCPA (unless using false name per §1692a(6))
- Creditor collecting its own debt = exempt
- Entity that acquires debt in default solely to facilitate collection for another = NOT a creditor, IS a debt collector
- Debt buyer that purchases and owns the debt = creditor post-Henson (not debt collector)
- Servicer collecting for current owner = depends on default status at time of assignment

**The false-name exception:** A creditor who uses any name other than its own which would indicate that a third person is collecting or attempting to collect such debts IS treated as a debt collector under the FDCPA. Example: Bank creates a fake "collections department" name like "National Recovery Bureau" — bank becomes subject to FDCPA.

---

## § 1692a(5) — "Debt"

Any obligation or alleged obligation of a consumer to pay money arising out of a transaction in which the money, property, insurance, or services which are the subject of the transaction are primarily for personal, family, or household purposes, whether or not such obligation has been reduced to judgment.

**Elements:**
1. Obligation (or alleged obligation) to pay money
2. Arising out of a transaction (consensual exchange)
3. Primarily for personal, family, or household purposes
4. May be reduced to judgment (post-judgment debts are still "debts")

**IS a "debt" under FDCPA:**
- Credit card balances
- Auto loans
- Personal loans
- Medical bills
- Student loans from private lenders
- Utility bills (gas, electric, water, internet, phone)
- Rent and lease obligations
- Gym memberships
- Insurance premiums
- Homeowner association dues
- Mortgage payments
- Payday loans
- Retail installment contracts
- Returned check obligations
- Cell phone contracts
- Veterinary bills
- Legal fee obligations (if for personal services)

**NOT a "debt" under FDCPA:**
- Business/commercial obligations
- Tax obligations owed to government
- Child support
- Alimony/spousal support
- Criminal fines and restitution
- Government-imposed penalties
- Tort judgments (no underlying "transaction")
- Federal student loans (separate statutory regime, but note CFPB may apply Reg F)
- Obligations not arising from a "transaction" (e.g., property damage liability)

**The "transaction" requirement:**
- Must be a consensual exchange — involuntary obligations (torts) are excluded
- A contract for services creates a transaction
- A bounced check in exchange for goods creates a transaction
- But a dishonored check given as a gift does not

---

## § 1692a(6) — "Debt Collector"

Any person who uses any instrumentality of interstate commerce or the mails in any business the principal purpose of which is the collection of any debts, or who regularly collects or attempts to collect, directly or indirectly, debts owed or due or asserted to be owed or due another.

**Two prongs (either satisfies the definition):**
1. **Principal purpose test:** The entity's PRIMARY business is collecting debts
2. **Regularly collects test:** The entity regularly collects debts owed to others (even if collection isn't its primary business)

**Includes (by statute):**
- Any creditor who, in the process of collecting its own debts, uses any name other than its own which would indicate that a third person is collecting or attempting to collect such debts
- Any person who uses any instrumentality of interstate commerce or the mails in any business the principal purpose of which is the enforcement of security interests

**Statutory exclusions — § 1692a(6)(A)-(F):**

**(A)** Any officer or employee of a creditor while, in the name of the creditor, collecting debts for such creditor

**(B)** Any person while acting as a debt collector for another person, both of whom are related by common ownership or affiliated by corporate control, if the person acting as a debt collector does so only for persons to whom it is so related or affiliated and if the principal business of such person is not the collection of debts

**(C)** Any officer or employee of the United States or any State to the extent that collecting or attempting to collect any debt is in the performance of his official duties

**(D)** Any person while serving or attempting to serve legal process on any other person in connection with the judicial enforcement of any debt

**(E)** Any nonprofit organization which, at the request of consumers, performs bona fide consumer credit counseling and assists consumers in the liquidation of their debts by receiving payments from such consumers and distributing such amounts to creditors

**(F)** Any person collecting or attempting to collect any debt owed or due or asserted to be owed or due another to the extent such activity (i) is incidental to a bona fide fiduciary obligation or a bona fide escrow arrangement; (ii) concerns a debt which was originated by such person; (iii) concerns a debt which was not in default at the time it was obtained by such person; or (iv) concerns a debt obtained by such person as a secured party in a commercial credit transaction involving the creditor

**Post-Henson analysis (2017):**
- Debt BUYERS who purchase defaulted debt and collect it for themselves are generally NOT debt collectors — they are now the "creditor" (they own the debt)
- BUT: entities that acquire debt solely to facilitate collection FOR ANOTHER remain debt collectors
- Regulation F partially overrides this by defining "debt collector" to include persons who acquire debts in default (12 C.F.R. § 1006.2(i)) — but this applies only to Reg F compliance, not FDCPA private suits

**The "regularly collects" question:**
- No bright-line test — totality of circumstances
- Factors: volume of collection activity, whether collection is advertised, consistency over time, proportion of business revenue from collection
- An attorney who files 1-2 collection cases = probably NOT a debt collector
- An attorney who files hundreds of collection cases = IS a debt collector (Heintz v. Jenkins)
- A property management company that sends late-rent notices = might be a debt collector if it regularly does so for multiple owners

---

## § 1692a(7) — "Location Information"

A consumer's place of abode and his telephone number at such place, or his place of employment.

**Relevance:** § 1692b restricts how debt collectors may acquire location information from third parties. The definition limits what qualifies as "location information" — only physical address, home phone, and workplace. Email addresses, cell phones, and social media accounts are NOT "location information" under this definition (though contacting third parties to get them may still violate § 1692c(b)).

---

## § 1692a(8) — "State"

Any State, territory, or possession of the United States, the District of Columbia, the Commonwealth of Puerto Rico, or any political subdivision of any of the foregoing.

---

## Additional Defined Terms (not in § 1692a but used throughout)

**"Initial communication"** — The first communication (as defined in §1692a(2)) sent to the consumer in connection with the collection of the debt. This triggers the § 1692g validation notice requirement.

**"Validation"** — The term used in § 1692g for the required notice content. Not separately defined but understood to mean the written notice containing the five required elements.

**"Verification"** — What the collector must provide if the consumer disputes within 30 days. § 1692g(b) requires "verification of the debt" or a copy of the judgment. Courts have interpreted this variably — some require only confirmation of the amount/creditor, others require documentation of the underlying debt.

**"Material false representation"** — Not defined in statute but developed in case law. Under the least sophisticated consumer standard, a representation is "material" if it could affect the consumer's decision-making about the debt. Even technically true statements can be "misleading" if they create a false impression.
