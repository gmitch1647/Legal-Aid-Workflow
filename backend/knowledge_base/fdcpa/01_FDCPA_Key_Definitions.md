# FDCPA Key Definitions (15 U.S.C. § 1692a)

## "Debt" — § 1692a(5)

Any obligation or alleged obligation of a consumer to pay money arising out of a transaction in which the money, property, insurance, or services which are the subject of the transaction are primarily for personal, family, or household purposes, whether or not such obligation has been reduced to judgment.

**Key points:**
- Must be a consumer obligation (personal/family/household — NOT business debts)
- Includes alleged obligations — debt need not actually be owed
- Includes obligations reduced to judgment
- Does NOT include: tax debts, fines, child support, student loans owed to government (these have separate statutory regimes)
- Includes: credit cards, auto loans, personal loans, medical debt, utility bills, rent, gym memberships, phone bills

**Critical case law:**
- *Henson v. Santander Consumer USA Inc.*, 582 U.S. 79 (2017) — debt buyers who purchased defaulted debt are NOT "debt collectors" under the FDCPA because they did not acquire the debt "solely for the purpose of collection" but as owners of the debt
- Note: Post-Henson, many circuits have narrowed this holding; Regulation F also addresses debt buyers

---

## "Debt Collector" — § 1692a(6)

Any person who uses any instrumentality of interstate commerce or the mails in any business the principal purpose of which is the collection of any debts, or who regularly collects or attempts to collect, directly or indirectly, debts owed or due or asserted to be owed or due another.

**Includes:**
- Third-party collection agencies
- Attorneys who regularly collect debts for others
- Companies whose principal purpose is debt collection
- Persons who collect debts using a name other than their own that would indicate a third party is involved (§ 1692a(6) final sentence)

**Excludes (§ 1692a(6)(A)-(F)):**
- Officers or employees of a creditor collecting the creditor's own debts
- Creditors collecting their own debts (unless using a different name suggesting third-party involvement)
- Government officials performing official duties
- Process servers
- Nonprofit credit counseling services
- Persons collecting debts incidental to a fiduciary obligation
- **Debt buyers** (post-Henson) — entities that purchased the debt and now own it are generally NOT debt collectors under the FDCPA

**The "regularly collects" test:**
- Not defined in the statute — courts look at volume, frequency, and whether collection is a regular part of the business
- Filing one or two collection suits does not make an attorney a "debt collector"
- Filing hundreds of collection cases does
- Sending dunning letters on behalf of multiple clients regularly = debt collector

**Creditor vs. Debt Collector distinction:**
- A creditor collecting its OWN debt is NOT subject to the FDCPA (unless using a false name)
- The moment a creditor assigns or sells the debt to a third party for collection, the third party is potentially a debt collector
- A servicer collecting for the current owner may or may not be a debt collector depending on when default occurred

**Default status at time of acquisition:**
- Under Regulation F (12 C.F.R. § 1006.2(i)), a person who acquires a debt in default is a "debt collector" — this partially overrides Henson for regulatory purposes
- But for FDCPA private-right-of-action purposes, Henson still controls in most circuits

---

## "Consumer" — § 1692a(3)

Any natural person obligated or allegedly obligated to pay any debt.

**Key points:**
- Must be a natural person (not a corporation or business entity)
- Includes persons who are merely alleged to owe — wrong-person collection attempts create standing
- The consumer need not actually owe the debt to have FDCPA rights

---

## "Communication" — § 1692a(2)

The conveying of information regarding a debt directly or indirectly to any person through any medium.

**Key points:**
- Extremely broad — includes letters, phone calls, voicemails, text messages, emails, faxes, in-person contact
- Includes indirect communication (communicating through intermediaries)
- A voicemail is a communication even if the consumer doesn't listen to it
- A text message is a communication
- A credit report entry can be a communication in certain contexts
- Regulation F adds: electronic messages (emails, text messages) are communications subject to all FDCPA restrictions

---

## "Creditor" — § 1692a(4)

Any person who offers or extends credit creating a debt or to whom a debt is owed, but does not include any person to the extent that he receives an assignment or transfer of a debt in default solely for the purpose of facilitating collection of such debt for another.

**The critical distinction:**
- Original creditor collecting its own debt = NOT subject to FDCPA
- Original creditor using a name that implies third-party collection = IS subject to FDCPA (§ 1692a(6) last sentence)
- Debt buyer who purchased the debt = generally NOT a debt collector post-Henson (they are now the "creditor")
- BUT: An entity that acquires debt in default solely to facilitate collection for another remains a debt collector

---

## "Location Information" — § 1692a(7)

A consumer's place of abode and his telephone number at such place, or his place of employment.

**Relevance:** § 1692b restricts what a debt collector may do when acquiring location information from third parties.
