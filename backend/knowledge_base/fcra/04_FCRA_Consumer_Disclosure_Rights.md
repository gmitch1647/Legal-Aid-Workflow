# FCRA Consumer Disclosure Rights (15 U.S.C. §§ 1681g, 1681h, 1681j)  
  
**Topic:** Analytical explainer of the consumer's right to obtain information about themselves from consumer reporting agencies  
**Primary statute:** 15 U.S.C. §§ 1681g (disclosures to consumers), 1681h (conditions and form), 1681j (charges for certain disclosures); 15 U.S.C. § 1681c-1 (free disclosures with security freezes / fraud alerts)  
**Cross-references:** 15 U.S.C. § 1681a(g) (definition of "file"), § 1681a(p), (x) (nationwide and nationwide specialty CRAs); 12 C.F.R. Part 1022 Subpart N (Duties of Consumer Reporting Agencies Regarding Disclosures to Consumers); FTC Free Annual File Disclosures Rule, 12 C.F.R. §§ 1022.130–1022.138; CFPB, *Fair Credit Reporting; File Disclosure* (January 2024)  
**Last updated:** May 16, 2026  
  
The FCRA gives consumers three layered rights to see what is in their files at consumer reporting agencies: (1) a substantive disclosure right covering the contents of the file, its sources, and its recipients (§ 1681g); (2) procedural protections governing how and where the disclosure happens (§ 1681h); and (3) cost rules — when the disclosure must be free, and when the CRA may charge (§ 1681j). These rights apply to every CRA, but several additional rules apply specifically to the nationwide CRAs (Equifax, Experian, TransUnion) and to the nationwide specialty CRAs.  
  
A separate but related disclosure regime applies when a security freeze or fraud alert is placed (§ 1681c-1). The disclosure right is one of the most important practical levers a consumer (or consumer-law practitioner) has — it is the gateway to the § 1681i dispute process.

## 1. Full file disclosure — § 1681g  
  
**Citation:** 15 U.S.C. § 1681g; source: https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/15/1681g  
  
### 1.1 The file — § 1681g(a)(1)  
  
**Operative language (excerpt):** "Every consumer reporting agency shall, upon request, and subject to section 1681h(a)(1) of this title, clearly and accurately disclose to the consumer: (1) All information in the consumer's file at the time of the request ..."  
  
"File" is defined in § 1681a(g) as "all of the information on that consumer recorded and retained by a consumer reporting agency regardless of how the information is stored." The disclosure right runs to that entire body of information, not just to what the CRA chooses to market as a "credit report."  
  
**Distinction — credit report vs. consumer disclosure:** A "credit report" delivered to a third-party user typically contains a subset of the file — tradelines, public records, inquiries, and a score. The "consumer disclosure" delivered to the consumer under § 1681g must include everything in the file. In practice this means consumer disclosures show internal codes, soft inquiries, employer pulls, and other data not visible on a routine third-party credit report. The Third Circuit's *Cortez v. Trans Union, LLC*, 617 F.3d 688 (3d Cir. 2010), is the leading authority on the breadth of "file" for purposes of § 1681g.  
  
### 1.2 Sources — § 1681g(a)(2)  
  
**Operative language (excerpt):** "The sources of the information; except that the sources of information acquired solely for use in preparing an investigative consumer report and actually used for no other purpose need not be disclosed: Provided, That in the event an action is brought under this subchapter, such sources shall be available to the plaintiff under appropriate discovery procedures in the court in which the action is brought."  
  
**Plain English:** Every source of the information in the file, with a narrow carve-out for investigative-report-only sources (which become available in discovery if litigation follows).  
  
### 1.3 Recipients — § 1681g(a)(3)  
  
**Operative language (excerpt):** "Identification of each person (including each end-user identified under section 1681e(e)(1)) that procured a consumer report — (i) for employment purposes, during the 2-year period preceding the date on which the request is made; and (ii) for any other purpose, during the 1-year period preceding the date on which the request is made ..."  
  
**Plain English:** Two windows. Employment-purpose pulls — disclosed for the prior **two years**. All other pulls — disclosed for the prior **one year**. (Note: the older version of § 1681g(a)(3) used a six-month window for non-employment pulls; FACTA expanded it to one year. Make sure you're citing the current code.)  
  
**Identification must include:** the recipient's name, the trade name under which the recipient does business if applicable, and (upon request of the consumer) the recipient's address and telephone number.  
  
### 1.4 Credit score — § 1681g(f)  
  
**Citation:** 15 U.S.C. § 1681g(f)  
  
**Operative language (excerpt):** "Upon the request of a consumer for a credit score, a consumer reporting agency shall supply to the consumer a statement indicating that the information and credit scoring model may be different than the credit score that may be used by the lender; and a notice which shall include the current credit score of the consumer or the most recent credit score of the consumer that was previously calculated by the credit reporting agency for a purpose related to the extension of credit; the range of possible credit scores under the model used; the key factors that adversely affected the credit score of the consumer in the model used ...; the date on which the credit score was created; and the name of the person or entity that provided the credit score or credit file upon which the credit score was created."  
  
**Plain English:** On request, the consumer may obtain a credit score plus statutorily required contextual information. Note: § 1681g(f) entitles the consumer to receive a score upon request; the CRA may charge a "fair and reasonable" fee (CFPB has periodically set the maximum reasonable fee — currently in the $15–$16 range, [VERIFY] current figure on the CFPB website).  
  
**Mortgage-context score disclosure — § 1681g(g):** Separate from (f); applies when a creditor that makes or arranges a closed-end consumer-purpose loan secured by 1-4 family dwelling uses a credit score in connection with the application. The creditor must provide the score and statutorily required information directly to the applicant (not the CRA).

## 2. Conditions and form of disclosure — § 1681h  
  
**Citation:** 15 U.S.C. § 1681h; source: https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/15/1681h  
  
### 2.1 Proper identification — § 1681h(a)(1)  
  
The CRA may require the consumer to furnish "proper identification" as a condition of receiving the § 1681g disclosure. In practice, CRAs require name, address, date of birth, Social Security number, and one or more verification questions or documents. Excessive ID demands — particularly requirements for original documents not generally held by most consumers — have generated litigation under § 1681g/§ 1681h.  
  
### 2.2 Form — § 1681h(b)  
  
**Operative language (excerpt):** "The disclosures required under section 1681g of this title shall be provided under that section in writing."  
  
The consumer may specify alternative methods:  
- **In person** at the CRA's place of business during normal business hours, on reasonable notice.  
- **By telephone** if the consumer made a written request.  
- **By electronic means** if available from the CRA.  
- **By any other reasonable means** available from the CRA.  
  
### 2.3 Trained personnel — § 1681h(c)  
  
"Any consumer reporting agency shall provide trained personnel to explain to the consumer any information furnished to him pursuant to section 1681g of this title."  
  
### 2.4 Accompanying person — § 1681h(d)  
  
The consumer "shall be permitted to be accompanied by one other person of his choosing, who shall furnish reasonable identification." The CRA may require a written authorization from the consumer permitting discussion of the file in that person's presence.  
  
### 2.5 Civil-suit immunity for accurate disclosures — § 1681h(e)  
  
A narrow immunity provision: no consumer may bring a state-law defamation, invasion-of-privacy, or negligence action against a CRA, user, or furnisher for information disclosed pursuant to §§ 1681g, 1681h, or 1681m, except for "false information furnished with malice or willful intent to injure such consumer." This preempts certain state-law theories but leaves FCRA federal claims fully available.

## 3. Charges for disclosures — § 1681j  
  
**Citation:** 15 U.S.C. § 1681j; source: https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/15/1681j  
  
### 3.1 Free annual disclosure — § 1681j(a)  
  
**Operative language (excerpt):** "All consumer reporting agencies described in subsections (p) and (w) of section 1681a shall make all disclosures pursuant to section 1681g once during any 12-month period upon request of the consumer and without charge to the consumer."  
  
[Note: the statute as enacted references (p) and (w); the "nationwide specialty CRA" definition is at current § 1681a(x), and the (w) reference in § 1681j(a) was a renumbering artifact. VERIFY the current cross-reference in the official prelim edition.]  
  
**Centralized source — § 1681j(a)(1)(C):** The nationwide CRAs must establish a centralized source through which consumers may obtain their free annual disclosure. That source is **AnnualCreditReport.com** — the only website authorized to provide free annual disclosures under federal law. The FTC's Free Annual File Disclosures Rule, codified for the CFPB at 12 C.F.R. §§ 1022.130–1022.138, governs the centralized source.  
  
**Nationwide specialty CRAs — § 1681j(a)(1)(C):** Must also provide free annual disclosure on request, but generally through a toll-free telephone number rather than a unified web portal. Compliance among the specialty CRAs is uneven and has been the subject of CFPB enforcement.  
  
**Advertising disclosure — § 1681j(a)(2):** Any advertisement of a free credit report in any medium shall prominently disclose that free credit reports are available under federal law at AnnualCreditReport.com.  
  
### 3.2 Free disclosure after adverse action — § 1681j(b)  
  
A CRA must provide a free disclosure if, not later than **60 days** after the consumer receives a notice under § 1681m (or under ECOA § 1691(d)), the consumer makes a request. The 60-day clock runs from notice receipt.  
  
### 3.3 Free disclosure for unemployed, public-welfare recipients, and fraud victims — § 1681j(c)  
  
A CRA must provide a free disclosure (once during any 12-month period) if the consumer certifies in writing that:  
- (1) the consumer is unemployed and intends to apply for employment within 60 days of the certification;  
- (2) the consumer is a recipient of public welfare assistance; or  
- (3) the consumer has reason to believe the file contains inaccurate information due to fraud.  
  
### 3.4 Free disclosure for identity-theft victims — § 1681j(d)  
  
Upon the consumer's request, a nationwide CRA (§ 1681a(p) entity) must make all § 1681g disclosures without charge to a consumer who has placed a fraud alert under § 1681c-1. The statute entitles a fraud-alert holder to **two free file disclosures** during the 12-month period beginning on the date the fraud alert was placed.  
  
### 3.5 Free disclosure with security-freeze placement — § 1681c-1  
  
**Citation:** 15 U.S.C. § 1681c-1(i) (security freezes); § 1681c-1(a) (initial fraud alerts); § 1681c-1(b) (extended fraud alerts); § 1681c-1(c) (active-duty alerts)  
  
Since the Economic Growth, Regulatory Relief, and Consumer Protection Act of 2018, **security freezes are free** at all CRAs — placement, removal, and temporary lifts. The statute also requires:  
- Initial fraud alert (one year) — placed on the consumer's request based on suspicion of identity theft;  
- Extended fraud alert (seven years) — for identity-theft victims with a police report or identity-theft report;  
- Active-duty alert (one year) — for deployed servicemembers.  
  
Each alert/freeze comes with associated free disclosure entitlements: an initial fraud alert entitles the consumer to one free file disclosure; an extended fraud alert entitles the consumer to two free disclosures within 12 months. See § 1681j(d) above.  
  
### 3.6 Other reasonable charges — § 1681j(f)  
  
For disclosures not falling within the free-disclosure categories above, the CRA may impose a "reasonable amount" determined by the CFPB. The CFPB updates the cap annually (currently in the $15 range, [VERIFY] current figure at consumerfinance.gov).

## 4. Difference between a "credit report" and a "consumer disclosure"  
  
A consistent practitioner-level point: the consumer disclosure under § 1681g is **broader** than the third-party credit report.  
  
| Item | Third-party credit report | Consumer disclosure (§ 1681g) |  
|---|---|---|  
| Tradelines | Yes | Yes |  
| Public records | Yes | Yes |  
| Inquiries — hard | Yes | Yes |  
| Inquiries — soft / promotional | Sometimes | Yes (all inquiries) |  
| Account-review pulls | No | Yes |  
| Employment pulls (prior 2 years) | No | Yes |  
| Internal codes / metadata | No | Yes (must be disclosed if in file) |  
| Sources | No | Yes |  
| Recipients (1 / 2 years) | No | Yes |  
  
Practitioners should always ask for the **consumer disclosure**, not "my credit report." The form labels differ across CRAs but the legal entitlement is to the full file.

## 5. Right to request file from nationwide specialty CRAs  
  
**Citation:** § 1681j(a)(1)(C); § 1681a(x)  
  
The nationwide specialty CRAs cover five verticals:  
- **Medical records or payments** — e.g., MIB Group.  
- **Residential or tenant history** — e.g., CoreLogic Rental Property Solutions, RealPage Consumer Services, TransUnion SmartMove, Experian RentBureau.  
- **Check-writing history** — e.g., ChexSystems, TeleCheck.  
- **Employment history** — e.g., The Work Number / Equifax Workforce Solutions; First Advantage; HireRight.  
- **Insurance claims** — e.g., LexisNexis C.L.U.E.; A-PLUS.  
  
Each must provide a free annual disclosure on consumer request. The CFPB publishes a list of specialty CRAs annually (see CFPB, *List of Consumer Reporting Companies*, updated periodically).  
  
**Practical mechanics:**  
- Most specialty CRAs accept requests by toll-free phone or by mail; some have online portals.  
- ID verification is typically stricter than at the Big Three because the data sets are narrower and identity correlation is harder.  
- Consumers requesting employment-history files from The Work Number may need to use an employee-self-service portal accessed with an employer-provided code.

## 6. Practical issues — how consumers exercise these rights, and what to do when CRAs refuse  
  
### 6.1 Standard request workflow  
  
1. **For Big Three free annual disclosure**: AnnualCreditReport.com, by mail to the central mailing address, or by toll-free phone (1-877-322-8228). Since the pandemic the Big Three have made *weekly* free reports available — a voluntary expansion above the statutory floor.  
2. **For specialty CRAs**: identify the appropriate specialty CRA (consult the CFPB list), request free disclosure under § 1681j(a)(1)(C). Typically by phone or mail.  
3. **For free disclosure after adverse action**: request within 60 days of receiving the § 1681m notice. The notice itself must identify the CRA and provide contact information.  
4. **For free disclosure as fraud victim / unemployed / public-welfare recipient**: provide written certification under § 1681j(c).  
5. **For credit score**: request separately. § 1681g(f) entitlement.  
  
### 6.2 Common CRA pushbacks and consumer responses  
  
- **"You already got your free annual."** Check the calendar. The 12-month clock under § 1681j(a) is independent of the 60-day post-adverse-action clock under § 1681j(b), the fraud-alert clock under § 1681j(d), and the § 1681j(c) certification right. Multiple free disclosures in a year are possible.  
- **"You need to send original ID documents."** § 1681h(a)(1) requires "proper identification" — not original government-issued documents. Excessive demands have been the subject of state attorney general and CFPB actions. Consumers should escalate via CFPB complaint (consumerfinance.gov/complaint).  
- **"That information isn't in your credit report."** Demand the **file** under § 1681g(a)(1), not just the marketed credit report. Reference § 1681a(g)'s definition.  
- **"We can only give you a summary."** § 1681g(a)(1) requires disclosure of all information in the file; § 1681h(b) requires it in writing on request.  
- **Refusal to identify a specific inquiry source.** § 1681g(a)(3) requires identification of recipients of consumer reports. Some CRAs have argued that internal "soft pulls" don't count; the CFPB has rejected that reading.  
  
### 6.3 Enforcement options  
  
- **§ 1681i dispute process** — once the consumer has the file, errors are challenged through the 30-day reinvestigation process (extended to 45 days in some circumstances).  
- **CFPB complaint** — public docket, frequently produces written CRA responses within 15 days; useful both as a remedy and as evidence-building.  
- **Private suit under §§ 1681n / 1681o** — for willful or negligent FCRA violations, with statutory damages of $100–$1,000 per willful violation, actual damages, punitive damages (for willful), and attorney's fees.  
- **State AG / state CRA statutes** — many states (CA, NY, VT, MN, MA) have additional disclosure rights and enforcement mechanisms.

## Practical compliance notes (for CRA-side counsel)  
  
- Build the disclosure flow to deliver the full **file**, not the consumer-facing "credit report" SKU. The risk-adjusted compliance posture is over-inclusion.  
- Document the ID-verification logic. Adverse outcomes for consumers who cannot satisfy ID verification have been a CFPB focus.  
- Track the source of every data element. § 1681g(a)(2) source-disclosure compliance fails when the CRA cannot reconstruct provenance.  
- Recipient logs must cover the full one-year / two-year windows under § 1681g(a)(3).  
- Free-disclosure entitlements stack — one CRA's "Annual disclosure used" flag must not block a § 1681j(b), (c), or (d) request.
