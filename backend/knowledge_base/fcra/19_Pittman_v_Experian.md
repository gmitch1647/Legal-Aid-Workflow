# Pittman v. Experian Information Solutions, Inc., 901 F.3d 619 (6th Cir. 2018)

## Case Information

**Full Citation:** Pittman v. Experian Information Solutions, Inc., 901 F.3d 619 (6th Cir. 2018)
**Court:** United States Court of Appeals for the Sixth Circuit
**Date:** August 23, 2018
**Docket:** No. 17-1677
**Statutes:** 15 U.S.C. § 1681e(b) (reasonable procedures), § 1681i(a) (reinvestigation), § 1681s-2(b) (furnisher investigation), § 1681n (willful), § 1681o (negligent)

---

## Facts

Pittman's mortgage note required monthly payments of $1,980.42. His mortgage was serviced by iServe. In 2011, Pittman missed two payments and iServe offered a Trial Period Plan (TPP) under the Home Affordable Mortgage Program (HAMP).

**The Trial Period Plan:** Pittman was required to make three reduced payments of $1,357.80 in early 2012. The agreement stated that "after all trial period payments are timely made... your mortgage will be permanently modified." Pittman made ALL three payments on time.

**The problem:** Despite completing the TPP, iServe never formalized the permanent modification in writing. Servicing was then transferred to BSI Financial Group. BSI sent Pittman a notice of default, and Pittman's attorney discovered that iServe had failed to report the modification to the Treasury Department.

**The credit reporting:** Experian reported Pittman as delinquent on his mortgage payments throughout the period covered by the TPP — without ever mentioning the modification plan or that Pittman was making agreed-upon reduced payments. The report showed only delinquency, creating the false impression that Pittman was simply not paying.

**Internal acknowledgment:** In January 2013, iServe emailed BSI confirming: "The borrower… made all payments on time, contractually entitling him to a permanent mod [sic] in April 2012."

**The dispute and reinvestigation:** Pittman disputed the inaccurate reporting. The CRAs conducted reinvestigations but accepted the furnisher's verification without independently reviewing documentation of the modification agreement.

---

## Holdings

### Holding 1: Omission of TPP Makes Report Materially Misleading

> "Reporting that a consumer was delinquent on his loan payments without reporting the TPP implies a much greater degree of financial irresponsibility."

A credit report that is technically accurate (consumer did not make full contractual payments) is nonetheless INACCURATE under the FCRA when it omits material context (the consumer was performing under an approved modification plan). The omission transforms a technically correct report into a materially misleading one.

### Holding 2: Reasonableness of Investigation Is a Jury Question

Whether a CRA's or furnisher's investigation was "reasonable" under §§ 1681e(b), 1681i(a), and 1681s-2(b) is a question of FACT for the jury — not a question of law for the court to decide on summary judgment. The Sixth Circuit reversed summary judgment on this basis.

### Holding 3: Jury Verdict on Compensatory Damages Upheld

The jury's award of compensatory damages for negligent FCRA violations under § 1681o was upheld. Pittman demonstrated actual harm from the misleading reporting.

### Holding 4: Punitive Damages Reversed (Willfulness Not Proven)

The Sixth Circuit vacated the punitive damages award, finding insufficient evidence of willfulness under the Safeco standard. The court found that Experian's interpretation of its reporting obligations — while ultimately wrong — was not "objectively unreasonable" at the time. This was a close call.

## Application to LegalFlow Cases

### When to Cite Pittman

**1. Forbearance/Modification Cases (DIRECTLY ON POINT)**

This case is your primary authority whenever:
- A consumer was in a forbearance or modification plan
- The CRA reported delinquency without mentioning the plan
- The consumer was performing under the plan (making agreed payments)

*Pittman* establishes that reporting delinquency without the modification context is materially misleading — even though the consumer technically wasn't making full contractual payments.

**2. Any "Technically Accurate But Misleading" Case**

Cite alongside *Chaitoff v. Experian* (7th Cir. 2023) and *Koropoulos v. Credit Bureau* (D.C. Cir. 1984) for the principle that FCRA accuracy requires completeness, not just technical correctness.

**3. Opposing Summary Judgment on Reasonableness**

When Experian/TransUnion/Equifax moves for summary judgment arguing their procedures or reinvestigation was reasonable, cite *Pittman* for the proposition that reasonableness is ALWAYS a jury question.

**4. Furnisher Investigation Claims**

When the furnisher (like ED Financial or a loan servicer) verified inaccurate information without checking its own records for forbearance/modification documentation.

### Ready-Made Pleading Language

**For § 1681e(b) accuracy claims:**
> "Defendant's credit report was materially misleading within the meaning of 15 U.S.C. § 1681e(b). As the Sixth Circuit held in *Pittman v. Experian Information Solutions, Inc.*, 901 F.3d 619, 639 (6th Cir. 2018), 'reporting that a consumer was delinquent on his loan payments without reporting the [modification/forbearance plan] implies a much greater degree of financial irresponsibility' than the actual facts warrant. Here, Defendant reported [X late payments] without disclosing that Plaintiff's loans were in administrative forbearance with payment obligations suspended."

**For opposing MSJ on reasonableness:**
> "Whether Defendant's reinvestigation was reasonable is a question of fact for the jury, not a question of law to be resolved on summary judgment. *Pittman v. Experian Information Solutions, Inc.*, 901 F.3d 619 (6th Cir. 2018) (reversing summary judgment; reasonableness of CRA's investigation is a jury question); accord *Cushman v. Trans Union Corp.*, 115 F.3d 220 (3d Cir. 1997)."

**For furnisher § 1681s-2(b) claims:**
> "Defendant [Furnisher] possessed internal records documenting Plaintiff's [forbearance/modification] status, yet upon receiving notice of Plaintiff's dispute, failed to review those records or report accurate results to the consumer reporting agencies. Under *Pittman*, 901 F.3d at 640, a furnisher that verifies inaccurate information while possessing contradicting documentation has not conducted a reasonable investigation under § 1681s-2(b)."

### Strategic Notes

1. **Pittman + Chaitoff = One-Two Punch for Forbearance Cases.** *Pittman* (6th Cir. 2018) and *Chaitoff* (7th Cir. 2023) both involved mortgage modification plans that weren't reported. Together they establish a strong multi-circuit consensus that omitting forbearance/modification information makes a report inaccurate.

2. **Willfulness Caution:** *Pittman* reversed punitive damages because the court found Experian's interpretation wasn't "objectively unreasonable" at the time. But now — post-*Pittman* and post-*Chaitoff* — this precedent EXISTS. A CRA that reports delinquency without modification context NOW, knowing these decisions exist, has a much harder time arguing its interpretation is reasonable.

3. **Your Wiggins Case Is Directly Analogous:** Your client's student loans were in forbearance with suspended payment obligations, yet CRAs reported late payments. *Pittman* says this exact scenario — reporting delinquency without the forbearance context — is materially misleading. The forbearance letters are your equivalent of Pittman's TPP agreement.

4. **Jury Trial Right Is Strong After Pittman:** Because reasonableness is a jury question, defendants can't escape to summary judgment on the "our procedures were reasonable" defense. This case preserves your right to get to trial.

### Discovery Requests Informed by Pittman

**Interrogatories:**
1. "Identify what information, if any, Defendant received from the furnisher regarding the forbearance or modification status of Plaintiff's accounts at the time the disputed information was reported."
2. "State whether Defendant's reinvestigation procedures include any step to determine whether a reported delinquency occurred during a period of forbearance or modification."
3. "Identify all policies or procedures Defendant maintains for distinguishing between accounts in forbearance and accounts that are actually delinquent."

**RFPs:**
1. "All documents reflecting Defendant's procedures for reporting accounts that are subject to forbearance agreements, trial modification plans, or other payment deferral arrangements."
2. "All communications between Defendant and [Furnisher] regarding Plaintiff's forbearance or modification status."
3. "Defendant's Metro 2 reporting guidelines for accounts with Special Comment Codes indicating forbearance (code AC) or modified payment plans (code AI)."

## Related Cases

- *Chaitoff v. Experian*, No. 21-2632 (7th Cir. 2023) — Same principle (omission of TPP is inaccuracy); ALSO holds ACDV-only reinvestigation unreasonable
- *Koropoulos v. Credit Bureau*, 734 F.2d 37 (D.C. Cir. 1984) — "Accuracy" includes completeness
- *Cushman v. Trans Union*, 115 F.3d 220 (3d Cir. 1997) — Reasonableness of reinvestigation is jury question
- *Safeco v. Burr*, 551 U.S. 47 (2007) — Willfulness standard (used to reverse punitive damages here)
- *Seamans v. Temple Univ.*, 744 F.3d 853 (3d Cir. 2014) — Materially incomplete report is inaccurate

---

## Key Takeaway

*Pittman* is the foundational case for any FCRA claim where a consumer was in forbearance or a modification plan but the credit report shows only delinquency. After *Pittman*, reporting delinquency without the modification/forbearance context is per se materially misleading — and whether the CRA's procedures and reinvestigation were reasonable enough to catch this is always a jury question that survives summary judgment.
