# Regulation V — 12 C.F.R. Part 1022 (Summary by Subpart)  
  
**Source**: Consumer Financial Protection Bureau (CFPB), Electronic Code of Federal Regulations (eCFR), Title 12, Chapter X, Part 1022 — "Fair Credit Reporting (Regulation V)."  
  
- Primary text: <https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-12/chapter-X/part-1022>  
- CFPB regulation portal: <https://www.consumerfinance.gov/rules-policy/regulations/1022/>  
  
**What Regulation V does.** Regulation V is the CFPB's implementing regulation for the Fair Credit Reporting Act (FCRA), 15 U.S.C. §§ 1681–1681x. It also incorporates rulemaking authority transferred to the CFPB from the FTC and the federal banking agencies under the Dodd-Frank Act (2010). For most regulated entities, Regulation V is the operational rulebook; the FCRA is the underlying statute that creates the private right of action and the civil/criminal penalty regime.  
  
**Note on subpart lettering.** The eCFR uses the following subpart letters for Part 1022. The user's outline refers to the furnisher subpart as "Subpart F" and the risk-based pricing subpart as "Subpart H"; in the eCFR, **furnishers are in Subpart E**, and risk-based pricing is in Subpart H. This summary follows the eCFR's actual lettering and flags the relevant sections (§§ 1022.40–43 for furnishers, etc.) so the bot can answer either way.

## Subpart A — General Provisions (§§ 1022.1–1022.3)  
  
**Scope.** Establishes the regulation's purpose, scope, and core definitions.  
  
**§ 1022.1 — Purpose, scope, and model forms.** Regulation V implements the FCRA. It generally applies to persons that:  
- obtain and use information about consumers to determine eligibility for products, services, or employment;  
- share such information among affiliates; or  
- furnish information to consumer reporting agencies (CRAs).  
  
The model forms and disclosures used to satisfy FCRA notice requirements are contained in the appendices (Appendix B for prescreen, Appendix C for affiliate marketing opt-out, Appendix D for the Notice of Furnisher Responsibilities, Appendix H for risk-based pricing notices, Appendix K for the Summary of Consumer Rights, Appendix L for the Summary of Identity Theft Rights, Appendix M for the Notice of User Responsibilities, and Appendix N for the streamlined annual file disclosure form).  
  
**§ 1022.2 — Examples.** Hypothetical examples are illustrative, not exhaustive; a transaction not described in an example may still be covered.  
  
**§ 1022.3 — Definitions.** Cross-references FCRA § 603 (15 U.S.C. § 1681a) for the core defined terms ("consumer," "consumer report," "consumer reporting agency," "person," "investigative consumer report," "file," etc.) and adds Regulation V–specific definitions, including:  
- **Affiliate** — any company related by common ownership or common corporate control with another company.  
- **Common ownership or common corporate control** — generally a 25% voting-securities threshold, or the power to elect a majority of the directors, or to exercise controlling influence over management or policies.  
- **Pre-existing business relationship** — relevant to the affiliate-marketing opt-out exceptions in Subpart C.  
- **Eligibility information** — information bearing on credit-worthiness, character, reputation, etc., received from an affiliate that would be a "consumer report" but for the FCRA § 603(d)(2)(A) sharing exclusions; this is the operative term for Subpart C.  
  
**Statutory interaction.** Subpart A is purely operational; substantive rights are in the FCRA statute. The definitions in § 1022.3 do most of the work in determining whether a particular entity or data flow is covered by the rest of Part 1022.

## Subpart C — Affiliate Marketing (§§ 1022.20–1022.27)  
  
**Scope.** Implements FCRA § 624 (15 U.S.C. § 1681s-3), added by the FACT Act (2003). Governs when a company can use "eligibility information" it receives from an affiliate to make a marketing solicitation to a consumer.  
  
**Key provisions.**  
- **§ 1022.20 — Coverage and definitions.** Applies to any person that receives eligibility information from an affiliate and wants to use it to market the person's own products or services to the consumer. Excludes information about the company's own transactions or experiences with the consumer (which can always be shared and used without an opt-out).  
- **§ 1022.21 — Affiliate marketing opt-out and exceptions.** Before using eligibility information from an affiliate to make a solicitation, the company must:  
  1. clearly and conspicuously disclose, in writing (or, with consent, electronically), that it may use eligibility information from an affiliate to make solicitations; and  
  2. give the consumer a reasonable opportunity and a reasonable, simple method to opt out.  
   
Exceptions include solicitations to consumers with a pre-existing business relationship with the company, solicitations in response to a communication initiated by the consumer, and solicitations to consumers who have authorized the affiliate to share the information.  
- **§ 1022.22 — Scope and duration of opt-out.** An opt-out must be effective for at least 5 years. The opt-out applies only to the use of eligibility information for solicitations — it does not block the underlying information sharing itself (which is governed by GLBA / FCRA § 603(d)(2)(A)).  
- **§ 1022.23 — Contents of opt-out notice.** Specifies required elements.  
- **§ 1022.24 — Reasonable opportunity to opt out.** Examples of acceptable methods (toll-free number, electronic means, detachable form returned by mail).  
- **§ 1022.25 — Reasonable and simple methods.** Cannot require a consumer to write their own letter or call during limited hours.  
- **§ 1022.26 — Delivery of opt-out notices.** Notice must be delivered such that the consumer can reasonably be expected to receive actual notice.  
- **§ 1022.27 — Renewal of opt-out.** Once the opt-out period expires, the company must give a renewal notice before resuming solicitations; each renewal must last at least 5 years.  
  
**Model forms.** Appendix C contains model affiliate-marketing opt-out notices.  
  
**Statutory interaction.** Implements FCRA § 624. Distinct from (but coordinated with) the GLBA's information-sharing opt-out (Regulation P). GLBA controls *sharing* of nonpublic personal information; FCRA § 624 / Subpart C controls *use* of shared eligibility information for marketing.

## Subpart D — Medical Information (§ 1022.30)  
  
**Scope.** Implements the FCRA's general prohibition on creditor use of medical information in eligibility determinations, FCRA § 604(g) (15 U.S.C. § 1681b(g)).  
  
**Key provisions.**  
- **§ 1022.30 — Obtaining or using medical information in connection with a determination of eligibility for credit.** General rule: **a creditor may not obtain or use medical information about a consumer in connection with any determination of the consumer's eligibility, or continued eligibility, for credit**.  
- **Statutory "financial information exception" (1022.30(d))** historically permitted creditors to use medical information as long as it is used "in a manner and to an extent no less favorable than [the creditor] would use comparable information that is not medical information," is necessary and appropriate, and is not used to take into account the consumer's physical, mental, or behavioral health, condition, or history.  
- **Specific exceptions** for: information related to debts arising from the consumer's medical condition where the creditor uses it in the same way it would treat any other debt; income or benefits paid in connection with disability or health (e.g., disability income relied on as a source of repayment); information used to determine the purpose of the loan; information used to accommodate the consumer's particular circumstances.  
- **Unsolicited medical information** received by a creditor may be retained and used to the extent any exception applies.  
  
**2025 amendments (vacated).** On January 7, 2025 the CFPB issued a final rule amending § 1022.30 to **remove the financial-information exception** and prohibit creditors from considering medical debt information in credit eligibility decisions, and to limit CRAs from furnishing such information to creditors. *See* 90 Fed. Reg. 3276 (Jan. 14, 2025). The U.S. District Court for the Eastern District of Texas **vacated the rule on July 11, 2025** (*Cornerstone Credit Union League v. CFPB*), holding the rule exceeded the CFPB's statutory authority. The pre-2025 § 1022.30 framework controls.  
  
**Statutory interaction.** Implements FCRA § 604(g). Does not bar all collection or reporting of medical debt — that is governed separately by CRA voluntary policies (e.g., the 2022–2023 industry agreement to remove paid medical collections and collections under $500) and by FCRA § 605(a)(6) restrictions on identifying providers in consumer reports.

## Subpart E — Duties of Furnishers of Information (§§ 1022.40–1022.43)  
  
> The user's outline labels this "Subpart F." In the current eCFR, **furnishers are in Subpart E**. The section numbers match the outline (§§ 1022.40–1022.43).  
  
**Scope.** Implements FCRA § 623 (15 U.S.C. § 1681s-2), the duties of furnishers — i.e., the banks, lenders, debt collectors, landlords, utilities, and others that provide information about consumers to CRAs.  
  
**Key provisions.**  
- **§ 1022.40 — Scope.** Applies to all furnishers of information to CRAs.  
- **§ 1022.41 — Definitions.** Defines "accuracy," "integrity," "direct dispute," "furnisher," "identity theft," "identity theft report."  
  - **"Accuracy"** = the information correctly reflects the terms and liability for the account or other relationship, the consumer's performance and other conduct, and any other appropriate information.  
  - **"Integrity"** = (1) the information is substantiated by the furnisher's records when furnished; (2) it is furnished in a form designed to minimize the likelihood that the information may be incorrectly reflected in a consumer report; and (3) the information includes sufficient identifiers (e.g., name, address, SSN) and other content (e.g., date of first delinquency, account closure status) to enable proper reporting.  
- **§ 1022.42 — Reasonable policies and procedures concerning accuracy and integrity.** Each furnisher must establish, implement, and regularly review **written** policies and procedures regarding accuracy and integrity, appropriate to the nature, size, complexity, and scope of its activities. Must consider the **Interagency Guidelines in Appendix E** in developing them.  
- **§ 1022.43 — Direct disputes.** A furnisher must conduct a **reasonable investigation** of a direct dispute submitted by a consumer if it concerns:  
  1. the consumer's liability for an account or debt with the furnisher;  
  2. the terms of such an account or debt;  
  3. the consumer's performance or conduct concerning the account; or  
  4. any other information contained in a consumer report regarding the furnisher's account or relationship that bears upon the consumer's credit standing or reputation.  
   
Direct disputes must be sent to (a) the address provided by the furnisher on the report, (b) an address clearly and conspicuously specified by the furnisher for direct disputes, or (c) any business address of the furnisher if no specific address was provided.  
   
Frivolous or irrelevant disputes (e.g., already investigated, lacking identifying info, lacking required documentation) can be refused, with notice to the consumer within 5 business days.  
   
After investigation, if information is inaccurate, the furnisher must promptly correct it and notify each CRA to which it furnished the information.  
  
**Appendix E.** "Interagency Guidelines Concerning the Accuracy and Integrity of Information Furnished to Consumer Reporting Agencies" — non-binding but the controlling reference for designing furnisher policies (data validation, internal controls, training, dispute handling, root-cause analysis, etc.).  
  
**Statutory interaction.** Implements FCRA § 623(e). The furnisher's separate duty to investigate **indirect** disputes routed through the CRA (FCRA § 623(b), 15 U.S.C. § 1681s-2(b)) is statutory and not reproduced in Subpart E — but indirect-dispute violations are the most common basis for furnisher liability in private litigation, because FCRA § 623(c) gives consumers a private right of action for § 1681s-2(b) violations but generally not for § 1681s-2(a) accuracy/integrity violations.

## Subpart H — Duties of Users Regarding Risk-Based Pricing (§§ 1022.70–1022.75)  
  
**Scope.** Implements FCRA § 615(h) (15 U.S.C. § 1681m(h)), added by the FACT Act. Requires creditors that use consumer reports to give a **risk-based pricing notice** to consumers who are offered credit on materially less-favorable terms than other consumers because of information in their reports.  
  
**Key provisions.**  
- **§ 1022.70 — Scope.** Applies to any person that uses a consumer report in connection with an application for, or extension of, credit primarily for personal, family, or household purposes and, based in whole or in part on the report, grants credit on **material terms materially less favorable than the most favorable material terms available to a substantial proportion of consumers** from or through that person.  
- **§ 1022.71 — Definitions.** Defines "annual percentage rate," "consummation," "credit card issuer," "credit score," "firm offer of credit," "material terms," "materially less favorable."  
- **§ 1022.72 — General requirements for risk-based pricing notices.** A user that engages in risk-based pricing must provide a notice to the consumer. Two methods to determine who gets a notice:  
  - **Direct comparison method** — compare the terms offered to this consumer to the terms offered to other consumers.  
  - **Credit-score proxy method** — set a cutoff credit score and provide notices to those below it.  
  - **Tiered pricing method** — for creditors with four or fewer pricing tiers, notice goes to consumers not in the top tier (or top two tiers if more than three tiers).  
- **§ 1022.73 — Content, form, and timing of risk-based pricing notices.** Must include statement that terms offered are based on the report, identify the CRA(s), explain the right to a free annual report and to dispute, and (after 2011 Dodd-Frank amendment) **disclose the credit score** used and key factors adversely affecting it. Timing varies by transaction (generally, after the credit decision but before consummation; for credit cards, before the first transaction).  
- **§ 1022.74 — Exceptions.** A risk-based pricing notice is **not** required if:  
  1. the consumer applied for and was granted **specific material terms** (i.e., a single specified APR rather than a range);  
  2. the creditor provides an **adverse action notice** under FCRA § 615(a);  
  3. the report was used only for a **prescreened firm offer of credit**;  
  4. the report was used for employment, insurance, or non-credit purposes;  
  5. the creditor regularly obtains credit scores from a CRA but no score is available for the applicant (in which case a "no-score" notice is required instead); or  
  6. the creditor provides a **credit score disclosure exception notice** to every applicant (a common compliance choice — see Appendix H model forms H-3, H-4, H-5).  
- **§ 1022.75 — Rules of construction.** Multiple notices, credit-score disclosure compliance, etc.  
  
**Model forms.** Appendix H — Model Forms H-1 through H-7 (risk-based pricing notices and credit-score disclosure exception notices).  
  
**Statutory interaction.** Implements FCRA § 615(h). Operates in parallel with the **adverse action notice** under FCRA § 615(a) (which is required when credit is denied) — most creditors choose either to give individual risk-based pricing notices or to use the credit-score disclosure exception notice to every applicant.

## Subpart I — Duties of Users of Consumer Reports Regarding Identity Theft (§§ 1022.80–1022.83)  
  
**Scope.** Implements FCRA §§ 605A and 605B (15 U.S.C. §§ 1681c-1, 1681c-2) as they apply to users of consumer reports — particularly the **notice of address discrepancy** rule, FCRA § 605(h).  
  
**Key provisions.**  
- **§ 1022.80 — Scope.** Applies to users of consumer reports that receive notices of address discrepancy from nationwide CRAs.  
- **§ 1022.81 — Definitions.** Defines "notice of address discrepancy" — a notice from a nationwide CRA informing the user of a substantial difference between the address the user provided when requesting the report and the address(es) in the CRA's file.  
- **§ 1022.82 — Duties of users regarding address discrepancies.**  
  - Users must develop and implement **reasonable policies and procedures** to form a reasonable belief that a consumer report relates to the consumer about whom it was requested. Examples: comparing against information used for CIP (Customer Identification Program) under the BSA, against the user's own records (applications, change-of-address notifications), or directly verifying with the consumer.  
  - When the user can form a reasonable belief that the report relates to the right consumer, establishes a continuing relationship with the consumer, and regularly furnishes information to the CRA, it must furnish a **confirmed address** for the consumer back to that CRA.  
- **§ 1022.83 — Disposal of consumer information.** (Cross-referenced with the FTC Disposal Rule, 16 C.F.R. Part 682.) Any person who maintains or possesses consumer information for a business purpose must properly dispose of it to protect against unauthorized access or use.  
  
**Statutory interaction.** Implements FCRA § 605(h)(2) and § 628 (disposal). Practical compliance overlaps heavily with BSA/AML CIP rules.

## Subpart J — Identity Theft Red Flags (§§ 1022.90–1022.91)  
  
**Scope.** Implements FCRA § 615(e) (15 U.S.C. § 1681m(e)), the FACT Act's "Red Flags Rule." Note that for most banks, savings associations, and credit unions, the Red Flags Rule is also located at the prudential regulator's parallel rule (12 C.F.R. Part 41 for OCC, Part 222 for FRB, Part 334 for FDIC, Part 717 for NCUA). For nonbank "creditors" subject to CFPB rulemaking, Subpart J controls.  
  
**Key provisions.**  
- **§ 1022.90 — Duties regarding the detection, prevention, and mitigation of identity theft.** Each **financial institution** or **creditor** that offers or maintains **covered accounts** must develop and implement a written **Identity Theft Prevention Program (ITPP)** designed to detect, prevent, and mitigate identity theft in connection with the opening of a covered account or any existing covered account. The program must:  
  1. **Identify** relevant red flags for the covered accounts the institution offers or maintains and incorporate those red flags into the program;  
  2. **Detect** red flags incorporated into the program;  
  3. **Respond appropriately** to any red flags detected to prevent and mitigate identity theft; and  
  4. **Update** the program periodically to reflect changes in risks.  
   
The program must be **approved by the board of directors** (or appropriate committee or senior management), include staff training, and oversee service provider arrangements.  
- **§ 1022.91 — Duties of card issuers regarding changes of address.** A card issuer that receives notice of a change of address followed shortly by a request for an additional or replacement card must assess the validity of the change of address before issuing the card (e.g., by notifying the cardholder at the former address, calling, or using another reasonable means).  
  
**Appendix J.** "Interagency Guidelines on Identity Theft Detection, Prevention, and Mitigation." Sets out a list of 26 illustrative red flags in five categories (alerts from CRAs, suspicious documents, suspicious personal identifying information, unusual or suspicious account activity, notice from customers/victims/law enforcement).  
  
**Statutory interaction.** Implements FCRA § 615(e). "Creditor" was narrowed by the **Red Flag Program Clarification Act of 2010** to exclude entities that merely advance funds on behalf of someone in connection with selling services — narrowing reach for many service providers.

## Subpart M — Duties of Consumer Reporting Agencies Regarding Identity Theft (§§ 1022.120–1022.138)  
  
> The user's outline lists §§ 1022.120–1022.138. In the current eCFR, Subpart M actually contains **§§ 1022.120–1022.124**; §§ 1022.130–1022.138 are in **Subpart N** (annual file disclosures). This summary covers both ranges so the bot has the relevant material.  
  
**Scope.** Implements FCRA §§ 605A (fraud alerts / active duty alerts, 15 U.S.C. § 1681c-1), § 605B (block of identity-theft information, 15 U.S.C. § 1681c-2), and § 609 (disclosures and summary of rights, 15 U.S.C. § 1681g) for nationwide CRAs.  
  
**Key provisions (Subpart M proper).**  
- **§ 1022.120 — Scope.** Applies to nationwide CRAs and (where indicated) nationwide specialty CRAs.  
- **§ 1022.121 — Active duty alerts.** Sets duration of active duty alerts (minimum 12 months). Requires CRAs to refer the alert to other nationwide CRAs.  
- **§ 1022.122 — Initial fraud alerts and extended fraud alerts.**  
  - **Initial fraud alert**: at the consumer's request (good-faith suspicion of identity theft), CRA must include alert in file for at least **one year** and refer to other nationwide CRAs.  
  - **Extended fraud alert**: with an **identity theft report**, CRA must include alert for **seven years**, exclude the consumer from prescreen lists for **five years**, and provide **two free file disclosures** during the 12 months following the request.  
- **§ 1022.123 — Appropriate proof of identity.** CRA must develop reasonable procedures for verifying identity before placing/removing alerts or blocks.  
- **§ 1022.124 — Disposal of records.** (Cross-references to disposal rule.)  
  
**Key provisions (Subpart N — annual file disclosures, §§ 1022.130–1022.138).**  
- **§ 1022.130 — Definitions.** Defines "annual file disclosure" — provided once in any 12-month period at no charge under FCRA § 612(a).  
- **§ 1022.136 — Centralized source for requesting annual file disclosures.** Nationwide CRAs must establish a centralized source — operationally, **AnnualCreditReport.com** — for consumer requests by Internet, toll-free phone, and mail.  
- **§ 1022.137 — Streamlined process for requesting annual file disclosures from nationwide specialty CRAs.** Each nationwide specialty CRA (e.g., LexisNexis, ChexSystems, Innovis, MIB) must have a toll-free number and streamlined process.  
- **§ 1022.138 — Prevention of deceptive marketing of free credit reports.** Restricts how the centralized source and other CRA-affiliated services market "free" credit reports (e.g., requires prominent disclosure that AnnualCreditReport.com is the official site, prohibits advertising on the centralized site itself).  
  
**Block of identity theft information.** Implementation of FCRA § 605B (block requirement upon receipt of an identity theft report, police report, proof of identity, and consumer's statement that the information does not relate to a transaction made by the consumer) is governed primarily by the statute and addressed in supervisory guidance (e.g., CFPB Supervisory Highlights Spring 2024 special edition on blocks for identity-theft and human-trafficking victims).  
  
**Summary of Rights.** The "Summary of Consumer Rights" (FCRA § 609(c)) is the model form in **Appendix K**; the "Summary of Identity Theft Rights" is in **Appendix L**.  
  
**Statutory interaction.** Subpart M operationalizes the FCRA's identity-theft toolbox; Subpart N operationalizes the consumer's right to a free annual credit report and the prohibition on "free credit report" bait-and-switch marketing.

## Subpart O — Miscellaneous Duties of Consumer Reporting Agencies (§§ 1022.140–1022.142)  
  
**Scope.** Implements miscellaneous FCRA duties of CRAs.  
  
**Key provisions.**  
- **§ 1022.140 — Prohibition against circumventing or evading treatment as a consumer reporting agency.** Implements FCRA § 603(p) anti-evasion. A CRA cannot reorganize itself or its information flows to escape "nationwide CRA" status. Examples include using a corporate affiliate to assemble and evaluate information for the purpose of furnishing reports while purporting not to be a CRA.  
- **§ 1022.141 — Reasonable charges by consumer reporting agencies for certain disclosures.** Implements FCRA § 612(f). Sets the maximum allowable charge a CRA may impose for a file disclosure when no statutory free disclosure applies. The CFPB adjusts the cap annually for inflation (e.g., the cap was $15.50 for 2024).  
- **§ 1022.142 — Prohibition on inclusion of adverse information in cases of human trafficking.** Implements FCRA § 605C (added by the **Debt Bondage Repair Act of 2021**, Pub. L. 117-103). Prohibits CRAs from including in any consumer report **any adverse item of information that resulted from a severe form of trafficking in persons or sex trafficking** if the consumer provides trafficking documentation. CRAs must establish a method for victims to submit documentation and block reporting within 4 business days.  
  
**Statutory interaction.** Implements FCRA §§ 603(p), 605C, and 612(f).

## Quick scope reminder for the bot  
  
- Regulation V applies to **persons that obtain or use consumer reports, share eligibility information among affiliates, or furnish information to CRAs**.  
- For depository institutions, the prudential regulator may have a parallel rule (12 C.F.R. Parts 41 / 222 / 334 / 717); content is largely identical to Part 1022.  
- The FTC retains rulemaking and enforcement authority over **motor-vehicle dealers** predominantly engaged in dealer-financed sales (Dodd-Frank carve-out) and shares enforcement authority for non-bank entities outside CFPB's "larger participant" thresholds.
