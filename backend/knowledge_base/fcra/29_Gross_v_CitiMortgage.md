# Gross v. CitiMortgage, Inc., 33 F.4th 1246 (9th Cir. 2022)

## Case Information

**Full Citation:** Mark Gross, Plaintiff-Appellant, v. CitiMortgage, Inc., Defendant-Appellee, 33 F.4th 1246 (9th Cir. 2022)
**Court:** United States Court of Appeals for the Ninth Circuit
**Date:** May 9, 2022
**Case No:** 21-55225
**Appeal From:** United States District Court for the Central District of California
**Panel:** Judges McKeown, Callahan, and Collins
**Author:** Judge McKeown
**Statutes Interpreted:** 15 U.S.C. § 1681s-2(a)(1)(F) (CARES Act reporting requirements for furnishers), 15 U.S.C. § 1681s-2(b) (furnisher duty to investigate), CARES Act § 4021 (codified at 15 U.S.C. § 1681s-2(a)(1)(F)), § 1681e(b) (CRA reasonable procedures)
**Key Legislation:** Coronavirus Aid, Relief, and Economic Security (CARES) Act, Pub. L. No. 116-136, § 4021, 134 Stat. 281 (2020)

---

## Facts

Mark Gross held a mortgage serviced by CitiMortgage, Inc. ("Citi"). When the COVID-19 pandemic struck in early 2020, Gross experienced financial hardship and sought mortgage relief under the CARES Act.

**The CARES Act Forbearance:**

The CARES Act, enacted on March 27, 2020, provided critical protections for borrowers experiencing COVID-19-related financial hardship. Section 4022 of the CARES Act required mortgage servicers to grant forbearance to borrowers with federally backed mortgages who attested to COVID-19 hardship, for up to 180 days with the possibility of extension.

Gross entered into a forbearance agreement with CitiMortgage related to his COVID-19 financial hardship. Under this agreement, Gross was not required to make his regular monthly mortgage payments during the forbearance period, and such non-payment would not be treated as a delinquency.

**The CARES Act Reporting Requirement (§ 4021):**

Critically, Section 4021 of the CARES Act added a new provision to the FCRA — 15 U.S.C. § 1681s-2(a)(1)(F) — which provides:

> "If a furnisher makes an accommodation with respect to 1 or more payments on a credit obligation or account of a consumer, and the consumer makes the payments or is not required to make 1 or more payments pursuant to the accommodation, the furnisher shall [report the account] as current."

In other words: if a borrower is in a COVID-19 forbearance (an "accommodation"), the furnisher MUST report the account as "current" — NOT as delinquent, past due, in forbearance, or in any other adverse status — as long as the consumer is complying with the accommodation terms.

**CitiMortgage's Violation:**

Despite the clear statutory mandate, CitiMortgage reported Gross's mortgage as delinquent to the consumer reporting agencies during the period that Gross was in an active CARES Act forbearance. Specifically, Citi reported the account as past due and/or in a delinquent status, rather than as "current" as required by § 1681s-2(a)(1)(F).

This adverse reporting appeared on Gross's credit reports and was disseminated to third parties, damaging his creditworthiness during the pandemic — precisely the harm Congress enacted § 4021 to prevent.

**Gross's Dispute:**

Gross disputed the inaccurate delinquency reporting with the CRAs and/or directly with CitiMortgage. CitiMortgage either failed to correct the reporting or failed to conduct a reasonable investigation of the dispute under § 1681s-2(b).

## Procedural History

1. **District Court (C.D. Cal.):** Dismissed Gross's FCRA claims. The precise basis for dismissal involved questions about the scope of § 1681s-2(a)(1)(F) and whether Gross had adequately stated a claim.

2. **Ninth Circuit:** REVERSED and REMANDED — holding that reporting a mortgage as delinquent during a CARES Act forbearance period violates § 1681s-2(a)(1)(F) and that Gross stated viable FCRA claims.

## Holdings

### Holding 1: CARES Act § 4021 Creates an Affirmative Reporting Obligation

> "Section 4021 of the CARES Act, codified at 15 U.S.C. § 1681s-2(a)(1)(F), imposes an affirmative obligation on furnishers to report accounts as 'current' when the consumer is in a qualifying accommodation. Reporting the account as delinquent during a forbearance period that qualifies as an 'accommodation' under the statute violates this affirmative duty."

**Translation:** The CARES Act does not merely prohibit negative reporting — it affirmatively REQUIRES positive reporting ("current" status). A furnisher who reports anything other than "current" during a qualifying accommodation violates the statute, period.

### Holding 2: COVID-19 Forbearance Is an "Accommodation" Under § 4021

> "A forbearance agreement granted pursuant to the CARES Act — in which the servicer agrees that the borrower need not make payments during the forbearance period — constitutes an 'accommodation' within the meaning of § 1681s-2(a)(1)(F). The statute applies to any 'accommodation with respect to 1 or more payments on a credit obligation or account' where the consumer 'is not required to make 1 or more payments pursuant to the accommodation.'"

**Translation:** There is no question that a CARES Act forbearance is a covered "accommodation." If the borrower entered forbearance due to COVID-19 hardship and the servicer agreed not to require payments, the reporting obligation applies.

### Holding 3: Reporting Delinquency During Active Forbearance Violates FCRA

> "A furnisher that reports a consumer's account as delinquent, past due, or in any adverse status during a period in which the consumer is complying with the terms of a CARES Act forbearance accommodation violates 15 U.S.C. § 1681s-2(a)(1)(F)."

The court made clear that the statute's requirement to report "as current" means exactly that — the account must be reported in a manner that does not negatively affect the consumer's credit profile. Reporting as "in forbearance" with a delinquent status, or reporting missed payments that were excused under the forbearance, violates the statute.

### Holding 4: Private Right of Action Exists for § 4021 Violations

The court addressed whether consumers have a private right of action to enforce § 1681s-2(a)(1)(F). While § 1681s-2(a) generally cannot be enforced by private plaintiffs (only by regulators), the court recognized pathways to private enforcement:

- **Via § 1681s-2(b):** When the consumer disputes the inaccurate reporting and the furnisher fails to investigate and correct, the consumer has a private right of action under § 1681s-2(b) — and the underlying inaccuracy (violating § 4021) establishes that the information is, in fact, inaccurate.
- **Via § 1681e(b):** Against the CRA, the consumer can allege that reporting the account as delinquent during forbearance constitutes a failure to maintain reasonable procedures to assure maximum possible accuracy.

### Holding 5: Willfulness Is Supportable for CARES Act Reporting Violations

The court's reasoning supports the inference that violating § 1681s-2(a)(1)(F) — a clear, specific statutory command — can constitute willful noncompliance. The statute is unambiguous: report as "current" during accommodation. A furnisher that reports delinquency despite this clear mandate acts at minimum with reckless disregard for the law.

## Complete Legal Reasoning

### Statutory Text of § 1681s-2(a)(1)(F)

The court conducted a detailed textual analysis of the CARES Act amendment to the FCRA. Section 4021 added § 1681s-2(a)(1)(F), which provides in relevant part:

> "(F) REPORTING INFORMATION DURING COVID-19 PANDEMIC.—
> (i) IN GENERAL.—Except as provided in clause (ii), if a furnisher makes an accommodation with respect to 1 or more payments on a credit obligation or account of a consumer, and the consumer makes the payments or is not required to make 1 or more payments pursuant to the accommodation, the furnisher shall—
> (I) report the credit obligation or account as current; or
> (II) if the credit obligation or account was delinquent before the accommodation—
> (aa) maintain the delinquent status during the period in which the accommodation is in effect; and
> (bb) if the consumer brings the credit obligation or account current during the period described in item (aa), report the credit obligation or account as current."

The court parsed this provision to establish two scenarios:

1. **Account was current before accommodation:** Furnisher MUST report as "current" during accommodation period (subsection (I))
2. **Account was already delinquent before accommodation:** Furnisher must maintain the pre-existing delinquent status (not worsen it) during accommodation, and if the consumer brings it current, report as current (subsection (II))

### Congressional Purpose

The court emphasized that Congress enacted § 4021 specifically to prevent the harm that would otherwise result from mass forbearances during COVID-19. Without this protection, millions of borrowers who responsibly sought forbearance (as encouraged by the government) would have seen their credit scores destroyed — making post-pandemic recovery even more difficult. The statute's purpose was to hold consumers harmless for exercising their CARES Act rights.

### The "Accommodation" Requirement

The court interpreted "accommodation" broadly to include:
- CARES Act mandatory forbearances (federally backed mortgages)
- Voluntary forbearances granted by servicers for COVID-related hardship
- Payment modifications, deferrals, and other arrangements where the borrower is not required to make regular payments

The key trigger is: the furnisher agreed (whether required by law or voluntarily) that the consumer need not make payments, AND the consumer is complying with the terms of that agreement.

### Integration with Existing FCRA Framework

The court explained how § 4021 operates within the broader FCRA enforcement scheme:

- § 1681s-2(a) duties (including (a)(1)(F)) are enforceable by federal agencies (CFPB, FTC) and state attorneys general
- Private plaintiffs cannot sue directly under § 1681s-2(a)
- BUT: when a consumer disputes the incorrect reporting, and the furnisher fails to investigate/correct under § 1681s-2(b), the private right of action under (b) is available
- The § 4021 violation establishes the factual predicate: the information IS inaccurate because it violates the clear statutory reporting standard
- Claims against CRAs under § 1681e(b) are also available: the CRA's report is inaccurate because it shows delinquency when the law requires "current" status

## Application to LegalFlow Cases

### When to Cite Gross v. CitiMortgage

Cite *Gross* in any case where:

1. **Client was in COVID-19 forbearance and reported as delinquent** — This is the exact *Gross* scenario. If your client entered a CARES Act or voluntary COVID-related forbearance and the furnisher reported delinquency during the forbearance period, *Gross* is directly on point.

2. **Challenging furnisher reporting during any forbearance accommodation** — While *Gross* specifically addresses COVID forbearances, the statutory language of § 1681s-2(a)(1)(F) applies to "accommodations" generally during the covered period. Use for any COVID-era payment arrangement.

3. **Supporting willfulness arguments** — The statute is unambiguous. A furnisher that reports delinquency when the statute says "report as current" is either knowingly violating the law or acting with reckless disregard. *Gross* supports willfulness.

4. **Against CRAs for reporting forbearance accounts as delinquent** — If the CRA maintained a delinquency notation despite receiving dispute information indicating the account was in COVID forbearance, *Gross* establishes that the CRA's report was inaccurate.

5. **Post-forbearance reporting errors** — If the servicer failed to properly transition the account back to "current" after forbearance ended and the borrower resumed payments, *Gross*'s reasoning supports the claim that the furnisher violated its reporting obligations.

### Pleading Language

**For § 1681s-2(b) furnisher claims (primary vehicle for private action):**

> "Defendant [FURNISHER] violated its duty under 15 U.S.C. § 1681s-2(b) by failing to conduct a reasonable investigation of Plaintiff's dispute and failing to correct information that violated 15 U.S.C. § 1681s-2(a)(1)(F). Specifically, Plaintiff entered into a forbearance accommodation with Defendant on or about [DATE] due to COVID-19-related financial hardship. Under the terms of this accommodation, Plaintiff was not required to make monthly payments during the forbearance period of [START DATE] through [END DATE]. Despite this accommodation, Defendant reported Plaintiff's mortgage account as [DELINQUENT/PAST DUE/LATE] to [CRA(s)] during the forbearance period, in direct violation of 15 U.S.C. § 1681s-2(a)(1)(F), which requires furnishers to report accommodated accounts as 'current.' See Gross v. CitiMortgage, Inc., 33 F.4th 1246 (9th Cir. 2022) (holding that reporting delinquency during CARES Act forbearance violates § 1681s-2(a)(1)(F)). After Plaintiff disputed this inaccurate reporting, Defendant failed to conduct a reasonable investigation and failed to correct the violation, in breach of § 1681s-2(b)."

**For § 1681e(b) CRA claims:**

> "Defendant [CRA] violated 15 U.S.C. § 1681e(b) by failing to maintain reasonable procedures to assure maximum possible accuracy in the preparation of Plaintiff's consumer report. Plaintiff's mortgage account with [SERVICER] was subject to a COVID-19 forbearance accommodation, during which Plaintiff was not required to make payments. Under 15 U.S.C. § 1681s-2(a)(1)(F), the account was required to be reported as 'current.' Despite this clear legal requirement, Defendant's consumer report on Plaintiff reflected the account as [DELINQUENT/PAST DUE], and Defendant disseminated this inaccurate report to [THIRD PARTIES]. See Gross v. CitiMortgage, Inc., 33 F.4th 1246 (9th Cir. 2022). Defendant's failure to identify and correct this legally-mandated reporting error demonstrates the absence of reasonable procedures."

**For willfulness:**

> "Defendant's violation of § 1681s-2(a)(1)(F) was willful. The CARES Act's reporting requirement is unambiguous: furnishers 'shall' report accommodated accounts as 'current.' 15 U.S.C. § 1681s-2(a)(1)(F)(i)(I). Defendant either knew of this requirement and violated it knowingly, or operated with reckless disregard of a clear statutory mandate. See Gross v. CitiMortgage, Inc., 33 F.4th 1246 (9th Cir. 2022); Safeco Ins. Co. of Am. v. Burr, 551 U.S. 47 (2007). Plaintiff is entitled to statutory damages of $100 to $1,000 under § 1681n(a)(1)(A), punitive damages under § 1681n(a)(2), and attorney's fees under § 1681n(a)(3)."

**For damages allegations related to COVID forbearance:**

> "As a direct and proximate result of Defendant's unlawful reporting of Plaintiff's mortgage as delinquent during Plaintiff's COVID-19 forbearance, Plaintiff suffered: (a) damage to Plaintiff's credit score and creditworthiness during a period of financial vulnerability caused by the pandemic; (b) inability to obtain [REFINANCING / NEW CREDIT / HOUSING] on favorable terms; (c) emotional distress including anxiety and stress caused by the knowledge that Plaintiff's credit was being damaged despite Plaintiff's compliance with the CARES Act forbearance agreement; and (d) [OTHER SPECIFIC DAMAGES]. The harm was particularly severe because Plaintiff complied fully with the forbearance terms and was entitled by federal law to be held harmless from credit reporting consequences."

### Strategic Notes for NDGA Practice

1. **Prevalence of COVID forbearance cases:** Many NDGA clients entered COVID forbearances in 2020-2021, and a significant number experienced improper adverse reporting. These cases are still actionable — the FCRA's statute of limitations (2 years from discovery, 5 years from occurrence under § 1681p) means cases involving 2020-2021 forbearance reporting errors may still be timely through 2025-2026.

2. **Statute of limitations calculation:** For continuing violations (where the furnisher reported delinquency each month during forbearance), each monthly report is a separate violation. The SOL runs from the last inaccurate report, not the first. This extends the filing window.

3. **CFPB enforcement actions as evidence:** The CFPB has taken enforcement actions against servicers for CARES Act reporting violations. These consent orders and civil investigative demands can be used as evidence that (a) the violation is widespread and (b) the statutory requirement was well-publicized, supporting willfulness.

4. **Metro 2 records are key:** Request the furnisher's Metro 2 reporting records (the actual data submitted to CRAs). These machine-readable records show exactly what status code was reported each month. Compare the reported status during the forbearance period against the § 4021 requirement of "current" status.

5. **Pairing with state law claims:** Georgia has the Fair Business Practices Act (FBPA), O.C.G.A. § 10-1-390 et seq. Consider whether CARES Act reporting violations also support state unfair practices claims. This can add attorney's fees and treble damages.

6. **Class action potential:** If discovery reveals that the furnisher systematically misreported forbearance accounts (common with large servicers that had system conversion issues), consider a class action. *Gross* provides the legal framework, and the uniformity of the violation (same furnisher, same statutory requirement, same type of error) supports class certification.

7. **CRA vs. furnisher strategy:** You can sue both the CRA (§ 1681e(b) — should have had procedures to catch obvious § 4021 violations) and the furnisher (§ 1681s-2(b) — failed to investigate and correct after dispute). The CRA may have received industry guidance about CARES Act reporting requirements, making its failure to flag violations a procedures failure.

8. **Document the forbearance thoroughly:** In initial client intake, obtain: (a) the forbearance agreement/approval letter, (b) credit reports showing delinquency during forbearance, (c) all dispute letters and responses, (d) evidence of harm (denials, rate increases, emotional impact). The forbearance documentation is your "smoking gun" — it proves the account should have been reported as current.

9. **Post-forbearance transition errors:** A common variant: the servicer reported correctly DURING forbearance but then reported delinquency for the forbearance period retroactively AFTER the forbearance ended, or failed to properly transition the account to current status when the borrower resumed payments. *Gross*'s reasoning applies to both real-time and retroactive reporting violations.

10. **Willfulness is strong here:** Unlike general accuracy disputes where reasonableness is debatable, CARES Act reporting violations involve an unambiguous statutory command. The furnisher "shall report as current." There is no judgment call, no reasonableness balancing, no interpretive ambiguity. Under *Safeco*, violating an unambiguous statute is at minimum reckless — supporting willful noncompliance and punitive damages.

## Key Takeaway for Practice

*Gross v. CitiMortgage* establishes that reporting delinquency during a CARES Act forbearance violates the FCRA's specific reporting mandate at § 1681s-2(a)(1)(F). For NDGA practice, this case is invaluable for the many clients who entered COVID-19 forbearances and discovered that their servicers nonetheless reported adverse information. The statute is unambiguous ("shall report as current"), making willfulness readily provable. Combined with the prevalence of these errors across the industry, *Gross* provides the foundation for both individual and class claims that can yield significant recoveries. Document the forbearance agreement, obtain the Metro 2 reporting records, and prove the gap between what the law required and what was actually reported.
