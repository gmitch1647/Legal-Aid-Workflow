# Trichell v. Midland Credit Management, Inc., 964 F.3d 990 (11th Cir. 2020)

## Case Information

**Full Citation:** Laura Trichell; Frank Paul Marotta, Jr.; Myles Primo, Plaintiffs-Appellants, v. Midland Credit Management, Inc., Defendant-Appellee, 964 F.3d 990 (11th Cir. 2020)
**Court:** United States Court of Appeals for the Eleventh Circuit
**Date:** July 6, 2020
**Case No:** 18-12838, 18-14008, 18-14937 (consolidated)
**Appeal From:** United States District Courts for the Northern District of Alabama and Middle District of Florida
**Panel:** Judges William Pryor, Newsom, and Brasher
**Author:** Judge Newsom
**Statutes Interpreted:** 15 U.S.C. § 1681s-2(b) (furnisher duty to investigate after notice), Article III standing requirements
**BINDING AUTHORITY:** Yes — 11th Circuit, binding on all district courts in Georgia, Florida, and Alabama, including the Northern District of Georgia

---

## Facts

This consolidated appeal involved three separate plaintiffs — Laura Trichell, Frank Paul Marotta, Jr., and Myles Primo — each of whom brought FCRA claims against Midland Credit Management, Inc. ("Midland"), a debt collector that furnishes information to consumer reporting agencies.

**Trichell:** Disputed a Midland tradeline on her credit report as inaccurate. After her dispute was forwarded to Midland through the CRA, Midland conducted an investigation pursuant to § 1681s-2(b) but allegedly failed to conduct a reasonable investigation and failed to correct the inaccuracy.

**Marotta:** Similarly disputed a Midland account. He alleged that Midland's investigation of his dispute was unreasonable because Midland failed to review all relevant information and simply verified its own records without meaningful analysis.

**Primo:** Disputed information furnished by Midland. He alleged that Midland's § 1681s-2(b) investigation was inadequate and that the continued reporting of inaccurate information damaged his creditworthiness.

**The Standing Question:**

In each case, the district courts dismissed for lack of Article III standing, holding that the plaintiffs failed to allege a sufficiently "concrete" injury. The courts reasoned that mere inaccuracy on a credit report, without more, did not constitute the type of concrete, de facto injury required by Article III after *Spokeo, Inc. v. Robins*, 578 U.S. 330 (2016).

The central question before the Eleventh Circuit was: **What must an FCRA plaintiff allege to establish Article III standing?**

---

## Procedural History

1. **District Courts (N.D. Ala. & M.D. Fla.):** Dismissed the complaints for lack of Article III standing, holding that the plaintiffs failed to allege concrete injuries sufficient to satisfy the injury-in-fact requirement.

2. **Eleventh Circuit:** REVERSED and REMANDED — holding that the plaintiffs adequately alleged concrete injuries sufficient for Article III standing. The court provided detailed guidance on what constitutes concrete injury in the FCRA context.

---

## Holdings

### Holding 1: Inaccurate Credit Reporting Bears a Close Relationship to Defamation

> "The publication of false and defamatory information about a person to a third party gives rise to a concrete, Article III injury — and that's essentially what an inaccurate credit report is. A credit report that inaccurately states that a consumer is delinquent on a debt, or that she owes a debt she does not actually owe, is the functional equivalent of a defamatory publication."

**Translation:** Inaccurate credit reporting is analogous to defamation at common law. Because defamation was a traditionally recognized harm supporting a lawsuit, FCRA claims based on inaccurate reporting satisfy Article III's concreteness requirement — they involve the same TYPE of harm (reputational injury through false publication) that courts have recognized for centuries.

### Holding 2: Credit Report Inaccuracy Disseminated to Third Parties = Concrete Injury

> "When inaccurate information is included in a consumer's credit file and that file is available to — or actually accessed by — third parties such as potential creditors, the consumer suffers a concrete harm analogous to the harm suffered by a victim of defamation."

The court held that where a plaintiff alleges that inaccurate information was included in a credit file that was (or could be) disseminated to third parties, this constitutes sufficient concrete injury. The plaintiff need not prove actual denial of credit to establish standing — the publication of the false information is itself the injury.

### Holding 3: Informational Injury Can Be Concrete

> "When a furnisher fails to conduct a reasonable investigation as required by § 1681s-2(b), and as a result inaccurate information continues to appear on the consumer's credit report, the consumer suffers an informational injury that is sufficiently concrete for Article III purposes."

The court recognized that the consumer's inability to have inaccurate information corrected — due to the furnisher's unreasonable investigation — constitutes a concrete harm because it perpetuates the reputational injury.

### Holding 4: Emotional Distress and Credit Harm Supplement Standing

The court noted that while not strictly required when defamation-analogous harm exists, additional allegations of emotional distress, credit denials, or higher interest rates further support concreteness:

> "To the extent there is any doubt about concreteness, the plaintiffs' allegations of emotional distress, inability to obtain credit, and actual financial harm dispel it."

### Holding 5: Standing Analysis Is Claim-Specific

> "The standing inquiry is claim-specific. A plaintiff may have standing to bring one FCRA claim but not another, depending on whether each particular statutory violation resulted in a concrete harm to that particular plaintiff."

**Translation:** Courts must analyze standing for each FCRA claim separately. A plaintiff might have standing for a § 1681e(b) claim (inaccuracy published) but not for a § 1681g formatting claim (no downstream harm from the formatting error).

## Complete Legal Reasoning

### The Spokeo Framework Applied to FCRA

The Eleventh Circuit's analysis begins with the Supreme Court's decision in *Spokeo, Inc. v. Robins*, 578 U.S. 330 (2016), which held that Article III requires "concrete" injury — not merely a "bare procedural violation" of a statute. The court then applied the framework clarified in *TransUnion LLC v. Ramirez*, noting that concreteness is assessed by whether the alleged injury has a "close relationship" to a harm "traditionally recognized as providing a basis for a lawsuit in American courts."

### Defamation Analogy

The court's central analytical move was to analogize FCRA inaccuracy claims to the common-law tort of defamation:

- **Defamation requires:** (1) a false statement of fact, (2) published to a third party, (3) causing reputational harm
- **FCRA inaccuracy involves:** (1) false information in a credit file, (2) available to/accessed by third-party creditors, (3) causing harm to creditworthiness (a form of reputation)

Because defamation has been a recognized basis for suit since before the founding of the Republic, FCRA claims involving inaccurate reporting published to third parties satisfy Article III's concreteness requirement.

### Distinguishing "Bare Procedural Violations"

The court drew a critical distinction between:

1. **Claims that allege actual inaccuracy + dissemination** — These have standing because they involve the same harm as defamation (concrete)
2. **Claims that allege only a procedural failing without downstream harm** — These may lack standing because a mere procedural misstep that causes no actual harm is a "bare procedural violation"

For § 1681s-2(b) claims (furnisher failure to investigate), the court held that standing exists when the inadequate investigation RESULTS in continued inaccurate reporting. The chain is: inadequate investigation → continued inaccuracy → continued publication of false information → concrete harm analogous to defamation.

### Rejection of Heightened Pleading for Standing

The court rejected the district courts' apparent requirement that FCRA plaintiffs plead specific credit denials or quantified financial losses to establish standing. The court held that:

- The mere publication of false information (inaccurate credit report available to third parties) is itself a concrete injury
- Actual credit denial, while helpful, is not required
- Emotional distress from knowing one's credit report is inaccurate (and being unable to get it corrected) supports concreteness
- The consumer does not need to "prove damages" at the standing stage — they merely need to allege facts showing concrete injury

### Binding Nature in the 11th Circuit

As a published Eleventh Circuit opinion, *Trichell* is binding precedent on all district courts within the circuit, including:
- Northern District of Georgia (Atlanta)
- Middle District of Georgia (Macon)
- Southern District of Georgia (Savannah)
- All Florida districts
- All Alabama districts

District courts cannot deviate from *Trichell*'s standing analysis unless and until it is overruled by the Eleventh Circuit en banc or the Supreme Court.

## Application to LegalFlow Cases

### When to Cite Trichell v. Midland Credit

Cite *Trichell* in any case where:

1. **Defendant raises standing challenge** — *Trichell* is your primary binding authority in the 11th Circuit for FCRA standing. Any time a CRA or furnisher files a 12(b)(1) motion to dismiss for lack of standing, *Trichell* is your first citation.

2. **Post-Ramirez standing challenges** — After the Supreme Court's 2021 decision in *TransUnion v. Ramirez*, defendants have become aggressive about standing challenges. *Trichell* provides the 11th Circuit framework for establishing concrete injury in FCRA cases and predates but is consistent with *Ramirez*.

3. **Furnisher investigation claims (§ 1681s-2(b))** — *Trichell* specifically addresses standing for furnisher investigation claims and holds that when inadequate investigation leads to continued inaccurate reporting, standing exists.

4. **Complaints where you need to plead standing allegations** — Use *Trichell*'s framework to draft your complaint's standing/injury allegations.

5. **Opposing motions to dismiss** — Defense routinely argues plaintiffs lack standing because they cannot show a specific credit denial. *Trichell* rejects this heightened standard.

### Pleading Language

**For standing allegations in complaint:**

> "Plaintiff has suffered concrete injury sufficient to establish Article III standing. Defendant's [inaccurate reporting / failure to investigate] resulted in the continued inclusion of materially inaccurate information on Plaintiff's consumer credit file maintained by [CRA]. This inaccurate information was disseminated to third-party creditors who accessed Plaintiff's credit report, constituting a harm bearing a 'close relationship' to the common-law tort of defamation. See Trichell v. Midland Credit Mgmt., Inc., 964 F.3d 990, 999-1000 (11th Cir. 2020) (holding that inaccurate credit reporting disseminated to third parties constitutes concrete Article III injury analogous to defamation). Specifically, [CREDITOR/LANDLORD/EMPLOYER] accessed Plaintiff's credit report containing the inaccurate information on or about [DATE], resulting in [DENIAL / UNFAVORABLE TERMS / OTHER HARM]."

**For opposing motion to dismiss for lack of standing:**

> "Defendant's motion to dismiss for lack of standing fails under binding Eleventh Circuit precedent. In Trichell v. Midland Credit Management, Inc., 964 F.3d 990 (11th Cir. 2020), the Eleventh Circuit held that FCRA claims based on inaccurate credit reporting satisfy Article III's concreteness requirement because they bear a 'close relationship' to the common-law tort of defamation. 'A credit report that inaccurately states that a consumer is delinquent on a debt, or that she owes a debt she does not actually owe, is the functional equivalent of a defamatory publication.' Id. at 999. Plaintiff alleges that Defendant [reported/continued to report] inaccurate information on Plaintiff's credit file, and that this information was disseminated to third parties. Under Trichell, this constitutes concrete injury as a matter of law."

**For alternative standing theories (emotional distress):**

> "Even if the Court were to require more than publication of inaccurate information, Plaintiff has additionally alleged concrete emotional distress resulting from Defendant's violations. Plaintiff has suffered anxiety, frustration, and stress from the knowledge that inaccurate information appears on [his/her] credit report and that [his/her] repeated efforts to correct it have been ignored. See Trichell, 964 F.3d at 1000 ('To the extent there is any doubt about concreteness, the plaintiffs' allegations of emotional distress, inability to obtain credit, and actual financial harm dispel it.')."

**For furnisher investigation claims specifically:**

> "Plaintiff has standing to pursue [his/her] § 1681s-2(b) claim because Defendant's failure to conduct a reasonable investigation resulted in the continued reporting of inaccurate information. Under Trichell, standing for furnisher investigation claims exists when 'the inadequate investigation results in continued inaccurate reporting' that is disseminated to third parties. 964 F.3d at 1000-01. Here, Defendant received notice of Plaintiff's dispute via [CRA], conducted an inadequate investigation, and as a result, the inaccurate [TRADELINE] continued to appear on Plaintiff's credit report, where it was accessed by [THIRD PARTY] on [DATE]."

### Discovery Requests Supported by Trichell

**Interrogatories (for furnisher investigation adequacy — the merits after surviving standing):**

1. "Describe in detail each step Defendant took in investigating Plaintiff's dispute after receiving notice from [CRA], including: (a) the date notice was received; (b) all records reviewed; (c) all persons consulted; (d) the analysis performed; (e) the conclusion reached; and (f) the response communicated to [CRA]."

2. "Identify all documents Defendant reviewed during its investigation of Plaintiff's dispute and state whether Defendant reviewed any documents beyond its own internal records."

3. "State whether Defendant's investigation of Plaintiff's dispute resulted in any modification to the information it reported to any consumer reporting agency, and if so, describe the modification."

**Requests for Production:**

4. "Produce all documents reflecting Defendant's investigation of Plaintiff's dispute, including but not limited to the ACDV received from [CRA], all internal records reviewed, all notes or memoranda created during the investigation, and the response sent to [CRA]."

5. "Produce Defendant's written policies and procedures for investigating consumer disputes received from consumer reporting agencies pursuant to 15 U.S.C. § 1681s-2(b)."

6. "Produce all credit reports on Plaintiff obtained or accessed by third parties during the period [DATE] through [DATE], or alternatively, produce Defendant's records showing which CRAs the disputed information was reported to and the dates of such reporting."

**Requests for Admission (to establish standing facts):**

7. "Admit that Defendant reported [SPECIFIC INFORMATION] to [CRA] regarding Plaintiff during the period [DATE] through [DATE]."

8. "Admit that [CRA] included the information reported by Defendant in Plaintiff's consumer credit file during the period [DATE] through [DATE]."

9. "Admit that Plaintiff's consumer credit file was accessed by one or more third parties during the period [DATE] through [DATE]."

10. "Admit that the information reported by Defendant regarding Plaintiff was inaccurate in that [DESCRIBE INACCURACY]."

### Strategic Notes for NDGA Practice

1. **Plead standing affirmatively:** After *Ramirez* and *Trichell*, always include a dedicated "Standing/Concrete Injury" section in your complaint. Allege: (a) inaccurate information, (b) included in credit file, (c) disseminated to identified third parties, (d) resulting harm (denial, higher rates, emotional distress). Do not assume standing is obvious — anticipate the challenge.

2. **Get credit pulls in discovery:** The strongest standing evidence is documentation that a third party actually pulled the consumer's report during the period the inaccuracy was present. Subpoena the CRA's records showing all inquiries/access during the relevant period. Each inquiry where the inaccurate data was present is a "publication" under the defamation analogy.

3. **Pre-suit preparation:** Before filing, obtain your client's credit reports from all three bureaus. Identify any hard inquiries during the inaccuracy period. These are your "third-party dissemination" facts for standing. Also have the client identify any credit applications that were denied or resulted in unfavorable terms.

4. **Trichell + Ramirez together:** *Trichell* (11th Cir. 2020) preceded *Ramirez* (SCOTUS 2021) but is consistent with it. When the defense cites *Ramirez* to attack standing, respond that *Trichell* already applied the same framework in the 11th Circuit: inaccuracy + dissemination = concrete injury. Note that the 1,853 class members in *Ramirez* whose reports were disseminated DID have standing — your client is in that category, not the 6,332 without dissemination.

5. **Anticipate the "no denial" argument:** Defense will argue that without an actual credit denial, there is no concrete injury. *Trichell* forecloses this: the publication of false information IS the injury (like defamation — you need not prove you actually lost a job because of the defamatory statement). Credit denial is additional evidence of harm, not a prerequisite.

6. **Emotional distress as backup:** Even if the court is skeptical about the defamation analogy in your specific case, *Trichell* recognizes emotional distress as an independent basis for concreteness. Plead emotional distress facts in your complaint — anxiety about the inaccuracy, time spent disputing, frustration, sleep disruption.

7. **Standing for § 1681i claims (reinvestigation):** *Trichell* primarily addresses § 1681s-2(b) furnisher claims, but its reasoning applies equally to § 1681i (CRA reinvestigation) claims: an inadequate reinvestigation that perpetuates inaccurate reporting leads to continued defamation-analogous harm.

8. **Distinguish "bare procedural violations":** If your case involves a procedural claim (notice timing, format of disclosure), be prepared to show downstream harm beyond the procedural failing itself. *Trichell* is strongest for substantive inaccuracy claims; for procedural claims, you need additional facts showing the procedural failure caused concrete harm.

9. **Class action standing:** If contemplating a class action in NDGA, *Trichell* provides the framework for establishing class-wide standing. Focus on class members whose reports were actually disseminated (the *Ramirez* 1,853 model, not the 6,332 model).

## Key Takeaway for Practice

*Trichell v. Midland Credit Management* is the binding Eleventh Circuit authority for establishing FCRA standing in NDGA. Its core holding — that inaccurate credit reporting disseminated to third parties is analogous to defamation and therefore constitutes concrete Article III injury — should be included in every FCRA complaint filed in the Northern District of Georgia. When defendants raise standing challenges (which they do in virtually every case post-*Ramirez*), *Trichell* is your first and strongest citation. Plead dissemination facts (who pulled the report, when, and what happened as a result), and you will survive standing challenges as a matter of binding circuit law.
