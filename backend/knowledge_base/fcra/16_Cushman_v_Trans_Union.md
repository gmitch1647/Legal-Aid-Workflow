# Cushman v. Trans Union Corporation, 115 F.3d 220 (3d Cir. 1997)

## Case Information

**Full Citation:** Jennifer Cushman, Appellant, v. Trans Union Corporation, 115 F.3d 220 (3d Cir. 1997)
**Court:** United States Court of Appeals for the Third Circuit
**Date:** June 9, 1997
**Case No:** 96-1553
**Appeal From:** United States District Court for the Eastern District of Pennsylvania, 920 F. Supp. 80
**Statutes Interpreted:** 15 U.S.C. § 1681i(a) (reinvestigation), § 1681e(b) (reasonable procedures), § 1681n (willful noncompliance / punitive damages)

---

## Facts

In the summer of 1993, an unknown person — possibly a member of Jennifer Cushman's household in Philadelphia — fraudulently applied for credit cards under Cushman's name from multiple credit grantors, including American Express. These fraudulent accounts became delinquent and were reported to Trans Union Corporation (TUC) as negative tradelines on Cushman's credit file.

Upon discovering the fraudulent accounts on her credit report, Cushman notified Trans Union that the delinquent accounts were not hers and were the product of identity theft/fraud. She sent a written dispute letter to TUC explaining that an unauthorized person had obtained credit in her name.

Cushman sent a SECOND letter to TUC reiterating her disagreement with the information on her report and offering to sign affidavits attesting that the delinquencies were not hers. She provided TUC with information to help verify the fraud.

## Trans Union's Reinvestigation (What They Did Wrong)

Trans Union's reinvestigation was **perfunctory** — it consisted essentially of:

1. Sending an automated verification request (the precursor to modern e-OSCAR ACDVs) back to the original creditors who had reported the accounts
2. When the creditors confirmed the accounts existed under Cushman's name, TUC verified the information and took no further action
3. TUC did NOT:
   - Obtain access to pertinent documents from the credit grantors (such as original applications)
   - Perform or request a handwriting comparison on the fraudulent applications
   - Review the supporting documentation Cushman offered to provide
   - Investigate beyond the original source (the creditor that reported the data)
   - Provide Cushman with a description of its reinvestigation procedures

At no time did TUC take steps to obtain the underlying credit applications that would have revealed the fraud (different handwriting, different addresses, etc.). TUC simply re-confirmed with the same source that originally reported the data — a circular process that guaranteed the same result.

**Evidence of cost-cutting:** Evidence showed that TUC's reinvestigation clerks were paid minimally and required to handle high volumes of disputes, suggesting the company prioritized cost savings over thorough investigation.

## Procedural History

1. **District Court (E.D. Pa.):** Granted summary judgment to Trans Union, concluding as a matter of law that TUC fulfilled its reinvestigation obligation under § 1681i(a)
2. **Third Circuit:** REVERSED and REMANDED — holding the district court erred in concluding TUC's reinvestigation was reasonable as a matter of law

## Holdings

### Holding 1: CRA May Be Required to Go Beyond Original Source

> "A credit reporting agency may be required, in certain circumstances, to verify the accuracy of its initial source of information" in order to fulfill its § 1681i(a) reinvestigation obligation.

**Translation:** Simply re-asking the same creditor "is this account accurate?" is NOT always sufficient. When the consumer provides specific reasons to doubt the source's accuracy (e.g., fraud allegations with supporting documentation), the CRA must investigate further.

### Holding 2: Cost-Benefit Framework for Reinvestigation Scope

The scope of the CRA's reinvestigation duty depends on TWO factors:

1. **The cost of verifying the accuracy of the source** versus **the potential harm inaccurately reported information may cause the consumer**
2. **Whether the consumer has alerted the CRA to the possibility that the source may be unreliable** OR **the CRA itself knows or should know the source is unreliable**

**Translation:** When the potential harm to the consumer (inability to get credit, reputational damage) is HIGH and the cost of further verification is LOW (requesting the original application is cheap), the CRA must do more than a rubber-stamp verification.

### Holding 3: Perfunctory Reinvestigation Is Not Reasonable

The court held that TUC's reinvestigation — which consisted solely of re-confirming with the original reporting creditor without examining underlying documents — was the kind of "perfunctory reinvestigation" that may violate § 1681i(a).

### Holding 4: Punitive Damages Available

If Cushman could prove that TUC adopted its reinvestigation policy either:
- **Knowing** that policy to be in contravention of consumer rights under the FCRA, OR
- **In reckless disregard** of whether the policy contravened those rights

...she could be awarded punitive damages under § 1681n.

**Translation:** A corporate POLICY of conducting only superficial reinvestigations — rather than a one-off mistake — supports willfulness and punitive damages.

---

## The Reinvestigation Standard (Key Legal Framework)

After *Cushman*, a CRA's § 1681i(a) reinvestigation must:

1. **Not merely re-confirm with the original source** when the consumer has provided specific reasons to doubt that source's accuracy
2. **Consider the cost-benefit balance**: cheap verification steps (requesting documents, comparing signatures) must be taken when the consumer faces significant harm
3. **Respond to the SPECIFIC dispute raised**: if the consumer says "this is fraud," the reinvestigation must actually investigate fraud (not just verify the account exists)
4. **Go beyond automated verification** when the circumstances demand it — human review of documents, contacting additional sources, examining underlying records
5. **Not be perfunctory**: a reinvestigation that reaches the same conclusion in minutes without examining any new evidence is suspect

## Application to LegalFlow Cases

### When to Cite Cushman

Cite *Cushman* in any case where:

1. **CRA conducted only an e-OSCAR ACDV verification** — This is the modern equivalent of what TUC did. If the CRA only sent an ACDV to the furnisher and accepted the response without reviewing consumer-submitted documents or investigating further, *Cushman* directly applies.

2. **Consumer provided supporting documentation with dispute** — If your client attached credit reports, dispute letters, account statements, or other evidence and the CRA ignored them, cite *Cushman*'s holding that CRAs must consider all information submitted.

3. **Identity theft / fraud cases** — *Cushman* arose from fraud. When the consumer specifically alleges fraud and the CRA merely re-confirms with the furnisher who reported the fraudulent account, *Cushman* is directly on point.

4. **Pattern of perfunctory reinvestigation** — Use *Cushman* to argue willfulness when the CRA has a POLICY of conducting superficial investigations (e.g., AHT targets, offshore handlers, no document review).

5. **Arguing willfulness / punitive damages** — *Cushman* establishes that a corporate POLICY of inadequate reinvestigation (rather than an individual mistake) supports willful noncompliance under § 1681n.

### Specific Pleading Applications

**In § 1681i(a)(1)(A) counts:**
> "Defendant's reinvestigation was unreasonable under *Cushman v. Trans Union Corp.*, 115 F.3d 220 (3d Cir. 1997), because Defendant merely re-verified the disputed information with the original furnisher without examining the supporting documentation Plaintiff submitted with [his/her] dispute, without obtaining account-level documentation from the furnisher, and without taking any steps beyond the automated e-OSCAR ACDV process."

**In willfulness sections:**
> "Defendant's reinvestigation policy — which relies exclusively on automated ACDV transmissions without human review of consumer-submitted documentation — constitutes the same 'perfunctory reinvestigation' condemned in *Cushman v. Trans Union Corp.*, 115 F.3d 220, 225 (3d Cir. 1997). Given that *Cushman* has been settled law for [X] years and Defendant is aware of this standard, Defendant's continued adherence to a policy of superficial reinvestigation constitutes willful noncompliance under 15 U.S.C. § 1681n."

**In discovery (interrogatories):**
> "Describe in detail the steps taken during the reinvestigation of Plaintiff's dispute, including but not limited to: (a) whether any documents submitted by Plaintiff were reviewed; (b) whether account-level documentation was obtained from the furnisher; (c) the total time spent on the reinvestigation; (d) whether any investigation occurred beyond transmission of an ACDV and receipt of a response."

### Cost-Benefit Argument Template

When arguing that the CRA should have done more:

> "Under *Cushman*'s cost-benefit framework, the scope of reinvestigation must be evaluated by weighing (1) the cost of verification against (2) the potential harm to the consumer. Here, [describe the low cost — e.g., 'requesting the original signed application from the furnisher would have cost virtually nothing'] while the harm to Plaintiff was significant [describe harm — credit denials, higher rates, emotional distress]. Under these circumstances, Defendant's failure to take this basic verification step renders its reinvestigation unreasonable as a matter of law."

## Related Cases (cite alongside Cushman)

- *Stevenson v. TRW Inc.*, 987 F.2d 288 (5th Cir. 1993) — "Parroting" the creditor's response without independent evaluation is not reasonable reinvestigation
- *Hinkle v. Midland Credit Mgmt.*, 827 F.3d 1295 (11th Cir. 2016) — Reinvestigation must be more than rubber-stamping furnisher verification
- *Gorman v. Wolpoff & Abramson*, 584 F.3d 1147 (9th Cir. 2009) — Furnisher duty to investigate under § 1681s-2(b) parallels CRA duty
- *Dennis v. BEH-1, LLC*, 520 F.3d 1066 (9th Cir. 2008) — Scope of reasonable reinvestigation is normally a jury question

## Key Takeaway for the AI Drafter

Every time a CRA conducts a reinvestigation that consists solely of sending an e-OSCAR ACDV to the furnisher and accepting the response without:
- Reviewing consumer-submitted documents
- Requesting account-level documentation
- Performing any human review beyond the automated system
- Taking ANY step beyond re-asking the original source

...that reinvestigation is potentially unreasonable under *Cushman*, and the CRA's continued reliance on such a policy after 27+ years of *Cushman* being settled law strongly supports a finding of willful noncompliance and punitive damages under § 1681n.
