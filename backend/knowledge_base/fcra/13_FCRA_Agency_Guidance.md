# FCRA Agency Guidance — FTC and CFPB  
  
A working reference of FTC and CFPB interpretive, supervisory, and enforcement materials under the Fair Credit Reporting Act. Citations point to primary documents on ftc.gov and consumerfinance.gov.  
  
---  
  
## Part I — FTC Guidance  
  
### 1. "40 Years of Experience with the Fair Credit Reporting Act: An FTC Staff Report with Summary of Interpretations" (July 2011)  
  
**Citation.** Federal Trade Commission, *40 Years of Experience with the Fair Credit Reporting Act: An FTC Staff Report with Summary of Interpretations* (July 2011).  
  
**Source.** <https://www.ftc.gov/reports/40-years-experience-fair-credit-reporting-act-ftc-staff-report-summary-interpretations>  
  
**Background.** When the Bureau issued this report it simultaneously **withdrew the 1990 FTC Commentary on the FCRA** (16 C.F.R. Part 600), which had become obsolete after the Consumer Credit Reporting Reform Act of 1996 and the FACT Act of 2003. The 2011 report is the FTC staff's consolidated interpretive guide and is, alongside the CFPB's official interpretations, the most-cited interpretive source for the statute. It is informal staff guidance, not a rule, but courts regularly treat it as persuasive authority.  
  
**Why it matters.** Because the FCRA itself was written in 1970 and amended piecemeal, the statutory text is often ambiguous on operational details. The 2011 report provides the FTC's view on most ambiguities and remains the canonical staff interpretation for sections not addressed in CFPB advisory opinions.  
  
**Section-by-section summary.**  
  
- **§ 603 ("Definitions" — 15 U.S.C. § 1681a).**  
  - "Consumer report" reaches any communication of information by a CRA bearing on the seven specified factors (credit-worthiness, character, general reputation, personal characteristics, mode of living, etc.) and used or expected to be used for one of the FCRA "permissible purposes" listed in § 604.  
  - The "**transaction or experience**" exclusion (§ 603(d)(2)(A)(i)) covers a person's own data about its own dealings with a consumer. Sharing such data with affiliates is not a "report."  
  - The "**other information**" affiliate-sharing exclusion (§ 603(d)(2)(A)(iii)) only applies if the consumer has been given notice and an opportunity to opt out under FCRA § 624 and Reg V Subpart C.  
  - "Employment purposes" includes evaluation for promotion, reassignment, or retention — not just hiring.  
  - "Investigative consumer report" is limited to reports that include information obtained through personal interviews with neighbors, friends, associates, etc.  
  - A "**consumer reporting agency**" is a person that, for monetary fees or on a cooperative nonprofit basis, regularly engages in the practice of assembling or evaluating consumer credit information or other information on consumers to furnish consumer reports to third parties.  
  - "**File**" includes everything in a CRA's possession about the consumer; CRAs must disclose all information in the consumer's file at the time of a disclosure request under § 609.  
  
- **§ 604 (Permissible Purposes — 15 U.S.C. § 1681b).**  
  - The permissible-purpose list is **exhaustive**; obtaining a report for any other reason is unlawful.  
  - "**Legitimate business need**" (§ 604(a)(3)(F)) must arise from a transaction initiated by the consumer or to review an existing account. The FTC reads this narrowly — pre-employment background checks must use § 604(a)(3)(B) (employment) and meet § 604(b)'s special procedures (disclosure, written authorization, pre-adverse-action notice).  
  - **Prescreening** under § 604(c) is permitted only for "firm offers of credit or insurance" — meaning the consumer who meets the criteria must in fact be granted the offered credit or insurance.  
  - Reports obtained for employment require: a stand-alone written disclosure to the applicant, written authorization, certification to the CRA, and **pre-adverse-action notice** with a copy of the report and the Summary of Consumer Rights (§ 604(b)(3)).  
  
- **§ 605 (Information contained in consumer reports — obsolescence / "7-year rule" — 15 U.S.C. § 1681c).**  
  - 7-year reporting limit (10 years for bankruptcies) does not apply to credit transactions of $150,000 or more, life insurance of $150,000 or more, or employment of $75,000 or more.  
  - The 7-year clock for collection accounts and charge-offs runs from the **date of first delinquency** (§ 605(c)).  
  - § 605(a)(6) restricts reporting of medical-provider identifying information.  
  
- **§ 606 (Investigative consumer reports).** Special disclosure to the consumer required within 3 days; consumer entitled to a complete and accurate disclosure of the nature and scope of the investigation upon written request.  
  
- **§ 607 (Compliance procedures — "maximum possible accuracy" — 15 U.S.C. § 1681e).**  
  - CRAs must maintain "reasonable procedures to assure maximum possible accuracy" (§ 607(b)). The 2011 report endorses the long-standing FTC position that **name-only matching is not reasonable** — multiple identifiers (SSN, DOB, address) are generally required.  
  - CRAs must use reasonable procedures to limit furnishing of reports to permissible-purpose users (§ 607(a)) and to require user certifications.  
  
- **§ 609 (Disclosures to consumers).** File disclosure must include all information in the file at the time of the request, sources of information (with limited exceptions for investigative reports), recipients of reports within the past year (2 years for employment), and the consumer's rights (the model **Summary of Consumer Rights**).  
  
- **§ 611 (Reinvestigation of disputes — 15 U.S.C. § 1681i).** CRA must conduct a free reinvestigation generally within 30 days (45 days if consumer provides additional information during the dispute period). Must consider all relevant information submitted by the consumer. After investigation, must promptly delete inaccurate or unverifiable information and provide notice to consumer.  
  
- **§ 613 (Public record information — employment reports).** When a CRA furnishes public record information likely to have adverse effect for employment purposes, it must either (a) notify the consumer that public-record information is being reported, or (b) maintain strict procedures to ensure the information is complete and up to date. The 2011 report notes the rise of dual-track compliance and the FTC's preference for option (a).  
  
- **§ 615 (Requirements on users — adverse action, risk-based pricing — 15 U.S.C. § 1681m).** Adverse-action notice (§ 615(a)) is required whenever the user takes adverse action based in whole or in part on a consumer report. Must include the CRA's name, address, and phone number; a statement that the CRA did not make the decision and cannot provide reasons; and notice of the consumer's right to a free copy and to dispute. The credit-score disclosure component was added by FACT Act and is mandatory when a credit score was used.  
  
- **§ 615(e) (Red Flags) and § 615(h) (Risk-Based Pricing).** Operational details now governed by Regulation V Subparts J and H. Report endorses the joint agency guidelines.  
  
- **§ 616 / § 617 (Civil liability — 15 U.S.C. §§ 1681n, 1681o).** Willful violations: actual damages or statutory damages of $100–$1,000 per violation, punitive damages, attorney's fees. Negligent violations: actual damages plus attorney's fees. The 2011 report flags the **employer pre-adverse-action notice** as a recurring source of class-action exposure (per *Safeco Insurance Co. v. Burr*, 551 U.S. 47 (2007), willfulness can be based on objectively unreasonable readings of the statute).  
  
- **§ 619 (Obtaining information under false pretenses) and § 620 (Unauthorized disclosures by CRA officers/employees).** Criminal penalties.  
  
- **§ 621 (Administrative enforcement — 15 U.S.C. § 1681s).** Pre-2011 enforcement was primarily FTC for nonbanks and the federal banking agencies for banks. After Dodd-Frank (2010), CFPB has primary rulemaking authority and shares enforcement with FTC and the banking agencies. FTC retained primary authority over motor-vehicle dealers and most nonbank, non-financial-institution entities.  
  
- **§ 623 (Duties of furnishers — 15 U.S.C. § 1681s-2).**  
  - **§ 623(a)** duties (accuracy, notice of dispute, correction) are enforced only by federal agencies and state AGs (no private right of action — *Nelson v. Chase Manhattan Mortgage Corp.*).  
  - **§ 623(b)** duties (investigation of indirect disputes routed through CRAs) **do** give rise to a private right of action — the leading basis for furnisher litigation.  
  
- **§ 624 (Affiliate sharing — 15 U.S.C. § 1681s-3).** Implemented by Reg V Subpart C.  
  
- **§ 625 (Preemption).** Discusses the complex FCRA preemption landscape — broad federal preemption of state laws relating to subject matters regulated under § 623 (furnishers), § 605 (information contained in reports), § 615(a) (adverse-action notices), and certain others. Some state laws are preserved (e.g., Massachusetts and Vermont consumer reporting laws expressly excepted; California ICRAA partly preserved).  
  
**Effect on practice.** Several courts and the CFPB have cited the 2011 report when interpreting ambiguous statutory terms. For RAG purposes the report is the closest thing to an "official commentary" available.

## Part II — CFPB Advisory Opinions  
  
The CFPB's Advisory Opinion Program (12 C.F.R. Part 1074) issues interpretive rules on the Bureau's view of specific FCRA questions. Each advisory opinion is published in the Federal Register and is binding on the Bureau in enforcement decisions; courts may give it deference.  
  
### 1. Advisory Opinion on "Fair Credit Reporting; Name-Only Matching Procedures" (Nov. 2021)  
  
**Citation.** *Fair Credit Reporting; Name-Only Matching Procedures*, 86 Fed. Reg. 62468 (Nov. 10, 2021); effective Nov. 10, 2021.  
  
**Sources.**  
- CFPB PDF: <https://files.consumerfinance.gov/f/documents/cfpb_name-only-matching_advisory-opinion_2021-11.pdf>  
- Federal Register: <https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2021/11/10/2021-24471/fair-credit-reporting-name-only-matching-procedures>  
- Director Chopra statement: <https://files.consumerfinance.gov/f/documents/cfpb_name-only-matching-advisory-opinion_directors-statement_2021-11.pdf>  
  
**What it says.** A CRA that uses inadequate matching procedures — in particular, **matching information to a consumer based only on identical or similar first and last names** — is not following "reasonable procedures to assure maximum possible accuracy" within the meaning of FCRA § 607(b) (15 U.S.C. § 1681e(b)). The opinion reasons that names are highly non-unique (especially for common names and for members of communities with concentrated surname distributions), and that mismatches systematically harm consumers, particularly Black and Hispanic consumers. The opinion does not prescribe a specific identifier mix but states that multiple identifiers beyond name (e.g., DOB, full SSN, address history) are generally required.  
  
**Why it matters.** Squarely directed at **background-screening companies and tenant-screening companies**, where name-only matching has been most prevalent. Used in subsequent CFPB enforcement; informs the Spring 2024 supervisory findings on background-check accuracy.  
  
---  
  
### 2. Advisory Opinion on "Fair Credit Reporting; Permissible Purposes for Furnishing, Using, and Obtaining Consumer Reports" (July 2022)  
  
**Citation.** *Fair Credit Reporting; Permissible Purposes for Furnishing, Using, and Obtaining Consumer Reports*, 87 Fed. Reg. 41243 (July 12, 2022).  
  
**Source.**  
- CFPB PDF: <https://files.consumerfinance.gov/f/documents/cfpb_fair-credit-reporting_advisory-opinion_2022-07.pdf>  
  
**What it says.**  
- The "permissible purposes" listed in FCRA § 604(a) are **consumer-specific**: they apply only with respect to the consumer who is the subject of the user's request. A CRA may not furnish a consumer report under § 604(a)(3) unless it has **reason to believe that all of the consumer report information** included pertains to the consumer who is the subject of the user's request. Combined with the name-only-matching opinion, this means a CRA that "matches" using insufficient identifiers cannot have the required "reason to believe."  
- Section 604(f) "**strictly prohibits**" users from obtaining or using consumer reports without a permissible purpose. The opinion describes this as a strict-liability privacy provision.  
- **Disclaimers** by users (e.g., "we will only pull a report if we have a permissible purpose") do not, by themselves, give a CRA reason to believe a permissible purpose exists; the CRA must use reasonable procedures (§ 607(a)).  
  
**Why it matters.** Heavily cited in litigation against CRAs and resellers; expands liability theories around impermissible-purpose pulls and obligates CRAs to actively verify user purposes.  
  
---  
  
### 3. Advisory Opinion on "Consumer Information Requests to Large Banks" (Oct. 2023)  
  
**Citation.** *Consumer Information Requests to Large Banks*, 88 Fed. Reg. 71279 (Oct. 16, 2023). Primarily implements CFPA § 1034(c), but touches FCRA at the margin.  
  
**Note.** Not strictly an FCRA opinion. Listed here because it intersects with consumer information disclosure regimes; not summarized further.

## Part III — CFPB Supervisory Highlights on Credit Reporting  
  
### 1. Supervisory Highlights — Consumer Reporting Special Edition, Issue 20 (Dec. 2019)  
  
**Source.** <https://files.consumerfinance.gov/f/documents/cfpb_supervisory-highlights_issue-20_122019.pdf>  
  
Findings included inadequate furnisher policies and procedures under Reg V § 1022.42; inadequate CRA dispute investigations under FCRA § 611; failure to provide adverse information dispute notice. Sets baseline that subsequent editions build on.  
  
---  
  
### 2. Supervisory Highlights, Issue 32 — Spring 2024 Consumer Reporting Special Edition (April 2024)  
  
**Citation.** CFPB, *Supervisory Highlights, Issue 32: Spring 2024 Consumer Reporting Special Edition*.  
  
**Sources.**  
- CFPB summary page: <https://www.consumerfinance.gov/data-research/research-reports/supervisory-highlights-issue-32-spring-2024/>  
- Full PDF: <https://files.consumerfinance.gov/f/documents/cfpb_supervisory-highlights_issue-32_2024-04.pdf>  
- Press release: <https://www.consumerfinance.gov/about-us/newsroom/cfpb-finds-violations-of-credit-report-accuracy-requirements-including-for-survivors-of-human-trafficking/>  
  
**Coverage period.** Exams completed April 1, 2023 – December 31, 2023.  
  
**Key findings.**  
- **Failure to block identity-theft and human-trafficking information.** CRCs refused to honor block requests based on **overbroad screening criteria** — e.g., automatically rejecting all blocks where the consumer had previously had an account with the furnisher, contrary to FCRA § 605B's 4-business-day block timeline.  
- **Inadequate "maximum possible accuracy" procedures (FCRA § 607(b)).** Findings included use of inadequate matching procedures and failure to remove information after disputes were resolved in the consumer's favor.  
- **Furnishers continuing to report fraudulent accounts** for years after determining the accounts were fraudulent.  
- **Furnishers not flagging disputed status** on information that was actively in dispute, in violation of FCRA § 623(a)(3).  
- **Reasonable investigation of indirect disputes** — furnishers' automated systems verifying disputed information despite insufficient records.  
  
**Why it matters.** Sets CFPB enforcement priorities for credit reporting; cited in subsequent enforcement actions (Equifax 2025) and in private litigation.  
  
---  
  
### 3. Supervisory Highlights, Issue 37 — Winter 2024 (Jan. 2025)  
  
**Citation.** CFPB, *Supervisory Highlights, Issue 37: Winter 2024*, 90 Fed. Reg. 607 (Jan. 6, 2025).  
  
**Sources.**  
- CFPB summary: <https://www.consumerfinance.gov/data-research/research-reports/supervisory-highlights-issue-37-winter-2024/>  
- Full PDF: <https://files.consumerfinance.gov/f/documents/cfpb_Supervisory-Highlights-Issue-37_Winter-2024.pdf>  
- Federal Register: <https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2025/01/06/2024-31670/supervisory-highlights-issue-37-winter-2024>  
  
**Coverage period.** Exams generally completed January 1, 2024 – October 1, 2024. Focuses on **deposits, furnishing, and short-term small-dollar lending**.  
  
**Key furnishing findings.**  
- **Furnisher policies and procedures** under § 1022.42 were inadequate; deficiencies persisted such that inaccuracies remained on consumer reports for over a year on average before remediation.  
- **Indirect-dispute investigations** were unreasonable where automated systems verified disputed information against records that were themselves insufficient to confirm the disputed data.  
- **Identity-theft procedures** failed to incorporate the required notifications to consumers and to CRAs.  
  
**Why it matters.** Continues the supervisory focus on furnishers as the upstream source of credit-report inaccuracies; foreshadows the January 2025 Equifax consent order and Experian lawsuit.

## Part IV — Recent CFPB Enforcement Actions  
  
### 1. *In re Equifax, Inc. and Equifax Information Services LLC* (Jan. 17, 2025)  
  
**Citation.** 2025-CFPB-0002 (Consent Order).  
  
**Sources.**  
- Consent order: <https://files.consumerfinance.gov/f/documents/cfpb_equifax-inc-consent-order_2025-01.pdf>  
- Stipulation: <https://files.consumerfinance.gov/f/documents/cfpb_equifax-inc-stipulation_2025-01.pdf>  
- Enforcement action page: <https://www.consumerfinance.gov/enforcement/actions/equifax-inc-and-equifax-consumer-services-llc/>  
  
**Violations found.** FCRA § 611 reinvestigation failures, including:  
- Failure to consider information submitted by consumers in disputes.  
- Inadequate handling of bankruptcy-related disputes (Equifax repeatedly failed to remove discharged debts).  
- Poorly drafted dispute-results letters that misrepresented the outcome.  
- Inadequate quality control over its third-party dispute vendors.  
  
**Relief.** **$15 million civil money penalty**. Binding compliance obligations through **2030**, including reforms to dispute-handling, governance, and vendor management.  
  
---  
  
### 2. *CFPB v. Experian Information Solutions, Inc.* (Jan. 7, 2025) — Pending Litigation  
  
**Citation.** Civil complaint filed in U.S. District Court, S.D. Cal.  
  
**Sources.**  
- Complaint: <https://files.consumerfinance.gov/f/documents/cfpb_experian-information-solutions-complaint_2025-01.pdf>  
- Press release: <https://www.consumerfinance.gov/about-us/newsroom/cfpb-sues-experian-for-sham-investigations-of-credit-report-errors/>  
- Enforcement page: <https://www.consumerfinance.gov/enforcement/actions/experian-information-solutions-inc/>  
  
**Allegations.** "Sham" reinvestigations under FCRA § 611 — the CFPB alleges Experian's processes are designed to rubber-stamp furnisher responses without independent CRA review, fail to intake unique information from consumers, and rely on form letters that mischaracterize dispute outcomes. **No consent order to date**; matter remains in litigation.  
  
---  
  
### 3. *In re TransUnion, LLC, et al.* (Oct. 2023)  
  
**Citation.** 2023-CFPB-0011.  
  
**Source.** <https://files.consumerfinance.gov/f/documents/cfpb_transunion-trans-union-llc-transunion-interactive-inc_consent-order_2023-10.pdf>  
  
**Violations.** Failure to properly process **security freeze and lock requests** — TransUnion's systems sent consumers confirmation messages indicating success even when the request had errored out, leaving consumers unprotected. Also addressed marketing-related FCRA and CFPA issues.  
  
---  
  
### 4. *In re TD Bank, N.A.* (Sept. 2024)  
  
**Citation.** 2024-CFPB-0009.  
  
**Source.** <https://files.consumerfinance.gov/f/documents/cfpb_td-bank-na-consent-order_2024-09.pdf>  
  
**Violations (FCRA furnisher).** TD Bank:  
- Furnished "inaccurate, negative information" to CRAs.  
- Failed to promptly correct information after determining it was inaccurate.  
- Failed to properly report account statuses and dates of first delinquency.  
- Failed to conduct reasonable and timely investigations of indirect disputes (§ 1681s-2(b)).  
- Failed to notify consumers when disputes were determined frivolous or irrelevant.  
- Violated the Furnisher Rule, Reg V § 1022.42.  
  
**Relief.** **$7.76 million in consumer redress** (approx. $150 per affected consumer) plus a **$20 million civil money penalty**.  
  
---  
  
### 5. *In re Commonwealth Financial Systems, Inc.* (Dec. 2023)  
  
**Citation.** 2023-CFPB-0018.  
  
**Source.** <https://files.consumerfinance.gov/f/documents/cfpb_commonwealth_consent-order_2023-12.pdf>  
  
**Violations.** Third-party medical-debt collector. Furnished disputed information to CRAs **without indicating it was disputed**, in at least 10,906 instances, in violation of FCRA § 623(a)(3). Also violated FDCPA.  
  
**Relief.** **$95,000 CMP** and a **permanent ban** from debt collection, debt buying/selling, and consumer reporting activities.

### 6. Earlier major actions

- *In re Bank of America, N.A.* (2023-CFPB-0007, July 2023) — among other counts, FCRA violations related to credit-card account openings (unauthorized credit pulls / failure of permissible purpose).

## Part V — CFPB Rulemakings on FCRA Topics  
  
### 1. Medical Debt / Medical Information Rule (Reg V Subpart D)  
  
**Status.** **Finalized January 2025, vacated July 2025.**  
  
**Sources.**  
- Final rule: <https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2025/01/14/2024-30824/prohibition-on-creditors-and-consumer-reporting-agencies-concerning-medical-information-regulation-v>  
- CFPB final rule PDF: <https://files.consumerfinance.gov/f/documents/cfpb_med-debt-final-rule_2025-01.pdf>  
- Executive summary: <https://files.consumerfinance.gov/f/documents/cfpb_FCRA-Med-Debt_Executive-Summary_2025-01.pdf>  
- Final-rule landing page: <https://www.consumerfinance.gov/rules-policy/final-rules/prohibition-on-creditors-and-consumer-reporting-agencies-concerning-medical-information-regulation-v/>  
- 2024 NPRM: <https://files.consumerfinance.gov/f/documents/cfpb_fcra-med-debt-proposed-rule_2024-06.pdf>  
  
**What it did.**  
- Removed the "**financial information exception**" in current Reg V § 1022.30(d) that allowed creditors to use coded medical information.  
- Restricted CRAs from furnishing medical-debt information to creditors in connection with credit-eligibility determinations.  
- Effective date was set for **March 17, 2025**.  
  
**Why it was vacated.** On July 11, 2025, the U.S. District Court for the Eastern District of Texas vacated the rule in *Cornerstone Credit Union League v. CFPB*, holding the CFPB exceeded its statutory authority because FCRA § 604(g) and § 605(a) themselves authorize the furnishing and consideration of coded medical debt information. The CFPB consented to the vacatur. The pre-2025 § 1022.30 framework remains in force.  
  
**Why it still matters.** Industry has largely moved toward voluntary removal of medical collection items consistent with the 2022 industry agreement (no paid medical collections; medical collections under $500 removed from reports; one-year delay before reporting unpaid medical collections). States (NY, CO, others) have enacted FCRA-equivalent laws — the *Cornerstone* court reasoned that FCRA preempts those state laws as well, though state-law litigation continues.

### 2. Data Broker Rule — "Protecting Americans from Harmful Data Broker Practices (Regulation V)"  
  
**Status.** **Proposed December 2024, withdrawn May 2025.**  
  
**Sources.**  
- NPRM: <https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2024/12/13/2024-28690/protecting-americans-from-harmful-data-broker-practices-regulation-v>  
- CFPB fact sheet: <https://files.consumerfinance.gov/f/documents/cfpb_fcra-nprm-fact-sheet_2024-12.pdf>  
- Comment-period extension: <https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2025/03/05/2025-03547/protecting-americans-from-harmful-data-broker-practices-regulation-v-extension-of-comment-period>  
- Withdrawal: <https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2025/05/15/2025-08644/protecting-americans-from-harmful-data-broker-practices-regulation-v-withdrawal-of-proposed-rule>  
- Rule landing page: <https://www.consumerfinance.gov/rules-policy/rules-under-development/protecting-americans-from-harmful-data-broker-practices-regulation-v/>  
  
**What it proposed.** Would have amended Reg V to:  
- Treat data brokers that sell any of four enumerated categories (credit history, credit score, debt payments, or income/financial tier) as **consumer reporting agencies** by definition.  
- Treat the resulting products as **consumer reports**, triggering permissible-purpose, accuracy, and dispute obligations.  
- Codify the **consumer-specific** scope of permissible purposes from the 2022 advisory opinion.  
- Restrict use of "**credit header data**" (name, address, SSN, DOB derived from credit files) outside of FCRA-permissible purposes.  
  
**Status update.** The CFPB **withdrew the NPRM on May 15, 2025**, citing "updates to Bureau policies." No final rule has issued. The 2022 advisory opinion on permissible purposes remains the operative CFPB position on data brokers and FCRA scope.

### 3. Other FCRA-adjacent rulemaking

- **Identity Theft Red Flags clarifications** — proposed amendments to Subpart J occasionally circulated; no major final rule recently.

## Part VI — Bot quick-reference: Who issues what  
  
| Document type | Issuer | Weight in litigation |  
|---|---|---|  
| Regulation V (12 C.F.R. Part 1022) | CFPB | Binding rule; *Chevron*-style deference where applicable |  
| Advisory Opinion (CFPB) | CFPB | Interpretive rule; Federal Register publication; persuasive |  
| Supervisory Highlights | CFPB | Non-binding; signals enforcement priorities |  
| Consent Order | CFPB / FTC | Binding only on the named respondent; cited as precedent on facts |  
| FTC 2011 "40 Years" Staff Report | FTC staff | Non-binding interpretive guide; widely cited |  
| Joint amicus brief (FTC/CFPB) | Both | Persuasive; signals agencies' legal positions |  
| State AG enforcement | State AGs | Authorized by FCRA § 621(c) for many provisions |
