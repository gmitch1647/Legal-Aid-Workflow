# Federal Rules of Civil Procedure — Rule 12 (Motions to Dismiss)

## Rule 12(b) — Defenses and Objections

Every defense to a claim must be asserted in the responsive pleading, except these may be raised by motion:

1. **(b)(1)** — Lack of subject-matter jurisdiction
2. **(b)(2)** — Lack of personal jurisdiction
3. **(b)(3)** — Improper venue
4. **(b)(4)** — Insufficient process
5. **(b)(5)** — Insufficient service of process
6. **(b)(6)** — Failure to state a claim upon which relief can be granted
7. **(b)(7)** — Failure to join a party under Rule 19

---

## Rule 12(b)(1) — Subject Matter Jurisdiction

**Post-Ramirez standing challenges:** Defendants now routinely file 12(b)(1) motions arguing lack of Article III standing (no concrete harm). In FCRA cases:

**How to defeat:**
- Allege dissemination (report provided to third party) — this creates defamation-analogous harm
- Identify specific inquiries during the inaccuracy period
- Plead specific credit denials or adverse terms
- For reinvestigation claims: the failure to investigate + continued reporting + subsequent dissemination = concrete harm

**Facial vs. Factual attacks:**
- Facial: Court accepts complaint allegations as true (like 12(b)(6))
- Factual: Court may consider evidence outside the pleadings (affidavits, declarations)

---

## Rule 12(b)(6) — Failure to State a Claim

### The Standard: Twombly/Iqbal

The complaint must contain sufficient factual matter, accepted as true, to state a claim that is plausible on its face. Court draws all reasonable inferences in plaintiff's favor.

**Two-step analysis:**
1. Identify conclusory allegations (legal conclusions not entitled to presumption of truth)
2. Determine whether remaining factual allegations plausibly give rise to entitlement to relief

### Common Defense 12(b)(6) Arguments in FCRA Cases and Responses

**"Report was accurate":**
- Response: Accuracy is a factual question rarely resolved on a motion to dismiss. Complaint alleges specific inaccuracy — court must accept as true at this stage.
- Cite: Chuluunbat v. Experian, 4 F.4th 562, 567 (7th Cir. 2021)

**"CRA had reasonable procedures":**
- Response: Reasonableness of procedures is inherently a factual/jury question. Cannot be resolved on the pleadings.
- Cite: Cushman v. Trans Union, 115 F.3d 220 (3d Cir. 1997); Losch v. Nationstar, 995 F.3d 937 (11th Cir. 2021)

**"No private right of action under § 1681s-2(a)":**
- Response: If properly pled under § 1681s-2(b) (triggered by CRA dispute), this argument fails. Ensure complaint clearly alleges (1) dispute to CRA, (2) CRA forwarded to furnisher, (3) furnisher failed to investigate.

**"Plaintiff lacks standing (Ramirez)":**
- Response: Allege specific dissemination — credit report pulled by [named third party] on [date] containing the inaccuracy. That's concrete harm per Ramirez.

**"Legal dispute immunity":**
- Response: After Chaitoff v. Experian (7th Cir. 2023), the existence of an agreement/modification is FACTUAL, not legal. Only the legal EFFECT of undisputed facts could potentially be beyond CRA competency.

### NDGA Briefing Rules for 12(b)(6)

- L.R. 7.1: Response due 14 days after motion; reply 7 days after response
- Page limits: 25 pages for briefs (unless leave granted)
- Must include proposed order
- Certificate of conference NOT required for 12(b)(6) (only required for discovery motions under L.R. 37.1)

---

## Rule 12(c) — Judgment on the Pleadings

After pleadings are closed but early enough not to delay trial, a party may move for judgment on the pleadings. Same standard as 12(b)(6) but can consider the answer.

---

## Rule 12(e) — More Definite Statement

If a pleading is so vague or ambiguous that the party cannot reasonably prepare a response. Rarely granted in FCRA cases if complaint identifies the inaccuracy, defendant, and statutory section.

---

## Rule 12(f) — Motion to Strike

Court may strike from a pleading any insufficient defense or any redundant, immaterial, impertinent, or scandalous matter.

**Offensive use:** Move to strike affirmative defenses that are legally insufficient (e.g., "plaintiff failed to mitigate" — FCRA has no duty to mitigate credit report errors).

---

## Timing Under Rule 12

- 12(b) motion: Must be filed BEFORE responsive pleading (answer)
- Answer deadline: 21 days after service (60 days if waiver of service)
- Filing a 12(b) motion EXTENDS the answer deadline until 14 days after court rules
- Waiver: 12(b)(2)-(5) defenses are waived if not raised in first response. 12(b)(6) and (7) may be raised later.
- 12(b)(1) (subject matter jurisdiction) can NEVER be waived — can be raised at any time

---

## Strategic Considerations for FCRA Plaintiffs

1. **Anticipate standing challenges** — plead dissemination explicitly in the complaint
2. **Attach exhibits** — dispute letters, CRA responses, credit reports become part of the pleading under Rule 10(c) and survive 12(b)(6)
3. **Plead specific facts, not conclusions** — "Defendant's report stated [X] when the true fact was [Y]" beats "Defendant reported inaccurate information"
4. **Separate § 1681n from § 1681o** — plead willful and negligent in the alternative so if willfulness fails on MTD, negligence claim survives
5. **Don't over-plead** — keep it focused. A bloated complaint invites a 12(f) motion to strike
