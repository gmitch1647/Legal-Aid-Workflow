# Federal Rules of Civil Procedure — Discovery (Rules 26-30)

## Rule 26 — Duty to Disclose; General Provisions

### Rule 26(a)(1) — Initial Disclosures (Required Without Request)

Within 14 days after the Rule 26(f) conference, each party must disclose:
- **(A)** Names/addresses/phone numbers of individuals likely to have discoverable information the disclosing party may use to support its claims or defenses
- **(B)** Copies or descriptions of all documents, ESI, and tangible things the disclosing party may use to support its claims or defenses
- **(C)** Computation of damages claimed (with supporting documents)
- **(D)** Insurance agreements under which an insurer may be liable for all or part of any judgment

**FCRA practice tip:** In initial disclosures for a CRA case, disclose:
- The consumer (plaintiff) as a witness
- All dispute letters sent
- Credit reports showing the inaccuracy
- Adverse action notices from denied applications
- Records of credit denials with dates and amounts

### Rule 26(b)(1) — Scope of Discovery

Parties may obtain discovery regarding any nonprivileged matter that is relevant to any party's claim or defense AND proportional to the needs of the case.

**Proportionality factors:**
1. Importance of the issues at stake
2. Amount in controversy
3. Parties' relative access to relevant information
4. Parties' resources
5. Importance of discovery in resolving the issues
6. Whether burden/expense outweighs likely benefit

**In FCRA cases:** CRAs often argue proportionality to resist producing system-wide data (all disputes for the furnisher, AHT data, training records). Counter: the amount in controversy includes statutory + punitive damages (potentially six figures), and the information is solely in defendant's possession.

### Rule 26(b)(3) — Trial Preparation Materials (Work Product)

Documents prepared in anticipation of litigation are protected UNLESS the requesting party shows substantial need and cannot obtain the substantial equivalent without undue hardship.

**FCRA practice:** CRAs sometimes claim their litigation hold letters and post-dispute internal reviews are "work product." Counter: if the document was created as part of the reinvestigation process (a statutory duty), it is NOT litigation work product — it existed because the statute required it, not because litigation was anticipated.

### Rule 26(b)(5) — Privilege Claims

When withholding information on privilege grounds, the party must:
- Expressly make the claim
- Describe the nature of the documents/communications withheld in a manner that enables assessment of the claim (privilege log)

### Rule 26(c) — Protective Orders

Court may issue a protective order to protect a party from annoyance, embarrassment, oppression, or undue burden/expense.

**CRA protective orders:** CRAs routinely seek protective orders over their reinvestigation procedures, matching algorithms, and e-OSCAR data. Argue: (1) this information goes to the core § 1681e(b) claim, (2) a confidentiality order (not sealing) adequately protects trade secrets, (3) consumer's right to know how their dispute was handled outweighs competitive concerns.

### Rule 26(e) — Supplementation

A party who has made a disclosure or responded to discovery must supplement or correct its response in a timely manner if it learns the disclosure was incomplete or incorrect.

### Rule 26(f) — Conference of the Parties

Before scheduling conference, parties must confer to discuss:
- Claims, defenses, and possibility of settlement
- Initial disclosures
- Discovery plan (subjects, timing, ESI issues, privilege)
- Preservation obligations

## Rule 30 — Depositions by Oral Examination

### Key Limits:
- **10 depositions per side** without leave of court (Rule 30(a)(2)(A)(i))
- **7 hours per deposition** (1 day of 7 hours) unless court orders or parties stipulate otherwise
- **30 days notice** required (though parties often stipulate to shorter notice)

### Rule 30(b)(6) — Corporate Designee Depositions

A party may name a corporation/organization and describe with reasonable particularity the matters for examination. The organization must designate one or more persons to testify on its behalf about those topics.

**Critical for FCRA cases:** This is how you depose CRAs and furnishers. You don't need to know the individual handler's name — you designate topics and the company produces a witness.

**Essential 30(b)(6) topics for CRAs:**
1. Reinvestigation procedures for plaintiff's specific dispute
2. Identity and qualifications of all persons who handled the dispute
3. Documents reviewed during reinvestigation
4. Communications with furnisher regarding plaintiff's dispute
5. Policies for determining when to go beyond ACDV verification
6. Average handle time targets and metrics
7. Training provided to reinvestigation personnel
8. Quality assurance procedures for reinvestigation
9. Compliance with § 1681i(a)(4) (considering consumer-submitted documents)
10. Procedures for reinsertion under § 1681i(a)(5)(B) (if applicable)

**Essential 30(b)(6) topics for furnishers:**
1. Investigation procedures upon receipt of ACDV
2. Account-level documentation reviewed
3. Identity of persons who conducted investigation
4. Response codes transmitted and basis for each
5. Policies for § 1681s-2(b) compliance
6. Metro 2 reporting procedures for plaintiff's account
7. Chain of title (for debt buyers)

**Preparation:** The designee must be prepared on ALL noticed topics — not just what they personally know. An unprepared designee = potential sanctions + adverse inference.

## NDGA Local Rules on Discovery

### L.R. 26.1 — Initial Disclosures
- Must be exchanged within 14 days of Rule 26(f) conference
- NDGA follows the federal default timeline

### L.R. 37.1 — Discovery Disputes (MANDATORY CONFERENCE)
- Before filing ANY discovery motion, parties MUST confer in good faith
- The motion must include a certificate of conference stating the date and manner of conferral
- Motions without certificate will be DENIED without consideration
- This is strictly enforced in NDGA

**Practice tip:** Always send a meet-and-confer letter BEFORE filing a motion to compel. Document the effort. If the opposing party refuses to confer, state that in your certificate.
