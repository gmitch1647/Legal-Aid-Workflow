# Federal Rules of Civil Procedure — Pleading (Rules 8, 9, 10)

## Rule 8 — General Rules of Pleading

### Rule 8(a) — Claim for Relief

A pleading that states a claim for relief must contain:
1. A short and plain statement of the grounds for the court's jurisdiction
2. A short and plain statement of the claim showing that the pleader is entitled to relief
3. A demand for the relief sought (may include alternative or different types of relief)

**Application to FCRA cases:**
- Jurisdiction: 15 U.S.C. § 1681p (exclusive federal jurisdiction) + 28 U.S.C. § 1331 (federal question)
- Claim: Identify the statutory section violated, the defendant's conduct, and how it harmed the plaintiff
- Relief: Actual damages, statutory damages ($100-$1,000 per § 1681n), punitive damages, attorney fees, costs

**Twombly/Iqbal standard:** The complaint must contain sufficient factual matter, accepted as true, to state a claim to relief that is plausible on its face. Bell Atlantic Corp. v. Twombly, 550 U.S. 544 (2007); Ashcroft v. Iqbal, 556 U.S. 662 (2009).

For FCRA § 1681e(b) claims, plead:
- The specific inaccuracy in the credit report
- That the report was furnished to a third party (concrete harm per Ramirez)
- That the CRA's procedures were not reasonable

For FCRA § 1681i(a) claims, plead:
- That the consumer disputed the information
- How the dispute was communicated (letter, online, phone)
- That the CRA failed to conduct a reasonable reinvestigation
- What steps the CRA failed to take

### Rule 8(b) — Defenses; Admissions and Denials

A party must state in short and plain terms its defenses to each claim and must admit or deny the allegations. A denial must fairly respond to the substance of the allegation.

**Practice tip:** Watch for general denials in FCRA cases. Under Rule 8(b)(3), a party that intends in good faith to deny all allegations must do so generally. But a general denial that denies obviously true facts (like "Defendant is a consumer reporting agency") can be sanctionable.

### Rule 8(c) — Affirmative Defenses

Must be raised in the answer or they are waived:
- Statute of limitations (1 year FDCPA, 2 year FCRA)
- Bona fide error (FDCPA § 1692k(c))
- Failure to mitigate damages
- Res judicata / collateral estoppel

### Rule 8(d) — Pleading to Be Concise and Direct

- Each allegation must be simple, concise, and direct
- Alternative or hypothetical statements permitted
- A party may state as many separate claims as it has, regardless of consistency

## Rule 9 — Pleading Special Matters

### Rule 9(b) — Fraud or Mistake; Conditions of Mind

In alleging fraud or mistake, a party must state with particularity the circumstances constituting fraud or mistake. Malice, intent, knowledge, and other conditions of a person's mind may be alleged generally.

**Critical for FCRA practice:** FCRA claims do NOT require Rule 9(b) heightened pleading. The 11th Circuit has held that FCRA negligence claims under § 1681o and willfulness claims under § 1681n are not subject to Rule 9(b) because they are not fraud claims. Willfulness (reckless disregard) may be alleged generally under Rule 9(b)'s second sentence.

**However:** If you add Georgia FBPA claims (which sound in fraud/deception), Rule 9(b) MAY apply to those claims. This is one reason to be cautious about adding state-law fraud-based claims.

### Rule 9(a) — Capacity or Authority to Sue

Except when required to show jurisdiction, a pleading need not allege a party's capacity to sue or be sued, authority to sue, or legal existence of an entity.

## Rule 10 — Form of Pleadings

### Rule 10(a) — Caption; Names of Parties

Every pleading must have a caption with:
- The court's name
- A title (names of all parties in the complaint; first named party only in subsequent filings)
- A file number
- A Rule 7(a) designation (Complaint, Answer, etc.)

### Rule 10(b) — Paragraphs; Separate Statements

- A party must state its claims in numbered paragraphs, each limited to a single set of circumstances
- Each claim founded on a separate transaction should be stated in a separate count

### Rule 10(c) — Adoption by Reference; Exhibits

- A statement in a pleading may be adopted by reference elsewhere in the same pleading or in any other pleading or motion
- A copy of a written instrument that is an exhibit to a pleading is a part of the pleading for all purposes

**Practice tip for FCRA:** Always attach key exhibits (dispute letters, CRA responses, credit report excerpts) under Rule 10(c). They become part of the pleading and survive a 12(b)(6) motion — the court must consider them.

## FCRA-Specific Pleading Checklist

For every FCRA complaint, ensure you have pled:

1. Federal question jurisdiction (§ 1681p + § 1331)
2. Venue (28 U.S.C. § 1391(b) — defendant resides or events occurred in district)
3. Each defendant's identity as a CRA or furnisher (with statutory definition)
4. The specific inaccuracy or procedural failure
5. Dissemination to a third party (required for standing post-Ramirez)
6. Consumer's dispute (for § 1681i and § 1681s-2(b) claims)
7. CRA/furnisher's response (or lack thereof)
8. Specific damages (not just conclusory "emotional distress")
9. Willfulness allegations with factual hooks (for § 1681n claims)
10. Each statutory section violated as a separate count
