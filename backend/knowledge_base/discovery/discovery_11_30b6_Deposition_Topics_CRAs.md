## I. Rule 30(b)(6) Framework and the 2020 Amendment  
  
Under Fed. R. Civ. P. 30(b)(6), a party may name a corporation as a deponent and "must describe with reasonable particularity the matters for examination." The corporation must then designate one or more officers, directors, managing agents, or other persons "who consent to testify on its behalf" and may "set out the matters on which each person designated will testify." The persons designated "must testify about information known or reasonably available to the organization."  
  
**The Dec. 1, 2020 amendment** added a meet-and-confer obligation: "Before or promptly after the notice or subpoena is served, the serving party and the organization must confer in good faith about the matters for examination." Counsel should:  
  
1. Send a draft notice in advance of the formal notice;  
2. Hold a real conference (not a perfunctory email exchange) addressing scope, the universe of relevant time periods, definitions, and which topics the CRA intends to designate which witness(es) for;  
3. Memorialize the conference and any agreements/disagreements in a follow-up letter; and  
4. Be prepared to demonstrate good-faith conferral if a motion to compel or for protective order follows.  
  
The Committee Note to the 2020 amendment emphasizes that the meet-and-confer is "iterative" — it can continue after the notice issues. It does not require agreement; it requires a good-faith attempt. Failure of the CRA to engage in good-faith conferral is itself a basis for sanctions and for refusing protective relief.  
  
**Rule 26(b)(1) proportionality** governs scope. Discovery must be "relevant to any party's claim or defense and proportional to the needs of the case." Proportionality factors include the importance of the issues, amount in controversy, the parties' relative access to information, the parties' resources, the importance of the discovery in resolving the issues, and whether the burden outweighs the likely benefit. CRAs control the disputed information almost entirely; the relative-access factor consistently weighs in the consumer's favor.

## II. Topic Bank  
  
### A. The Consumer's Specific File  
  
**Topic A.1 — Composition and history of plaintiff's file.**  
> The complete composition, history, and content of any consumer file, credit file, header file, or other record maintained by [CRA] keyed to plaintiff's name, Social Security number, date of birth, or any address associated with plaintiff during the relevant period, including each trade line, public record, inquiry, collection, header datum, and identifier; the source of each datum; and each change to the file during the relevant period.  
  
*Discoverable.* File composition and provenance are core § 1681e(b) facts. *Cushman v. Trans Union Corp.*, 115 F.3d 220 (3d Cir. 1997) (procedures must be evaluated against information CRA had); *Sarver v. Experian Info. Solutions*, 390 F.3d 969 (7th Cir. 2004) (court evaluates whether CRA's procedures were reasonable in light of what was reported).  
  
*Likely designee.* A "consumer file" or "file research" analyst; in specialty CRAs (First Advantage, LexisNexis), a product-line operations manager.  
  
*Foundation/admissions.* Authenticate the disclosure file; confirm completeness; lock in field definitions and date stamps; identify which fields are displayed to which subscriber categories.  
  
*Typical objection / response.* "Overbreadth" — defeated by the topic's tie to plaintiff. "Trade secret" — irrelevant to plaintiff's own file. Use a protective order to neutralize confidentiality objections (Rule 26(c)).  
  
**Topic A.2 — All disputes from plaintiff and processing.**  
> Each dispute, reinvestigation request, or communication received by [CRA] from or on behalf of plaintiff during the relevant period; the date received; the medium; the ACDV(s) or AUD(s) generated; the furnisher(s) contacted; the response received; the time spent by any [CRA] employee or contractor on the dispute; the result; and the notice sent to plaintiff under § 1681i(a)(6).  
  
*Discoverable.* § 1681i(a)(1)–(7) reinvestigation duties are at the heart of most CRA cases. *Hinkle v. Midland Credit Mgmt., Inc.*, 827 F.3d 1295 (11th Cir. 2016) (although addressing furnisher, articulates "reasonable investigation" standard equally relevant to CRA reinvestigation).  
  
*Likely designee.* Consumer Relations / Dispute Operations manager.  
  
*Foundation/admissions.* Authenticate the ACDV; lock in the codes used; confirm time spent and tools used; admit no human eyes reviewed plaintiff's documentation, if true.  
  
**Topic A.3 — All consumer reports furnished about plaintiff.**  
> Each consumer report or other communication of information about plaintiff that [CRA] furnished to any user, subscriber, or reseller during the relevant period; the user/recipient; the date; the permissible purpose certified or otherwise relied upon; the product or report type; and the content furnished.  
  
*Discoverable.* Permissible-purpose evidence under § 1681b; foundation for § 1681e(b) damages (downstream adverse actions).  
  
**Topic A.4 — Communications with plaintiff.**  
> All communications between [CRA] and plaintiff during the relevant period, including disclosures under § 1681g, dispute correspondence, and notices under § 1681i(a)(6).  
  
*Foundation/admissions.* Establish what plaintiff was told and what § 1681g and § 1681i(a)(6) disclosures were issued.

### B. The Matching Algorithm (the Central Battleground)  
  
**Topic B.1 — Matching logic.**  
> The matching logic, algorithms, rules, and configurations used by [CRA] during the relevant period to associate incoming trade-line, public-record, inquiry, or header data with existing consumer files, including: required identifiers; tolerated mismatches; weighting; tiebreak rules; the role of name, address, date of birth, and full or partial SSN; and any changes during the relevant period.  
  
*Discoverable.* The matching algorithm is the procedure under § 1681e(b). Courts treat it as discoverable subject to protective order. The FTC's *40 Years of Experience With the Fair Credit Reporting Act* (2011) and FTC *Report to Congress Under Section 319* studies are public benchmarks against which to compare the CRA's logic.  
  
*Likely designee.* Director of data integrity / matching logic; vendor data engineer (often unprepared — strong sanctions hook under Rule 30(b)(6)).  
  
*Foundation/admissions.* Whether name-only matching is permitted in any product; whether a partial SSN (last 4) alone can override name and address mismatches; whether two records with the same name but different DOB and SSN can merge.  
  
*Typical objection / response.* "Trade secret." Authority: *Williams v. First Advantage LNS Screening Solutions Inc.*, 947 F.3d 735 (11th Cir. 2020) (matching logic squarely relevant to willfulness and reasonable procedures); courts compel under protective order. *In re Equifax Inc. Customer Data Sec. Breach Litig.*, 362 F. Supp. 3d 1295 (N.D. Ga. 2019) (rejecting trade-secret blanket withholding).  
  
**Topic B.2 — Partial-matching thresholds.**  
> The thresholds, scores, or rules by which [CRA] accepts partial matches on name, SSN, date of birth, and address, including each threshold value and how each was set, tested, or validated.  
  
**Topic B.3 — Algorithm changes.**  
> Each change, version, parameter update, or configuration release to [CRA]'s matching logic during the relevant period; the reason for the change; testing performed before deployment; and rollback events.  
  
**Topic B.4 — Internal testing of matching error rates.**  
> All internal testing, validation, sampling, audits, or studies measuring the rate of mixed files, false-positive matches, or false-negative matches by [CRA]'s matching logic during the relevant period.  
  
*Discoverable.* Critical to willfulness under *Safeco Ins. Co. of Am. v. Burr*, 551 U.S. 47 (2007) — known error rates that were not remediated bear on objectively reckless disregard.  
  
**Topic B.5 — Name-only matching and FTC critique.**  
> [CRA]'s awareness of, response to, and any changes made in response to the FTC's *40 Years of Experience With the Fair Credit Reporting Act* (July 2011) and the FTC's Section 319 studies, particularly with respect to name-only and partial-identifier matching.  
  
**Topic B.6 — Mixed-file detection and remediation.**  
> [CRA]'s procedures, definitions, metrics, and remediation steps for "mixed files," "merged files," or "commingled files," including triggers, queues, manual review, and prevention controls.  
  
*Authority.* *Bryant v. TRW, Inc.*, 689 F.2d 72 (6th Cir. 1982) (mixed file actionable); FTC *40 Years* (mixed files identified as a top FCRA harm).

### C. Reinvestigation Procedures — § 1681i  
  
**Topic C.1 — e-OSCAR and ACDV.**  
> [CRA]'s use of the e-OSCAR system and the Automated Consumer Dispute Verification (ACDV) form during the relevant period, including dispute-code selection, free-text fields, attachments, time limits, and routing.  
  
*Likely designee.* Dispute Operations / e-OSCAR operations manager.  
  
*Authority.* *Cortez v. Trans Union, LLC*, 617 F.3d 688 (3d Cir. 2010) (CRA may not parrot furnisher response; must conduct meaningful reinvestigation).  
  
**Topic C.2 — Coding and routing.**  
> How [CRA] translates each consumer dispute into one or more dispute codes, and how the choice of dispute code drives routing, automated handling, and the content of the ACDV transmitted to the furnisher.  
  
**Topic C.3 — Time per dispute; automation vs. human.**  
> The average and median time spent by [CRA] personnel and contractors on consumer disputes during the relevant period; the proportion of disputes resolved without human review; the role of off-shore vendors; and the training and qualifications of dispute personnel.  
  
*Authority.* *Cushman*, 115 F.3d at 225 (reasonableness measured by what additional steps could have been taken).  
  
**Topic C.4 — Frivolous determinations under § 1681i(a)(3).**  
> [CRA]'s procedures, criteria, and decisions to terminate reinvestigations as frivolous or irrelevant under 15 U.S.C. § 1681i(a)(3), and the notice provided to the consumer under § 1681i(a)(3)(B).  
  
**Topic C.5 — Reinsertion under § 1681i(a)(5)(B).**  
> [CRA]'s procedures for reinsertion of previously deleted information under § 1681i(a)(5)(B), including certification from the furnisher, notice to the consumer within five business days, and the statement of the reinsertion procedures.  
  
**Topic C.6 — Forwarding documentation under § 1681i(a)(2).**  
> [CRA]'s procedures for forwarding to the furnisher "all relevant information regarding the dispute that the [CRA] has received from the consumer," including documentation, narratives, and attachments, within five business days of receipt.  
  
*Authority.* § 1681i(a)(2)(A); CFPB *Supervisory Highlights* (Summer 2017, Winter 2019, Summer 2021) repeatedly cited failure to forward consumer documentation.  
  
---  
  
### D. Accuracy Procedures — § 1681e(b)  
  
**Topic D.1 — Source vetting (subscriber/furnisher onboarding).**  
> [CRA]'s procedures during the relevant period for vetting and onboarding new data furnishers and subscribers, including investigation of identity, business legitimacy, data quality, and certification under § 1681e(a) and § 1681b(f).  
  
**Topic D.2 — Quality control sampling and audits.**  
> Quality control sampling, audits, or studies measuring the accuracy of [CRA]'s consumer reports during the relevant period, including methodology, results, and remediation.  
  
*Authority.* FTC Section 319 reports (2008, 2012, 2015) provide a public benchmark.  
  
**Topic D.3 — Public-record sourcing.**  
> The sourcing of public-record data (judgments, liens, bankruptcies, criminal records) reflected in [CRA]'s consumer files during the relevant period, including vendors, frequency of refresh, identifier-matching standards, and the *Henson v. CSC Credit Servs.*, 29 F.3d 280 (7th Cir. 1994), framework's role, if any, in [CRA]'s procedures.  
  
**Topic D.4 — Mixed-file remediation.**  
> [CRA]'s procedures for detecting, separating, and remediating mixed files, including any "fragmented file" team, manual review queue, or automated unmerge logic.  
  
**Topic D.5 — Pattern of similar errors (Safeco willfulness).**  
> Internal complaint, dispute, and litigation statistics regarding errors of the same type alleged by plaintiff during the relevant period; root-cause analyses; and any decisions made about remediation.  
  
*Authority.* *Safeco*, 551 U.S. at 69 (recklessness = objectively unreasonable interpretation, "not only a violation under a reasonable reading of the statute, but shows that the company ran a risk of violating the law substantially greater than the risk associated with a reading that was merely careless"); pattern-of-similar-error evidence is the principal vehicle.

### E. Furnisher Relationship Management  
  
**Topic E.1 — Subscriber/furnisher agreements and certifications.**  
> The form and content of [CRA]'s subscriber/furnisher agreements during the relevant period; the certifications required under § 1681e(a) and § 1681b(f); and the negotiation, training, and enforcement of those certifications.  
  
**Topic E.2 — Data integrity standards.**  
> The data integrity standards [CRA] imposes on furnishers (Metro 2 compliance, status code conventions, header data accuracy, dispute response standards) and how compliance is measured.  
  
**Topic E.3 — Furnisher audits and termination.**  
> [CRA]'s audits of furnishers' data quality and dispute handling during the relevant period; furnisher remediation; and the history of furnishers terminated for accuracy or dispute-handling failures.  
  
*Authority.* § 1681e(a) (procedures to "limit the furnishing of consumer reports"); § 1681e(b); CFPB *Supervisory Highlights* (CRA furnisher-management findings).  
  
**Topic E.4 — Reseller relationships.**  
> [CRA]'s relationships with resellers under § 1681e(e), including reseller agreements, certifications, and dispute handling under § 1681i(f).  
  
---  
  
### F. Permissible Purpose — § 1681b  
  
**Topic F.1 — User certification process.**  
> [CRA]'s procedures for obtaining and documenting user certifications of permissible purpose under § 1681b(a) and § 1681b(f).  
  
**Topic F.2 — Verification of permissible-purpose claims.**  
> [CRA]'s procedures for verifying the accuracy of user permissible-purpose claims at onboarding and on a continuing basis, including audits and sampling.  
  
*Authority.* *Trans Union LLC v. FTC*, 295 F.3d 42 (D.C. Cir. 2002); *Pintos v. Pacific Creditors Ass'n*, 605 F.3d 665 (9th Cir. 2010) (permissible purpose requires actual permissible use, not just certification).  
  
**Topic F.3 — Employment-purpose certification under § 1681b(b).**  
> [CRA]'s procedures for obtaining user certification under § 1681b(b)(1) that the consumer has been provided written disclosure and has authorized the procurement of the report, and that the user will comply with adverse-action requirements under § 1681b(b)(3).

### G. Identity Theft — §§ 1681c-1, 1681c-2  
  
**Topic G.1 — Fraud alerts and security freezes.**  
> [CRA]'s procedures for receiving, placing, communicating, and removing fraud alerts under § 1681c-1 and security freezes under § 1681c-1(i) during the relevant period.  
  
**Topic G.2 — Block procedures — § 1681c-2.**  
> [CRA]'s procedures for blocking the reporting of information that a consumer identifies as resulting from identity theft, including the four required elements under § 1681c-2(a), the four-business-day timing, and notice to the furnisher.  
  
**Topic G.3 — Rescission — § 1681c-2(c).**  
> [CRA]'s procedures for declining or rescinding blocks under § 1681c-2(c), including the determination that the block was based on material misrepresentation or that information was not a result of identity theft.

### H. Compliance and Regulatory  
  
**Topic H.1 — CFPB and FTC examination findings.**  
> Each non-privileged finding, matter requiring attention, supervisory letter, or examination report from the CFPB, FTC, or any state regulator addressing [CRA]'s FCRA compliance during the relevant period, and [CRA]'s response.  
  
*Authority.* The deliberative-process and bank-examination privileges are CFPB's, not the CRA's, to assert; many examination materials are obtainable subject to a *Touhy*-style process. CFPB *Supervisory Highlights* are public.  
  
**Topic H.2 — Consent orders and remediation.**  
> Each public consent order, stipulated judgment, or settlement between [CRA] and any regulator during the relevant period, and the steps taken in remediation.  
  
*Authority.* *In re Equifax* consent orders (CFPB, 2017, 2018); FTC Equifax consent order (2019); *In re TransUnion Rental Screening Solutions* (CFPB 2023, public); FTC *Real Page* consent (public).  
  
**Topic H.3 — Internal compliance audits.**  
> Internal audits, second- and third-line-of-defense reviews, and self-assessments of [CRA]'s FCRA compliance during the relevant period.  
  
**Topic H.4 — Senior management and board oversight.**  
> The structure, reporting lines, and content of senior management and board reporting on FCRA compliance, dispute volumes, accuracy metrics, and litigation during the relevant period.  
  
**Topic H.5 — Document retention and litigation holds.**  
> [CRA]'s document retention policies and litigation hold practices applicable to plaintiff's file, plaintiff's disputes, and the categories of documents responsive to plaintiff's discovery.

### I. Willfulness-Relevant Topics (Safeco)  
  
**Topic I.1 — Knowledge of contrary FCRA interpretations.**  
> [CRA]'s knowledge during the relevant period of (a) federal court decisions, (b) CFPB or FTC guidance, including *Supervisory Highlights* and the FTC *40 Years* report, and (c) industry commentary that interpreted the FCRA contrary to [CRA]'s procedures.  
  
**Topic I.2 — Decisions to maintain challenged procedures.**  
> Decisions by [CRA] during the relevant period to maintain, despite known error rates, dispute complaint volumes, or contrary guidance, the matching algorithm, reinvestigation procedures, or other procedures challenged in this litigation.  
  
**Topic I.3 — Cost-benefit analyses.**  
> Any cost-benefit, return-on-investment, or business-case analyses prepared during the relevant period concerning changes to (a) the matching algorithm, (b) dispute-handling automation, (c) manual review of consumer disputes, or (d) source-data quality controls.  
  
*Authority.* *Safeco*, 551 U.S. 47; *Cortez*, 617 F.3d at 721–22 (knowledge of contrary interpretation supports willfulness).

## III. Designee Preparation Issues  
  
Rule 30(b)(6) imposes an *affirmative* duty on the organization to designate a witness who can testify to information "known or reasonably available to the organization." This includes information held by current and former employees, contractors, and third-party vendors. Counsel preparing for the deposition should:  
  
1. **Lock in preparation on the record.** At the outset, ask the designee what topics they were designated for; what they did to prepare; what documents they reviewed; with whom they spoke; and what they were unable to learn.  
2. **Treat the binder as a document.** The materials reviewed in preparation are typically discoverable under Fed. R. Evid. 612 once used to refresh recollection.  
3. **Build the inadequate-preparation record.** If the designee cannot answer noticed topics, ask narrowly and repeatedly; note each "I don't know"; identify the people or documents that would know; and confirm that the witness was designated for that topic.  
4. **Reserve and move.** Reserve the right to re-depose on inadequately-covered topics. Common remedies for inadequate preparation include re-deposition at the CRA's expense, evidentiary sanctions (preclusion of contrary evidence at trial), adverse inferences, and in egregious cases default. *See* *Black Horse Lane Assoc., L.P. v. Dow Chem. Corp.*, 228 F.3d 275, 304 (3d Cir. 2000) (producing unprepared 30(b)(6) witness "is tantamount to a failure to appear"); *QBE Ins. Corp. v. Jorda Enters., Inc.*, 277 F.R.D. 676 (S.D. Fla. 2012) (collecting authorities).

## IV. Cross-Cutting Objection Authority  
  
| Objection | Response / Authority |  
|---|---|  
| Overbreadth | Tie each topic to plaintiff, plaintiff's account, or the procedures applied to plaintiff's file. Rule 26(b)(1) relevance is satisfied. |  
| Trade secret / proprietary | Protective order under Rule 26(c). *Williams v. First Advantage*, 947 F.3d 735 (matching logic discoverable). The CRA may not invoke trade secret to defeat discovery of the very procedures whose reasonableness is the issue. |  
| Proportionality | Rule 26(b)(1) factors favor consumer: CRAs have exclusive access; importance of accuracy is recognized in the FCRA itself; burden is largely incurred in any case for the CRA's own compliance program. |  
| Work product / privilege | A privilege log is required for each withheld document. Rule 26(b)(5). Underlying *facts* are not privileged. *Upjohn Co. v. United States*, 449 U.S. 383 (1981). |  
| Vagueness | Cured by the topic's specificity, definitions section of the notice, and the meet-and-confer record. |  
| Apex (senior executives) | Apex doctrine generally inapplicable to 30(b)(6) corporate designations because the *corporation* — not the individual — is the deponent. |
