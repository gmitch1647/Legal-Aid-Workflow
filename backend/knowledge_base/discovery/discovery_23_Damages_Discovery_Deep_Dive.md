# Discovery Module 23: Damages Discovery Deep Dive in FCRA Cases  
  
---  
  
**Topic:** Damages discovery in FCRA consumer cases - actual, statutory, punitive, attorney's fees  
**Primary FRCP Rules:** Fed. R. Civ. P. 26, 33, 34, 36, 45  
**Primary FCRA Provisions:** 15 U.S.C. Sec. 1681n (willful); 15 U.S.C. Sec. 1681o (negligent); 15 U.S.C. Sec. 1681n(b) (false pretenses); 15 U.S.C. Sec. 1681m (adverse-action notices)  
**Constitutional / Standing:** *TransUnion LLC v. Ramirez*, 594 U.S. 413 (2021); *Spokeo, Inc. v. Robins*, 578 U.S. 330 (2016)  
**Last Updated:** 2026-05-16  
  
---  
  
## A. The FCRA Damages Framework  
  
### Sec. 1681n - Willful Noncompliance  
  
For willful violations, a plaintiff may recover:  
  
- Actual damages OR statutory damages of $100 to $1,000, at the consumer's election; 15 U.S.C. Sec. 1681n(a)(1)(A);  
- Punitive damages "as the court may allow"; Sec. 1681n(a)(2);  
- Costs and reasonable attorney's fees; Sec. 1681n(a)(3).  
  
"Willful" includes reckless disregard of statutory duty. *Safeco Ins. Co. of Am. v. Burr*, 551 U.S. 47, 56-60 (2007).  
  
### Sec. 1681o - Negligent Noncompliance  
  
For negligent violations:  
  
- Actual damages only (no statutory minimum);  
- Costs and reasonable attorney's fees; Sec. 1681o(a).  
  
### Sec. 1681n(b) - Information Under False Pretenses  
  
A person who obtains a consumer report under false pretenses or knowingly without a permissible purpose is liable for actual damages or $1,000, whichever is greater. 15 U.S.C. Sec. 1681n(b).  
  
### Each Category Requires Distinct Discovery  
  
The damages theory dictates the discovery plan. A pure statutory-damages case looks very different from a credit-denial actual-damages case or a mortgage-rate-impact case.  
  
### Standing After *TransUnion v. Ramirez*  
  
*TransUnion* made concrete-harm standing analysis a damages question as much as a pleading question. 594 U.S. at 425-32. The Court held that internal information that was never disseminated to third parties did not cause concrete harm sufficient for Article III standing. Discovery on dissemination, on materialization of harm, and on "close historical or common-law analogue" is now central to most FCRA actual-damages cases.

## B. Emotional Distress Discovery  
  
### The Foundational Cases  
  
- *Sloane v. Equifax Info. Servs., LLC*, 510 F.3d 495 (4th Cir. 2007) - plaintiff's own testimony can support emotional distress recovery; corroboration strengthens but is not required.  
- *Riley v. Equifax Info. Servs., LLC*, 194 F. Supp. 3d 1330 (S.D. Fla. 2016) - emotional distress framework in the consumer-report context.  
- *Cousin v. Trans Union Corp.*, 246 F.3d 359 (5th Cir. 2001) - upholding emotional distress recovery in an FCRA case.  
- *Wantz v. Experian Info. Sols.*, 386 F.3d 829 (7th Cir. 2004) - emotional distress must be supported by sufficient evidence; bare assertion insufficient.  
- *Robinson v. Equifax Info. Servs., LLC*, 560 F.3d 235 (4th Cir. 2009) - corroborating evidence (family, friends, conduct) supports emotional distress findings.  
  
The collective framework: testimony alone may suffice but the strength of the recovery scales with corroboration, and the trial record must contain "competent evidence" of "genuine injury."  
  
### Sample Discovery Plaintiff Should Expect  
  
Defense counsel will typically propound:  
  
- Identity of all health care providers (mental health and primary care) for a defined lookback period;  
- Authorizations for medical/mental health records;  
- Identification of all medications including psychotropics;  
- Identification of all witnesses to whom plaintiff communicated distress;  
- Identification of life events that may have caused independent distress (divorce, death, job loss).  
  
The discovery scope cliff lies between "garden variety" emotional distress and heightened claims.  
  
### Garden-Variety vs. Heightened Distress  
  
**Garden-variety:** Common, generalized distress (anxiety, sleeplessness, embarrassment) without medical treatment or diagnosis. Most courts hold that garden-variety claims do not waive the patient-psychotherapist privilege. *Jaffee v. Redmond*, 518 U.S. 1 (1996) (recognizing federal psychotherapist-patient privilege); *Fitzgerald v. Cassil*, 216 F.R.D. 632, 637 (N.D. Cal. 2003); *Vanderbilt v. Town of Chilmark*, 174 F.R.D. 225, 229 (D. Mass. 1997).  
  
**Heightened claims** - those alleging specific diagnoses, treatment, or symptoms attributed to a particular condition - usually result in subject-matter waiver of the patient-psychotherapist privilege as to that condition. *Schoffstall v. Henderson*, 223 F.3d 818, 823 (8th Cir. 2000); *Sarko v. Penn-Del Directory Co.*, 170 F.R.D. 127, 130 (E.D. Pa. 1997).  
  
Plaintiffs typically plead garden-variety distress unless heightened claims are essential to damages valuation.  
  
### Defending Emotional-Distress Damages - Subject-Matter Waiver  
  
Where plaintiff puts emotional distress at issue and identifies treatment or diagnosis, defendant is entitled to relevant medical/mental health records. The waiver is narrow; courts often impose temporal and substantive limits. *EEOC v. Nichols Gas & Oil, Inc.*, 256 F.R.D. 114, 122 (W.D.N.Y. 2009).  
  
### Sample Discovery Requests on Emotional Distress  
  
> TEMPLATE - Plaintiff Interrogatory to Defendant (offensive):  
>  
> Identify by date and category every communication, report, or analysis you received or generated reflecting that consumers experienced emotional distress, anxiety, or comparable injury as a result of the conduct alleged in this case, including without limitation consumer complaints, CFPB complaint narratives, BBB complaints, internal customer-service notes, and social-media communications.  
  
> TEMPLATE - Defendant Interrogatory to Plaintiff (defensive):  
>  
> Describe in detail each symptom of emotional distress you attribute to Defendant's conduct, including the date of onset, duration, severity (on a 1-10 scale or equivalent), the conduct you contend caused the symptom, and any other event you contend contributed to the symptom.  
  
> TEMPLATE - Defendant RFP to Plaintiff:  
>  
> Produce all journals, diaries, social media posts, text messages, and emails between [date] and the present in which You discussed Your credit report, Your dispute with Defendant, the events alleged in the Complaint, Your mental or emotional state, or any symptom You attribute to Defendant.  
  
> TEMPLATE - Plaintiff RFP to Defendant:  
>  
> Produce all consumer complaints (CFPB, BBB, AG, internal, and social-media) received during the relevant period that mention emotional distress, anxiety, sleeplessness, embarrassment, humiliation, or comparable injury attributed to credit reporting inaccuracy.

## C. Credit Denial Discovery  
  
### Proving Causation  
  
The plaintiff must show that the inaccurate report caused a denial of credit (or worse terms). *Wright v. Experian Info. Sols., Inc.*, 805 F.3d 1232, 1239 (10th Cir. 2015); *Wantz*, 386 F.3d at 834.  
  
### Sample Plaintiff Interrogatories (to Plaintiff or supporting)  
  
> TEMPLATE - Plaintiff's Damages Interrogatory (often answered as part of initial disclosures):  
>  
> Identify every application for credit, insurance, employment, housing, or any other transaction during [date range] for which a consumer report was obtained, including:  
> (a) The date of application;  
> (b) The institution, lender, landlord, or employer;  
> (c) The outcome (approved, denied, withdrawn, approved on less-favorable terms);  
> (d) Any reason codes, risk-based pricing disclosures, or adverse-action notices received;  
> (e) The credit-reporting agency identified as the source of the report relied upon.  
  
### RFPs  
  
> TEMPLATE - RFP to Plaintiff (often produced affirmatively):  
>  
> All adverse-action notices, risk-based pricing notices, and similar communications received pursuant to 15 U.S.C. Sec. 1681m during [date range].  
  
### Subpoenas to Specific Lenders  
  
Once denials are identified, subpoenas under Rule 45 to specific lenders are critical to establish *reliance* on the inaccurate report:  
  
> TEMPLATE - Lender Subpoena (Rule 45):  
>  
> Documents sufficient to identify:  
> (1) Every consumer report obtained on [Plaintiff Name, DOB, last four] between [dates];  
> (2) The CRA from which each report was obtained;  
> (3) The reason codes, scores, and tradeline data the lender's underwriting system relied upon;  
> (4) Any internal underwriting notes or memos referencing [the inaccurate tradeline or item];  
> (5) The lender's underwriting policy or credit-score cutoff applicable to the application(s);  
> (6) The decision and any rate, terms, or amount offered or denied.  
  
### Quantifying Credit Denial Damages  
  
Methods of proof include:  
  
- **Interest-rate differential:** the rate offered vs. the rate plaintiff would have qualified for absent the inaccuracy; computed over the loan term.  
- **Foreclosed credit lines:** undeniable concrete loss (e.g., HELOC denied at $50,000 line; alternative borrowing at higher cost).  
- **Mortgage denial costs:** appraisal fees, application fees, opportunity cost of delayed home purchase, rent paid during delay.

## D. Lost Opportunity Damages  
  
### Employment  
  
Section 1681b(b) governs employment use. Damages include lost job offers, lost promotions, lost wages, and (in some cases) emotional distress and reputation harm. *Robinson*, 560 F.3d at 240-41. Discovery should include:  
  
- The employer's hiring decision file;  
- Internal communications mentioning the report;  
- The job description and wage scale;  
- The plaintiff's mitigation efforts.  
  
### Housing  
  
Tenant screening reports under Sec. 1681a(d). Damages can include:  
  
- Application fees;  
- Increased rent at alternative housing;  
- Required additional security deposits;  
- Cost of co-signers;  
- Moving expenses.  
  
### Business  
  
Business credit denials may produce delay costs, alternative financing costs, and lost contracts. Speculative lost profits are typically excluded; concrete, documented losses survive.  
  
### Speculation Limits  
  
Damages must be reasonably certain. *Bryan v. Credit Control, LLC*, 954 F.3d 576, 580 (3d Cir. 2020) (FDCPA analog, principle of damages certainty). Not every unfilled application becomes a damages line item.  
  
### Sample Discovery  
  
> TEMPLATE - Plaintiff Interrogatory (offensive, to Plaintiff for narrative):  
>  
> For each opportunity (employment, housing, credit, insurance, business) that You contend was denied, delayed, or made less favorable as a result of Defendant's conduct, state in detail the nature of the opportunity, the date applied, the date and content of any decision, the resulting financial harm, and any mitigation efforts.

## E. Alternative-Credit-Rate Impact Analysis  
  
### The Score-Rate-Payment Model  
  
The dominant econometric model in FCRA damages: compute (1) plaintiff's actual credit score, (2) the score plaintiff would have had absent the inaccuracy, (3) the rate corresponding to each, and (4) the cost differential over the loan term. Forensic economists deploy industry-standard rate sheets and historical FICO/rate matrices.  
  
### Expert Testimony  
  
A qualified forensic economist or financial expert typically presents this analysis. Rule 26(a)(2) disclosure and *Daubert*/*Kumho Tire* admissibility apply. Plan early for expert depositions and rebuttal experts.  
  
### Discovery to Develop  
  
- Plaintiff's full file disclosures from all three CRAs;  
- Plaintiff's actual loan documents and rate;  
- Comparable rate evidence (rate sheets, lender published rates);  
- Plaintiff's credit history before and after the inaccuracy;  
- The lender's risk-based pricing tier and the score breakpoints.  
  
### Lifetime Cost for Long-Term Loans  
  
A 0.5% rate differential on a $300,000 30-year mortgage compounds materially. Expert reports typically include present-value calculations using an appropriate discount rate.  
  
### Defense Themes  
  
- **Contributory causation:** other negative information (late payments, charge-offs, high utilization) was on the report.  
- **Mitigation:** failure to obtain alternative financing or refinance.  
- **Other negative information:** even a clean version of the disputed item would not have moved the plaintiff above the relevant pricing breakpoint.  
  
### Sample Discovery  
  
> TEMPLATE - Plaintiff RFP to Defendant (CRA or furnisher):  
>  
> Produce all internal documents, training materials, and analyses regarding the impact on consumer credit scores of [the specific data field or tradeline type at issue], including any analyses by FICO, VantageScore, or comparable score-modeling vendor.  
  
> TEMPLATE - Defendant Interrogatory to Plaintiff:  
>  
> Identify each item of negative information appearing on Your credit reports during the relevant period other than the disputed item, and state how You contend each did or did not contribute to any credit denial or rate differential You claim.

## F. Time-and-Effort Damages  
  
Compensable, modest, frequently undervalued. *Crabill v. Trans Union, L.L.C.*, 259 F.3d 662, 664-65 (7th Cir. 2001) (recognizing recoverable time and effort).  
  
Categories:  
  
- Hours spent disputing;  
- Hours obtaining substitute credit;  
- Postage, certified mail fees;  
- Document copying;  
- Parking, travel;  
- Hours obtaining records to support disputes;  
- PTO used.  
  
### Sample Discovery  
  
> TEMPLATE - Plaintiff Interrogatory (to Plaintiff for narrative response):  
>  
> For each dispute You filed with any CRA or furnisher concerning the inaccuracy at issue, state the date, the recipient, the method (mail, phone, online), the time You spent preparing and filing the dispute, and the out-of-pocket expense incurred (postage, copies, fees, travel).  
  
> TEMPLATE - Plaintiff RFP (to Plaintiff for own production):  
>  
> Produce all receipts, postage records, certified-mail return receipts, parking stubs, and similar documentation evidencing time-and-effort damages.

## G. Standing-Related Damages Discovery Post-TransUnion  
  
### The Concrete-Harm Test  
  
After *TransUnion*, internal possession of inaccurate information by a CRA is not by itself a concrete injury for damages purposes; dissemination to a third party (or comparable materialized harm) generally is required. 594 U.S. at 432-34.  
  
### Discovery on Dissemination  
  
> TEMPLATE - Plaintiff Interrogatory:  
>  
> Identify every disclosure of any consumer report or other consumer information about Plaintiff that You made, transmitted, or otherwise provided to any third party from [date] to the present, including the date of disclosure, the recipient, the medium (e.g., online, batch tape, soft pull, hard pull), and the type of report or information disclosed.  
  
> TEMPLATE - Plaintiff RFP:  
>  
> Produce the access log, disclosure log, or comparable record showing every disclosure of consumer information about Plaintiff to any third party during the relevant period.  
  
### Common-Law Analogue Discovery  
  
*TransUnion* analogized concrete harm to common-law torts such as defamation. 594 U.S. at 433. Discovery should develop facts supporting the analogue:  
  
- Whether the inaccurate information would have damaged plaintiff's reputation if read by a third party;  
- Whether the recipient(s) understood the information as derogatory;  
- Whether plaintiff knows of any reputational consequences.  
  
### Materialized Harm vs. Risk of Future Harm  
  
*TransUnion* held that risk of future harm alone, without materialization, generally does not confer standing for damages. 594 U.S. at 435-37. Discovery should focus on actually-materialized consequences (credit denied, application withdrawn after learning of the report, etc.).  
  
### Class-Action Implications  
  
Each class member must satisfy concrete-harm standing for damages. *TransUnion*, 594 U.S. at 431. This shifts discovery: class-wide proof of dissemination becomes a fundamental commonality question, often resolved through the defendant's disclosure logs.

## H. Punitive Damages Discovery  
  
### Tied to Willfulness Under *Safeco*  
  
Punitive damages require willfulness. *Safeco* defined willful to include reckless disregard of a statutory duty - "an objectively unreasonable" reading of the statute, with consideration of authoritative guidance from courts and agencies. 551 U.S. at 69-70.  
  
### Discovery Targets  
  
- **Knowledge of contrary authority:** judicial opinions, FTC/CFPB guidance, consent orders, regulatory bulletins received and circulated within the defendant;  
- **Prior consumer complaints:** specifically alleging the same conduct;  
- **Internal audits showing the problem was known:** see Module 22 on self-critical analysis (generally discoverable);  
- **Consent decrees and enforcement actions:** prior CFPB / FTC / state AG matters;  
- **Compliance management system documents:** policies, exception logs, escalation reports;  
- **Net worth and financial condition.**  
  
### Net Worth Discovery  
  
Defendant's financial condition is relevant to punitive amount under *State Farm Mut. Auto. Ins. Co. v. Campbell*, 538 U.S. 408 (2003), and the due-process ratio analysis. Many courts permit net-worth discovery after a prima facie showing of entitlement to punitives; others permit it as a matter of course. *Mid Continent Cabinetry, Inc. v. George Koch Sons, Inc.*, 130 F.R.D. 149, 152 (D. Kan. 1990); *Caruso v. Coleman Co.*, 157 F.R.D. 344, 348-49 (E.D. Pa. 1994). Bifurcation is sometimes ordered.  
  
### *Williams v. First Advantage* Pattern  
  
*Williams v. First Advantage LNS Screening Sols. Inc.*, 947 F.3d 735, 758-66 (11th Cir. 2020), demonstrates how internal documents showing repeated mismatched-identity errors supported a substantial punitive award. Discovery in pattern-evidence cases should target:  
  
- All prior consumer complaints alleging same root cause;  
- Internal "false positive" or "mixed file" rate metrics;  
- Quality-assurance findings on the identical issue;  
- Communications among compliance, operations, and counsel about known problem rates.  
  
### Sample Discovery  
  
> TEMPLATE - Plaintiff Interrogatory (Willfulness/Punitives):  
>  
> Identify by date and category every consumer complaint, regulatory communication, internal audit, judicial opinion, or other authority received or generated by Defendant during the five years preceding [date] addressing the issue of [identify root cause: mixed files, failure to remove disputed tradelines, deletion-then-reinsertion, etc.].  
  
> TEMPLATE - Plaintiff RFP (Net Worth):  
>  
> Defendant's audited consolidated financial statements for the last three completed fiscal years; the most recent quarterly balance sheet; documents sufficient to identify Defendant's net worth, total assets, total liabilities, and retained earnings; and any documents sufficient to identify any actual or contemplated transactions materially affecting Defendant's net worth.

## I. Statutory Damages  
  
### The $100-$1,000 Range  
  
Under Sec. 1681n(a)(1)(A), a willful violation triggers a floor of $100 and ceiling of $1,000 per violation when actual damages are not proven. The amount is for the trier of fact. Discovery is generally limited but factors include the defendant's conduct, scale, and the consumer's experience.  
  
### Per-Violation Calculus  
  
A frequent question: what counts as "a violation"? Each inaccurate report? Each dispute mishandled? Each consumer? Courts diverge. Plaintiffs' counsel typically argue for per-report or per-dispute counting; defendants for per-consumer. *Murray v. GMAC Mortg. Corp.*, 434 F.3d 948, 953 (7th Cir. 2006), is the canonical class-context authority (statutory damages may be substantial but constitutional due-process limits exist).  
  
### Class-Action Implications  
  
Statutory-damages classes raise (a) due-process aggregation concerns post-*Wal-Mart* and (b) standing concerns post-*TransUnion*. Class discovery focuses on the size of the class (dissemination logs) and the per-member damages denominator.

## J. Attorney's Fees Discovery  
  
### Limited but Relevant  
  
Fee-shifting under Sec. 1681n(a)(3) and Sec. 1681o(a)(2). Discovery is generally limited to fee motion practice, not the underlying merits. Reasonable scope includes:  
  
- Counsel's hourly rates and supporting affidavits;  
- Time entries (often produced with privileged content redacted);  
- Comparable fee awards in the jurisdiction;  
- Plaintiff counsel's qualifications and experience;  
- Lodestar cross-checks against percentage of recovery in class settlements.  
  
### Defending Fee Petitions  
  
- **Reasonableness of hourly rates:** comparable forum rates, attorney experience;  
- **Reasonableness of hours:** duplication, block-billing, excessive partner time;  
- **Unsuccessful claims:** *Hensley v. Eckerhart*, 461 U.S. 424, 434-37 (1983), permits reduction for unsuccessful, distinct claims;  
- **Results obtained:** degree of success is the "most critical factor." *Hensley*, 461 U.S. at 436.  
  
### Sample Discovery  
  
> TEMPLATE - Defendant RFP (in fee-petition phase):  
>  
> Produce all time entries, billing records, fee agreements, contingency-fee agreements, and engagement letters between Plaintiff and Plaintiff's counsel; all communications regarding fees among Plaintiff's counsel and co-counsel; all retainer and lien documents; and all fee awards entered in favor of Plaintiff's counsel in FCRA matters in this district during the past five years.  
  
> TEMPLATE - Plaintiff Affidavit (in support of fee petition):  
>  
> Detailed lodestar showing hours by timekeeper and task category, supporting hourly rates with comparable fee awards, *Johnson v. Georgia Highway Express* / *Hensley* factor analysis, and contingency-risk adjustment where applicable.
