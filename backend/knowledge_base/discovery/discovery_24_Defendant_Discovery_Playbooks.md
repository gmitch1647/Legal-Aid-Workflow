# Section A. The Big Three Nationwide CRAs  
  
The Big Three are "nationwide consumer reporting agencies" under 15 U.S.C. § 1681a(p). All three participate in the e-OSCAR system operated by the Consumer Data Industry Association (CDIA), through which they exchange Automated Consumer Dispute Verification (ACDV) forms with furnishers. Published opinions describe the ACDV process as the dominant indirect-dispute channel and a recurring source of "parrot-the-furnisher" liability theories.  
  
## A.1 Equifax  
  
### Corporate Structure  
- **Equifax Inc.** (Delaware corporation; headquartered Atlanta, GA) — publicly traded parent (NYSE: EFX).  
- **Equifax Information Services LLC** (Georgia LLC) — the operating CRA entity that compiles consumer files and is the proper FCRA defendant in most accuracy/dispute cases. The 2025 CFPB consent order names both Equifax Inc. and Equifax Information Services LLC. (CFPB Consent Order 2025-CFPB-0002, Jan. 17, 2025.)  
- **Equifax Workforce Solutions** (d/b/a TALX Corporation) — operates **The Work Number**, the employment- and income-verification database. Separate operating unit; separately suable.  
- **Registered agent:** Corporation Service Company (Georgia and most states); verify state-by-state.  
  
### Business Units Relevant to FCRA  
- USIS (U.S. Information Solutions) — consumer credit file business  
- Workforce Solutions / The Work Number — employment & income data  
- International, Global Consumer Solutions (direct-to-consumer)  
  
### Dispute System Architecture  
- Indirect disputes processed through e-OSCAR / ACDV. Equifax's dispute portal historically routes through eOSCAR codes, often producing 1- or 2-character dispute descriptions (the "Dispute Code") that strip the consumer's written narrative. CFPB's January 2025 consent order found that Equifax's process "limits the ability of consumers to fully and accurately describe their disputes." (2025-CFPB-0002.)  
- Internal coding errors identified by CFPB caused Equifax to miscalculate and share inaccurate credit scores for several hundred thousand consumers. (Id.)  
  
### Known Litigation/Discovery Themes  
- **Soutter v. Equifax Info. Servs. LLC**, 498 F. App'x 260 (4th Cir. 2012), and subsequent class settlement (~$3M; Equifax agreed to stop reporting Virginia General District Court judgments for five years) — class-certification typicality litigation; produced extensive discovery on public-records procurement procedures.  
- **Sloane v. Equifax Info. Servs., LLC**, 510 F.3d 495 (4th Cir. 2007) — $351,000 verdict (reduced on appeal); jury found Equifax negligently failed to follow reasonable procedures, conduct reasonable reinvestigation, delete unverified information, and to prevent reinsertion. Useful template for mental-anguish damages discovery.  
- **Robinson v. Equifax Info. Servs., LLC**, 560 F.3d 235 (4th Cir. 2009) — $200,000 verdict affirmed; identity-theft mixed-file pattern with repeated failed reinvestigations.  
  
### Notable Enforcement  
- **2017 CFPB consent order** (2017-CFPB-0001) — deceptive marketing of credit scores; $2.5M penalty; $3.8M restitution.  
- **2019 global data-breach settlement** with CFPB, FTC, and 50 states/territories: up to $700M total, including $425M consumer fund and $100M CFPB civil penalty. (CFPB/FTC press releases July 22, 2019.) Produced extensive public discovery into Equifax data-security practices; not directly a § 1681 case but useful for corporate-witness baselines.  
- **2025 CFPB consent order** (2025-CFPB-0002, Jan. 17, 2025) — $15M civil penalty for FCRA violations: failure to conduct reasonable reinvestigations, failure to prevent reinsertion, inadequate § 1681i(a)(6) notices, accuracy/§ 1681e(b) procedures, and § 1681c-2 identity-theft block failures.  
  
### Document Categories Likely to Exist  
- ACDV logs and dispute-resolution scripts; "Dispute Code Reference Guide"  
- "Resolutions" or "Customer Care Centers" training manuals (historically located in Atlanta, GA, and offshore vendor sites — Equifax's Costa Rica and Philippines operations are publicly disclosed in 10-K filings)  
- Audit reports for vendor dispute processors **[VERIFY current vendors]**  
- Quality-assurance metrics (handle time, AHT, dispute volume reports)  
  
### Custodian Categories  
- Consumer Affairs / Customer Care leadership  
- Office of Consumer Affairs (executive-level dispute escalations)  
- Data Operations / Subject Identification (matching-algorithm owners)  
- Public Records department leadership  
  
### Discovery Start Points (Equifax)  
1. RFP for the current Dispute Code Reference Guide and any prior versions in effect during the relevant period.  
2. RFP for all training materials provided to ACDV processors, including offshore vendors.  
3. Interrogatory: identify every employee or agent who handled any of plaintiff's disputes, by name, title, location, and employer (Equifax vs. vendor).  
4. RFP for all CFPB and state AG examination reports concerning dispute handling 2015-present.  
5. 30(b)(6) topics on matching logic (the "FAN/CIN" or successor algorithm) and on dispute-routing.  
  
### Common Defense Counsel (public dockets)  
- King & Spalding (Atlanta); Troutman Pepper Locke; Seyfarth Shaw. [VERIFY current case]

## A.3 TransUnion  
  
### Corporate Structure  
- **TransUnion** (NYSE: TRU) — Delaware holding company.  
- **Trans Union LLC** (Delaware LLC; HQ Chicago) — operating CRA; proper FCRA defendant.  
- **TransUnion Rental Screening Solutions, Inc.** (formerly MySmartMove) — tenant-screening; separate operating subsidiary.  
  
### Key Cases  
- **TransUnion LLC v. Ramirez**, 594 U.S. 413 (2021) — Article III standing; ruled that 6,332 of 8,185 class members lacked standing because their misleading OFAC-Advisor reports were never disseminated to a third party. Critical for plaintiff dissemination-discovery: under Ramirez, every consumer-file case requires evidence that the report was published to a third party.  
- **Ramirez v. TransUnion LLC**, 951 F.3d 1008 (9th Cir. 2020) — pre-Supreme-Court class-cert opinion; describes TransUnion's "OFAC Name Screen" product as a name-only matching tool that flagged consumers with similar names to OFAC Specially Designated Nationals without considering date of birth or other identifiers.  
- **Cortez v. Trans Union, LLC**, 617 F.3d 688 (3d Cir. 2010) — held OFAC-Advisor alerts are part of the consumer's "file" under § 1681a(g) and "consumer report" under § 1681a(d); rejected TransUnion's "third-party-vendor-stored data not in our file" theory. Jury verdict of $50,000 compensatory and $750,000 punitive (remitted to $100,000) affirmed in relevant part.  
- **2017 CFPB consent order** (2017-CFPB-0002) — deceptive credit-score marketing; $3M restitution + $1.5M penalty.  
- **2022 CFPB lawsuit** — alleging violations of 2017 consent order against TransUnion and a former executive (CFPB v. Trans Union LLC et al., N.D. Ill. 2022). [VERIFY current status]  
  
### Dispute System  
- Participates in e-OSCAR / ACDV. Cortez opinion describes TransUnion's vendor-storage architecture (OFAC data stored by third-party processor) — useful template for discovery requests on outsourced data flows.  
  
### Document Categories  
- "OFAC Name Screen" / "OFAC Advisor" product documentation (now or formerly Accuity-sourced data; **[VERIFY]**)  
- ACDV processing scripts  
- Class notice and dissemination logs (post-Ramirez, plaintiffs must prove dissemination)  
  
### Discovery Start Points (TransUnion)  
1. RFP for the "dissemination log" for plaintiff's consumer report (every third party that received any version of the file during the relevant period). Cite Ramirez as the basis.  
2. RFP for OFAC Name Screen or successor product documentation if OFAC reporting is at issue.  
3. 30(b)(6) topics on the third-party vendor that hosts disputed data fields (Cortez-style theory).  
  
### Common Defense Counsel  
- Stroock & Stroock & Lavan; Jones Day; Reed Smith. [VERIFY current case]

# Section B. Nationwide Specialty CRAs  
  
These are "nationwide specialty consumer reporting agencies" under 15 U.S.C. § 1681a(w). They have separate annual-disclosure duties (§ 1681j(a)(1)(C)) and are commonly named as direct defendants when their reports cause adverse action.  
  
## B.1 LexisNexis Risk Solutions / CLUE / A-PLUS  
- **Corporate:** LexisNexis Risk Solutions Inc. (Georgia); LexisNexis Risk Solutions FL Inc. — both operate consumer-reporting products. Parent: RELX Group plc.  
- **Key products:** CLUE (Comprehensive Loss Underwriting Exchange — auto/property insurance claims history); A-PLUS (similar; competing/complementary). LexisNexis also provides public-records data feeds to the Big Three.  
- **Dispute considerations:** Insurance-claims reporting under §§ 1681e(b) and 1681i; public-record reporting under § 1681k.  
- **Discovery start points:** RFP for the data-source agreements showing which insurers contribute claims data; RFP for the public-records-vendor contracts between LexisNexis and the Big Three.

# Section C. Major Debt Buyers  
  
## C.1 Encore Capital Group / Midland Funding / Midland Credit Management  
- **Corporate:** Encore Capital Group, Inc. (publicly traded NASDAQ: ECPG). Operating subsidiaries: Midland Funding, LLC (debt purchaser/account-holder of record); Midland Credit Management, Inc. (the servicer/collector); Asset Acceptance Capital Corp. (acquired 2013). Encore also operates Cabot Credit Management internationally.  
- **Key case:** **Hinkle v. Midland Credit Mgmt., Inc.**, 827 F.3d 1295 (11th Cir. 2016) — leading authority on furnisher reasonable investigation of indirect disputes (§ 1681s-2(b)). Court held a reasonable jury could find Midland's investigation unreasonable where it limited its inquiry to comparing PII in its own file to PII in the CRA file (the same PII Midland had originally reported), without consulting account-level documentation. Establishes that for debt-buyer furnishers without original-creditor records, comparing data to itself is not a "reasonable investigation."  
- **CFPB enforcement:**  
 - **2015 consent order** (2015-CFPB-0022): $42M consumer refunds + $10M civil penalty; cease collection on $125M of debts. Findings of CFPA/FDCPA/FCRA violations including collecting on debts without sufficient documentation and using robo-signed affidavits.  
 - **2020 lawsuit and settlement** (CFPB v. Encore Capital Group, S.D. Cal. 2020): Alleged violations of the 2015 consent order; $15M civil penalty, redress to consumers, and a five-year extension of conduct provisions.  
- **Discovery start points (Encore/Midland):**  
 1. RFP for the bill of sale, purchase-and-sale agreement (PSA), and all schedules transferring plaintiff's account to Midland Funding (Hinkle-style theory).  
 2. RFP for all seller representations and warranties concerning data accuracy.  
 3. RFP for chain-of-title documentation from the original creditor forward.  
 4. RFP for Encore's "Account-Level Documentation Standards" post-2015 consent order.  
 5. RFP for ACDV scripts and dispute-handling SOPs used by MCM dispute representatives.  
- **Defense counsel:** Locke Lord; Stradley Ronon. [VERIFY current matters]  
  
## C.2 Portfolio Recovery Associates (PRA Group)  
- **Corporate:** PRA Group, Inc. (NASDAQ: PRAA; Virginia). Operating subsidiary: Portfolio Recovery Associates, LLC.  
- **CFPB enforcement:**  
 - **2015 consent order** (2015-CFPB-0023): $19M consumer refunds + $8M civil penalty (CFPB total reported as "more than $27M"); findings of FDCPA/CFPA violations including unsubstantiated debts, robo-signed affidavits, and time-barred lawsuits.  
 - **2023 stipulated final judgment** (CFPB v. Portfolio Recovery Assocs., LLC, E.D. Va. 2023): $12.18M consumer redress + $12M civil penalty. CFPB found PRA continued to collect on unsubstantiated debt, failed to provide required documentation/disclosures, threatened legal action without offering or possessing required documentation, sued on time-barred debt, and failed to properly investigate consumer credit-reporting disputes. (CFPB Press Release, Mar. 23, 2023.)  
- **Discovery start points:** Same Hinkle-style framework as Encore; specifically request post-2023 dispute-investigation SOPs and any quarterly compliance reports filed under the consent judgment.  
  
## C.3 Crown Asset Management / LVNV Funding / Resurgent Capital Services  
- **Structure (typical debt-buying chain):**  
 - **LVNV Funding, LLC** — Delaware LLC; debt-buyer holding entity; the named creditor/plaintiff in most state-court collection suits.  
 - **Resurgent Capital Services, L.P.** — South Carolina LP; servicer of LVNV-held debts; usually the entity that furnishes data to CRAs.

 2. RFP for Resurgent's furnishing log for plaintiff's account.  
 3. Interrogatory identifying every entity that has held legal or beneficial title to the debt.  
 4. RFP for Resurgent's dispute-investigation SOPs.

# Section E. Cross-Cutting Defendant Discovery Notes  
  
## E.1 Subscriber / Data-Furnishing Agreements  
- CRA-to-furnisher agreements (or "subscriber agreements") are typically asserted as confidential/trade-secret; consumer-attorney must press through a protective order. Public-record references appear in the Equifax 10-K filings ("we have data-contribution agreements with millions of furnishers") and CFPB supervisory highlights.  
- Negotiate a two-tier protective order: Confidential (counsel + experts) and Highly Confidential — Attorneys' Eyes Only (matching algorithms, scoring weights, vendor contracts).  
  
## E.2 Common Defense Counsel by Defendant Type
