## I. Rule 30(b)(6) Framework — Furnisher Application  
  
The Rule 30(b)(6) framework and the Dec. 1, 2020 meet-and-confer obligation discussed in Discovery 11 apply equally to furnishers. Two furnisher-specific points:  
  
1. **Multiple-designee allocation.** Furnishers typically have functionally siloed operations: servicing, collections, credit reporting, dispute resolution. The notice should permit designation of multiple witnesses but require designation by topic, so that any "I don't know" answer is attributable to a designee actually charged with that topic.  
2. **Vendors and off-shore operations.** Many furnishers contract dispute investigation, e-OSCAR handling, and Metro 2 mapping to vendors. Rule 30(b)(6) reaches information "known or reasonably available to the organization," which includes vendor-held information that the organization controls or has the right to obtain. The meet-and-confer should pin this down explicitly so vendor outsourcing cannot later be used to evade scope.

## II. Topic Bank  
  
### A. The Consumer's Specific Account  
  
**Topic A.1 — Origination, history, and status.**  
> The complete origination, application, underwriting, history, status, and disposition of plaintiff's account, including all changes to balance, status code, payment history, ownership, and delinquency status during the relevant period.  
  
*Discoverable.* Core to whether the information furnished was "accurate" under § 1681s-2(a) and whether the furnisher's investigation under § 1681s-2(b) was reasonable.  
  
*Likely designee.* Servicing operations / account management.  
  
*Foundation/admissions.* Authenticate account-level system of record screens; lock in field definitions; identify all systems where account data resides.  
  
**Topic A.2 — All furnishings to CRAs.**  
> Each communication, tradeline submission, Metro 2 file, or update that [Furnisher] transmitted to any CRA about plaintiff during the relevant period, including the date, CRA recipient, fields populated, status codes, payment history grid values, special comment codes, and compliance condition codes (CCCs).  
  
*Foundation/admissions.* Authenticate the Metro 2 submission; confirm what was reported; identify whether the account was marked disputed (CCC "XB"), the dates that flag was applied/removed, and the basis.  
  
**Topic A.3 — All disputes received about plaintiff's account.**  
> Each indirect dispute (ACDV) received via the CRAs and each direct dispute received from or on behalf of plaintiff under § 1681s-2(a)(8) about plaintiff's account during the relevant period; receipt date; assignment; investigation steps; response; and resulting tradeline changes.  
  
*Authority.* § 1681s-2(b); § 1681s-2(a)(8); 12 C.F.R. § 1022.43.  
  
---  
  
### B. Dispute Handling — § 1681s-2(b)  
  
**Topic B.1 — ACDV routing and assignment.**  
> [Furnisher]'s procedures during the relevant period for receiving ACDVs from the CRAs, routing them to investigators, and assigning ownership.  
  
*Likely designee.* Credit Reporting Operations or Consumer Disputes manager.  
  
**Topic B.2 — Investigator review.**  
> What an investigator handling an ACDV at [Furnisher] during the relevant period actually reviewed, including: systems of record consulted; account-level documentation; the consumer's narrative if forwarded by the CRA; prior dispute history on the same account; and any external data sources.  
  
*Authority.* *Hinkle v. Midland Credit Mgmt., Inc.*, 827 F.3d 1295 (11th Cir. 2016) (reasonable investigation requires more than checking the furnisher's own records when the dispute identifies a basis the records would not refute); *Gorman v. Wolpoff & Abramson, LLP*, 584 F.3d 1147 (9th Cir. 2009) (reasonableness is jury question; rubber-stamp verification is unreasonable).  
  
**Topic B.3 — Time per dispute; automation vs. human.**  
> Average and median time spent by [Furnisher] personnel or contractors per ACDV during the relevant period; the proportion of ACDVs resolved by automated rule without human review; and the training and qualifications of dispute investigators.  
  
**Topic B.4 — "Reasonable investigation" standards.**  
> [Furnisher]'s internal definition, written standards, training materials, and quality assurance criteria for what constitutes a "reasonable investigation" under § 1681s-2(b).  
  
**Topic B.5 — Account-level documentation (debt buyers especially).**  
> Whether and how [Furnisher] obtains, maintains, and reviews account-level documentation — original creditor agreements, account statements, signed contracts, payment histories — in connection with disputes under § 1681s-2(b).  
  
*Authority.* *Hinkle*, 827 F.3d at 1305 (debt buyer's failure to obtain account-level documentation supports finding of unreasonable investigation); CFPB *Consent Order, In re Encore Capital Group* (2015, 2020) and *In re Portfolio Recovery Associates* (2015, 2023) (public consent orders on debt-buyer documentation deficiencies).  
  
**Topic B.6 — Marking accounts as disputed (Saunders).**  
> [Furnisher]'s procedures for marking an account "disputed" in credit reporting after receipt of a dispute, including the conditions for applying CCC "XB," the conditions for removing the dispute flag, and the time within which the flag is applied.  
  
*Authority.* *Saunders v. Branch Banking & Trust Co. of Va.*, 526 F.3d 142 (4th Cir. 2008) (failure to note that the consumer disputes the account renders the report misleading and can be inaccurate under § 1681s-2(b)); CFPB *Bulletin 2013-09* (furnisher duty to report dispute notation).  
  
---  
  
### C. Direct Disputes — § 1681s-2(a)(8) and 12 C.F.R. § 1022.43  
  
**Topic C.1 — Receipt and processing of direct disputes.**  
> [Furnisher]'s procedures during the relevant period for receiving, logging, and investigating direct disputes from consumers under § 1681s-2(a)(8) and 12 C.F.R. § 1022.43, including the address(es) designated for direct disputes and the notice to consumers of those addresses.  
  
**Topic C.2 — Excluded categories.**  
> The categories of direct disputes that [Furnisher] excludes from investigation under 12 C.F.R. § 1022.43(b) and the procedures for making and documenting those determinations.  
  
**Topic C.3 — Frivolous determinations.**  
> [Furnisher]'s procedures for determining a direct dispute frivolous or irrelevant under 12 C.F.R. § 1022.43(f), and the notice to the consumer required by § 1022.43(f)(2).  
  
---  
  
### D. Furnishing Accuracy — § 1681s-2(a)  
  
**Topic D.1 — Metro 2 mapping.**  
> How [Furnisher] maps fields from its servicing system(s) of record to the Metro 2 fields submitted to the CRAs, including each translation rule, default value, and exception path.  
  
**Topic D.2 — Status code conventions and changes.**  
> [Furnisher]'s conventions during the relevant period for use of Metro 2 account status codes, payment rating codes, special comment codes, and compliance condition codes, and each material change to those conventions.  
  
**Topic D.3 — Data flow.**  
> The end-to-end data flow from [Furnisher]'s servicing systems through any data warehouse, vendor, or intermediary to the CRAs, including each transformation, validation, suppression, and reject queue.  
  
*Authority.* § 1681s-2(a)(1) (prohibition on furnishing information the furnisher knows or has reasonable cause to believe is inaccurate); § 1681s-2(a)(2) (duty to correct and update).  
  
---  
  
### E. Reg V Subpart E — 12 C.F.R. § 1022.42  
  
**Topic E.1 — Written policies.**  
> [Furnisher]'s written policies and procedures regarding the accuracy and integrity of information furnished to CRAs, as required by 12 C.F.R. § 1022.42(a) and Appendix E.  
  
**Topic E.2 — Implementation, training, audits.**  
> Implementation, employee training, monitoring, and internal audits of [Furnisher]'s Reg V Subpart E policies during the relevant period.  
  
**Topic E.3 — Reasonableness review and updates.**  
> [Furnisher]'s periodic review and updating of its Reg V Subpart E policies under 12 C.F.R. § 1022.42(c), including the dates, scope, and outcomes of each review during the relevant period.  
  
*Authority.* 12 C.F.R. § 1022.42; CFPB *Supervisory Highlights* (numerous editions) repeatedly cite Reg V Subpart E deficiencies as findings supporting unreasonable investigation and inaccuracy claims.  
  
---  
  
### F. For Debt Buyers Specifically  
  
**Topic F.1 — Purchase agreements.**  
> The purchase agreements, forward-flow agreements, and bills of sale by which [Furnisher] acquired plaintiff's account (and the broader portfolio in which plaintiff's account was included), including seller representations and warranties regarding accuracy, ownership, statute-of-limitations status, and prior dispute history.  
  
**Topic F.2 — Account-level documentation at acquisition.**  
> What account-level documentation [Furnisher] obtained (or had the contractual right to obtain) from the seller at acquisition, including original signed agreements, statements, payment histories, and charge-off documentation.  
  
*Authority.* CFPB *Consent Orders* against major debt buyers (*Encore Capital*, *Portfolio Recovery*) — public sources documenting industry-wide deficiencies in account-level documentation; FTC, *The Structure and Practices of the Debt Buying Industry* (Jan. 2013) (public).  
  
**Topic F.3 — Verifying ownership.**  
> [Furnisher]'s procedures for verifying ownership of debts at acquisition and prior to furnishing to CRAs.  
  
**Topic F.4 — Verifying account-level data.**  
> [Furnisher]'s procedures for verifying account-level data accuracy (balance, status, last payment date, date of first delinquency for re-aging purposes under § 1681c(a)) at acquisition and prior to furnishing.  
  
*Authority.* § 1681c(a)(4)–(5); § 1681s-2(a)(5) (duty to provide accurate date of delinquency).  
  
**Topic F.5 — Identity theft claims.**  
> [Furnisher]'s procedures for handling consumer claims of identity theft on purchased debts, including investigation steps, treatment of FTC Identity Theft Reports, and ceasing collection/furnishing during investigation.  
  
---  
  
### G. Identity Theft — § 1681s-2(a)(6)–(7)  
  
**Topic G.1 — Response to § 1681c-2 blocks.**  
> [Furnisher]'s procedures for responding to notices from CRAs of blocks placed under § 1681c-2, including the prohibition on furnishing the blocked information under § 1681s-2(a)(6)(B).  
  
**Topic G.2 — Refurnishing prohibition.**  
> [Furnisher]'s procedures for ensuring that information identified by the consumer as resulting from identity theft is not refurnished to any CRA, including controls within the servicing and credit-reporting systems.  
  
**Topic G.3 — Notice to debt collector under § 1681s-2(a)(6)(B).**  
> [Furnisher]'s procedures for notifying any subsequent debt collector or assignee that the debt has been identified as the result of identity theft, as required by § 1681s-2(a)(6)(B).  
  
---  
  
### H. Compliance and Regulatory  
  
**Topic H.1 — Examination findings.**  
> Each non-privileged finding, matter requiring attention, supervisory letter, or examination report from the CFPB, the OCC, the FDIC, the Federal Reserve, the NCUA, or any state regulator addressing [Furnisher]'s FCRA furnisher compliance during the relevant period, and [Furnisher]'s response.  
  
**Topic H.2 — Consent orders and remediation.**  
> Each public consent order, stipulated judgment, or settlement between [Furnisher] and any regulator during the relevant period that involved credit reporting or furnisher compliance, and the steps taken in remediation.  
  
**Topic H.3 — Internal compliance audits.**  
> Internal audits, second-line risk reviews, and third-line internal audit reports addressing [Furnisher]'s credit reporting and dispute investigation during the relevant period.  
  
**Topic H.4 — Senior management and board oversight.**  
> The structure, reporting lines, and content of senior management and board (or committee) reporting on credit reporting accuracy, dispute volumes, dispute outcomes, audit findings, and FCRA litigation during the relevant period.  
  
**Topic H.5 — Document retention and litigation holds.**  
> [Furnisher]'s document retention policies and litigation hold practices applicable to plaintiff's account, plaintiff's disputes, and the categories of documents responsive to plaintiff's discovery.

### I. Willfulness Topics — Safeco  
  
**Topic I.1 — Knowledge of Hinkle and related precedent.**  
> [Furnisher]'s knowledge during the relevant period of *Hinkle v. Midland Credit Mgmt.*, 827 F.3d 1295 (11th Cir. 2016); *Saunders v. Branch Banking & Trust*, 526 F.3d 142 (4th Cir. 2008); *Gorman v. Wolpoff & Abramson*, 584 F.3d 1147 (9th Cir. 2009); CFPB *Bulletin 2013-09*; CFPB *Supervisory Highlights* editions on furnisher compliance; and other authorities interpreting § 1681s-2(b).  
  
**Topic I.2 — Decisions despite known accuracy issues.**  
> Decisions by [Furnisher] during the relevant period to maintain — despite known accuracy issues, dispute volumes, audit findings, or contrary regulatory guidance — the credit reporting procedures, dispute investigation procedures, or status code conventions challenged in this litigation.  
  
**Topic I.3 — Audit findings ignored.**  
> Each internal audit finding, regulatory finding, or third-party review finding during the relevant period concerning credit reporting or dispute handling that was deferred, accepted as residual risk, or otherwise not promptly remediated, and the rationale.  
  
*Authority.* *Safeco Ins. Co. of Am. v. Burr*, 551 U.S. 47 (2007); *Cortez v. Trans Union*, 617 F.3d 688 (3d Cir. 2010); *Pinson v. Equifax Credit Info. Servs.*, 316 F. App'x 744 (10th Cir. 2009) (willfulness can rest on disregard of clear obligations).

## III. Designee Preparation Issues — Furnishers  
  
The Rule 30(b)(6) preparation duty is heightened in the furnisher context because of operational silos. Counsel should:  
  
1. **Confirm at the outset** what each designee was designated for, what they did to prepare, who they spoke to in servicing, in disputes, in credit reporting, in compliance, in audit, and in any vendor, and what they could not learn.  
2. **Develop the inadequate-preparation record** if the designee cannot answer noticed topics, particularly Reg V Subpart E policies (Topic E), audit findings (Topic H.3), and willfulness topics (Topic I).  
3. **Use Fed. R. Evid. 612** to obtain materials the designee used to refresh recollection.  
4. **Be prepared to move** for re-deposition, sanctions, or evidentiary preclusion. *See* *Black Horse Lane Assoc. v. Dow Chem. Corp.*, 228 F.3d 275, 304 (3d Cir. 2000); *Resolution Trust Corp. v. Southern Union Co.*, 985 F.2d 196 (5th Cir. 1993); *QBE Ins. Corp. v. Jorda Enters.*, 277 F.R.D. 676 (S.D. Fla. 2012).

## IV. Cross-Cutting Objection Authority  
  
| Objection | Response / Authority |  
|---|---|  
| Overbreadth | Tie each topic to plaintiff's account, plaintiff's disputes, or the procedures applied to plaintiff. Rule 26(b)(1) relevance is satisfied. |  
| Proprietary / trade secret | Protective order under Rule 26(c). Furnisher dispute procedures, Reg V policies, and Metro 2 mappings are routinely produced under protective order. |  
| Proportionality | The furnisher's exclusive control of account-level information and dispute investigation files makes Rule 26(b)(1) relative-access factor decisive. |  
| Bank examination privilege | The privilege belongs to the regulator, not the furnisher; the furnisher cannot unilaterally invoke it for documents it created. Public CFPB *Supervisory Highlights* are not privileged. |  
| Work product / attorney-client | Privilege log required (Rule 26(b)(5)). Investigation files prepared in the ordinary course of business under § 1681s-2(b) are *not* work product merely because litigation eventually followed. *See* *United States v. Adlman*, 134 F.3d 1194 (2d Cir. 1998) (work product requires the "because of litigation" test). |  
| Apex | Inapplicable to 30(b)(6) corporate designations; the corporation is the deponent. |
