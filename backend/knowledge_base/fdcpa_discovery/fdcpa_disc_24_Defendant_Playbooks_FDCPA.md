For any FDCPA defendant in your case:  
  
1\. Identify the category (debt buyer; collection law firm; collection agency; in-house creditor; medical/subrogation).

\#\# 1. Major Debt Buyers  
  
\#\#\# 1.1 Encore Capital Group / Midland Funding, LLC / Midland Credit Management, Inc. / Asset Acceptance Capital Corp.  
  
\*\*Corporate structure (public record):\*\* Encore Capital Group, Inc. is the publicly traded parent (NASDAQ: ECPG). Midland Funding, LLC is the debt-buyer entity that purchases portfolios; Midland Credit Management, Inc. ("MCM") is the licensed collector that services the accounts. Asset Acceptance Capital Corp. is a former competitor acquired by Encore in 2013.  
  
\*\*Public enforcement record:\*\*  
  
\- \*\*2015 CFPB consent order\*\*, In re Encore Capital Group, Inc., et al., File No. 2015-CFPB-0022. The CFPB found that Encore and its subsidiaries violated the CFPA and FDCPA by:  
 - Collecting on debts that were unverified or that they could not legally enforce;  
 - Using false or unsubstantiated affidavits in state-court collection actions;  
 - Misrepresenting to consumers that they would prove the debt if contested;  
 - Suing to collect on time-barred debts.  
 Order required restitution and a $10 million civil money penalty, plus specific compliance obligations including documentation requirements before suit. Available at consumerfinance.gov.  
\- \*\*2020 CFPB lawsuit\*\*, \*CFPB v. Encore Capital Group, Inc., et al.\*, S.D. Cal. No. 3:20-cv-01750, alleging violations of the 2015 consent order, the FDCPA, and the CFPA, including: suing without required documentation; using internal legal department without required disclosures; suing on time-barred debt without required disclosures.  
\- \*\*2020 stipulated final judgment\*\*: Encore and subsidiaries ordered to pay redress and a $15 million civil money penalty. Order extended consent-order obligations.  
  
\*\*Recurring published-opinion themes:\*\*  
  
\- Reliance on summary affidavits at state-court collection stage; sufficiency of business-records foundation under FRE 803(6) and \*Beal Bank, SSB v. Eurich\*, 444 Mass. 813 (2005)-type integrated-records doctrine.  
\- Application of \*Henson v. Santander Consumer USA Inc.\*, 582 U.S. 79 (2017) — Midland is a debt buyer but its principal purpose is collection, so it remains an FDCPA "debt collector." \*See Tepper v. Amos Fin., LLC\*, 898 F.3d 364 (3d Cir. 2018) (post-\*Henson\* principal-purpose analysis).  
\- Time-barred-debt suit issues — \*Midland Funding, LLC v. Johnson\*, 581 U.S. 224 (2017) (filing a proof of claim on time-barred debt in bankruptcy is not an FDCPA violation, but this holding is bankruptcy-specific).  
\- Affidavit defects — pre-\*Bock\*-style meaningful-involvement issues bleed over to debt-buyer affidavit practice.  
  
\*\*Discovery priorities:\*\*  
  
\- The chain of title — original creditor → debt seller(s) → Midland Funding. Get the bill of sale, asset schedule, and pool data file for the plaintiff's account.  
\- Account-level documentation — was the account part of a portfolio with "media" (original statements) or "media-light"?  
\- The MCM compliance program post-2015 consent order — internal CMS, documentation requirements, time-barred-debt review process.  
\- Affidavit production process — who reviewed; was there meaningful affiant knowledge.  
\- State-court complaint volume and outcome rates.
