\# FDCPA Discovery — Defense-Side Discovery to Consumer Plaintiffs  
  
\*\*Topic:\*\* Defense-side written discovery — interrogatories, RFPs, and topic notes — directed to consumer plaintiffs in FDCPA cases, individual and class.  
\*\*Primary authority:\*\* Fed. R. Civ. P. 26, 33, 34; Fair Debt Collection Practices Act, 15 U.S.C. §§ 1692–1692p; Regulation F, 12 C.F.R. Part 1006; Credit Repair Organizations Act, 15 U.S.C. §§ 1679–1679j.

\#\# 1. Defense Discovery Strategy  
  
The 2021 \*TransUnion v. Ramirez\* decision reshaped FDCPA defense discovery. The Supreme Court held that "no concrete harm, no standing" — even where a federal statute creates a cause of action, a plaintiff must show concrete injury tied to a common-law analogue. 594 U.S. at 424–27. Defense discovery now drives heavily toward Article III standing facts, with subordinate emphasis on credibility (prior litigation history, credit-repair relationships) and damages predicates.  
  
Defense topics fall into nine zones:  
  
1\. Identity and contact history.  
2\. The debt and its history.  
3\. Communications received from the collector.  
4\. Damages — actual, emotional, time-and-effort, mitigation.  
5\. Standing post-\*TransUnion v. Ramirez\*.  
6\. Prior FDCPA / debt-collection litigation.  
7\. Communications with credit-repair organizations.  
8\. Other debts and disputes (pattern evidence).  
9\. Class-related facts (typicality, adequacy, common harm).  
  
Each topic below provides 8–20 interrogatories and 5–15 RFPs.

\#\#\# Topic 1 — Identity and Contact History  
  
\*\*Interrogatories.\*\*  
  
1\. State Your full legal name and every other name (married, maiden, aliases, AKAs) by which You have been known during the seven (7) years preceding the Complaint.  
2\. State Your date of birth, last four digits of Social Security number (subject to protective order), and driver's-license history during that period.  
3\. List every residence address You have occupied during that period, including dates and persons residing with You at each.  
4\. List every telephone number You have used during that period, the carrier, the dates of use, and whether the line was landline or wireless.  
5\. List every email address You have used during that period.  
6\. Identify Your employer(s) during that period, including dates of employment.  
7\. Identify every household member or other person who has had access to Your mail, telephone, or email during the period of the alleged FDCPA violations.  
8\. Identify every other person at any residence address who shared a surname with You during the relevant period.  
  
\*\*RFPs.\*\*  
  
1\. Every Document reflecting Your residence at each address listed in response to the preceding interrogatories (lease, utility bill, voter registration, etc.).  
2\. Every Document reflecting Your telephone numbers during the relevant period (carrier invoices, port-out confirmations).  
3\. Every voicemail greeting You used during the relevant period that identifies a name other than Your full legal name.  
4\. Documents sufficient to show every cellular plan or family-plan arrangement that included any telephone number listed above.  
5\. Every Document reflecting any change of address request filed with the United States Postal Service during the relevant period.
