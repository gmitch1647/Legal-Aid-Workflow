\# FDCPA Discovery: Meet-and-Confer Letter Templates  
  
\*\*Topic:\*\* Meet-and-confer letters in FDCPA litigation — interrogatories, document production, 30(b)(6) preparation, call recordings, form letter libraries, defense overreach  
\*\*Cross-references:\*\* Fed. R. Civ. P. 26, 30, 33, 34, 37(a)(1); 15 U.S.C. §§ 1692c, 1692e, 1692g, 1692k; 12 C.F.R. Part 1006  
\*\*Last updated:\*\* May 16, 2026

\#\# Overview  
  
Rule 37(a)(1) conditions any motion to compel on a certification that the movant has in good faith conferred or attempted to confer with the opposing party in an effort to obtain disclosure without court action. Most local rules and standing orders impose more granular meet-and-confer requirements. The written meet-and-confer letter serves three purposes: (1) it satisfies the Rule, (2) it crystallizes the dispute for the court, and (3) it preserves a record of the responding party's positions and shifting rationales.  
  
Letters should be specific, dispassionate, and structured. The letters below are templates; counsel should adapt them to the facts of the case and the local culture.

\#\# Template 1: Insufficient Interrogatory Responses  
  
\*\*TEMPLATE:\*\*  
  
\> \[Date\]  
\>  
\> \[Defense counsel\]  
\> Re: \[Caption\]; Case No. \_\_\_\_  
\>  
\> Dear Counsel:  
\>  
\> I write pursuant to Fed. R. Civ. P. 37(a)(1) and Local Rule \_\_\_ regarding Defendant's responses to Plaintiff's First Set of Interrogatories, served on \[date\] and responded to on \[date\]. I have identified the following deficiencies and request that we confer.  
\>  
\> \*\*Interrogatory No. 3.\*\* This interrogatory asks Defendant to state the volume of the form letter identified as Plaintiff's Exhibit A that was sent during the period \[date range\]. Defendant has objected on grounds of burden and relevance. As we have discussed in prior cases, the volume of a form letter sent is directly relevant to (i) the "frequency and persistence of noncompliance" factor under 15 U.S.C. § 1692k(b)(1), (ii) the adequacy of the procedures asserted under § 1692k(c) (whether they were "reasonably adapted"), and (iii) class certification factors in the alternative pleading. \*See Bartlett v. Heibl\*, 128 F.3d 497 (7th Cir. 1997); \*Hicks v. Client Servs., Inc.\*, 257 F.R.D. 699 (S.D. Fla. 2009). The information is presumptively in Defendant's letter-vendor system. Please provide a verified count.  
\>  
\> \*\*Interrogatory No. 5.\*\* Defendant has responded that it "lacks personal knowledge" of facts concerning the chain of title. As a corporate defendant, Defendant may not respond on "personal knowledge" grounds; the obligation is to investigate the question and provide information available to the corporation. \*See\* Fed. R. Civ. P. 33(b)(1)(B); \*Smith v. Pfizer Inc.\*, 265 F.R.D. 278 (M.D. Tenn. 2010).  
\>  
\> \*\*Interrogatory No. 8.\*\* Defendant's response refers to "documents to be produced" without identifying any. Rule 33(d) requires specification of the records "in sufficient detail to enable the interrogating party to locate and identify them as readily as the responding party could." Please supplement with bates-range citations or provide a narrative response.  
\>  
\> \*\*Interrogatory No. 12.\*\* This interrogatory asks Defendant to identify the affirmative defenses on which it intends to rely and the factual basis for each. Defendant's response that this is "an improper contention interrogatory" is overstated. Rule 33(a)(2) expressly allows contention interrogatories. Courts routinely require fact specification of pleaded affirmative defenses by the close of discovery. \*See\* fact-specification authorities in this Circuit.  
\>  
\> Please let me know your availability on \[three proposed dates and times\] for a telephonic conferral. I propose to confer for 30 minutes. If we cannot resolve the deficiencies, I intend to file a motion to compel.  
\>  
\> Sincerely,  
\>  
\> \[Counsel\]  
  
\*\*Citation anchors.\*\* Fed. R. Civ. P. 33(a)(2), (b), (d); § 1692k(b)(1), (c); \*Bartlett v. Heibl\*, 128 F.3d 497; \*Hicks\*, 257 F.R.D. 699.
