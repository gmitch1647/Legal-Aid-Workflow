# FDCPA Discovery — Interrogatories to Creditors and Debt Buyers  
  
**Topic:** Plaintiff-side interrogatories to entities sued as "debt collectors" under the FDCPA — including creditors collecting "in a name other than" their own and debt buyers whose principal purpose is debt collection.  
**Primary authority:** Fair Debt Collection Practices Act, 15 U.S.C. §§ 1692–1692p; Regulation F, 12 C.F.R. Part 1006; Fed. R. Civ. P. 26, 33.  
**Key cases:** *Henson v. Santander Consumer USA Inc.*, 137 S. Ct. 1718 (2017); *Barbato v. Greystone Alliance, LLC*, 916 F.3d 260 (3d Cir. 2019); *Tepper v. Amos Fin., LLC*, 898 F.3d 364 (3d Cir. 2018); *McAdory v. M.N.S. & Assocs., LLC*, 952 F.3d 1089 (9th Cir. 2020); *Davidson v. Capital One Bank (USA), N.A.*, 797 F.3d 1309 (11th Cir. 2015).  
**Last updated:** May 16, 2026.  
  
---  
  
## 1. Doctrinal Framework  
  
Two distinct "debt collector" theories drive interrogatory scope under § 1692a(6):  
  
**(a) "In a name other than" exception, § 1692a(6).** A creditor who, in the process of collecting its own debts, uses any name other than its own that would indicate a third person is collecting or attempting to collect such debts, is a "debt collector." See *Maguire v. Citicorp Retail Servs., Inc.*, 147 F.3d 232, 235 (2d Cir. 1998). Interrogatories must develop the corporate identity used in dunning letters, on caller ID, and on payment instructions.  
  
**(b) Principal-purpose test post-*Henson*.** The Supreme Court held in *Henson* that a debt purchaser is not automatically a "debt collector" merely because it acquired defaulted debt for collection; courts must determine whether the entity's "principal purpose" is debt collection or whether it "regularly collects" debts "owed or due another." 137 S. Ct. at 1721–24. The Third Circuit's *Barbato* and *Tepper* decisions clarify that a debt buyer whose business model is to acquire defaulted debts and outsource collection still has the "principal purpose" of debt collection. 916 F.3d at 267; 898 F.3d at 371. The Ninth Circuit followed suit in *McAdory*, 952 F.3d at 1093.  
  
Interrogatories thus serve four discovery aims: (i) identify the entity and its alter egos; (ii) prove principal-purpose facts; (iii) document the chain of title and account-level paperwork relevant to validation under 12 C.F.R. § 1006.34; and (iv) develop Reg F § 1006.30 violations (passive collection, sale of paid debts).

## 2. Sample Interrogatories — TEMPLATE  
  
> **TEMPLATE — INTERROGATORIES TO DEFENDANT [Creditor/Debt Buyer]**  
> Pursuant to Fed. R. Civ. P. 33, Plaintiff requests that Defendant answer the following interrogatories under oath within thirty (30) days. "Account" means the alleged debt identified in paragraph __ of the Complaint. "Communication" has the meaning given in 15 U.S.C. § 1692a(2). "You" and "Your" mean Defendant and any parent, subsidiary, affiliate, agent, employee, attorney, or other person acting on Defendant's behalf.  
  
### A. Identification and Corporate Structure  
  
1. State Your full legal name, all assumed or fictitious names, every state of incorporation or formation, and Your principal place of business during the four years preceding the filing of the Complaint.  
2. Identify each parent, subsidiary, and affiliate that has had any role in the acquisition, servicing, collection, or litigation of the Account.  
3. Identify by name, title, and dates of employment every person who has had personal knowledge of the Account, including custodians of records.  
4. State whether You are or have been licensed as a debt collector in [forum state] during the relevant period; if so, identify each license by number and date.  
5. Identify each name, trade style, or d/b/a under which You communicated with Plaintiff, including names appearing on letterhead, envelopes, caller ID, voicemail, payment portals, or court pleadings. (Probes the § 1692a(6) "in a name other than" exception; see *Maguire*, 147 F.3d at 235.)  
  
### B. Principal-Purpose Analysis (Debt Buyers)  
  
6. For each of the four calendar years preceding the Complaint, state the total dollar volume and account count of consumer debts You acquired, broken down by whether the debt was in default at the time of acquisition.  
7. State Your gross revenue for each such year, broken down by source (e.g., collection on owned accounts, collection on third-party accounts, debt resale, other).  
8. Describe Your business model in sufficient detail to permit application of the *Henson* / *Barbato* "principal purpose" test, including the percentage of revenue attributable to debt collection from defaulted accounts.  
9. Identify every third-party collection agency, law firm, or servicer to whom You placed or assigned the Account or any other Plaintiff debt, and state the terms of compensation. (Goes to *Barbato* — outsourced collection does not defeat principal-purpose status.)  
10. State whether You have ever represented in any SEC filing, investor presentation, or marketing material that Your principal business is the acquisition or collection of consumer debt; identify each such representation.  
  
### C. Acquisition History — Chain of Title  
  
11. Identify by name and address every prior owner of the Account, in chronological order from the original creditor to You.  
12. For each transfer of the Account, state: (a) the date of transfer; (b) the consideration paid; (c) the form of the transfer instrument (bill of sale, assignment, electronic file transfer); and (d) the location of the executed transfer document.  
13. State the purchase price You paid for the Account, both as a per-account dollar figure and as a percentage of face value. (Relevant under *Crawford v. LVNV Funding, LLC*, 758 F.3d 1254 (11th Cir. 2014), and to bona fide error analysis under § 1692k(c).)  
14. State whether the Account was purchased as part of a larger portfolio; if so, state the portfolio size, vintage, asset class, and the master debt sale agreement reference number.  
15. Identify any representations and warranties made by the seller regarding the Account, including warranties as to: (a) accuracy of the balance; (b) absence of payment in full; (c) absence of bankruptcy discharge; (d) absence of statute-of-limitations expiration; and (e) consumer dispute history.  
  
### D. Account-Level Documentation Received  
  
16. Itemize every document You received from the seller relating specifically to the Account, including (without limitation) the application, terms and conditions, periodic statements, payment history, charge-off statement, and any prior dispute correspondence.  
17. State the date on which You received each such document.  
18. For each item of account-level documentation not received at the time of acquisition, state whether You have since obtained it, when, and from whom.  
19. Identify Your record-retention policy applicable to acquired consumer debt accounts during the relevant period.  
20. State whether You possess (or have ever possessed) the original signed account-opening document for the Account.  
  
### E. Communications with the Consumer  
  
21. Identify by date, time, channel (call, letter, email, text, voicemail, in-app message), and participant every Communication directed to or received from Plaintiff regarding the Account.  
22. For each outbound telephone call, state whether the call was recorded; if so, identify the custodian of the recording.  
23. For each dunning letter or written Communication, identify the template or form number used and the version control log entry.  
24. State whether any Communication occurred after You received written notice from Plaintiff disputing the debt under 15 U.S.C. § 1692g(b) or 12 C.F.R. § 1006.34(c); if so, describe.  
25. Identify every "limited-content message" left for Plaintiff under 12 C.F.R. § 1006.2(j) and state the script used.  
  
### F. Internal Procedures — Verification Before Collection  
  
26. Describe Your policies and procedures, in effect during the relevant period, for verifying the validity of consumer debts before initiating collection activity.  
27. State whether You performed an account-specific review of the Account before sending the first Communication; if so, identify the reviewer and the documents reviewed.  
28. Describe Your scrub procedures for: (a) deceased consumers; (b) active bankruptcies; (c) debts beyond the statute of limitations; (d) servicemembers under the SCRA; and (e) debts subject to prior payment-in-full.  
29. State whether Your procedures distinguish between accounts purchased with full account-level documentation and accounts purchased with summary data only.  
30. Identify each audit, internal or external, of Your debt-validation procedures during the relevant period.  
  
### G. Regulation F § 1006.34 Validation Notice Procedures  
  
31. Identify the validation notice sent to Plaintiff under 12 C.F.R. § 1006.34, including its date, version, and delivery method.  
32. State whether the validation notice contained each of the "validation information" items required by 12 C.F.R. § 1006.34(c)(2), including the itemization date, the itemization-date balance, fees and interest accrued since the itemization date, payments and credits since the itemization date, and the current balance.  
33. State which "itemization date" option under § 1006.34(b)(3) You used (last statement date, charge-off date, last payment date, transaction date, or judgment date) and why.  
34. Describe Your procedures for responding to consumer disputes received during the validation period under § 1006.34(c)(3) and § 1006.38.  
35. State whether You ceased collection upon receipt of a written dispute, as required by 15 U.S.C. § 1692g(b), and identify any exception applied.  
  
### H. Regulation F § 1006.30 — Passive Collection and Sale of Paid Debts  
  
36. State whether You have furnished information about the Account to any consumer reporting agency before having any Communication with Plaintiff in compliance with 12 C.F.R. § 1006.30(a).  
37. Describe Your procedures for ensuring that the Account is not sold, transferred, or placed for collection if it has been paid, settled, discharged in bankruptcy, or is the subject of an identity-theft report, as required by 12 C.F.R. § 1006.30(b).  
38. State whether You have ever sold, transferred, or placed for collection a debt that was paid in full, settled, discharged, or the subject of an identity-theft report; if so, describe the circumstances and remedial action.  
  
### I. Bona Fide Error Defense Procedures  
  
39. Identify each policy, procedure, training program, and audit upon which You intend to rely to establish the bona fide error defense under 15 U.S.C. § 1692k(c).  
40. Describe each step You took to maintain procedures "reasonably adapted" to avoid the violations alleged in the Complaint. (See *Jerman v. Carlisle, McNellie, Rini, Kramer & Ulrich LPA*, 559 U.S. 573 (2010) — bona fide error does not cover legal mistakes.)  
41. Identify the person(s) responsible for compliance oversight during the relevant period.  
  
### J. State-Court Litigation Procedures  
  
42. Identify every state-court collection action You (or an entity acting on Your behalf) filed against Plaintiff and every other consumer in [forum state] during the relevant period.  
43. Describe Your standard practice for verifying the statute of limitations before filing suit; identify the procedure version in effect when the Complaint was filed.  
44. State whether You filed any proof of claim against Plaintiff in bankruptcy; if so, identify the case and the documentation supporting the claim. (See *Midland Funding, LLC v. Johnson*, 137 S. Ct. 1407 (2017) — but note pre- and post-*Johnson* circuit splits on FDCPA liability for time-barred POCs.)  
45. Identify counsel of record in each such collection action.

## 3. Practice Notes  
  
- **Numerical limits.** Under Fed. R. Civ. P. 33(a)(1), parties are limited to 25 interrogatories, "including all discrete subparts," without leave of court. Stipulate to expansion at the Rule 26(f) conference, or front-load the most critical subjects (principal purpose; chain of title; validation notice).  
- **Verification.** Insist on verification under Rule 33(b)(3) by an officer with personal knowledge — debt-buyer 30(b)(6) designees often lack first-hand knowledge of seller-side facts; the verification preserves attack lines.  
- **Coordinate with RFPs.** Pair interrogatories 11–20 with the RFP set in `fdcpa_disc_06_RFPs_to_Creditors_DebtBuyers.md` to obtain the underlying chain-of-title documents.  
- **Proportionality.** Under Rule 26(b)(1), be ready to defend principal-purpose interrogatories with the *Henson*/*Barbato* framework; courts have allowed broad inquiry into business model. See, e.g., *Barbato*, 916 F.3d at 267.
