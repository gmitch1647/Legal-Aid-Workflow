\# Interrogatories to Debt Collectors  
  
\*\*Topic:\*\* Plaintiff interrogatories to debt collectors under Fed. R. Civ. P. 33  
\*\*Primary statutes:\*\* 15 U.S.C. §§ 1692a-1692p; 12 C.F.R. Part 1006 (Reg F)  
\*\*Reg F cross-references:\*\* §§ 1006.2, 1006.6, 1006.14, 1006.18, 1006.22, 1006.26, 1006.30, 1006.34, 1006.38, 1006.42  
\*\*Procedural authority:\*\* Fed. R. Civ. P. 33 (25-interrogatory limit absent leave); local rules vary  
\*\*Last updated:\*\* May 16, 2026  
  
\---  
  
\#\# 1. Strategic Use of Interrogatories  
  
Interrogatories in FDCPA cases work best for (1) identifying corporate structure, custodians, and systems; (2) flushing out the defense's bona fide error theory; (3) pinning the defense to a specific Reg F compliance posture; (4) identifying the class population in class cases; and (5) authenticating documents. They are inefficient for narrative discovery and should not duplicate RFAs.  
  
Rule 33's 25-interrogatory cap (with discrete subparts counted) requires triage. The templates below are organized by topic; you will not use all of them in one case. Always request leave to exceed 25 in complex/class cases — district courts routinely grant leave on stipulation. \*See, e.g.,\* \*Vinton v. Adam Aircraft Indus., Inc.\*, 232 F.R.D. 650 (D. Colo. 2005).  
  
Cases cited throughout: \*Heintz v. Jenkins\*, 514 U.S. 291 (1995); \*Jerman v. Carlisle, McNellie, Rini, Kramer & Ulrich LPA\*, 559 U.S. 573 (2010); \*Henson v. Santander Consumer USA Inc.\*, 137 S. Ct. 1718 (2017); \*Obduskey v. McCarthy & Holthus LLP\*, 139 S. Ct. 1029 (2019) (nonjudicial foreclosure activity is largely outside FDCPA principal-purpose definition); \*Rotkiske v. Klemm\*, 140 S. Ct. 355 (2019) (one-year SOL runs from violation, not discovery); \*Avila v. Riexinger & Assocs.\*, 817 F.3d 72 (2d Cir. 2016); \*Foti v. NCO Fin. Sys.\*, 424 F. Supp. 2d 643 (S.D.N.Y. 2006); \*Bartlett v. Heibl\*, 128 F.3d 497 (7th Cir. 1997); \*Douglass v. Convergent Outsourcing\*, 765 F.3d 299 (3d Cir. 2014); \*Clomon v. Jackson\*, 988 F.2d 1314 (2d Cir. 1993); \*Miller v. Wolpoff & Abramson, L.L.P.\*, 321 F.3d 292 (2d Cir. 2003); \*Kort v. Diversified Collection Servs.\*, 394 F.3d 530 (7th Cir. 2005); \*Johnson v. Riddle\*, 443 F.3d 723 (10th Cir. 2006); \*Reichert v. Nat'l Credit Sys.\*, 531 F.3d 1002 (9th Cir. 2008); \*Edwards v. Niagara Credit Solutions\*, 584 F.3d 1350 (11th Cir. 2009); \*Midland Funding v. Johnson\*, 137 S. Ct. 1407 (2017); \*TransUnion LLC v. Ramirez\*, 594 U.S. 413 (2021); \*Hunstein v. Preferred Collection\*, 48 F.4th 1236 (11th Cir. 2022) (en banc).

\#\# 2. Definitions and Instructions (place at the front of any set)  
  
\`\`\`  
TEMPLATE — DEFINITIONS  
  
1\. "You," "your," and "Defendant" refer to \[Collector\], any predecessor or  
 successor in interest, any parent, subsidiary, or affiliate, and any  
 employees, agents, attorneys, and vendors acting on Defendant's behalf.  
2\. "Account" means the alleged debt of Plaintiff identified as \[account number\].  
3\. "Communication" has the meaning in 15 U.S.C. § 1692a(2).  
4\. "Letter" means any written collection correspondence sent to Plaintiff,  
 including the \[date\] letter attached as Exhibit A.  
5\. "CMS" means Defendant's collection-management system(s) of record.  
6\. "Reg F" means 12 C.F.R. Part 1006.  
7\. "Identify" when used with respect to a person, means state the name, last  
 known address, telephone number, and employer and job title; when used  
 with respect to a document, state the date, author, recipients, type, and  
 present custodian.  
8\. "Relevant period" means \[date 1 year before first communication\] to the  
 present, unless otherwise specified.  
  
Instructions: Pursuant to Rule 33(d), you may produce business records in  
lieu of answering when the burden of deriving the answer is substantially  
the same for either party, provided you specify the records with particularity.  
Supplement under Rule 26(e).

\#\# 3. Topic A — General / Identification (5-8 interrogatories)  
  
\`\`\`  
INTERROGATORY A-1. Identify Defendant by full legal name, all d/b/a names,  
state of incorporation, principal place of business, and registered agent for  
service of process in each state in which Defendant conducted collection  
activity during the relevant period.  
  
Annotation: Establishes "debt collector" status under § 1692a(6) and provides  
the platform for personal-jurisdiction analysis.  
  
INTERROGATORY A-2. Identify each parent, subsidiary, and affiliate that  
participated, directly or indirectly, in collection activity directed at  
Plaintiff or that owns or maintains records concerning the Account.  
  
Annotation: Surfaces the corporate web that often hides the true principal —  
e.g., master servicer vs. sub-servicer. Important for "principal purpose"  
analysis post-Henson.  
  
INTERROGATORY A-3. State whether Defendant is licensed as a debt collector  
in \[Plaintiff's state\] and identify the license number, dates of issuance,  
and any conditions or limitations.  
  
INTERROGATORY A-4. Describe Defendant's business model with respect to  
consumer debt, including (a) whether Defendant is a third-party collector,  
debt buyer, or both; (b) the volume of accounts placed or owned during the  
relevant period; and (c) the type(s) of debt collected (credit card,  
medical, auto, student, telecom, utility, judgment, other).  
  
Annotation: Distinguishes Henson "purchaser" from § 1692a(6) "principal purpose"  
collector. Frames whether the FDCPA applies at all to the named entity.  
  
INTERROGATORY A-5. Identify the corporate officer(s) responsible for FDCPA  
compliance during the relevant period, by name, title, dates, and reporting  
chain.  
  
INTERROGATORY A-6. Identify all consent orders, cease-and-desist orders,  
deferred prosecution agreements, or similar regulatory resolutions to which  
Defendant has been subject during the past five years (CFPB, FTC, state AG,  
state regulator), and the docket number or matter number for each.  
  
Annotation: CFPB consent orders are public; this interrogatory locks the  
defense to its acknowledged regulatory history.  
  
INTERROGATORY A-7. Identify the CMS(s) used by Defendant during the relevant  
period and state, for each, the vendor, version, dates in use, and the  
custodian of records.  
  
INTERROGATORY A-8. State Defendant's net worth as of the date of the most  
recent audited financial statement, and identify the statement.  
  
Annotation: For class cases, § 1692k(a)(2)(B). Subject to protective order.

\#\# 4. Topic B — The Specific Account (8-10 interrogatories)  
  
\`\`\`  
INTERROGATORY B-1. State the date Defendant acquired the Account (by purchase,  
placement, or otherwise), and identify the assignor or placement source by  
name and address.  
  
INTERROGATORY B-2. Describe the chain of title from the original creditor  
to Defendant, identifying every prior owner or holder and the date of each  
transfer. For each transfer, identify the bill of sale and any account-level  
data file delivered.  
  
Annotation: Critical for debt buyers. § 1692e(2) liability often flows from  
chain-of-title gaps. See Midland Funding v. Johnson, 137 S. Ct. 1407 (2017),  
for context-specific FDCPA application to stale debts.  
  
INTERROGATORY B-3. State the principal balance, accrued interest, fees, and  
total balance at the time Defendant acquired the Account.  
  
INTERROGATORY B-4. State the date of last payment and the date of charge-off  
or default as reported to Defendant by the prior owner.  
  
INTERROGATORY B-5. Identify every "itemization date" considered by Defendant  
under 12 C.F.R. § 1006.34(b)(3) for the validation notice sent to Plaintiff,  
and the basis for selecting the date used.  
  
INTERROGATORY B-6. State the date and amount of every charge, fee, interest  
accrual, or other addition to the balance after Defendant acquired the  
Account, and identify the contractual or statutory authority for each.  
  
Annotation: Tests § 1692f(1) (unauthorized fees) and Avila § 1692e(2)  
(failure to disclose accruing balance).  
  
INTERROGATORY B-7. Identify each communication Defendant directed to  
Plaintiff concerning the Account by date, time, mode (call, letter, email,  
text, voicemail), and CMS reference number.  
  
INTERROGATORY B-8. Identify each dispute, request for verification, or  
cease-and-desist communication received from Plaintiff (or anyone purporting  
to act on Plaintiff's behalf) concerning the Account by date, mode, and  
recipient.  
  
INTERROGATORY B-9. Describe Defendant's response to each dispute, request  
for verification, or cease-and-desist communication identified in  
Interrogatory B-8, including the date Defendant ceased or resumed collection.  
  
Annotation: Tests § 1692g(b) and § 1692c(c) compliance.  
  
INTERROGATORY B-10. State whether Defendant filed or threatened to file  
suit on the Account, and if so, identify the date, court, case number,  
and signing attorney.  
  
Annotation: Combined with Topic K below, sets up time-barred-debt  
(Reg F § 1006.26) and venue (§ 1692i) analysis.

\#\# 5. Topic C — Communications History (5-10 interrogatories)  
  
\`\`\`  
INTERROGATORY C-1. For each telephone call between Defendant (or its agents,  
employees, or vendors) and Plaintiff or any other person at Plaintiff's  
number(s) during the relevant period, state: (a) date and time; (b) inbound  
or outbound; (c) the number dialed and the number from which the call  
originated; (d) the name and employee ID of any agent participating;  
(e) the disposition code applied and its meaning; (f) the duration; and  
(g) the CMS notes entered following the call.  
  
Annotation: Use Rule 33(d) and demand the call log in native format with a  
field dictionary. This is one of the most-disputed interrogatories; meet-and-  
confer toward a CSV production with metadata.  
  
INTERROGATORY C-2. For each written communication (letter, email, text)  
sent to Plaintiff or any other person concerning the Account, state:  
(a) date sent; (b) mode; (c) recipient name and address/email/number;  
(d) the template ID or version used; (e) the variables/merge fields  
populated; (f) the sender of record; and (g) the print vendor or e-delivery  
vendor.  
  
INTERROGATORY C-3. State whether Defendant left any voicemail for Plaintiff,  
and if so, identify each by date, time, number called, and the script or  
template used.  
  
Annotation: Foti-style voicemail claims (424 F. Supp. 2d 643) — failure to  
provide mini-Miranda or "limited content" message under Reg F § 1006.2(j).  
  
INTERROGATORY C-4. State whether Defendant sent any "limited content  
message" as defined by 12 C.F.R. § 1006.2(j) to Plaintiff or to a third  
party concerning Plaintiff, and identify each by date, recipient, and  
script.  
  
INTERROGATORY C-5. Identify every third party contacted concerning the  
Account, by name, relationship to Plaintiff, date, mode of contact, and  
purpose, and state the script or talking points used.  
  
Annotation: § 1692b location-information rules and § 1692c(b) third-party  
prohibition.  
  
INTERROGATORY C-6. State whether any communication concerning the Account  
was sent to or routed through any vendor (letter vendor, mail house, dialer  
vendor, BPO), and identify the vendor, the data transferred, the contract,  
and the data-protection obligations.  
  
Annotation: Post-Hunstein, vendor disclosures remain a litigated issue.  
  
INTERROGATORY C-7. Identify every communication received by Defendant from  
Plaintiff, including telephone calls, returned mail, postal forwarding,  
e-mails, texts, and physical visits.  
  
INTERROGATORY C-8. State whether and when Defendant learned that Plaintiff  
was represented by counsel concerning the Account, the source of that  
information, the CMS field in which it was recorded, and any communications  
sent after that date.  
  
Annotation: § 1692c(a)(2).

\#\# 6. Topic D — Call Recordings (3-5 interrogatories)  
  
\`\`\`  
INTERROGATORY D-1. Describe Defendant's call-recording infrastructure,  
including the vendor and product, the percentage of calls recorded, the  
trigger for recording (all calls vs. event-based), the format(s) of  
recordings, and the metadata stored with each recording.  
  
INTERROGATORY D-2. State the retention period for call recordings during  
the relevant period and identify any change to the retention period since  
the date of Plaintiff's first communication.  
  
Annotation: Spoliation hook. If retention dropped after filing, set up a  
Rule 37(e) motion.  
  
INTERROGATORY D-3. State whether all calls between Defendant and Plaintiff  
identified in Interrogatory C-1 were recorded, and if any recording is no  
longer accessible, state the date of deletion and the reason.  
  
INTERROGATORY D-4. Identify the person or persons responsible for the  
preservation of call recordings concerning Plaintiff after Defendant became  
aware of Plaintiff's dispute or litigation hold.  
  
INTERROGATORY D-5. Describe the process to retrieve a call recording from  
Defendant's system, including the typical time required and the personnel  
needed.

\#\# 7. Topic E — Letter Approval and Creation (5-8 interrogatories)  
  
\`\`\`  
INTERROGATORY E-1. Identify the letter template used to generate the letter  
attached as Exhibit A, by template ID, version, and effective dates.  
  
INTERROGATORY E-2. Identify each person who drafted, edited, reviewed, or  
approved the template referenced in E-1, by name, title, role, and date of  
participation.  
  
INTERROGATORY E-3. State whether the template referenced in E-1 was reviewed  
by counsel (in-house or outside), and identify the reviewing attorney by  
name, firm, and date of review. Do not disclose the substance of legal  
advice; identify the fact of review only.  
  
Annotation: Walks the line on privilege — the fact of review is not  
privileged. See Upjohn Co. v. United States, 449 U.S. 383 (1981).  
  
INTERROGATORY E-4. Identify each person who signed the letter referenced  
in Exhibit A, and state whether the signatory personally reviewed the  
specific letter (as opposed to the template) before dispatch.  
  
Annotation: Clomon v. Jackson, 988 F.2d 1314 (2d Cir. 1993); Miller v.  
Wolpoff & Abramson, 321 F.3d 292 (2d Cir. 2003) — meaningful attorney  
involvement.  
  
INTERROGATORY E-5. Describe the workflow by which the template at E-1 is  
merged with consumer-specific data and dispatched, including every system  
involved and every quality-control step.  
  
INTERROGATORY E-6. Identify every other letter template Defendant used to  
collect debts of the same type as Plaintiff's Account during the relevant  
period, by template ID and effective dates.  
  
Annotation: Class hook — Foti, Bartlett — but limit to "same type" to keep  
relevance defensible.  
  
INTERROGATORY E-7. State the total number of letters in the template at  
E-1 that Defendant sent to consumers in the relevant period, broken down  
by month and by state.  
  
INTERROGATORY E-8. Identify any changes made to the template at E-1 during  
or after the relevant period and the reason for each change.

the source of verification, and the date.  
  
INTERROGATORY F-6. Describe Defendant's procedures under 12 C.F.R. § 1006.38  
for handling disputes, including cessation of collection and resumption only

where it is "inconvenient" to contact a consumer under § 1692c(a)(1) and

INTERROGATORY G-2. Describe Defendant's procedures for honoring requests

INTERROGATORY G-6. Describe Defendant's procedures for ensuring that  
communications with third parties are limited to location information  
permitted by § 1692b and that Defendant does not communicate with third  
parties more than once except as permitted.  
  
INTERROGATORY G-7. Describe Defendant's procedures for determining whether  
a consumer is represented by an attorney and for routing communications  
accordingly under § 1692c(a)(2).

\#\# 10. Topic H — Reg F § 1006.14 Call Frequency / 7-in-7 (4-5 interrogatories)  
  
\`\`\`  
INTERROGATORY H-1. Describe Defendant's procedures for compliance with  
12 C.F.R. § 1006.14(b), including the "7-in-7" presumption (seven calls in  
seven consecutive days; one call within seven days after a telephone  
conversation).  
  
INTERROGATORY H-2. State, for each consecutive seven-day period during  
which Defendant attempted contact with Plaintiff, the number of telephone  
call placements concerning the Account, distinguishing between attempts  
(no answer / busy / voicemail) and connected calls.  
  
INTERROGATORY H-3. State whether Defendant's CMS or dialer system enforces  
a 7-in-7 cap as a system control, and if so, describe the configuration  
and any overrides.  
  
INTERROGATORY H-4. Identify the person(s) responsible for monitoring call  
frequency for compliance with § 1006.14 and describe the audit cadence.  
  
INTERROGATORY H-5. State whether Defendant claims any safe-harbor under  
§ 1006.14(b)(2) (frequency presumption) or any of the exceptions in  
§ 1006.14(b)(4) with respect to calls to Plaintiff.

\#\# 11. Topic I — Bona Fide Error Defense (8-12 interrogatories) — Centerpiece  
  
\`\`\`  
INTERROGATORY I-1. State whether Defendant intends to rely on the bona fide  
error defense at 15 U.S.C. § 1692k(c), and if so, identify each specific  
violation as to which Defendant asserts the defense.  
  
INTERROGATORY I-2. Identify, by Bates range or specific title and date,  
every written policy or procedure Defendant contends satisfies the  
"procedures reasonably adapted to avoid any such error" prong of § 1692k(c)  
with respect to each violation identified in I-1.  
  
Annotation: Locks the defense to a closed universe of documents. Defense  
cannot later claim "additional procedures" not identified here.  
  
INTERROGATORY I-3. For each policy or procedure identified in I-2, state  
the date of adoption, every revision date, the author, and the approver.  
  
INTERROGATORY I-4. Describe each training program (live, computer-based,  
or otherwise) Defendant contends supports its § 1692k(c) defense, including  
title, length, audience, frequency, and the dates of completion by the  
agents who handled the Account.  
  
INTERROGATORY I-5. Describe every audit or quality-assurance review performed  
by Defendant during the relevant period that touches the type of conduct at  
issue in this case, including the audit scope, the findings, and the  
corrective actions taken.  
  
INTERROGATORY I-6. State whether Defendant or any person on its behalf has  
identified the root cause of the specific violation alleged in this case,  
and if so, describe the root cause, the date determined, and any corrective  
action.  
  
Annotation: Johnson v. Riddle, 443 F.3d 723 — procedures must be reasonably  
adapted to avoid the specific error.  
  
INTERROGATORY I-7. State whether Defendant has received any consumer  
complaint (regulator, lawsuit, demand letter, or written complaint to  
Defendant) concerning the same or substantially similar conduct as that  
alleged in this case during the past three years, and if so, identify each  
complaint by date, complainant, and disposition.  
  
Annotation: Reichert, 531 F.3d 1002; Edwards, 584 F.3d 1350 — recurrence  
undercuts BFE.  
  
INTERROGATORY I-8. Identify any vendor on which Defendant relies for any  
element of its § 1692k(c) defense, the contract with the vendor, and the  
SLAs and QA procedures applicable to the vendor.  
  
INTERROGATORY I-9. State whether Defendant contends that its § 1692k(c)  
defense rests in whole or in part on a mistake of law, and if not, confirm  
that all asserted errors are factual or clerical.  
  
Annotation: Jerman, 559 U.S. 573 — mistake of law is unavailable. Forces  
defense to identify factual basis or waive BFE.  
  
INTERROGATORY I-10. Identify each person who, at the time of the violation,  
was responsible for the procedures relied upon, by name, title, dates of  
service, and current contact information.  
  
INTERROGATORY I-11. State whether the procedures relied upon were certified,  
accredited, or reviewed by any third party (e.g., ACA International,  
Receivables Management Association International), and identify any such  
review.  
  
INTERROGATORY I-12. State whether Defendant contends that the conduct alleged  
in this case was performed by an agent acting outside the scope of authority  
or against express direction, and identify all facts supporting that  
contention.  
  
Annotation: Sets up the "rogue employee" defense for early elimination.

SOL applicable to debts in Defendant's portfolio under 12 C.F.R. § 1006.26.  
  
INTERROGATORY J-3. Identify the CMS data fields used to determine the SOL

fields.  
  
INTERROGATORY J-4. State whether Defendant has filed or threatened to file

INTERROGATORY J-5. State whether Defendant provided any time-barred-debt  
disclosure to Plaintiff and identify the language and date.  
  
INTERROGATORY J-6. Describe Defendant's procedures for handling a debt  
where the SOL is uncertain or the trigger date is missing from the  
account-level data file.

\#\# 15. Topic M — Subscribers to ACDV / e-OSCAR (4 interrogatories)  
  
\`\`\`  
INTERROGATORY M-1. State whether Defendant furnishes information about the  
Account to any consumer reporting agency, and if so, identify each CRA, the  
furnishing start and end dates, and the channel (Metro 2, e-OSCAR, other).  
  
INTERROGATORY M-2. State whether Defendant is a subscriber to e-OSCAR or  
any equivalent ACDV system, and identify the user account(s) used to handle  
disputes concerning the Account.

\#\# 18. Common Defense Objections and Responses  
  
\- \*\*"Compound" / "discrete subparts"\*\*: be ready to redraft to reduce subparts.  
\- \*\*"Vague"\*\*: define key terms upfront, as in the template above.  
\- \*\*"Trade secret"\*\*: respond with a stipulated protective order; the FDCPA cannot be enforced without the template library, script, and CMS data dictionary.  
\- \*\*"Privileged"\*\*: identify the \*fact\* of attorney review without compelling content. \*Upjohn\*.  
\- \*\*"Overbroad as to class"\*\*: in individual cases, narrow to the consumer's account; in class cases, cite \*Foti\* and the form-letter line of authority.
