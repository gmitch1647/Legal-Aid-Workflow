\# FDCPA Discovery: Rule 30(b)(6) Deposition Topics for Debt Collectors  
  
\*\*Topic:\*\* Rule 30(b)(6) deposition notice topics for third-party debt collectors in FDCPA litigation  
\*\*Cross-references:\*\* Fed. R. Civ. P. 30(b)(6); 15 U.S.C. §§ 1692–1692p; 12 C.F.R. Part 1006 (Regulation F); CFPB Bulletins; FTC staff commentary  
\*\*Last updated:\*\* May 16, 2026  
\*\*Source discipline:\*\* Public-domain primary sources only (statute, regulation, published federal opinions, CFPB/FTC guidance)  
  
\---  
  
\#\# Overview  
  
Rule 30(b)(6) permits a party to depose an organization on enumerated topics; the organization must designate one or more witnesses to testify about "information known or reasonably available to the organization." Fed. R. Civ. P. 30(b)(6). The 2020 amendments require the parties to "confer in good faith about the matters for examination." \*Id.\* In FDCPA cases, 30(b)(6) testimony anchors strict-liability claims under §§ 1692c–1692f, develops the elements of the bona fide error defense under § 1692k(c), and surfaces evidence on Regulation F compliance. Counsel should serve a topic list narrow enough to survive an Rule 26(b)(1) proportionality objection yet broad enough to reach institutional knowledge about scripts, systems, and policies that no individual custodian alone can authenticate.  
  
The topics below assume a typical individual or putative class action against a third-party debt collector. Each topic carries (1) suggested notice language, (2) annotation on discoverable scope, (3) likely designee role, (4) foundation and admissions to elicit, and (5) statutory/regulatory and case-law anchors.

\#\# A. The Specific Consumer's Account and Communications  
  
\*\*TEMPLATE — Topic A:\*\*  
\> "All facts concerning the account identified by Defendant as belonging to Plaintiff \[name\], internal account number \[\_\_\_\_\], from the date the account was placed with Defendant through the present, including without limitation: (i) the date of placement and the placing entity; (ii) all data fields received at placement; (iii) all communications by or to Defendant concerning the account, whether by mail, telephone, electronic mail, text message, or any other medium; (iv) all account notes, collector notes, and system entries; (v) all payments, credits, charges, and fee assessments; (vi) all disputes received and Defendant's response; and (vii) the present status of the account."  
  
\*\*Discoverable scope.\*\* This is the foundational topic. It covers the "account file" or "collection log" maintained in the collector's system of record (typically Latitude, CUBS, FACS, or Columbia Ultimate). Courts routinely compel production of complete account notes in FDCPA cases. \*See, e.g., Donaldson v. Pharmacia Pension Plan\*, 2006 WL 1308582 (S.D. Ill. May 10, 2006) (discovery of account records); \*Sambor v. Omnia Credit Servs., Inc.\*, 183 F.R.D. 472 (D.S.C. 1998).  
  
\*\*Likely designee.\*\* Operations manager or collection floor supervisor; supplemented by IT custodian for system metadata.  
  
\*\*Foundation/admissions.\*\* Authenticate the account log; admit each communication's date, recipient, and method; admit the substance of every script or template used. Establish absence of any pre-suit validation or dispute resolution if applicable.  
  
\*\*Anchors.\*\* 15 U.S.C. § 1692g (validation); § 1692e (false or misleading representations); § 1692f (unfair practices); 12 C.F.R. § 1006.30(b) (record retention — three years from last communication or attempted communication).

\#\# B. Form Letters and Template Communications  
  
\*\*TEMPLATE — Topic B:\*\*  
\> "All facts concerning Defendant's library of form, template, or model written communications sent to consumers from \[date\] to present, including: (i) the identity of each form letter (by template ID, version, and effective dates); (ii) the persons or committees who drafted, reviewed, or approved each form; (iii) the legal review (if any) performed before deployment; (iv) the volume of each form sent during the relevant period; (v) the criteria triggering use of each form; and (vi) any revisions to each form and the reasons therefor."  
  
\*\*Discoverable scope.\*\* Form-letter libraries are the heart of FDCPA litigation because liability turns on the "least sophisticated consumer" standard applied to standardized text. \*Avila v. Riexinger & Assocs., LLC\*, 817 F.3d 72, 75 (2d Cir. 2016); \*Bartlett v. Heibl\*, 128 F.3d 497, 500 (7th Cir. 1997); \*Jacobson v. Healthcare Fin. Servs., Inc.\*, 516 F.3d 85, 90 (2d Cir. 2008). Courts have allowed discovery into all consumers who received the same form. \*See Hicks v. Client Servs., Inc.\*, 257 F.R.D. 699 (S.D. Fla. 2009).  
  
\*\*Likely designee.\*\* Compliance officer or letter-vendor liaison; legal/compliance counsel if approval workflow is in-house.
