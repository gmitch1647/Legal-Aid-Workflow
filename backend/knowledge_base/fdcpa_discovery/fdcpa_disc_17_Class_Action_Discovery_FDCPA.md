\# Class-Action Discovery in FDCPA Cases  
  
| Field | Value |  
|---|---|  
| \*\*Topic\*\* | Discovery strategy specific to FDCPA Rule 23 class actions |  
| \*\*Primary Authority\*\* | Fed. R. Civ. P. 23; 15 U.S.C. §§ 1692k(a)(2)(B), 1692k(b); 28 U.S.C. § 1715 (CAFA) |  
| \*\*Cross-References\*\* | \`fdcpa\_disc\_16\` (general case law), \`fdcpa\_disc\_19\` (ESI) |  
| \*\*Last Updated\*\* | May 16, 2026 |  
  
\> FDCPA class actions are almost always built on a uniform written communication (a form letter, validation notice, or scripted disclosure) or a uniform automated practice (auto-dialer pattern, software-generated dunning sequence). The discovery strategy follows: extract the uniform artifact, prove its circulation, and develop class-wide concrete harm.  
  
\---  
  
\#\# 1. Pre-cert vs. post-cert discovery — sequencing  
  
\#\#\# Pre-certification  
  
The dominant practice is to bifurcate, focusing pre-cert discovery on Rule 23 elements:  
\- form letter / script / template \*\*content\*\* (every variant in use during the class period);  
\- \*\*circulation\*\* (numerosity — how many sent, where, when);  
\- the \*\*mechanism\*\* by which letters are generated (template database, mail-merge system, vendor relationship);  
\- the \*\*approval\*\* workflow (compliance review, legal sign-off);  
\- \*\*net worth\*\* for damages cap purposes (§ 1692k(a)(2)(B)).  
  
Pre-cert discovery should \*not\* be limited to the named plaintiff's individual claim — many courts permit reasonable class-wide discovery before certification, recognizing that Rule 23 findings require an evidentiary record. Plaintiffs should resist defense efforts to confine pre-cert discovery to the named plaintiff's file.  
  
\#\#\# Post-certification  
  
After certification (or denial), discovery widens to class-wide damages, defenses to specific subgroups, and any individualized issues the court has flagged. If decertification motions loom, expect renewed discovery into individualized harm/standing under \*TransUnion\*.  
  
\---  
  
\#\# 2. Rule 23(a) and 23(b)(3) — what to chase in discovery  
  
\#\#\# Numerosity — Rule 23(a)(1)  
  
For FDCPA form-letter classes, numerosity is proven by \*\*circulation data\*\*: how many copies of the offending letter were mailed during the class period, to how many unique consumers, and in what geographic distribution. Targeted discovery:  
  
\- Mail-house production logs (often a third-party letter vendor — subpoena it).  
\- Print-spool data, mail-merge logs, mail counts by SKU/template ID.  
\- USPS NCOA records or mailer affidavits.  
\- The collector's internal "letters generated" report by template code.  
  
A single deposition of the IT or print-services manager often produces the per-template circulation number that drives numerosity. Plaintiffs typically meet Rule 23(a)(1) with circulation of just 40+ consumers in most circuits, often much fewer.  
  
\#\#\# Commonality — Rule 23(a)(2) post-\*Dukes\*  
  
After \*Wal-Mart Stores, Inc. v. Dukes\*, 564 U.S. 338 (2011), commonality requires a question whose resolution will "drive resolution of the litigation." For an FDCPA form-letter class, the common question is whether the letter as written violates §§ 1692e, 1692f, or 1692g. Discovery should establish (a) \*\*uniformity of text\*\* (every recipient received the same operative language) and (b) \*\*uniformity of context\*\* (no triggering variant by debt type, state, or consumer subclass).  
  
\#\#\# Typicality — Rule 23(a)(3)  
  
The named plaintiff's claim must be \*\*typical\*\*. Discovery considerations:  
\- The named plaintiff received the same form letter / script.  
\- The named plaintiff is not subject to a unique defense (statute of limitations, individual settlement, arbitration agreement). The collector will probe these aggressively.  
\- Defendants commonly seek discovery into the plaintiff's prior litigation history, bankruptcy filings, and unrelated debt status to defeat typicality — plaintiffs should resist as disproportionate (Rule 26(b)(1)) unless directly relevant.  
  
\#\#\# Adequacy — Rule 23(a)(4)  
  
Both the plaintiff and class counsel must be adequate. The defense routinely deposes the named plaintiff on (a) understanding of the case, (b) absence of conflicts, (c) commitment to vigorously prosecute. Plaintiffs should produce class-counsel firm résumés, prior class-action appointments, and litigation-fund or fee-sharing arrangements.  
  
\#\#\# Predominance — Rule 23(b)(3)  
  
For statutory-damages classes, predominance is usually straightforward: the same form letter to every member yields the same liability finding and mechanical § 1692k(a)(2)(B) damages calculation. Plaintiffs should preempt the defense argument that individualized \*\*concrete harm\*\* issues post-\*TransUnion\* defeat predominance — see Section 4 below.
