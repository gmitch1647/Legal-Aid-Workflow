\# FDCPA Discovery: Rule 30(b)(6) Deposition Topics for Debt Buyers  
  
\*\*Topic:\*\* Rule 30(b)(6) deposition notice topics tailored to debt buyers (e.g., Encore/Midland, Portfolio Recovery Associates, LVNV/Resurgent)  
\*\*Cross-references:\*\* Fed. R. Civ. P. 30(b)(6); 15 U.S.C. §§ 1692a(4), 1692a(6), 1692e, 1692f, 1692g; 12 C.F.R. Part 1006; CFPB consent orders against major debt buyers  
\*\*Last updated:\*\* May 16, 2026  
\*\*Source discipline:\*\* Public-domain primary sources only (statute, regulation, published federal opinions, CFPB enforcement materials)

\#\# Overview  
  
Debt buyers occupy a distinct posture in FDCPA litigation. After \*Henson v. Santander Consumer USA Inc.\*, 582 U.S. 79 (2017), a purchaser collecting on debts it owns is not a "debt collector" under the third clause of § 1692a(6) ("regularly collects . . . debts owed or due another"). Coverage instead turns on the "principal purpose" clause of § 1692a(6) — whether the entity's principal purpose is the collection of any debts. The Third Circuit's decisions in \*Barbato v. Greystone Alliance, LLC\*, 916 F.3d 260 (3d Cir. 2019), and \*Tepper v. Amos Financial, LLC\*, 898 F.3d 364 (3d Cir. 2018), hold that a debt buyer whose business is to acquire defaulted receivables for collection satisfies the principal-purpose clause even if it outsources collection to third parties. Other circuits have largely aligned. \*See McAdory v. M.N.S. & Assocs., LLC\*, 952 F.3d 1089 (9th Cir. 2020).  
  
The discovery program against a debt buyer therefore differs from that against a third-party collector in three respects: (1) corporate-coverage discovery is itself a contested issue; (2) chain-of-title and account-level documentation are central, because the debt buyer typically receives only a thin data tape from the seller; and (3) the debt buyer's collection vendors — both call centers and law firms — are themselves typically debt collectors whose conduct may be imputed.  
  
The topics below assume an action against a debt-buyer defendant in federal district court.

\#\# A. Corporate Structure and Business Model — Principal Purpose  
  
\*\*TEMPLATE — Topic A:\*\*  
\> "All facts concerning Defendant's corporate structure and business model from \[date\] to present, including: (i) Defendant's date of formation, state of incorporation, and corporate parent(s); (ii) Defendant's principal business activities and the percentage of revenue derived from purchased-receivable collections; (iii) the number of accounts owned by Defendant; (iv) Defendant's total receivables under collection, by face value and by paid-for-value; (v) the basis on which Defendant has been characterized as a 'debt collector' or 'consumer debt buyer' in any SEC filing, financial statement, or regulatory submission; (vi) all subsidiaries or affiliates that perform any portion of the collection function; and (vii) whether Defendant is 'regularly engaged, directly or indirectly, in the collection of debts.'"  
  
\*\*Discoverable scope.\*\* This topic locks in the principal-purpose analysis. \*Barbato\* and \*Tepper\* both relied on corporate documents — purchase agreements, SEC filings, business descriptions — to find principal-purpose status. The "regularly engaged" prong of § 1692a(6) is also relevant if the entity is sued in any capacity other than as owner.  
  
\*\*Likely designee.\*\* Chief financial officer or general counsel; SEC reporting officer if applicable.  
  
\*\*Foundation/admissions.\*\* Admit revenue mix; admit business description as it appears in 10-K or analogous filings; admit reliance on collection activity for principal revenue; admit corporate-affiliate structure.  
  
\*\*Anchors.\*\* 15 U.S.C. § 1692a(6); \*Henson v. Santander Consumer USA Inc.\*, 582 U.S. 79 (2017); \*Barbato v. Greystone Alliance, LLC\*, 916 F.3d 260 (3d Cir. 2019); \*Tepper v. Amos Fin., LLC\*, 898 F.3d 364 (3d Cir. 2018); \*McAdory v. M.N.S. & Assocs., LLC\*, 952 F.3d 1089 (9th Cir. 2020).

\#\# B. The Specific Portfolio and the Consumer's Account  
  
\*\*TEMPLATE — Topic B:\*\*  
\> "All facts concerning the portfolio containing Plaintiff's account, including: (i) the date Defendant purchased the portfolio; (ii) the seller; (iii) the original creditor and any intervening owners; (iv) the purchase price as a percentage of face value; (v) the total number of accounts in the portfolio; (vi) the data fields received from the seller, on a field-by-field basis; (vii) any data fields not received from the seller that Defendant subsequently populated and the source thereof; (viii) any 'media' (i.e., account-level documents) received from the seller and which categories were not received; and (ix) the present status of Plaintiff's account within Defendant's records."
