# FDCPA Discovery — Requests for Production to Creditors and Debt Buyers  
  
**Topic:** Plaintiff-side Rule 34 document requests targeting chain of title, account-level documentation, and policy materials from creditors and debt buyers.  
**Primary authority:** Fed. R. Civ. P. 26, 34; Fair Debt Collection Practices Act, 15 U.S.C. §§ 1692–1692p; Regulation F, 12 C.F.R. Part 1006.  
**Key cases:** *Henson v. Santander Consumer USA Inc.*, 137 S. Ct. 1718 (2017); *Barbato v. Greystone Alliance, LLC*, 916 F.3d 260 (3d Cir. 2019); *Midland Funding, LLC v. Johnson*, 137 S. Ct. 1407 (2017); *Crawford v. LVNV Funding, LLC*, 758 F.3d 1254 (11th Cir. 2014); *Bock v. Pressler & Pressler, LLP*, 30 F. Supp. 3d 283 (D.N.J. 2014), aff'd in part, 658 F. App'x 63 (3d Cir. 2016).  
**Last updated:** May 16, 2026.  
  
---  
  
## 1. Strategic Overview  
  
Document discovery in FDCPA debt-buyer and creditor cases typically pursues four lines:  
  
1. **Chain of title** — establish whether each transfer was supported by a writing identifying the specific Account, not just a portfolio summary. Missing chain-of-title proof drives § 1692e(2)(A) (false representation of character or legal status) and § 1692e(8) (communicating false information to CRAs) theories, as well as state-law unfair practices claims.  
2. **Account-level documentation** — what the defendant actually possessed at the moment of each communication is critical to § 1692e and § 1692f false-or-misleading-representation and unfair-practices analyses, and to the bona fide error defense.  
3. **Communications and policies** — internal scripts, dialers, dunning templates, validation notice templates, and version control logs supply the systemic proof needed for class certification under Rule 23 and for willfulness.  
4. **Inter-party communications** — between the original creditor, debt buyer, servicer, and collection counsel — to track what was known when, and to rebut bona-fide-error defenses under § 1692k(c).  
  
Rule 34 requests should be paired with the interrogatories in `fdcpa_disc_05_Interrogatories_to_Creditors_DebtBuyers.md`; the interrogatories identify documents to be produced under these requests.

## 2. Sample Requests for Production — TEMPLATE  
  
> **TEMPLATE — REQUESTS FOR PRODUCTION TO DEFENDANT [Creditor/Debt Buyer]**  
> Pursuant to Fed. R. Civ. P. 34, Plaintiff requests that Defendant produce the following documents and electronically stored information for inspection and copying within thirty (30) days. Documents shall be produced in native format with metadata where reasonably practicable, in accordance with the parties' ESI protocol. "Account" means the alleged debt identified in paragraph __ of the Complaint. "You" and "Your" mean Defendant and any parent, subsidiary, affiliate, agent, employee, attorney, or other person acting on Defendant's behalf. "Document" has the meaning given in Fed. R. Civ. P. 34(a)(1)(A).  
  
### A. Chain of Title — Portfolio-Level Documents  
  
1. Every master debt sale agreement, purchase-and-sale agreement, forward-flow agreement, or comparable instrument under which the Account was transferred, including all schedules, addenda, amendments, and side letters.  
2. Every bill of sale, assignment, or other transfer instrument evidencing the transfer of the Account from the original creditor to any subsequent owner and from each such owner to You.  
3. Every electronic file (in native format, including header rows and field dictionaries) transmitted from a prior owner to You that included the Account, together with the loading log or receipt confirmation.  
4. Every representation, warranty, indemnity, repurchase, or chargeback provision, including any amendments or waivers, applicable to the portfolio in which the Account was sold.  
5. Every dispute, repurchase request, chargeback, indemnity claim, or remediation request — by either party — under those representations and warranties between You and any prior owner during the four years preceding the Complaint.  
  
### B. Chain of Title — Account-Level Documents  
  
6. Every Document identifying the Account by account number, consumer name, or other unique identifier, on any seller-provided portfolio schedule or transmittal file.  
7. Every "seller affidavit," "affidavit of sale," or comparable sworn statement You possess concerning the Account or the portfolio containing the Account. (Foundational to admissibility issues raised in state collection actions; see *Bock*, 30 F. Supp. 3d at 305–06.)  
8. Every Document reflecting the consideration paid for the Account and for the portfolio in which it was acquired.  
9. Every Document reflecting any intermediate transfer of the Account between the original creditor and You.  
10. Every Document reflecting the purchase price per dollar of face value paid by You and any prior owner for the portfolio containing the Account.  
  
### C. Account-Level Documentation — Origination and Servicing  
  
11. The application, terms-and-conditions, cardmember or loan agreement, and any amendment thereto that governs the Account.  
12. Every periodic statement, billing statement, or month-end statement issued on the Account from origination through charge-off.  
13. The complete payment history for the Account, including each debit, credit, fee, interest accrual, and adjustment, in machine-readable format.  
14. Every charge-off statement, charge-off ledger entry, or other Document evidencing the charge-off of the Account, identifying the charge-off balance and date.  
15. Every Document evidencing post-charge-off interest, fees, or other amounts added to the Account by any owner, together with the contractual or statutory basis for each addition. (Goes to § 1692f(1) — collection of any amount not authorized by the agreement or permitted by law.)  
  
### D. Original-Creditor Records (If Defendant Is the Original Creditor or Has Acquired Them)  
  
16. Every Document constituting the original creditor's books and records concerning the Account, including welcome packets, change-in-terms notices, late notices, and collection notices.  
17. Every recording, transcript, or note of any inbound or outbound telephone communication between the original creditor and Plaintiff concerning the Account.  
18. Every Document reflecting Plaintiff's dispute of the Account at any time before charge-off.  
19. Every Document reflecting fraud, identity-theft, deceased-debtor, or bankruptcy markers placed on the Account at any time.  
20. The complete CRA-furnishing history for the Account, including each Metro 2 file, dispute response (ACDV/AUD), and deletion or correction record.  
  
### E. Communications With the Consumer  
  
21. Every dunning letter, validation notice, settlement letter, payment receipt, or other written Communication sent by You (or any agent acting on Your behalf) to Plaintiff regarding the Account.  
22. Every recording of every telephone Communication between You (or any agent) and Plaintiff or any third party concerning the Account, in native audio format.  
23. Every collector note, call-log entry, account memo, or comparable record of every Communication with Plaintiff or any third party regarding the Account.  
24. Every voicemail left for Plaintiff regarding the Account, in native audio format, together with the corresponding script.  
25. Every text message, email, or in-app message sent to or received from Plaintiff regarding the Account.  
26. Every "limited-content message" under 12 C.F.R. § 1006.2(j) left for Plaintiff, together with the script and any compliance documentation.  
  
### F. Communications Between Creditor, Debt Buyer, and Collection Agents  
  
27. Every Communication between You and the original creditor (or any prior owner) regarding the Account, including emails, secure-portal messages, and ticketing-system entries.  
28. Every Communication between You and any third-party collection agency, law firm, debt seller, or other servicer regarding the Account.  
29. Every placement file, recall notice, or status report exchanged with such third parties regarding the Account.  
30. Every Document reflecting compensation paid to, or recovered from, such third parties on the Account (e.g., contingency-fee statements, commission reports).  
  
### G. Charge-Off and Pre-Suit Records  
  
31. Every Document reflecting Your or any prior owner's determination that the Account was uncollectible, written off for tax or accounting purposes, or otherwise charged off.  
32. Every Document reflecting the application of the statute of limitations to the Account, including any internal scrub report identifying the Account as out-of-statute. (Relevant to *Crawford*, 758 F.3d at 1258–59, and *Midland Funding v. Johnson*, 137 S. Ct. at 1411–12.)  
33. Every Document reflecting the consumer's last payment date on the Account.  
34. Every Document reflecting any prior settlement, payment in full, or other resolution of the Account.  
  
### H. Validation Notice and Reg F Compliance  
  
35. The validation notice sent to Plaintiff under 12 C.F.R. § 1006.34, together with the production proof and mailing log.  
36. Every template, form, or model validation notice used by You during the relevant period, together with version-control history.  
37. Every internal procedure, training module, or audit Document concerning compliance with the itemization-date, validation-information, and dispute-response requirements of 12 C.F.R. § 1006.34.  
38. Every Document reflecting Plaintiff's written or oral dispute of the Account under § 1006.34(c)(3) or 15 U.S.C. § 1692g(b), and Your response.  
39. Every Document reflecting compliance with the seven-in-seven call frequency presumption of 12 C.F.R. § 1006.14(b).  
40. Every Document reflecting consumer consent under § 1006.6(d) for email or text communications.  
  
### I. Policies, Procedures, Training, and Audits  
  
41. Every written policy or procedure governing: (a) account validation; (b) handling consumer disputes; (c) statute-of-limitations scrubs; (d) bankruptcy scrubs; (e) deceased-consumer scrubs; (f) servicemember/SCRA scrubs; (g) identity-theft scrubs; (h) cease-communication requests; (i) telephone-call frequency; (j) e-communications consent.  
42. Every employee training Document — including manuals, presentations, e-learning modules, and quiz materials — concerning the FDCPA, Regulation F, or state debt-collection law during the relevant period.  
43. Every internal or external audit, examination, or compliance review of Your debt-collection practices during the relevant period, including CFPB supervisory examination reports and remediation correspondence (subject to applicable privileges).  
44. Every consumer complaint received by You or forwarded to You by the CFPB Consumer Complaint Database, BBB, state attorney general, or state regulator concerning conduct similar to that alleged in the Complaint.  
45. Every Document reflecting disciplinary action taken against employees for violations of the FDCPA or Regulation F during the relevant period.  
  
### J. Collection-and-Litigation Policies  
  
46. Every Document reflecting Your policy or practice for filing state-court collection actions, including criteria for filing, documentation requirements, and statute-of-limitations review.  
47. Every state-court complaint, affidavit of debt, or judgment filed by You (or counsel acting on Your behalf) against Plaintiff or any other consumer in [forum state] during the relevant period, on a sample or full basis as the Court directs.  
48. Every proof of claim filed by You in any bankruptcy proceeding involving Plaintiff or any putative class member.  
49. Every retention or engagement letter, fee agreement, or compensation arrangement with collection counsel relating to the Account or to the class.  
50. Every Document reflecting Your supervision of collection counsel, including periodic case reviews, performance reports, and instructions regarding affidavits of debt.  
  
### K. Reg F § 1006.30 — Passive Collection and Prohibited Transfers  
  
51. Every Document reflecting Your furnishing of information about the Account to any consumer reporting agency, including the date and content of each furnishing.  
52. Every policy or procedure addressing the prohibition in 12 C.F.R. § 1006.30(b) on transferring debts that have been paid, settled, discharged in bankruptcy, or are the subject of an identity-theft report.  
53. Every Document reflecting Your decision to sell, transfer, or place for collection any debt within the categories described in § 1006.30(b), and the basis for that decision.  
  
### L. Financial and Corporate Records (Principal-Purpose Discovery)  
  
54. Audited or management-prepared financial statements showing revenue by source (collection on owned debt; servicing fees; debt resale; other) for each of the four years preceding the Complaint.  
55. SEC filings, investor presentations, lender presentations, and marketing materials describing Your business and principal purpose.  
56. Organizational charts identifying business units, subsidiaries, and affiliates engaged in debt acquisition, collection, or litigation.  
  
### M. Litigation-Hold and Document-Retention Records  
  
57. Every litigation-hold notice issued in connection with this matter, the Plaintiff's pre-suit demand, or any substantially similar matter.  
58. The document-retention schedule applicable to consumer-debt records during the relevant period.

## 3. Practice Notes  
  
- **Form of production.** Specify under Rule 34(b)(1)(C) that recordings be produced in native audio format, that databases be produced in machine-readable form with field dictionaries, and that emails be produced with metadata. Negotiate a Rule 26(f) ESI protocol early.  
- **Confidentiality.** Expect a protective order under Rule 26(c) covering portfolio purchase prices, master debt sale agreements, and consumer data of non-parties. Use a tiered "Confidential / Attorneys' Eyes Only" structure.  
- **Burden objections.** Rule 26(b)(1) proportionality is the main pressure point on system-wide policy and class-related requests. Cite *Henson*, *Barbato*, and *Midland Funding* to anchor relevance. Class-related document discovery is generally permissible at the certification stage; see *Wal-Mart Stores, Inc. v. Dukes*, 564 U.S. 338 (2011), on the need for common policy proof.  
- **Privilege.** CFPB supervisory information is protected by 12 U.S.C. § 1828(x) and CFPB regulations at 12 C.F.R. § 1070.42; do not waive by overbroad demands. Distinguish supervisory examination materials from underlying business records, which remain producible.  
- **Original-creditor records.** If the defendant did not receive these records from the seller, pair with a Rule 45 subpoena to the original creditor; see `fdcpa_disc_09_Third_Party_Subpoenas_FDCPA.md`.
