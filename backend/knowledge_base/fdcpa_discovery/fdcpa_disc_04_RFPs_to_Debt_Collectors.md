\# Requests for Production to Debt Collectors  
  
\*\*Topic:\*\* Plaintiff Requests for Production under Fed. R. Civ. P. 34  
\*\*Primary statutes:\*\* 15 U.S.C. §§ 1692a-1692p; 12 C.F.R. Part 1006 (Reg F)  
\*\*Reg F cross-references:\*\* §§ 1006.2, 1006.6, 1006.14, 1006.18, 1006.22, 1006.26, 1006.30, 1006.34, 1006.38, 1006.42  
\*\*Procedural authority:\*\* Fed. R. Civ. P. 34, 26(b), 26(g), 37(b)  
\*\*Last updated:\*\* May 16, 2026

\#\# 1. Strategic Use of RFPs in FDCPA Cases  
  
RFPs do the heavy lifting in FDCPA discovery. Unlike interrogatories, RFPs have no numerical cap (subject to proportionality) and force the defense to deliver primary evidence: form letters, call recordings, CMS data, policies, training, and chain-of-title.  
  
Three structural principles:  
\- \*\*Pair every interrogatory with an RFP\*\*: ask the defense to identify something, then produce the documents that prove what was identified.  
\- \*\*Use CFPB-style "form letter library" requests\*\*: a debt collector's letter library is the single most productive corpus in any FDCPA case. The CFPB's supervisory framework treats template libraries as central, and federal courts allow discovery into them. \*Foti v. NCO Fin. Sys., Inc.\*, 424 F. Supp. 2d 643 (S.D.N.Y. 2006); \*Bartlett v. Heibl\*, 128 F.3d 497 (7th Cir. 1997).  
\- \*\*Request native format with metadata\*\*: letter templates lose meaning without merge fields; call recordings need the disposition log alongside the audio; CMS exports need the data dictionary.  
  
Anchoring cases: \*Heintz v. Jenkins\*, 514 U.S. 291 (1995); \*Jerman v. Carlisle\*, 559 U.S. 573 (2010); \*Henson v. Santander\*, 137 S. Ct. 1718 (2017); \*Obduskey v. McCarthy & Holthus\*, 139 S. Ct. 1029 (2019); \*Rotkiske v. Klemm\*, 140 S. Ct. 355 (2019); \*Midland Funding v. Johnson\*, 137 S. Ct. 1407 (2017); \*Avila v. Riexinger\*, 817 F.3d 72 (2d Cir. 2016); \*Douglass v. Convergent Outsourcing\*, 765 F.3d 299 (3d Cir. 2014); \*Clomon v. Jackson\*, 988 F.2d 1314 (2d Cir. 1993); \*Miller v. Wolpoff & Abramson\*, 321 F.3d 292 (2d Cir. 2003); \*TransUnion LLC v. Ramirez\*, 594 U.S. 413 (2021); \*Hunstein v. Preferred Collection\*, 48 F.4th 1236 (11th Cir. 2022).

\#\# 2. Definitions and Instructions  
  
\`\`\`  
TEMPLATE — DEFINITIONS AND INSTRUCTIONS  
  
1\. "You," "your," "Defendant" — as in interrogatory definitions.  
2\. "Document" includes ESI as defined by Rule 34(a)(1)(A), including audio  
 files, system logs, database extracts, metadata, and instant messages.  
3\. "Communication" has the meaning in 15 U.S.C. § 1692a(2).  
4\. "Account" means the alleged debt of Plaintiff \[account ID\].  
5\. "Reg F" means 12 C.F.R. Part 1006.  
6\. "Relevant period" means \[date 1 year before first communication\] to the  
 present unless otherwise specified. For "form letter" and "template" RFPs,  
 the period is the class period (or the period for which the same template  
 was in use).  
7\. "Native format" means the form in which the document is ordinarily  
 maintained, with all metadata intact.  
  
Instructions:  
A. Produce documents as they are kept in the ordinary course of business or  
 organized and labeled to correspond to the categories in the request.  
B. Produce ESI in native format with load files, in accordance with the  
 parties' ESI protocol.  
C. If any document is withheld on a claim of privilege, provide a  
 privilege log under Rule 26(b)(5).  
D. Supplement under Rule 26(e).

\#\# 3. Topic A — The Consumer's Complete Account File (8-12 RFPs)  
  
\`\`\`  
RFP A-1. Produce the complete collection file for the Account, including all  
CMS records, notes, dispositions, work-flow tasks, system messages, and any  
"deleted" or archived entries.  
  
RFP A-2. Produce a complete export of the CMS account record for the Account  
in native format, with a data dictionary.  
  
RFP A-3. Produce every document received by Defendant from the original  
creditor, any prior owner, or any placement source concerning the Account,  
including the account-level data file, statements, contracts, and payment  
history.  
  
RFP A-4. Produce all bills of sale, assignment agreements, allonges, and  
chain-of-title documentation for the Account.  
  
RFP A-5. Produce every account statement, billing record, ledger, and  
balance reconciliation for the Account.  
  
RFP A-6. Produce all documents reflecting the calculation of the principal  
balance, interest, fees, costs, and total balance as represented in any  
communication to Plaintiff.  
  
RFP A-7. Produce all documents reflecting payments, credits, refunds, or  
adjustments to the Account.  
  
RFP A-8. Produce all documents identifying the date of last payment, date  
of charge-off, and date of default for the Account.  
  
RFP A-9. Produce every document reflecting the SOL applicable to the Account,  
including any SOL field in the account-level data file and any internal  
analysis.  
  
RFP A-10. Produce every dispute, request for verification, or cease-and-desist  
correspondence concerning the Account, and every response.  
  
RFP A-11. Produce every legal filing concerning the Account, including  
complaints, motions, judgments, garnishments, and proofs of claim.  
  
RFP A-12. Produce every document concerning the placement, return, or  
withdrawal of the Account by any party.

(including its agents and vendors) and Plaintiff or any other person at

or at Plaintiff's number, with metadata.  
  
RFP B-6. Produce all e-mail and text messages exchanged between Defendant  
and Plaintiff, including any opt-in/consent records and opt-out records.  
  
RFP B-7. Produce the configuration of any predictive dialer, autodialer, or  
auto-attendant used to call Plaintiff, including the campaign settings,  
calling-time windows, and 7-in-7 controls.  
  
RFP B-8. Produce every communication between Defendant and any third party  
(including relatives, neighbors, employers) concerning Plaintiff or the  
Account.  
  
RFP B-9. Produce all communications between Defendant and Plaintiff's  
counsel.  
  
RFP B-10. Produce all communications between Defendant and any vendor  
concerning Plaintiff or the Account, including letter vendors, dialer  
vendors, skip tracers, and BPOs.  
  
RFP B-11. Produce all internal e-mails, Slack/Teams messages, and instant  
messages within Defendant concerning Plaintiff or the Account, including

Annotation: Internal IM/Slack content is frequently the most candid.

RFP B-12. Produce all returned mail, undeliverable notices, NCOA (national  
change of address) records, and skip-tracing results concerning Plaintiff.  
  
RFP B-13. Produce the disposition-code field dictionary and any business  
rules associated with each disposition code used in Plaintiff's account.  
  
RFP B-14. Produce all communications between Defendant and any consumer  
reporting agency concerning the Account, including Metro 2 furnishings,  
ACDVs, and AUDs.  
  
RFP B-15. Produce all communications by Defendant to or about Plaintiff  
that disclosed Plaintiff's information (including account number, debt amount,  
or name) to any third party.  
  
Annotation: Hunstein-style claims; tracks every Reg F-relevant vendor  
disclosure.

\#\# 5. Topic C — Letter Templates and Form Letters (8-12 RFPs) — Central  
  
\`\`\`  
RFP C-1. Produce the complete library of letter templates and form letters  
used by Defendant to collect consumer debts during the class period,  
including for each (a) the template ID; (b) version history; (c) effective  
dates; (d) author; (e) approver; (f) merge-field map; and (g) a sample  
fully rendered letter.  
  
Annotation: This is the central RFP in any FDCPA form-letter case. The CFPB's  
examination procedures specifically inventory letter libraries; courts have  
required production of similar libraries. Foti, 424 F. Supp. 2d 643;  
Bartlett, 128 F.3d 497. Limit by debt type if relevant. If the defense  
objects, narrow to "every template containing \[the offending language\]"  
or "every template used to collect the same debt type as Plaintiff's during  
the class period."  
  
RFP C-2. Produce the specific template used to generate the letter attached  
as Exhibit A, in native format, with all variable fields and conditional  
logic visible.  
  
RFP C-3. Produce all prior versions of the template at C-2, with redline  
comparisons if maintained.  
  
RFP C-4. Produce all documents reflecting the drafting, review, and approval  
of the template at C-2, including drafts, comments, e-mails, and signoffs.  
  
RFP C-5. Produce all documents identifying every person who drafted, edited,  
reviewed, or approved the template at C-2.  
  
RFP C-6. Produce all communications between Defendant and any vendor (legal,  
compliance, or print/mail) concerning the template at C-2.  
  
RFP C-7. Produce all documents reflecting any internal or external review  
or audit of the template at C-2.  
  
RFP C-8. Produce all documents reflecting any change to the template at C-2  
during or after the relevant period, and the reason for each change.  
  
RFP C-9. Produce the print-and-mail vendor's production logs (volume, dates,  
recipients) for the template at C-2.  
  
RFP C-10. Produce the data extract or query used to identify recipients of  
the template at C-2 during the class period.  
  
RFP C-11. Produce all consumer complaints, lawsuits, or demand letters  
received by Defendant concerning the template at C-2 or its predecessor  
versions.  
  
RFP C-12. Produce all documents reflecting any safe-harbor language used in  
the template at C-2 (including Avila/Miller-style disclosures).  
  
Annotation: Avila v. Riexinger, 817 F.3d 72 — safe-harbor for accruing  
balances; Miller, 321 F.3d 292 — attorney-involvement disclaimers.

\#\# 8. Topic F — Bona Fide Error Defense Documentation (8-12 RFPs)  
  
\`\`\`  
RFP F-1. Produce every document Defendant contends supports its bona fide  
error defense under 15 U.S.C. § 1692k(c), including procedures, training,  
audits, and incident reports.  
  
Annotation: Track interrogatory I-2; lock the defense to a defined corpus.  
  
RFP F-2. Produce all written procedures Defendant contends were "reasonably  
adapted to avoid" the specific violation(s) alleged, with effective dates.  
  
RFP F-3. Produce all training records (attendance, completion, assessments)  
for the agents and supervisors who handled the Account.  
  
RFP F-4. Produce all quality-assurance scorecards, call-monitoring reports,  
and letter-review reports for the agents who handled the Account during  
the period of the conduct alleged.  
  
RFP F-5. Produce all internal audit reports, compliance reviews, and risk  
assessments covering the type of conduct alleged in the Complaint.  
  
RFP F-6. Produce all root-cause analyses, incident reports, and post-mortems  
concerning the specific violation alleged or any substantially similar  
violation during the past three years.  
  
Annotation: Johnson v. Riddle, 443 F.3d 723; Kort v. Diversified Collection  
Servs., 394 F.3d 530.  
  
RFP F-7. Produce all change-management records for the templates, scripts,  
policies, training, and system controls relied on for the § 1692k(c)  
defense.  
  
RFP F-8. Produce all consumer complaints, demand letters, lawsuits, and  
regulatory complaints received by Defendant during the past three years  
concerning conduct substantially similar to that alleged in the Complaint.  
  
Annotation: Reichert, 531 F.3d 1002; Edwards, 584 F.3d 1350. Recurrence  
undermines BFE.  
  
RFP F-9. Produce all corrective-action plans, remediation memoranda, and  
implementation evidence concerning any substantially similar prior violation.  
  
RFP F-10. Produce all vendor agreements, SLAs, and QA records for vendors  
on which Defendant relies for any element of its BFE defense.  
  
RFP F-11. Produce all certifications, accreditations, and third-party reviews  
of Defendant's procedures (ACA, RMAI, SOC reports) for the relevant period.  
  
RFP F-12. Produce all communications with counsel concerning the procedures  
relied on for the BFE defense, to the extent not protected by the  
attorney-client privilege, and provide a privilege log for withheld  
communications.  
  
Annotation: Jerman, 559 U.S. 573 — mistake-of-law not available. Pin the  
defense to factual, not legal, reliance.

\#\# 20. Form-Letter and Template Discovery — Strategic Note  
  
Form-letter and template discovery is the \*single most productive\* area in any FDCPA case. A debt collector's template library is the corpus of its consumer-facing speech. Three propositions follow:  
  
1\. The library is \*quasi-public\*: hundreds or thousands of consumers receive each template, and protective-order treatment beyond standard "confidential" is rarely justified.  
2\. The library is \*unitary\*: template ID, version, effective dates, drafter, approver, and merge logic come together. Demand all five in native format.  
3\. The library is \*audit-rich\*: every template typically passes through compliance review. Pull the review trail.  
  
Drafting tip — pair template discovery with custodian-narrow internal e-mail discovery: the compliance officer, the operations lead, and the in-house lawyer. Most of the litigation value lies in the e-mail thread that approved (or flagged) the letter.

\#\# 21. Common Defense Objections  
  
\- \*\*"Overbroad"\*\* — narrow by template ID, date range, and debt type. Avoid "all letters ever sent."  
\- \*\*"Trade secret"\*\* �� counter with a stipulated protective order. Cite \*Foti\* and \*Bartlett\*.  
\- \*\*"Privileged"\*\* — privilege log under Rule 26(b)(5); identify \*fact\* of legal review without compelling content.  
\- \*\*"Undue burden"\*\* — request the data dictionary and propose an ESI protocol; force the defense to quantify the burden.  
\- \*\*"Not in Defendant's possession, custody, or control"\*\* — request the vendor agreement; "control" includes the legal right to demand from a vendor. \*In re NTL, Inc. Sec. Litig.\*, 244 F.R.D. 179 (S.D.N.Y. 2007).  
\- \*\*"Proportionality"\*\* — in a $1,000 statutory case, frame issues at stake broadly and limit custodians/date ranges to keep the request defensible.
