\# ESI Preservation and Production Protocols in FDCPA Cases  
  
| Field | Value |  
|---|---|  
| \*\*Topic\*\* | Litigation holds, ESI protocols, and call-recording preservation in FDCPA litigation |  
| \*\*Primary Authority\*\* | Fed. R. Civ. P. 16(b), 26(f), 34, 37(e); Fed. R. Evid. 502(d); 12 C.F.R. § 1006.100 (Reg F) |  
| \*\*Cross-References\*\* | \`fdcpa\_disc\_16\` (Zubulake / Rule 37(e)), \`fdcpa\_disc\_17\` (template circulation), \`fdcpa\_disc\_18\` (pre-suit demand) |  
| \*\*Last Updated\*\* | May 16, 2026 |  
  
\> FDCPA cases generate a distinct ESI footprint: call recordings, dialer logs, voicemail systems, automated dunning-letter generation, CRM "consumer notes," dispute logs, e-OSCAR records, training/QA materials, and complaint-handling systems. The single most consequential mistake — by either side — is failing to scope and execute a litigation hold that captures all of these by the date litigation became reasonably foreseeable.  
  
\---  
  
\#\# 1. The duty to preserve — when, what, who  
  
\#\#\# When the duty attaches  
  
The duty to preserve attaches when litigation is \*\*reasonably anticipated\*\*, not when filed. \*Zubulake v. UBS Warburg LLC\*, 220 F.R.D. 212 (S.D.N.Y. 2003). In FDCPA practice, that point typically arrives at the earliest of:  
\- Receipt of a written cease-and-desist letter (§ 1692c(c)).  
\- Receipt of a § 1692g written dispute.  
\- Receipt of a pre-suit demand letter from counsel.  
\- Receipt of a consumer complaint (CFPB, state AG, BBB) referencing the same account.  
\- Receipt of a litigation-hold notice.  
  
The collector's compliance and legal departments should have \*\*triggering events\*\* mapped to automated litigation-hold issuance. Failure to issue a hold once any of the above arrives is itself the misconduct \*Zubulake V\* sanctions.  
  
\#\#\# The Rule 37(e) framework  
  
Fed. R. Civ. P. 37(e), as amended in 2015, addresses ESI specifically:  
\- \*\*37(e)(1)\*\* — upon a finding of prejudice from lost ESI that "should have been preserved," the court may order measures "no greater than necessary to cure the prejudice."  
\- \*\*37(e)(2)\*\* — upon a finding that the party "acted with the intent to deprive another party of the information's use," the court may impose adverse inference, dismissal, or default.  
  
The Rule 37(e) framework supersedes inconsistent prior common-law spoliation doctrine in federal courts.  
  
\#\#\# Who must be on the hold  
  
For FDCPA, a properly scoped hold must reach (at minimum):  
\- The named collector(s) (employees who contacted the consumer).  
\- The collector's supervisors (call monitoring, QA).  
\- The collector's compliance staff (responsible for templates and complaint handling).  
\- The IT custodian for the CRM, dialer, recording system, and email.  
\- The mail-letter vendor account manager (if templates were sent via vendor).  
\- In-house and outside counsel involved in approving templates.  
\- Marketing staff who participated in letter design (an often-overlooked custodian).

\#\# 2. Call recording retention and spoliation  
  
\#\#\# The regulatory floor — 12 C.F.R. § 1006.100  
  
CFPB Regulation F, \*\*12 C.F.R. § 1006.100\*\*, requires a debt collector that records any portion of a telephone call made in connection with the collection of a debt to retain the recording for \*\*three years\*\* after the date of the call. This is a \*floor\*, not a ceiling: longer retention may be required by litigation hold, state law, or contractual obligations to clients.  
  
\#\#\# Spoliation framework for recordings  
  
Where a recording is destroyed after the duty to preserve attached, Rule 37(e) governs:  
\- \*\*Was the destruction routine?\*\* If automated retention purged the file before any litigation trigger, the duty had not attached.  
\- \*\*Was prejudice shown?\*\* Plaintiff must show the recording would have advanced its case. Contemporaneous notes, dispute letters, and partial transcripts help establish prejudice.  
\- \*\*Was intent shown?\*\* Documentary evidence that the collector knew of the dispute and proceeded with routine purge anyway supports a 37(e)(2) finding.  
  
\#\#\# Recording-system custodians  
  
For each recording system in use during the relevant period, identify:  
\- Vendor (NICE, Verint, CallMiner, Calabrio, Five9, Genesys, Aspect, RingCentral).  
\- The collector's recording-system administrator.  
\- The retention configuration (per-recording, per-account, per-agent).  
\- Any selective-recording rules (inbound-only, escalation-flagged, supervisor-flagged).  
\- Backup-tape rotation and offsite-archive practices.
