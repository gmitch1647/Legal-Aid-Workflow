\# Pre-Suit Informal Discovery in FDCPA Cases  
  
| Field | Value |  
|---|---|  
| \*\*Topic\*\* | Investigation, evidence-gathering, and demand practice before filing an FDCPA complaint |  
| \*\*Primary Authority\*\* | 15 U.S.C. §§ 1692a, 1692c(c), 1692g; 5 U.S.C. § 552 (FOIA); 12 C.F.R. Part 1070 (CFPB FOIA/Touhy) |

| \*\*Last Updated\*\* | May 16, 2026 |  
  
\> The single biggest mistake in FDCPA practice is filing without a pre-suit record. Most of the evidentiary universe — the dunning letter, call recordings, the consumer's own contemporaneous notes, the CFPB complaint log, and prior collector litigation — is obtainable before suit through statutory triggers and public records. A well-built pre-suit file converts an FDCPA case from a $1,000-statutory-damages flyer into a serious negotiation.  
  
\---  
  
\#\# 1. The cease-and-desist letter (§ 1692c(c)) as evidence-building  
  
\#\#\# The statute  
  
15 U.S.C. § 1692c(c) provides that if a consumer notifies the debt collector \*\*in writing\*\* that the consumer refuses to pay the debt or wishes the debt collector to cease further communication, the debt collector shall not communicate further except (a) to advise the consumer that further efforts are being terminated, (b) to notify the consumer that the debt collector may invoke specified remedies, or (c) to notify the consumer that the debt collector intends to invoke a specified remedy. \*\*Anything else is a per se violation.\*\*  
  
\#\#\# Tactical use  
  
A cease-and-desist letter does \*\*three\*\* evidentiary jobs simultaneously:  
1\. \*\*Triggers strict-liability tripwires.\*\* Any communication received afterward (except the three statutory exceptions) is a § 1692c(c) violation regardless of intent.  
2\. \*\*Documents the consumer's identity, address, and account.\*\* It creates a clean writing that the collector cannot later claim never to have received (especially when sent via certified mail / return receipt or USPS tracking).  
3\. \*\*Forces a record-keeping decision.\*\* Collectors must update their CRM with a "do not contact" flag. Failure to do so reveals procedural gaps relevant to bona-fide-error rebuttal under \*Jerman v. Carlisle, McNellie, Rini, Kramer & Ulrich LPA\*, 559 U.S. 573 (2010).  
  
\#\#\# Sample cease-and-desist letter  
  
\`\`\`text  
\[Date\]  
  
Via Certified Mail, Return Receipt Requested  
Receipt No. \[\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\]  
  
\[Debt Collector Name\]  
\[Address\]  
  
Re: Account No. \[\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\]  
 \[Original creditor, if known\]  
  
Dear Sir/Madam:  
  
I am writing pursuant to 15 U.S.C. § 1692c(c). Please CEASE ALL COMMUNICATION  
with me concerning the above-referenced alleged debt, including but not limited to  
telephone calls, letters, electronic mail, text messages, and any communications to  
third parties.  
  
Please also be advised that I refuse to pay the alleged debt. Pursuant to § 1692c(c),  
you may communicate with me only (i) to advise that further collection efforts are  
being terminated, (ii) to notify me that you may invoke specified remedies that you  
ordinarily invoke, or (iii) to notify me that you intend to invoke a specified remedy.  
Any other communication will be a violation of the Fair Debt Collection Practices Act.  
  
This letter does not constitute an acknowledgment that the debt is owed or that the  
limitations period is tolled, and I expressly reserve the right to dispute the debt under  
§ 1692g and to seek validation.  
  
Sincerely,  
\[Consumer name\]  
\[Address\]  
\[Telephone — for service of permitted notices only\]  
\`\`\`  
  
\*\*Practice note.\*\* Send via certified mail with return receipt and retain the green card. If sent electronically, request a read receipt and retain the email metadata.

\#\# 2. The validation request (§ 1692g) as discovery  
  
\#\#\# The statute  
  
15 U.S.C. § 1692g(a) requires the collector, within five days of the initial communication, to send a notice advising of the consumer's right to dispute the debt within 30 days. § 1692g(b) requires the collector, upon receipt of a written dispute or request for verification, to cease collection until it sends the consumer "verification of the debt or a copy of a judgment, or the name and address of the original creditor."  
  
\#\#\# Tactical use as pre-suit discovery  
  
A validation request is the cheapest pre-suit discovery vehicle available. It forces the collector to produce:  
\- The identity of the \*\*original creditor\*\* (key to § 1692e(2) chain-of-title analysis).  
\- A \*\*verification\*\* of the debt (which, when thin or absent, supports a § 1692e claim and feeds \*Hinkle\*-style chain-of-title litigation).  
\- A pause in collection — useful if the consumer needs time to retain counsel.
