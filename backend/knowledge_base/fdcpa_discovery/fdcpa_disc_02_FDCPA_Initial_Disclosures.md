\# FDCPA Initial Disclosures (Rule 26(a)(1))  
  
\*\*Topic:\*\* Rule 26(a)(1) initial disclosures in FDCPA actions  
\*\*Primary statutes:\*\* 15 U.S.C. §§ 1692k (damages), 1692g, 1692e, 1692d; 12 C.F.R. Part 1006 (Reg F)  
\*\*Procedural authority:\*\* Fed. R. Civ. P. 26(a)(1), 26(e), 26(g), 37(c)(1)  
\*\*Reg F cross-references:\*\* §§ 1006.14, 1006.18, 1006.34, 1006.38  
\*\*Last updated:\*\* May 16, 2026  
  
\---  
  
\#\# 1. Scope and Timing  
  
Rule 26(a)(1)(A) requires each party — absent stipulation or order — to disclose within 14 days of the Rule 26(f) conference:  
\- (i) the name and contact of each individual likely to have discoverable information the disclosing party may use to support its claims or defenses;  
\- (ii) a copy or description by category and location of documents, ESI, and tangible things that the party may use to support its claims or defenses;  
\- (iii) a computation of each category of damages claimed, with documents on which the computation is based; and  
\- (iv) any insurance agreement under which an insurer may be liable.  
  
FDCPA cases are not exempt under Rule 26(a)(1)(B). Failure to disclose triggers Rule 37(c)(1) preclusion.  
  
Initial disclosures are not discovery; they are the \*floor\* of mandatory disclosure. Treat them as a tactical tool, not a check-the-box exercise. They lock the opponent into a witness list and damages theory that can be enforced through Rule 37(c)(1) at trial. See \*Yeti by Molly, Ltd. v. Deckers Outdoor Corp.\*, 259 F.3d 1101, 1106 (9th Cir. 2001) (exclusion is "automatic" and "self-executing" absent substantial justification or harmlessness).

\#\# 2. Plaintiff's Disclosures  
  
\#\#\# 2.1 Witnesses (Rule 26(a)(1)(A)(i))  
Typical FDCPA plaintiff witnesses:  
\- \*\*The consumer\*\* — facts of communications received, emotional distress, time and money spent responding.  
\- \*\*Family/household members and co-workers\*\* — corroboration of calls, voicemails, emotional state.  
\- \*\*Treating mental-health or medical providers\*\* — if emotional distress is severe and treatment-supported. Note disclosure of treaters can trigger HIPAA waivers and increased defense discovery into mental-health history.  
\- \*\*Other recipients of the form letter\*\* (for class) — typically deferred to the class-cert stage and limited to named class representatives at the initial-disclosure phase.  
\- \*\*Custodians of authentication\*\* — bank, employer, telephone carrier (for call logs), wireless provider (for voicemails).  
\- \*\*The plaintiff's prior attorney\*\* if a cease-and-desist or representation letter is relevant — careful with privilege.  
\- \*\*Custodian of records for the defendant\*\* — to be obtained from defendant's disclosures.  
  
Common mistake: under-disclosing family members and friends who can corroborate emotional distress, then trying to call them at trial. Rule 37(c)(1) will likely exclude them. Over-disclose at this stage; supplement under Rule 26(e) as cases progress.  
  
\#\#\# 2.2 Documents (Rule 26(a)(1)(A)(ii))  
Plaintiff should disclose by category:  
\- All collection letters, e-mails, texts, and voicemails received (photographed/scanned with envelopes if relevant under § 1692f(8)).  
\- Plaintiff's call log, screenshots of caller ID, and any audio recordings (mind one-party/two-party consent state law).  
\- Plaintiff's cease-and-desist or dispute letters to the collector.  
\- Mail-tracking and return-receipt records.  
\- Medical, counseling, and prescription records if emotional distress is claimed (subject to protective order).  
\- Financial records reflecting any payments made or fees incurred.  
\- Credit reports and credit-monitoring screenshots (for derivative reporting harm).  
\- Correspondence with the original creditor, the CFPB complaint portal, state AG, or BBB.  
  
\#\#\# 2.3 Damages Computation (Rule 26(a)(1)(A)(iii)) — FDCPA-Specific  
  
FDCPA damages categories under § 1692k(a):  
  
\- \*\*Actual damages\*\* — § 1692k(a)(1). Includes out-of-pocket, lost wages, attorneys' fees paid pre-suit, and emotional distress. The Fifth and Eleventh Circuits import a \*Carey v. Piphus\*-style proof requirement; the Seventh and Ninth Circuits accept lay testimony for "garden variety" emotional distress. See \*Wantz v. Experian Info. Solutions\*, 386 F.3d 829 (7th Cir. 2004) (FCRA context); \*Rivera v. Allstate Ins. Co.\*, 913 F.3d 603 (7th Cir. 2018); \*Rasor v. Retrieval-Masters Creditors Bureau, Inc.\*, No. 2:16-cv-00373, 2017 WL 3097794 (W.D. Wash. July 20, 2017).  
\- \*\*Statutory damages\*\* — § 1692k(a)(2)(A): up to $1,000 per action (not per violation) for individual cases. \*Wright v. Finance Service of Norwalk, Inc.\*, 22 F.3d 647 (6th Cir. 1994) (en banc); \*Goodmann v. People's Bank\*, 209 F. App'x 111 (3d Cir. 2006).  
\- \*\*Statutory damages — class\*\* — § 1692k(a)(2)(B): the lesser of $500,000 or 1% of the collector's net worth.  
\- \*\*Attorneys' fees and costs\*\* — § 1692k(a)(3) (mandatory for prevailing plaintiff). Computed separately; not typically required to be quantified at initial disclosure but should be flagged.  
\- \*\*Treble or punitive damages\*\*: not available under the FDCPA. Punitive damages are not a category of FDCPA recovery; the statutory cap is the deterrent. (Pendent state-law claims may carry punitive exposure.)  
  
Template damages computation for the initial disclosure:  
  
\`\`\`  
TEMPLATE — PLAINTIFF DAMAGES COMPUTATION  
  
A. Actual damages — 15 U.S.C. § 1692k(a)(1):  
 1. Out-of-pocket expenses:  
 - Long-distance/cellular minutes spent on collection calls: $\[X\]  
 - Postage/certified-mail for dispute letters: $\[X\]  
 - Copying and document-storage charges: $\[X\]  
 Subtotal: $\[X\]  
 2. Lost wages / lost time from work:  
 - \[Y\] hours at $\[Z\]/hour, for total $\[X\]  
 3. Emotional distress, anxiety, humiliation, and embarrassment  
 arising from the collection conduct described in the Complaint:  
 $\[X\] (supported by Plaintiff's testimony, the testimony of  
 \[family members\], and treatment records of \[provider\] if any).  
 Subtotal — actual damages: $\[X\]  
  
B. Statutory damages — 15 U.S.C. § 1692k(a)(2)(A):  
 Up to $1,000.00.  
  
C. Attorneys' fees and costs — 15 U.S.C. § 1692k(a)(3):  
 To be determined; computed through lodestar after judgment.  
  
D. Pendent state claims (if any): see separate computation under  
 \[state UDAP statute / state debt-collection act / state IIED claim\].  
\`\`\`  
  
Common mistakes:  
\- Stating only "$1,000 + fees" and waiving emotional distress by silence.  
\- Listing emotional distress without supporting witnesses/documents — invites a \*Doe v. Cutter Biological\* preclusion motion.  
\- Quantifying attorneys' fees too low and being capped by the initial disclosure.  
\- Failing to itemize pendent state-law damages, which may be uncapped.  
  
\#\#\# 2.4 Insurance (Rule 26(a)(1)(A)(iv))  
Plaintiffs ordinarily have nothing to disclose here — but identify any claim against your own insurer (e.g., umbrella) if relevant to subrogation.

\#\# 3. Defendant's Disclosures  
  
\#\#\# 3.1 Witnesses  
Defendant's required witness list typically includes:  
\- \*\*The collector(s) who handled the consumer's account\*\* — pulled from CMS dispositions.  
\- \*\*The supervisor and team lead\*\* for those collectors.  
\- \*\*Compliance officer / Chief Compliance Officer.\*\*  
\- \*\*Operations leader\*\* who owns the dialer and call-recording system.  
\- \*\*Legal/compliance reviewer\*\* of the letter at issue.  
\- \*\*Custodian of records\*\* for CMS data, recordings, and policies.  
\- \*\*30(b)(6) designee(s)\*\* by topic (often disclosed by name and topic).  
\- \*\*Vendor representatives\*\* (letter vendor, dialer vendor) if the defense will rely on vendor processes for bona fide error.  
  
Defendants commonly under-disclose vendor witnesses and then try to invoke vendor reliance at summary judgment. Object early; demand supplementation under Rule 26(e).  
  
\#\#\# 3.2 Documents — FDCPA-Specific Categories  
  
Initial disclosures from a debt collector should describe by category and location:  
  
\- \*\*Account file\*\*: the complete CMS record for the consumer's account, including notes, dispositions, and call/letter logs.  
\- \*\*The letter template(s)\*\* sent to the consumer, with version history and effective dates.  
\- \*\*Call recordings\*\* of communications with the consumer, by recording-system ID.  
\- \*\*Bona fide error procedures (§ 1692k(c))\*\*: written policies, training materials, audit reports, and incident logs. Defendants raising BFE \*must\* identify these in initial disclosures or risk \*Yeti\*-style preclusion at summary judgment.  
\- \*\*Reg F policies\*\*: written procedures for §§ 1006.6 (communications), 1006.14 (call frequency), 1006.18 (false/misleading), 1006.26 (time-barred), 1006.34 (validation), 1006.38 (disputes), 1006.42 (e-delivery).  
\- \*\*Chain of title\*\* (debt buyers): bill of sale, PSA, account-level data file, seller representations.  
\- \*\*Compliance audits\*\*: monthly/quarterly QA reports, call-monitoring scorecards.  
\- \*\*CFPB and state regulator correspondence\*\* concerning the practices at issue (consent orders are public; supervisory letters may be confidential — produce under protective order).  
\- \*\*The collector's net worth statements\*\* if class allegations are pleaded (§ 1692k(a)(2)(B)).  
\- \*\*Insurance policies\*\*, including E\&O and CGL, and any reservation-of-rights letters.  
  
Defense common mistakes:  
\- "Documents in defendant's files" is not a disclosure. \*Lujan v. Cabana Mgmt., Inc.\*, 284 F.R.D. 50 (E.D.N.Y. 2012) (insufficiently specific). Categories should be specific enough to allow plaintiff to evaluate the case.  
\- Failing to identify the letter template's drafter and reviewer.  
\- Disclosing only the policies in force at the time of the litigation, not at the time of the communication.  
\- Withholding vendor agreements as "third-party confidential" without producing under protective order.  
  
\#\#\# 3.3 Damages Computation (Defense)  
Defendant typically discloses "no affirmative damages claim" unless counterclaiming on the debt (which is procedurally disfavored in many districts but not categorically barred). If counterclaiming, the disclosure must include principal, interest, fees, and the contract or statutory basis.  
  
\#\#\# 3.4 Insurance (Rule 26(a)(1)(A)(iv))  
Critical disclosure. Demand:  
\- All E\&O and CGL policies for the policy period and the relevant coverage triggers (occurrence vs. claims-made).  
\- Excess and umbrella policies.  
\- Reservation-of-rights letters and coverage opinions (subject to privilege analysis — the \*fact\* of a reservation is not privileged).  
  
Insurance often drives settlement. Aggressively address insurance in the Rule 26(f) report.  
  
\---  
  
\#\# 4. Supplementation (Rule 26(e))  
  
Both sides must supplement when they learn the original disclosure is incomplete or incorrect. In FDCPA cases:  
\- Plaintiff updates emotional-distress witnesses as they develop.  
\- Plaintiff updates damages as the case progresses (fees, additional out-of-pocket).  
\- Defendant supplements when CMS searches surface additional recordings, when vendor witnesses emerge, when post-event audits are completed.  
  
Set a recurring supplementation cadence in the scheduling order; many courts will adopt a "rolling supplementation" provision on stipulation.

\#\# 5. Strategy: Leveraging Rule 26(a)(1) Disclosures  
  
\- \*\*Lock the defense to its BFE evidence\*\*: a thin initial disclosure under (a)(1)(A)(ii) for BFE procedures, combined with a follow-up interrogatory ("identify every document the defendant contends supports its § 1692k(c) defense"), narrows the BFE record dramatically.  
\- \*\*Pin the witness list early\*\*: meaningful witness disclosure is required even though "use to support" is a self-limiting phrase. Hold the defense to it.  
\- \*\*Cite Rule 37(c)(1)\*\* in the meet-and-confer where the defense disclosure is vague.  
\- \*\*Use 26(a)(1) as a settlement lever\*\*: where the disclosed insurance limits are modest and the case is class-postured, early disclosure of net worth and insurance frames a structured resolution.
