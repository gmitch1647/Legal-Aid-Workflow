\# FDCPA Discovery — Expert Discovery  
  
\*\*Topic:\*\* Expert disclosure, discovery, and Daubert preview in FDCPA cases — damages experts (emotional distress, economic), communications experts (call recording and content analysis), industry/compliance experts, statistical experts (class certification), and psychological experts.  
\*\*Primary authority:\*\* Fed. R. Civ. P. 26(a)(2), 26(b)(4), 37(c)(1); Fed. R. Evid. 702, 703, 705; Fair Debt Collection Practices Act, 15 U.S.C. §§ 1692–1692p; Regulation F, 12 C.F.R. Part 1006.  
\*\*Key cases:\*\* \*Daubert v. Merrell Dow Pharms., Inc.\*, 509 U.S. 579 (1993); \*Kumho Tire Co. v. Carmichael\*, 526 U.S. 137 (1999); \*General Electric Co. v. Joiner\*, 522 U.S. 136 (1997); \*Wal-Mart Stores, Inc. v. Dukes\*, 564 U.S. 338 (2011); \*Comcast Corp. v. Behrend\*, 569 U.S. 27 (2013); \*Tyson Foods, Inc. v. Bouaphakeo\*, 577 U.S. 442 (2016); \*TransUnion LLC v. Ramirez\*, 594 U.S. 413 (2021).  
\*\*Last updated:\*\* May 16, 2026.

\#\# 1. Expert Discovery Framework  
  
The 2010 amendments to Fed. R. Civ. P. 26 govern expert practice. The key provisions are:  
  
\- \*\*Rule 26(a)(2)(B) — Retained experts.\*\* Written report containing: a complete statement of opinions and basis; facts and data considered; exhibits to be used; qualifications and prior testimony (10 years); compensation; and a list of cases in which the expert has testified at trial or deposition (4 years).  
\- \*\*Rule 26(a)(2)(C) — Non-retained experts (e.g., treating physicians).\*\* Disclosure of subject matter and a summary of facts and opinions; no full report required.  
\- \*\*Rule 26(b)(4)(B), (C) — Trial-preparation protection.\*\* Draft reports and most attorney-expert communications are protected from discovery, with three exceptions (compensation; facts/data provided; assumptions provided that the expert relied upon).  
\- \*\*Rule 26(a)(2)(D) — Timing.\*\* Disclosures must be made at the time and sequence directed by the court, or, absent direction, at least 90 days before trial; rebuttal within 30 days.  
\- \*\*Rule 37(c)(1) — Sanction.\*\* Failure to disclose information under Rule 26(a) results in exclusion unless the failure was substantially justified or harmless.  
  
Rule 702, as amended effective December 1, 2023, requires the proponent to demonstrate by a preponderance that the expert's testimony rests on sufficient facts/data, reliable principles/methods, and reflects a reliable application of those methods. Fed. R. Evid. 702 (eff. Dec. 1, 2023).

\#\# 2. Damages Experts  
  
\#\#\# A. Emotional Distress (Mental-Health Professionals)  
  
FDCPA actual damages under § 1692k(a)(1) commonly include emotional distress. Defense counsel routinely retains or cross-designates psychiatric or psychological experts to challenge the link between collection conduct and claimed distress.  
  
\> \*\*TEMPLATE — DISCOVERY DIRECTED TO OPPOSING EMOTIONAL-DISTRESS EXPERT\*\*  
\>  
\> \*Interrogatories.\*  
\>  
\> 1. Identify every diagnostic instrument You administered or relied upon (e.g., PCL-5, PHQ-9, MMPI-3), the raw scores, and the published reliability/validity literature supporting use of each instrument in the population to which Plaintiff belongs.  
\> 2. Describe Your differential diagnosis, identifying every plausible cause of Plaintiff's claimed symptoms (financial stress, other debts, unrelated life events, pre-existing conditions) and the basis for excluding or weighting each.  
\> 3. State Your methodology for attributing a specific percentage or quantum of Plaintiff's distress to Defendant's conduct.  
\> 4. Identify every treatment record, deposition transcript, and other Document You reviewed.  
\> 5. Identify every assumption provided to You by counsel and on which You relied.  
\>  
\> \*RFPs.\*  
\>  
\> 1. Every test protocol, scoring sheet, and raw data underlying Your evaluation.  
\> 2. Every draft of Your report exchanged with anyone other than counsel (note Rule 26(b)(4)(B) protection for drafts exchanged with counsel).  
\> 3. Every prior expert report You have authored in an FDCPA, FCRA, or TCPA case in the past four (4) years.  
\> 4. Every fee agreement, invoice, and receipt of payment.  
\> 5. Every publication, presentation, or treatise You have authored on emotional-distress damages in consumer-protection litigation.  
  
\*\*Daubert preview.\*\* Courts have applied Daubert rigorously to emotional-distress quantification. The expert should rely on validated instruments, conduct a differential diagnosis, and tie symptoms causally to defendant's conduct. Unstructured "clinical impressions" without supporting instruments invite \*Joiner\* "ipse dixit" exclusion. 522 U.S. at 146.  
  
\#\#\# B. Economic Damages  
  
Economic-loss experts may calculate lost wages, lost credit opportunities, or out-of-pocket expense. \*TransUnion v. Ramirez\*, 594 U.S. at 432, emphasizes the need to tie damages to concrete harm.
