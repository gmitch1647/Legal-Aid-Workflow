\# FDCPA Discovery — Requests for Admission (Both Sides)  
  
\*\*Topic:\*\* Rule 36 requests for admission in FDCPA litigation — plaintiff-side and defense-side templates for authenticating evidence, narrowing factual issues, and trapping policy gaps.  
\*\*Primary authority:\*\* Fed. R. Civ. P. 36, 37(c)(2); Fair Debt Collection Practices Act, 15 U.S.C. §§ 1692–1692p; Regulation F, 12 C.F.R. Part 1006.  
\*\*Key cases:\*\* \*TransUnion LLC v. Ramirez\*, 594 U.S. 413 (2021); \*Spokeo, Inc. v. Robins\*, 578 U.S. 330 (2016); \*Heintz v. Jenkins\*, 514 U.S. 291 (1995); \*Jerman v. Carlisle, McNellie, Rini, Kramer & Ulrich LPA\*, 559 U.S. 573 (2010); \*Marx v. General Revenue Corp.\*, 568 U.S. 371 (2013); \*Midland Funding, LLC v. Johnson\*, 137 S. Ct. 1407 (2017).  
\*\*Last updated:\*\* May 16, 2026.  
  
\---  
  
\#\# 1. Strategic Role of RFAs in FDCPA Cases  
  
Rule 36 requests for admission perform three distinct functions in FDCPA litigation:  
  
1\. \*\*Authentication.\*\* Letters, recordings, scripts, and database extracts can be authenticated through admissions without producing a custodian at trial. Failure to admit triggers fee-shifting under Rule 37(c)(2).  
2\. \*\*Narrowing.\*\* Date stamps, account numbers, mailing dates, and policy-version identifiers are routinely admitted to compress trial proof.  
3\. \*\*Pinning policy gaps.\*\* Defendants that admit the absence of a specific procedure (e.g., a statute-of-limitations scrub) effectively concede an element of the bona fide error defense under § 1692k(c). Cf. \*Jerman\*, 559 U.S. at 604–05.  
  
RFAs are not limited in number under the federal rules (some districts impose local-rule caps), but they must be drafted as discrete factual propositions, not compound or argumentative statements. \*See\* Fed. R. Civ. P. 36(a)(1).

\#\# 2. Plaintiff-Side RFAs to Debt Collector — TEMPLATE  
  
> \*\*TEMPLATE — REQUESTS FOR ADMISSION TO DEFENDANT \[Collector\]\*\*  
> Pursuant to Fed. R. Civ. P. 36, Plaintiff requests that Defendant admit or deny the following within thirty (30) days. A matter is admitted unless answered or objected to within the time provided. Each admission is for the purpose of this action only and is not an admission for any other purpose. "Account" means the alleged debt identified in paragraph \_\_ of the Complaint.  
  
\#\#\# A. Authentication of Letters and Recordings  
  
1\. Admit that the letter attached as Exhibit A was sent by You (or an agent acting on Your behalf) to Plaintiff on or about \[date\].  
2\. Admit that Exhibit A is a true and accurate copy of the letter Defendant sent to Plaintiff on \[date\].  
3\. Admit that the audio recording produced at Bates \[DEF-0001\] is a true and accurate recording of a telephone call between Defendant's agent \[name\] and Plaintiff on \[date\].  
4\. Admit that the transcript attached as Exhibit B is a true and accurate transcript of the recording produced at Bates \[DEF-0001\].  
5\. Admit that Defendant placed the telephone call recorded at Bates \[DEF-0001\] from telephone number \[\#\#\#\].  
  
\#\#\# B. Timeline of Communications  
  
6\. Admit that Defendant first communicated with Plaintiff concerning the Account on \[date\].  
7\. Admit that Defendant sent Plaintiff a validation notice under 12 C.F.R. § 1006.34 on \[date\].  
8\. Admit that on \[date\] Defendant received the letter attached as Exhibit C, in which Plaintiff disputed the Account.  
9\. Admit that Defendant communicated with Plaintiff concerning the Account on \[date\], a date after Defendant received Exhibit C.  
10\. Admit that Defendant did not mail or otherwise provide verification of the Account to Plaintiff before resuming collection following Exhibit C. (Targets § 1692g(b) overshadowing/continued-collection liability.)  
11\. Admit that Defendant placed \[number\] outbound telephone calls to Plaintiff between \[date\] and \[date\].  
12\. Admit that on \[date\] Defendant placed more than seven telephone calls to Plaintiff within a seven-day period. (Reg F § 1006.14(b)(2)(i) presumption.)  
  
\#\#\# C. Status of the Debt  
  
13\. Admit that the Account was charged off by \[original creditor\] on \[date\].  
14\. Admit that no payment has been credited to the Account since \[date\].  
15\. Admit that the statute of limitations applicable to the Account under \[state\] law is \[N\] years.  
16\. Admit that, as of \[date the lawsuit was filed/letter sent\], more than \[N\] years had elapsed since the last payment on the Account.  
17\. Admit that the Account was time-barred at the time Defendant sent the letter attached as Exhibit A. (Cf. \*Midland Funding v. Johnson\*, 137 S. Ct. at 1410.)  
18\. Admit that Plaintiff's obligations on the Account were discharged in a Chapter 7 bankruptcy proceeding closed on \[date\].  
19\. Admit that Defendant filed a proof of claim against Plaintiff in bankruptcy case \[number\] on \[date\].  
20\. Admit that the proof of claim referenced in the prior request asserted a debt that was time-barred under \[state\] law.  
21\. Admit that as of \[date\], Defendant did not possess any document signed by Plaintiff in connection with the Account.  
  
\#\#\# D. Validation Notice and Itemization (Reg F § 1006.34)  
  
22\. Admit that the validation notice attached as Exhibit D did not identify the "itemization date" as defined in 12 C.F.R. § 1006.34(b)(3).  
23\. Admit that Exhibit D did not separately state fees, interest, payments, and credits since the itemization date as required by 12 C.F.R. § 1006.34(c)(2)(v)–(viii).  
24\. Admit that Defendant did not send Plaintiff a validation notice meeting the requirements of 12 C.F.R. § 1006.34 before \[date\].  
25\. Admit that Defendant continued collection activity after receiving Plaintiff's written dispute identified in Exhibit C without first providing verification under 15 U.S.C. § 1692g(b).  
  
\#\#\# E. Policy Gaps (Pinning the Bona Fide Error Defense)  
  
26\. Admit that, as of \[date\], Defendant did not maintain a written policy requiring review of the statute of limitations before filing a collection lawsuit in \[state\].  
27\. Admit that, as of \[date\], Defendant did not maintain a written policy requiring scrubs for bankruptcy filings before each communication with a consumer.  
28\. Admit that the employee(s) who handled Plaintiff's Account did not receive training specific to 12 C.F.R. § 1006.14 (call frequency presumption) before the events alleged in the Complaint.  
29\. Admit that Defendant did not perform any audit of compliance with 12 C.F.R. § 1006.34 between \[date\] and \[date\].  
30\. Admit that Defendant has been the subject of consumer complaints filed with the CFPB Consumer Complaint Database alleging conduct similar to that alleged in the Complaint.  
  
\#\#\# F. Chain of Title  
  
31\. Admit that Defendant did not possess an executed bill of sale identifying the Account by account number on \[date\].  
32\. Admit that the "Affidavit of Sale" attached as Exhibit E refers to a portfolio rather than to the Account specifically.  
33\. Admit that no document in Defendant's possession identifies the consideration Defendant paid for the Account, as distinct from the portfolio in which the Account was acquired.  
34\. Admit that the seller of the Account made no representation or warranty that the Account was not time-barred at the time of sale.  
  
\#\#\# G. "Debt Collector" Status  
  
35\. Admit that more than 50% of Defendant's revenue during the four years preceding the Complaint was derived from the collection of consumer debts originated by another person. (\*Henson\*; \*Barbato\*.)  
36\. Admit that Defendant's principal purpose is the collection of consumer debts within the meaning of 15 U.S.C. § 1692a(6).  
37\. Admit that Defendant regularly collects debts owed or due another within the meaning of 15 U.S.C. § 1692a(6).  
38\. Admit that in collecting the Account, Defendant used a name other than its own that would indicate a third person was collecting the debt. (§ 1692a(6) creditor exception.)

\#\# 3. Defense-Side RFAs to Consumer Plaintiff — TEMPLATE  
  
> \*\*TEMPLATE — REQUESTS FOR ADMISSION TO PLAINTIFF\*\*  
> Pursuant to Fed. R. Civ. P. 36, Defendant requests that Plaintiff admit or deny the following within thirty (30) days.  
  
\#\#\# A. Status and Knowledge of the Debt  
  
1\. Admit that Plaintiff applied for and received the credit account ending in \[\#\#\#\#\] from \[original creditor\].  
2\. Admit that Plaintiff used the account ending in \[\#\#\#\#\] to make purchases or obtain credit.  
3\. Admit that Plaintiff received periodic billing statements on the account ending in \[\#\#\#\#\].  
4\. Admit that Plaintiff made at least one payment on the account ending in \[\#\#\#\#\].  
5\. Admit that Plaintiff did not pay the balance on the account ending in \[\#\#\#\#\] in full.  
6\. Admit that Plaintiff is not the victim of identity theft with respect to the account ending in \[\#\#\#\#\].  
7\. Admit that Plaintiff did not report identity theft on the account ending in \[\#\#\#\#\] to any law enforcement agency.  
  
\#\#\# B. Communications and Receipt  
  
8\. Admit that Plaintiff received the letter attached as Exhibit 1.  
9\. Admit that Plaintiff did not respond in writing to the letter attached as Exhibit 1 within thirty (30) days of receipt.  
10\. Admit that Plaintiff did not dispute the debt referenced in Exhibit 1 in writing within thirty (30) days of receipt.  
11\. Admit that Plaintiff answered the telephone call placed by Defendant on \[date\].  
12\. Admit that during the telephone call on \[date\] Plaintiff did not ask Defendant to cease communications.  
13\. Admit that Plaintiff did not request that Defendant communicate with Plaintiff only through counsel before \[date\].  
  
\#\#\# C. Damages and Standing (\*TransUnion v. Ramirez\*)  
  
14\. Admit that Plaintiff did not seek medical treatment for any condition attributed to Defendant's conduct.  
15\. Admit that Plaintiff did not consult a mental-health professional concerning any condition attributed to Defendant's conduct.  
16\. Admit that Plaintiff did not lose wages as a result of Defendant's conduct.  
17\. Admit that Plaintiff did not incur out-of-pocket expense in responding to Defendant's communications, other than postage.  
18\. Admit that no third party heard or saw any communication from Defendant concerning Plaintiff's debt before Plaintiff filed this lawsuit.  
19\. Admit that Plaintiff's credit score did not decrease as a result of any furnishing by Defendant to a consumer reporting agency.  
20\. Admit that Plaintiff suffered no concrete harm of the type traditionally recognized at common law as a result of the conduct alleged in the Complaint. (Targets the \*TransUnion v. Ramirez\*, 594 U.S. at 424–25, concrete-harm requirement.)  
  
\#\#\# D. Credit Repair and Litigation History  
  
21\. Admit that Plaintiff retained or communicated with a credit repair organization within the meaning of 15 U.S.C. § 1679a(3) at any time during the two years preceding this lawsuit.  
22\. Admit that Plaintiff has been a plaintiff in \[number\] prior consumer protection lawsuits in the past five years.  
23\. Admit that Plaintiff has previously filed an FDCPA lawsuit against another debt collector.  
24\. Admit that Plaintiff records telephone calls from debt collectors as a matter of practice.  
25\. Admit that Plaintiff did not request a copy of Plaintiff's credit report from any consumer reporting agency in connection with the conduct alleged in the Complaint.  
  
\#\#\# E. Other Debts and Pattern Evidence  
  
26\. Admit that Plaintiff currently owes one or more consumer debts to creditors other than the original creditor of the Account.  
27\. Admit that Plaintiff disputed \[number\] other consumer debts with consumer reporting agencies during the two years preceding the Complaint.  
28\. Admit that Plaintiff has previously sent the letter attached as Exhibit 2 (or a substantially similar form letter) to other debt collectors.  
  
\#\#\# F. Class-Certification Issues (Where Applicable)  
  
29\. Admit that Plaintiff does not have personal knowledge of the experiences of other consumers who received Defendant's letter form \[number\].  
30\. Admit that Plaintiff has not communicated with any other putative class member.  
31\. Admit that Plaintiff's communications with Defendant included an oral request to settle the Account.  
32\. Admit that Plaintiff has previously served as a class representative in another consumer protection action.

\#\# 4. Practice Notes  
  
\- \*\*Fee-shifting under Rule 37(c)(2).\*\* Where an opposing party fails to admit a matter later proven true, the propounding party may recover the reasonable expenses, including attorney's fees, incurred in making that proof. This is a powerful lever in FDCPA litigation where defendants reflexively deny.  
\- \*\*Compound RFAs.\*\* Each RFA must address a single fact. Compound requests are routinely objected to and parsed at the court's discretion. Draft narrowly.  
\- \*\*Authentication strategy.\*\* Pair RFA Section A above with Fed. R. Evid. 901 foundation requirements; admissions can also be packaged with stipulations for trial efficiency.  
\- \*\*Standing RFAs.\*\* Post-\*TransUnion v. Ramirez\*, the defense uses Section C aggressively to set up summary judgment. Plaintiffs should be prepared to articulate the concrete harm (intrusion upon seclusion, reputational injury, etc.) tying the bare statutory violation to common-law analogues.  
\- \*\*Bona fide error.\*\* Admissions under Plaintiff-Side Section E (Policy Gaps) cumulatively defeat the § 1692k(c) defense; sequence them carefully and pair with Rule 30(b)(6) deposition topics on the same subjects.
