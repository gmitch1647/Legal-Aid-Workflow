\# FDCPA Discovery — Rule 45 Third-Party Subpoenas  
  
\*\*Topic:\*\* Federal Rule 45 subpoenas duces tecum and ad testificandum in FDCPA litigation, covering original creditors, prior debt buyers, telecommunications carriers, consumer reporting agencies, employers, credit-repair organizations, third-party recipients of communications, and federal agencies (Touhy).  
\*\*Primary authority:\*\* Fed. R. Civ. P. 45; Fair Debt Collection Practices Act, 15 U.S.C. §§ 1692–1692p; Regulation F, 12 C.F.R. Part 1006; Stored Communications Act, 18 U.S.C. §§ 2701–2712; Right to Financial Privacy Act, 12 U.S.C. §§ 3401–3423; \*United States ex rel. Touhy v. Ragen\*, 340 U.S. 462 (1951); 12 C.F.R. Part 1070, subpart D (CFPB Touhy regulations).  
\*\*Key cases:\*\* \*Mount Hope Church v. Bash Back\!\*, 705 F.3d 418 (9th Cir. 2012) (Rule 45 reasonable steps); \*In re Subpoena Duces Tecum to AOL, LLC\*, 550 F. Supp. 2d 606 (E.D. Va. 2008) (SCA limits on civil subpoenas).  
\*\*Last updated:\*\* May 16, 2026.  
  
\---  
  
\#\# 1. Overview  
  
Rule 45 subpoenas fill the gaps left by party discovery in FDCPA litigation. Three structural rules anchor practice:  
  
\- \*\*Subpoena from the court where the action is pending.\*\* Fed. R. Civ. P. 45(a)(2). Compliance is "within 100 miles of where the person resides, is employed, or regularly transacts business in person" for testimony, Rule 45(c)(1)(A), or within the state for hearing/trial of a party officer, Rule 45(c)(1)(B).  
\- \*\*Notice to other parties before service.\*\* Rule 45(a)(4) requires service of the document subpoena on every party before service on the recipient.  
\- \*\*Quash/modify protections.\*\* Rule 45(d)(3) requires the issuing court, on timely motion, to quash subpoenas that fail to allow a reasonable time to comply, require travel beyond geographic limits, require disclosure of privileged or protected matter, or subject a person to undue burden.  
  
The targets below recur in FDCPA practice. Sample schedules of documents are provided as TEMPLATEs.

\#\# 2. Target 1 — Original Creditor (Non-Party)  
  
\*\*When to use.\*\* The debt buyer defendant does not possess full account-level documentation; the original creditor's records establish the balance, payment history, and contractual basis for fees/interest underlying § 1692e(2)(A) and § 1692f(1) claims.  
  
\> \*\*TEMPLATE — SCHEDULE A: DOCUMENTS REQUESTED FROM \[Original Creditor\]\*\*  
\>  
\> 1. The application, terms-and-conditions, cardmember or loan agreement governing account ending \[\#\#\#\#\] in the name of \[Consumer\], including every amendment.  
\> 2. Every periodic statement issued on the account from origination through charge-off.  
\> 3. The complete payment history, in machine-readable form, for the account.  
\> 4. The charge-off statement and any internal charge-off ledger entry.  
\> 5. Every Document reflecting any dispute by \[Consumer\] of the account.  
\> 6. Every Document reflecting any bankruptcy, identity-theft, deceased, or fraud notation on the account.  
\> 7. Every recording of any inbound or outbound telephone call between You and \[Consumer\] concerning the account.  
\> 8. Every Document reflecting the sale or transfer of the account, including the bill of sale, portfolio schedule entry identifying the account, and the purchase-and-sale agreement.  
\> 9. Every Document reflecting communications with \[Defendant Debt Buyer\] concerning the account, including post-sale dispute or remediation correspondence.  
\> 10. The complete CRA furnishing history (Metro 2 records) for the account.  
  
\*\*Notes.\*\* Originals are commonly maintained in core servicing systems; specify acceptable file formats and certify under Fed. R. Evid. 902(11)/(12). Consider a Right to Financial Privacy Act analysis if the consumer is uninvolved in the subpoena (RFPA generally applies only to federal-agency requests but state analogues exist).
