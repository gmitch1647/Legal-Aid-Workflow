\# FDCPA Discovery — Damages Deep Dive  
  
\*\*Topic:\*\* Damages discovery in FDCPA cases — actual, statutory, class statutory, fees; standing under \*TransUnion v. Ramirez\*.  
\*\*FDCPA cross-references:\*\* 15 U.S.C. §§ 1692k(a)(1) (actual damages), 1692k(a)(2)(A) (individual statutory), 1692k(a)(2)(B) (class statutory), 1692k(a)(3) (fees), 1692k(b) (factors).  
\*\*FRCP cross-references:\*\* Rules 26(a)(1)(A)(iii), 26(b)(1), 33, 34, 36, 45; FRE 401, 702, 1006.  
\*\*Standing:\*\* \*TransUnion LLC v. Ramirez\*, 594 U.S. 413 (2021); \*Spokeo, Inc. v. Robins\*, 578 U.S. 330 (2016).  
\*\*Last updated:\*\* May 16, 2026.  
  
\---  
  
\#\# 1. The FDCPA Damages Framework  
  
\#\#\# 1.1 Actual damages — § 1692k(a)(1)  
  
A debt collector is liable for "any actual damage sustained by such person as a result of \[the FDCPA violation\]." 15 U.S.C. § 1692k(a)(1). The statute does not define "actual damage." Courts have included:  
  
\- Emotional distress damages.  
\- Out-of-pocket economic loss (amounts paid that were not owed; postage; phone; therapy copays).  
\- Lost wages from harassment-induced time away.  
\- Loss of credit / increased rates (rare; usually FCRA territory but possible on § 1692e(8) facts).  
  
\#\#\# 1.2 Statutory damages — individual — § 1692k(a)(2)(A)  
  
"Such additional damages as the court may allow, but not exceeding $1,000." This is a \*\*per-action\*\* cap, not a per-violation cap. \*Wright v. Fin. Serv. of Norwalk, Inc.\*, 22 F.3d 647 (6th Cir. 1994) (en banc); \*Goodmann v. People's Bank\*, 209 F. App'x 111, 113-14 (3d Cir. 2006).  
  
\#\#\# 1.3 Statutory damages — class — § 1692k(a)(2)(B)  
  
For class actions, "such amount for each named plaintiff as could be recovered under subparagraph (A), and . . . such amount as the court may allow for all other class members, without regard to a minimum individual recovery, \*\*not to exceed the lesser of $500,000 or 1 per centum of the net worth of the debt collector\*\*."  
  
\#\#\# 1.4 Attorney's fees and costs — § 1692k(a)(3)  
  
A successful consumer recovers "the costs of the action, together with a reasonable attorney's fee as determined by the court." Mandatory on a finding of any violation. \*De Jesus v. Banco Popular de Puerto Rico\*, 951 F.2d 3 (1st Cir. 1991); \*Graziano v. Harrison\*, 950 F.2d 107 (3d Cir. 1991).  
  
\#\#\# 1.5 Statutory factors — § 1692k(b)  
  
In assessing statutory damages, the court considers:  
  
\- For individual actions: "the frequency and persistence of noncompliance by the debt collector, the nature of such noncompliance, and the extent to which such noncompliance was intentional."  
\- For class actions: same factors plus "the resources of the debt collector, the number of persons adversely affected, and the extent to which the debt collector's noncompliance was intentional."  
  
\#\#\# 1.6 No punitive damages under the FDCPA  
  
The FDCPA does not authorize punitive damages. The § 1692k(a)(2)(A)/(B) caps occupy the punitive field. \*See Crossley v. Lieberman\*, 868 F.2d 566, 571 (3d Cir. 1989) (statutory damages "in the nature of punitive damages"). State-law analogue claims (UDAP, mini-FDCPAs, intentional infliction of emotional distress, defamation, invasion of privacy) \*\*may\*\* permit punitive damages and should be pleaded if the facts support and the forum allows.

\#\# 2. Actual-Damages Discovery — Emotional Distress  
  
\#\#\# 2.1 Recoverability  
  
Emotional-distress damages are recoverable under § 1692k(a)(1) in every circuit. Standard varies:  
  
\- \*\*Without separate corroboration\*\*: 11th Circuit (\*Minnifield v. Johnson & Freedman, LLC\*, 448 F. App'x 914 (11th Cir. 2011) (per curiam)) — testimony alone may suffice if credible and detailed.  
\- \*\*Some corroboration / heightened\*\*: 7th Circuit applies the \*Sarver v. Experian\* standard from defamation/FCRA (corroborating evidence required for more than nominal ED). \*Aiello v. Providian Fin. Corp.\*, 239 F.3d 876 (7th Cir. 2001).  
\- \*\*Garden variety allowed without expert\*\*: most circuits permit a plaintiff to testify to "garden variety" emotional distress without medical or psychological corroboration; expert testimony required for "severe" or "egregious" claims.  
  
\#\#\# 2.2 Discovery on emotional distress (defense side)  
  
\- Interrogatory: "Describe with particularity all emotional distress you contend you suffered as a result of the alleged conduct, including symptoms, onset, duration, severity, and any treatment received."  
\- RFP: "All documents, including medical records, therapist records, journals, e-mails, texts, and social-media posts, that reflect emotional distress alleged in this action."  
\- HIPAA authorization: most defendants seek a HIPAA-compliant authorization for any mental-health provider seen in the 5 years before and through the alleged conduct.
