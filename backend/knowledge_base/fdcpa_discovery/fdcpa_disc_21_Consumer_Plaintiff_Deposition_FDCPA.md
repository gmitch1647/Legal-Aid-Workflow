\# FDCPA Discovery — Defense Deposition of the Consumer Plaintiff  
  
\*\*Topic:\*\* Defense-side deposition of the consumer plaintiff in an FDCPA case.  
\*\*FRCP cross-references:\*\* Rules 26(b)(1), 30, 34, 45; FRE 401, 403, 404, 608, 611, 612, 801(d)(2).  
\*\*FDCPA cross-references:\*\* 15 U.S.C. §§ 1692a (consumer; debt), 1692c (communications), 1692d (harassment), 1692e (false/misleading), 1692g (validation), 1692k(a) (damages and statutory damages).  
\*\*Standing:\*\* \*TransUnion LLC v. Ramirez\*, 594 U.S. 413 (2021); FDCPA-specific lower-court framework (\*Pierre v. Midland Credit Mgmt., Inc.\*, 29 F.4th 934 (7th Cir. 2022); \*Maddox v. Bank of N.Y. Mellon Tr. Co., N.A.\*, 19 F.4th 58 (2d Cir. 2021); \*Trichell v. Midland Credit Mgmt., Inc.\*, 964 F.3d 990 (11th Cir. 2020)).  
\*\*Last updated:\*\* May 16, 2026.  
  
\---  
  
\#\# 1. Pre-Deposition Document Review  
  
Before deposing the consumer, review:  
  
1\. \*\*The complaint\*\* — every paragraph, every allegation. The plaintiff will be locked to it (or contradiction creates impeachment).  
2\. \*\*Initial disclosures\*\* — witnesses, damages computation (FRCP 26(a)(1)(A)(iii)).  
3\. \*\*Plaintiff's interrogatory answers and RFA responses.\*\*  
4\. \*\*Plaintiff's document production\*\* — phone records, call logs, voicemails, screen captures, texts, e-mails, social-media posts, medical records, tax returns (if economic damages claimed), pay stubs (if lost wages claimed).  
5\. \*\*Plaintiff's call recordings\*\* (consumer-recorded, if produced).  
6\. \*\*The defendant's own records\*\* — account notes, recorded calls, dunning letters, mailing logs.  
7\. \*\*Third-party subpoena returns\*\* — medical, prior credit-counseling or credit-repair organization records, prior employer (if work calls / disruption alleged), other debt-collector defendants (CCRO sweeps).  
8\. \*\*PACER hits on plaintiff\*\* — prior bankruptcies, prior FDCPA / FCRA / TCPA / state-FDCPA actions, other consumer-protection actions; appearances as a witness; criminal records to the extent admissible under FRE 609.  
9\. \*\*Social-media review\*\* — Facebook, Instagram, LinkedIn (publicly available only; no false-friending).  
10\. \*\*The original creditor's records\*\* if obtainable — original application, statements, payment history.  
  
\---  
  
\#\# 2. Themes  
  
A defense deposition of a consumer plaintiff serves three coordinated functions:  
  
\- \*\*Standing\*\* — does the plaintiff allege concrete, particularized injury under \*TransUnion v. Ramirez\*?  
\- \*\*Damages\*\* — what are the actual damages, if any? Is emotional distress corroborated? Is there economic loss?  
\- \*\*Credibility\*\* — is the plaintiff a "professional plaintiff"? Inconsistencies with documents?  
  
Plan to spend most of the day on damages and standing, not liability. Liability is largely in the defendant's documents; the plaintiff's testimony rarely changes it.

\#\# 3. Background, Employment, Financial Literacy  
  
\#\#\# 3.1 Background  
  
\- Full name, all prior names, aliases, maiden names (relevant to skip-tracing identity issues and prior litigation).  
\- Date of birth, social-security-number (last 4 only — protective order; FRCP 5.2).  
\- Current and prior addresses for last 10 years (skip-tracing accuracy, § 1692b location info issues, mail-vendor verification).  
\- Family — spouse, children at home, others receiving calls in household (for third-party-disclosure under § 1692c(b) claims).  
\- Phone numbers — all phone numbers in last 5 years, including landline; date acquired; whether listed publicly.  
  
\#\#\# 3.2 Employment  
  
\- Current employer, position, dates, supervisor.  
\- Prior employment for 10 years.  
\- Workplace policy on personal calls (relevant to § 1692c(a)(3) calls-at-work claims).  
\- Hours / shift / lunch availability.  
\- Whether plaintiff received any debt-collector calls at work — from any collector — and how the workplace handled them.  
  
\#\#\# 3.3 Financial literacy  
  
\- Education.  
\- Whether plaintiff has ever worked in financial services, banking, collections, debt buying, mortgage, credit reporting, credit repair, legal services.  
\- Familiarity with credit reports, dispute processes, the FDCPA.  
\- Whether plaintiff regularly pulls credit reports.  
\- Prior credit accounts; current credit accounts.  
\- Prior bankruptcies; pending bankruptcies; prior workouts/settlements; debt-management plans.
