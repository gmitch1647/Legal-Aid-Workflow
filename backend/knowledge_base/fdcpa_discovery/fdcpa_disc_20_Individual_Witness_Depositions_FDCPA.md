\# FDCPA Discovery — Individual Witness Depositions  
  
\*\*Topic:\*\* Deposition strategy and outlines for individual fact witnesses in FDCPA cases (collectors, compliance officers, letter-approval committees, IT/records custodians, collection-firm associates, document custodians).  
\*\*FRCP cross-references:\*\* Rules 26(b)(1), 30, 32, 34, 37, 45; FRE 602, 612, 801(d)(2), 803(6).  
\*\*FDCPA cross-references:\*\* 15 U.S.C. §§ 1692a (definitions), 1692c (communications), 1692d (harassment), 1692e (false/misleading), 1692f (unfair), 1692g (validation), 1692k(c) (bona fide error).  
\*\*Reg F cross-references:\*\* 12 C.F.R. §§ 1006.6, 1006.14, 1006.18, 1006.22, 1006.26, 1006.30, 1006.34, 1006.38.  
\*\*Last updated:\*\* May 16, 2026.  
  
\---  
  
\#\# 1. Overview — When to Depose an Individual  
  
In FDCPA cases, individual (non-30(b)(6)) depositions are most productive in five situations:  
  
1\. \*\*The actual caller / letter signer\*\* named in the record. Personal knowledge of the conduct at issue.  
2\. \*\*The bona fide error witness\*\* — compliance officer or compliance manager who designed the "procedures reasonably adapted to avoid such error" under 15 U.S.C. § 1692k(c).  
3\. \*\*The letter approval committee\*\* (or "compliance committee," "legal review committee") — in form-letter cases where the standardized letter was reviewed by counsel or a committee.  
4\. \*\*The IT / records custodian\*\* — call recording retention, dialer logs, account notes (Latitude, Columbia Ultimate, FACS, FICS, etc.), ESI preservation.  
5\. \*\*The collection law firm associate or paralegal\*\* — for \*Heintz v. Jenkins\*, 514 U.S. 291 (1995), meaningful-attorney-involvement claims under § 1692e(3) and (10).  
  
Each is distinct from a 30(b)(6) corporate representative (covered separately in file 17). Individual depositions test personal knowledge, contradiction of 30(b)(6) testimony, and credibility. They are also the principal vehicle for testing the \*bona fide error\* defense, which the defendant bears the burden of proving (15 U.S.C. § 1692k(c); \*Reichert v. Nat'l Credit Sys., Inc.\*, 531 F.3d 1002 (9th Cir. 2008)).

\#\# 2. The Debt-Collector Employee Deposition (Caller / Letter Signer)  
  
\#\#\# 2.1 Pre-deposition essentials  
  
\- Account notes (full export, not summary) — every screen and code.  
\- Call recordings — all calls on the account, not just the alleged violations.  
\- The collector's individual training file, performance reviews, write-ups, QA scores.  
\- The collector's call statistics — daily call volume, daily contact rates, daily promise-to-pay (PTP) statistics, "right party contact" rates.  
\- Compensation records — base salary, commission/incentive structure, any bonus tied to collections.  
\- Any prior FDCPA complaints involving this collector.  
\- The script(s), call flow, or "talk-off" the collector was instructed to follow.  
  
\#\#\# 2.2 Topic outline  
  
1\. Identity / employment history.  
2\. Training — initial hire training (hours, topics, FDCPA-specific content); ongoing training; certifications (ACA International credentialing if any).  
3\. Knowledge of FDCPA — specific knowledge tests; ability to identify §§ 1692c, 1692d, 1692e, 1692g requirements unprompted.  
4\. Knowledge of Reg F — 7-in-7 call frequency presumption (12 C.F.R. § 1006.14(b)(2)); validation notice content (§ 1006.34); electronic communications rules (§§ 1006.6(d), 1006.42).  
5\. Scripts and talk-off — what the collector says first, how disputes are handled, how cease requests are handled, how attorney-representation is handled.  
6\. Supervision — who the supervisor was; QA review process; whether this call was QA'd.  
7\. Daily call volume and pressure — daily quotas, "stat shifts," dialer-pacing.  
8\. Dispute handling — what the collector does when a consumer says "this isn't my debt," "I dispute this," or "I want validation."  
9\. The specific account at issue — first contact, every contact, the collector's recollection vs. notes vs. recordings.  
10\. The specific violation conduct — the collector's understanding of what was said and why.  
11\. Compensation and incentives — did the conduct increase pay?  
12\. Post-call documentation — what gets noted, what does not.  
13\. Discipline — was the collector disciplined after the complaint?
