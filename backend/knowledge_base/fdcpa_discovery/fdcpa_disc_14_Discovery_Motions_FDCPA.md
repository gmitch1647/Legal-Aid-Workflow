\# FDCPA Discovery: Common Discovery Motions  
  
\*\*Topic:\*\* Discovery motions in FDCPA litigation — motions to compel, for protective order, for sanctions, and to quash  
\*\*Cross-references:\*\* Fed. R. Civ. P. 26, 30, 34, 37, 45; 15 U.S.C. §§ 1692c–1692f; 12 C.F.R. §§ 1006.14, 1006.30, 1006.34, 1006.100  
\*\*Last updated:\*\* May 16, 2026  
\*\*Source discipline:\*\* Public-domain primary sources only (FRCP, published federal opinions, CFPB guidance)  
  
\---  
  
\#\# Overview  
  
FDCPA discovery generates a recurring set of motions practice. The dominant lines are:  
  
1\. Plaintiff motions to compel call recordings, account documentation, form letter libraries, and other-consumer complaint records, against defense responses pleading "system limitations," "retention policy," or "burden."  
2\. Defense motions for protective order against overbroad plaintiff requests, particularly for plaintiff financial history, employment records, and emotional-distress treatment.  
3\. Plaintiff motions for spoliation sanctions when call recordings or account notes have been destroyed.  
4\. Rule 45 motions to quash third-party subpoenas served by defendants on plaintiff's family, employer, or medical providers.  
  
This memorandum surveys each motion category, supplies the operative standard, and provides skeletal brief structures with the controlling case law.  
  
\---  
  
\#\# I. Motions to Compel  
  
\#\#\# A. Call Recordings Withheld for "System Limitations" or "Retention Policy"  
  
\*\*Recurring fact pattern.\*\* Plaintiff requests all recordings of calls to or from plaintiff. Defendant responds: "Recordings have been overwritten under our retention policy of \[60/90/180\] days." Plaintiff thereupon learns that some recordings exist and others do not.  
  
\*\*Standard.\*\* Rule 34 obligates production of "responsive items in the responding party's possession, custody, or control." A retention policy that destroys recordings post-litigation hold triggers Rule 37(e) spoliation analysis. \*See Konica Minolta Bus. Sols., U.S.A., Inc. v. Lowery Corp.\*, 2016 WL 4537847 (E.D. Mich.). Reg F now requires retention of all records evidencing compliance, including telephone call recordings if made, for at least three years after the last collection activity. 12 C.F.R. § 1006.100. A "retention policy" of less than three years post-November-30-2021 violates Reg F on its face.  
  
\*\*Brief structure.\*\*  
\- I. Introduction. Plaintiff seeks recordings central to the harassment claim under § 1692d.  
\- II. Background. Defendant admits some recordings exist; defendant claims others were destroyed per a 60-day retention policy.  
\- III. Argument.  
 - A. Reg F requires three-year retention of records evidencing compliance. 12 C.F.R. § 1006.100(a). To the extent recordings were destroyed within the retention period, Defendant has violated Reg F.  
 - B. The litigation hold attached at the latest upon Defendant's notice of plaintiff's dispute or representation. \*Goodman v. Praxair Servs., Inc.\*, 632 F. Supp. 2d 494 (D. Md. 2009).  
 - C. Defendant must search affirmatively, including backup tapes and dialer system logs.  
 - D. To the extent recordings are unavailable, Defendant must produce a sworn declaration identifying who searched, what systems were searched, and what was found.  
\- IV. Relief. Order compelling production within fourteen days; affirmative declaration regarding destroyed recordings; reservation of spoliation arguments.  
  
\*\*Key authorities.\*\* Fed. R. Civ. P. 34(a)(1); 12 C.F.R. § 1006.100; \*Konica\*, 2016 WL 4537847; \*Goodman\*, 632 F. Supp. 2d 494.  
  
\#\#\# B. ACDV Records and Furnisher Communications (FDCPA/FCRA Overlap)  
  
Where the defendant has reported the debt to a CRA and the consumer disputed it, the Automated Consumer Dispute Verification (ACDV) records show what was investigated and what was reported back. These records are routinely sought in FDCPA actions where § 1692e(8) is alleged ("communicating . . . credit information which is known or which should be known to be false").  
  
\*\*Brief structure.\*\*  
\- I. ACDV records are responsive to RFP no. \_\_\_.  
\- II. ACDV records are stored in e-OSCAR and are within Defendant's control as a furnisher. \*Cushman v. Trans Union Corp.\*, 115 F.3d 220 (3d Cir. 1997) (furnisher investigation duties).  
\- III. Defendant's response that ACDVs are "with the CRA" misrepresents the e-OSCAR architecture; the furnisher's transmission record is in Defendant's possession.  
\- IV. Relief. Order compelling production of complete e-OSCAR records for each dispute submitted concerning Plaintiff.
