# Individual FDCPA Complaint Drafting  
  
**Topic:** Claim-by-claim drafting guide for individual FDCPA complaints  
**FRCP Cross-References:** Rules 8(a), 9(b), 10(c), 12(b)(6), 38  
**FDCPA Cross-References:** 15 U.S.C. §§ 1692b, 1692c, 1692d, 1692e, 1692f, 1692g, 1692i, 1692k  
**Regulation F Cross-References:** 12 C.F.R. §§ 1006.6, 1006.14, 1006.18, 1006.22, 1006.26, 1006.34, 1006.38  
**Last Updated:** May 16, 2026  
**Sources:** Public-domain primary materials only (FRCP, FDCPA statute, Regulation F, published federal opinions, CFPB/FTC public guidance)

## 1. Introduction  
  
This document provides per-claim drafting templates and sample language for individual FDCPA complaints. Each section covers: (a) statutory elements; (b) sample factual allegations; (c) post-*TransUnion* concrete-harm allegations; (d) damages allegations. Strategy considerations are in `fdcpa_plead_01`. Class allegations are in `fdcpa_plead_03`. Full sample complaints are in `fdcpa_plead_04`.

## 2. Section 1692c — Communications  
  
### 2.1 § 1692c(a)(1) — Inconvenient Time or Place  
  
**Elements:**  
1. Defendant is a debt collector;  
2. Plaintiff is a consumer;  
3. Defendant communicated with the consumer in connection with collection of a debt;  
4. The communication was at a time or place known or which should be known to be inconvenient;  
5. Default presumption: before 8:00 a.m. or after 9:00 p.m. in the consumer's local time.  
  
**Sample factual allegations (TEMPLATE — ADAPT TO CASE):**  
  
> 25. On [date], at approximately 7:14 a.m. local time, Defendant placed a telephone call to Plaintiff's cellular telephone, number ending [xxxx], for the purpose of collecting the Debt.  
>  
> 26. At all relevant times, Plaintiff resided in [City, State], in the [Eastern/Central/etc.] Time Zone, as reflected in Defendant's account records.  
>  
> 27. Defendant placed similar early-morning calls to Plaintiff on [dates], each before 8:00 a.m. local time. Call detail is attached as Exhibit A.  
  
**Concrete-harm allegations (post-*TransUnion*):**  
  
> 28. Defendant's early-morning calls disrupted Plaintiff's sleep, caused anxiety, and constituted an unreasonable intrusion upon Plaintiff's seclusion analogous to the common-law tort of intrusion upon seclusion. See *TransUnion LLC v. Ramirez*, 594 U.S. 413, 425 (2021) (recognizing intrusion-upon-seclusion analogs as concrete harms).  
  
### 2.2 § 1692c(a)(2) — Communication After Notice of Attorney Representation  
  
**Elements:**  
1. Debt collector knows consumer is represented by an attorney with respect to the debt;  
2. Collector can readily ascertain the attorney's name and address;  
3. Collector communicates with the consumer rather than the attorney;  
4. Attorney has not failed to respond within a reasonable time or consented.  
  
### 2.3 § 1692c(a)(3) — Communication at the Workplace  
  
**Elements:**  
1. Debt collector knows or has reason to know that consumer's employer prohibits such communications;  
2. Collector contacts consumer at workplace.  
  
### 2.4 § 1692c(b) — Communications with Third Parties  
  
**Elements:**  
1. Debt collector communicates with a person other than the consumer (or the consumer's attorney, consumer reporting agency, creditor, creditor's attorney, or debt collector's own attorney);  
2. In connection with the collection of a debt;  
3. Without consumer's prior consent given directly to the debt collector or express permission of a court.  
  
**Sample factual allegations:**  
  
> 30. On [date], Defendant's representative spoke by telephone with Plaintiff's [neighbor/parent/employer], [Name], stating: "I'm calling about a serious legal matter involving [Plaintiff]. She needs to call me back at [number] today."  
>  
> 31. Plaintiff did not give Defendant prior consent to discuss the Debt with [Name], and no court order authorized the communication.  
  
**Concrete-harm allegations:**  
  
> 32. As a result of Defendant's disclosure, Plaintiff suffered embarrassment, damage to her standing in the community, and emotional distress closely analogous to the common-law tort of public disclosure of private facts. See *TransUnion*, 594 U.S. at 425.  
  
**Note on letter vendors:** In *Hunstein v. Preferred Collection & Mgmt. Servs., Inc.*, 48 F.4th 1236 (11th Cir. 2022) (en banc), the Eleventh Circuit held that transmittal of consumer information to a third-party mail vendor did not cause concrete harm sufficient for Article III standing. Counsel pleading vendor-disclosure theories in federal court should anticipate this holding. State-court filings may avoid the *Hunstein* problem.

## 3. Section 1692d — Harassment or Abuse  
  
### 3.1 General Prohibition  
  
> *"A debt collector may not engage in any conduct the natural consequence of which is to harass, oppress, or abuse any person in connection with the collection of a debt."* 15 U.S.C. § 1692d.  
  
### 3.2 § 1692d(5) — Repeated or Continuous Telephone Calls  
  
**Elements:**  
1. Telephone calls (or causing telephone to ring);  
2. Repeated or continuous;  
3. With intent to annoy, abuse, or harass.  
  
**Regulation F call-frequency safe harbor (12 C.F.R. § 1006.14(b)):**  
- More than seven calls in seven consecutive days to a particular consumer about a particular debt creates a rebuttable presumption of violation;  
- Or a call within seven days of a telephone conversation with the consumer about the debt.  
  
**Sample factual allegations:**  
  
> 35. Between [start date] and [end date], a period of seven consecutive days, Defendant placed twelve (12) telephone calls to Plaintiff's cellular telephone in connection with the Debt. Call detail attached as Exhibit B.  
>  
> 36. Defendant's call frequency exceeded the rebuttable presumption of harassment established by 12 C.F.R. § 1006.14(b)(2)(i).  
>  
> 37. Defendant continued calling after Plaintiff told Defendant's representative on [date] to "stop calling me."  
  
**Concrete-harm allegations:**  
  
> 38. Defendant's repeated calls invaded Plaintiff's privacy, disrupted her workday, caused anxiety and frustration, and constituted an intrusion upon seclusion sufficient to satisfy *TransUnion*. See, e.g., *Foti v. NCO Fin. Sys., Inc.*, 424 F. Supp. 2d 643 (S.D.N.Y. 2006); *Pierre v. Midland Credit Mgmt., Inc.*, 29 F.4th 934 (7th Cir. 2022) (distinguishing concrete harassment from bare informational injury).  
  
### 3.3 § 1692d(2) — Obscene or Profane Language  
  
Quote the language verbatim or describe with specificity. Sanitization for filing purposes can use brackets ("[expletive]").  
  
### 3.4 § 1692d(6) — Failure to Disclose Identity  
  
Plead the specific call in which the collector failed to meaningfully identify itself.

## 4. Section 1692e — False, Deceptive, or Misleading Representations  
  
### 4.1 General Framework  
  
> *"A debt collector may not use any false, deceptive, or misleading representation or means in connection with the collection of any debt."* 15 U.S.C. § 1692e.  
  
Section 1692e is non-exhaustive; the sixteen enumerated subsections are examples, not limits.  
  
### 4.2 Least-Sophisticated-Consumer Standard  
  
The majority rule is that representations are evaluated from the perspective of the least-sophisticated consumer (or, in the Seventh Circuit and some others, the "unsophisticated consumer"). See *Avila v. Riexinger & Assocs., LLC*, 817 F.3d 72 (2d Cir. 2016) (collection letter stating current balance, without disclosing that interest and fees may increase the balance, can be misleading to least-sophisticated consumer); *Bartlett v. Heibl*, 128 F.3d 497 (7th Cir. 1997) (collection letter stating consumer "has thirty days" to dispute while simultaneously demanding payment within one week is misleading).  
  
### 4.3 § 1692e(2)(A) — False Representation of Character, Amount, or Legal Status  
  
**Elements:**  
1. Debt collector makes a representation;  
2. About the character, amount, or legal status of the debt;  
3. The representation is false.  
  
**Common applications:**  
- Misstated balance;  
- Failure to disclose accruing interest (*Avila* problem);  
- Representation that time-barred debt is legally enforceable (see § 6 below);  
- Misidentification of the creditor.  
  
**Sample factual allegations:**  
  
> 42. On [date], Defendant mailed Plaintiff a collection letter (Exhibit C) stating that the "Current Balance" of the Debt was $4,732.18.  
>  
> 43. The letter did not disclose that interest and fees were continuing to accrue, nor that the balance would increase after the date of the letter.  
>  
> 44. The least-sophisticated consumer could reasonably understand the "Current Balance" to be a static amount payable to satisfy the Debt in full.  
  
### 4.4 § 1692e(5) — Threat of Action That Cannot Be Taken  
  
Plead the specific threat (e.g., suit, garnishment, arrest) and facts demonstrating the action could not legally be taken or was not intended.  
  
### 4.5 § 1692e(10) — Use of False Representation or Deceptive Means  
  
Catch-all subsection often pleaded with § 1692e(2)(A).  
  
### 4.6 § 1692e(11) — Mini-Miranda  
  
Initial communication must disclose that the communication is from a debt collector and is an attempt to collect a debt; subsequent communications must disclose they are from a debt collector. Plead the specific communication lacking the disclosure.  
  
### 4.7 Concrete-Harm Allegations  
  
> 46. As a result of Defendant's misleading statement, Plaintiff was confused about the amount owed, paid $[amount] in reliance on the misrepresentation, and incurred costs in attempting to verify the Debt. See *Trichell v. Midland Credit Mgmt., Inc.*, 964 F.3d 990 (11th Cir. 2020) (bare confusion without action insufficient for standing); *Maddox v. Bank of N.Y. Mellon Tr. Co., N.A.*, 19 F.4th 58 (2d Cir. 2021) (same).

## 5. Section 1692f — Unfair Practices  
  
> *"A debt collector may not use unfair or unconscionable means to collect or attempt to collect any debt."* 15 U.S.C. § 1692f.  
  
### 5.1 § 1692f(1) — Collection of Amount Not Authorized  
  
**Elements:**  
1. Collection or attempt to collect any amount (including any interest, fee, charge, or expense);  
2. Not expressly authorized by the agreement creating the debt or permitted by law.  
  
### 5.2 § 1692f(6) — Nonjudicial Action Threats  
  
After *Obduskey v. McCarthy & Holthus LLP*, 586 U.S. 466 (2019), § 1692f(6) is the only FDCPA provision that applies to entities engaged exclusively in nonjudicial foreclosure.  
  
### 5.3 § 1692f(8) — Marking the Outside of Envelope  
  
Plead the visible markings (account number, "DEBT COLLECTOR," etc.) and attach photograph or scanned image.

## 6. Section 1692g — Validation Notice  
  
### 6.1 Statutory Validation Requirements  
  
Within five days after the initial communication, the debt collector must send a written notice containing:  
  
1. The amount of the debt;  
2. The name of the creditor to whom the debt is owed;  
3. A statement that the consumer has 30 days to dispute the debt or any portion;  
4. A statement that if the consumer disputes within 30 days, the collector will obtain verification and mail it;  
5. A statement that, upon written request within 30 days, the collector will provide the name and address of the original creditor if different.  
  
15 U.S.C. § 1692g(a).  
  
### 6.2 Regulation F § 1006.34 — Model Validation Notice  
  
12 C.F.R. § 1006.34 establishes detailed content and form requirements for the validation information. The "Model Form B-3" provides a safe harbor. Counsel should compare the defendant's notice to Model Form B-3 and plead each discrepancy.  
  
**Required information under § 1006.34(c):**  
- Debt collector communication disclosure;  
- Information about the debt;  
- Consumer protection information (30-day dispute right, request for original-creditor name and address);  
- Consumer-response information (tear-off or directions).  
  
### 6.3 Sample Factual Allegations  
  
> 50. Defendant's initial written communication to Plaintiff, dated [date] (Exhibit D), was Defendant's first written notice within the meaning of 15 U.S.C. § 1692g and 12 C.F.R. § 1006.34.  
>  
> 51. The letter failed to disclose [the name of the original creditor / the itemization date / the 30-day dispute notice / etc.] as required by § 1692g(a)(_) and § 1006.34(c)(_).  
>  
> 52. The letter overshadowed the 30-day validation period by demanding payment "WITHIN 10 DAYS" in bold red type at the top of the page, in violation of § 1692g(b). See *Bartlett v. Heibl*, 128 F.3d 497 (7th Cir. 1997).  
  
### 6.4 Concrete-Harm Allegations  
  
> 53. As a result of the defective validation notice, Plaintiff was confused about her dispute rights, failed to dispute the Debt within 30 days, paid $[amount] she did not in fact owe, and incurred attorney's fees to investigate. See *Pierre*, 29 F.4th at 938–39 (requiring downstream consequence).

## 7. Section 1692b — Acquisition of Location Information  
  
When communicating with third parties to acquire location information, the debt collector must:  
  
1. Identify himself;  
2. State that he is confirming or correcting location information;  
3. Identify his employer only if expressly requested;  
4. Not state that the consumer owes any debt;  
5. Not communicate more than once unless requested or believes earlier response was erroneous;  
6. Not communicate by postcard;  
7. Not use symbols or language on the envelope indicating debt collection;  
8. Once the collector knows the consumer is represented by an attorney, communicate only with the attorney.  
  
**Sample factual allegations:**  
  
> 56. On [date], Defendant's representative called Plaintiff's mother and stated: "I'm calling about [Plaintiff]'s overdue account with [Creditor]. Do you know how to reach her?"  
>  
> 57. The statement violated 15 U.S.C. § 1692b(2) because it disclosed the existence of a debt to a third party.

## 8. Section 1692i — Venue  
  
A debt collector who brings any legal action on a debt against a consumer must bring it (other than in real-property actions) only in the judicial district:  
  
1. In which the consumer signed the contract sued upon; or  
2. In which the consumer resides at the commencement of the action.  
  
15 U.S.C. § 1692i(a).  
  
**Sample factual allegations:**  
  
> 60. On [date], Defendant filed a state-court collection action against Plaintiff in the [County] District Court, Civil Action No. [number] (Exhibit E).  
>  
> 61. At commencement of the action, Plaintiff resided in [County], [State] — a different judicial district from the venue in which Defendant filed.  
>  
> 62. The contract giving rise to the Debt was signed in [County], [State] — also a different judicial district from the filing venue.  
>  
> 63. Defendant's filing venue violated 15 U.S.C. § 1692i(a)(2).

## 9. Anchor Cases — Quick Reference  
  
- **Heintz v. Jenkins**, 514 U.S. 291 (1995) — attorneys engaged in litigation collection are "debt collectors."  
- **Jerman v. Carlisle**, 559 U.S. 573 (2010) — bona fide error defense does not cover mistakes of law.  
- **Henson v. Santander**, 582 U.S. 79 (2017) — debt buyer collecting on its own account not a "debt collector" under "owed another" prong (principal-purpose prong unaddressed).  
- **Obduskey v. McCarthy & Holthus**, 586 U.S. 466 (2019) — entities engaged solely in nonjudicial foreclosure are not "debt collectors" except as to § 1692f(6).  
- **Rotkiske v. Klemm**, 589 U.S. 8 (2019) — limitations runs from violation, not discovery.  
- **Avila v. Riexinger & Assocs.**, 817 F.3d 72 (2d Cir. 2016) — failure to disclose accruing interest can be misleading.  
- **Bartlett v. Heibl**, 128 F.3d 497 (7th Cir. 1997) — overshadowing of validation rights.  
- **Foti v. NCO Fin. Sys.**, 424 F. Supp. 2d 643 (S.D.N.Y. 2006) — prerecorded voicemail can violate § 1692e(11) and § 1692d(6).  
- **Hunstein v. Preferred Collection**, 48 F.4th 1236 (11th Cir. 2022) (en banc) — letter-vendor disclosure lacks Article III standing absent concrete harm.  
- **Maddox v. Bank of N.Y. Mellon**, 19 F.4th 58 (2d Cir. 2021) — bare statutory violation without harm fails *TransUnion*.  
- **Pierre v. Midland Credit Mgmt.**, 29 F.4th 934 (7th Cir. 2022) — confusion alone insufficient; must allege detrimental action.  
- **Trichell v. Midland Credit Mgmt.**, 964 F.3d 990 (11th Cir. 2020) — informational injury insufficient without downstream consequence.

## 10. Damages Allegations — Per Claim  
  
Each count should conclude with a damages paragraph block:  
  
> [N]. As a direct and proximate result of Defendant's violations of 15 U.S.C. § 1692[_], Plaintiff has suffered:  
>  
> a. Actual damages, including lost wages of $[amount], emotional distress in the form of anxiety, sleeplessness, and embarrassment, and out-of-pocket expenses of $[amount];  
>  
> b. Statutory damages up to $1,000 pursuant to 15 U.S.C. § 1692k(a)(2)(A);  
>  
> c. Reasonable attorney's fees and costs pursuant to 15 U.S.C. § 1692k(a)(3).

## 11. Pleading Caution — *Hunstein*, *Maddox*, *Pierre*, *Trichell*  
  
The post-*TransUnion* federal standing landscape has narrowed the no-injury FDCPA case. For every count:  
  
- Plead the **communication or conduct**;  
- Plead **what the consumer did or felt** in response;  
- Plead the **traditional tort analog** (intrusion upon seclusion, defamation, public disclosure);  
- Plead **out-of-pocket loss** or **lost time** where possible;  
- Avoid relying on "mere confusion" or "mere receipt of a defective letter."
