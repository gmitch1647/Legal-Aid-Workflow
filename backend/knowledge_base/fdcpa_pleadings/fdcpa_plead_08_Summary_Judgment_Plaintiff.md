## 1. Rule 56 Framework in FDCPA Cases  
  
### 1.1 Standard  
Summary judgment is appropriate where, viewing the evidence in the light most favorable to the non-movant, there is no genuine dispute of material fact and the movant is entitled to judgment as a matter of law. *Celotex Corp. v. Catrett*, 477 U.S. 317, 322–24 (1986); *Anderson v. Liberty Lobby, Inc.*, 477 U.S. 242, 247–48 (1986); *Matsushita Elec. Indus. Co. v. Zenith Radio Corp.*, 475 U.S. 574, 587 (1986).  
  
### 1.2 Why FDCPA Cases Are Especially Suited to Plaintiff MSJ  
The FDCPA imposes **strict liability** on most violations. *Bentley v. Great Lakes Collection Bureau*, 6 F.3d 60, 63 (2d Cir. 1993); *Russell v. Equifax A.R.S.*, 74 F.3d 30, 33 (2d Cir. 1996); *Jerman v. Carlisle, McNellie, Rini, Kramer & Ulrich LPA*, 559 U.S. 573, 580 (2010). Intent is irrelevant to the prima facie case; a single violation suffices. *Clark v. Capital Credit & Collection Servs.*, 460 F.3d 1162, 1175 (9th Cir. 2006).  
  
When the operative communication is a **form letter** (or template script, or template email), the document speaks for itself. Whether it violates the FDCPA is generally a question of law for the court under the "least sophisticated consumer" standard (or "unsophisticated consumer" in the Seventh Circuit). *Clomon v. Jackson*, 988 F.2d 1314, 1318–20 (2d Cir. 1993); *Gammon v. GC Servs. Ltd. P'ship*, 27 F.3d 1254, 1257 (7th Cir. 1994); *Jeter v. Credit Bureau, Inc.*, 760 F.2d 1168, 1175 (11th Cir. 1985).  
  
### 1.3 Plaintiff's Burden on MSJ  
Plaintiff must establish:  
1. **Consumer** status — natural person obligated on a "debt" as defined in § 1692a(5);  
2. **Debt** — primarily for personal, family, or household purposes;  
3. **Debt collector** status of defendant under § 1692a(6) (with *Henson v. Santander Consumer USA Inc.*, 137 S. Ct. 1718 (2017), narrowing this for purchasers of defaulted debt acting on own account);  
4. **Conduct** in connection with collection of a debt;  
5. **Specific statutory violation** (§§ 1692c–1692j).  
  
For partial MSJ, plaintiff need only prove the discrete claim element(s) put at issue. Fed. R. Civ. P. 56(g); *see also* Fed. R. Civ. P. 56(a) ("A party may move for summary judgment, identifying each claim or defense — or the part of each claim or defense — on which summary judgment is sought.").

## 2. Partial MSJ — Liability on Form-Letter § 1692e / § 1692g Violations  
  
### 2.1 The Letter as Operative Fact  
When the collector's communication is a template generated by a mail-merge or letter-management system, the form, recipient class, and content are typically undisputed. The court may construe the letter as a matter of law. *Avila v. Riexinger & Assocs., LLC*, 817 F.3d 72, 75 (2d Cir. 2016) (whether a letter is misleading is a question of law where the language unambiguously violates the statute); *Wahl v. Midland Credit Mgmt., Inc.*, 556 F.3d 643, 645–46 (7th Cir. 2009); *Pollard v. Law Office of Mandy L. Spaulding*, 766 F.3d 98 (1st Cir. 2014).  
  
### 2.2 Common Form-Letter Violations Suitable for MSJ  
- **§ 1692e(2)(A)** — false representation of the character, amount, or legal status of a debt. *Avila*, 817 F.3d at 76 (failure to disclose that stated balance was increasing due to interest/fees).  
- **§ 1692e(5)** — threat of action that cannot legally or is not intended to be taken. *Gonzales v. Arrow Fin. Servs., LLC*, 660 F.3d 1055 (9th Cir. 2011) (implying litigation on time-barred debt).  
- **§ 1692e(10)** — use of false representation or deceptive means.  
- **§ 1692e(11)** — failure to disclose "mini-Miranda."  
- **§ 1692g(a)(1)–(5)** — defective validation notice (missing required disclosures, contradicted by other letter content, or "overshadowed" by demands). *Russell*, 74 F.3d at 34–35; *Foti v. NCO Fin. Sys., Inc.*, 424 F. Supp. 2d 643, 656 (S.D.N.Y. 2006).  
  
### 2.3 Proof Package  
- Certified copy of the letter (or production from defendant), authenticated under FRE 901/902(11).  
- Defendant's Rule 30(b)(6) testimony confirming the letter is a template sent to a class of recipients.  
- Mail-house data or class list showing volume.  
- Rule 36 admissions on form, sender identity, and date.  
  
> Tip: Coordinate Rule 36 RFAs early. Admissions are conclusive under FRCP 36(b) and short-circuit foundation issues at MSJ.

## 3. Partial MSJ — § 1692c Third-Party Communication Violations  
  
### 3.1 The Statute  
Section 1692c(b) prohibits any communication "in connection with the collection of any debt, with any person other than the consumer," except in narrow circumstances (consumer's prior consent, court permission, location information under § 1692b, or to the creditor/attorneys/CRAs).  
  
### 3.2 Why These Cases Suit MSJ  
The fact of the communication is usually documented: a call log, a letter to a third party, an email, a posted letter to a roommate, or a voicemail audible to a third party. *See Foti*, 424 F. Supp. 2d at 657 (voicemail that does not identify the collector is itself a § 1692e(11) violation; companion 1692c(b) issue when overheard).  
  
Recent authority on **letter-vendor disclosure** (transmission of consumer data to a commercial mail vendor) is unsettled but generally favorable to plaintiffs post-*Hunstein v. Preferred Collection & Mgmt. Servs.*, 17 F.4th 1016 (11th Cir. 2021), *vacated and reh'g en banc*, 17 F.4th 1103 (11th Cir. 2021), *en banc op.*, 48 F.4th 1236 (11th Cir. 2022) (no Article III standing on facts pleaded). The substantive § 1692c(b) question survives even if standing is contested in particular factual records.  
  
### 3.3 Elements to Establish on MSJ  
1. Defendant is a debt collector.  
2. Defendant communicated.  
3. With a third party.  
4. In connection with collection of a debt.  
5. None of the § 1692c(b) exceptions applies (i.e., no consent, no court permission, not a § 1692b location call).  
  
### 3.4 Proof  
- Call recordings or transcripts.  
- Defendant's admissions on third-party identity.  
- Plaintiff declaration on lack of consent.  
- Discovery responses identifying recipients.

## 4. Partial MSJ — Time-Barred Debt Collection (Reg F § 1006.26)  
  
### 4.1 The Rule  
Regulation F, 12 C.F.R. § 1006.26(b), prohibits a debt collector from suing or threatening to sue on a time-barred debt. The CFPB adopted a **strict-liability** structure: "A debt collector must not bring or threaten to bring a legal action against a consumer to collect a time-barred debt." Knowledge or intent is not an element. Compare *Midland Funding, LLC v. Johnson*, 137 S. Ct. 1407 (2017) (filing proof of claim on time-barred debt in bankruptcy not actionable under FDCPA on those facts) — Reg F prospectively closes the gap for state-court suits and threats.  
  
### 4.2 MSJ Posture  
Where the parties stipulate (or discovery establishes) the date of last payment, charge-off, or accrual, the SOL question is mechanical. Plaintiff can move for partial summary judgment on liability and leave damages for trial.  
  
### 4.3 Proof Package  
- Account statements / origination documents showing accrual.  
- State SOL caselaw (UCC Article 3 / Article 9 / contract / open account).  
- Defendant's collection letter, complaint, or call recording where suit was threatened or filed.  
- Conformed copy of state-court complaint (if filed).  
  
### 4.4 Companion Claims  
- § 1692e(2)(A) — misrepresentation of legal status.  
- § 1692e(5) — threat of unlawful action.  
- § 1692e(10) — deception.  
- § 1692f — unfair or unconscionable means.  
  
*See Phillips v. Asset Acceptance, LLC*, 736 F.3d 1076, 1079 (7th Cir. 2013) (Posner, J.) (filing time-barred state suit can violate FDCPA); *Crawford v. LVNV Funding, LLC*, 758 F.3d 1254 (11th Cir. 2014).

## 5. Partial MSJ — Envelope Cases (§ 1692f(8))  
  
### 5.1 The Statute  
Section 1692f(8) prohibits "using any language or symbol, other than the debt collector's address, on any envelope when communicating with a consumer by use of the mails or by telegram, except that a debt collector may use his business name if such name does not indicate that he is in the debt collection business."  
  
### 5.2 Glassine Window / Account-Number Disclosures  
Following *Douglass v. Convergent Outsourcing*, 765 F.3d 299 (3d Cir. 2014), an account number visible through a glassine window is a "benign language" violation. Other circuits split, but the disclosure question reduces to **a single fact**: was the number visible.  
  
### 5.3 MSJ Proof  
- The envelope itself (or color photocopy / scan).  
- Authentication by sender / recipient.  
- Defendant's print-vendor specifications.  
- If on a label inside a window, an exemplar mailing.  
  
### 5.4 Limits  
*Strand v. Diversified Collection Serv., Inc.*, 380 F.3d 316 (8th Cir. 2004), and *Goswami v. Am. Collections Enter., Inc.*, 377 F.3d 488 (5th Cir. 2004), limit the rule to non-benign markings. Movants should plead and brief the circuit's controlling test.

## 6. Partial MSJ — Validation-Notice Deficiencies (§ 1692g)  
  
### 6.1 The Required Disclosures  
Section 1692g(a) requires, within five days of initial communication (or in the initial communication), written notice of:  
1. Amount of the debt;  
2. Name of creditor to whom the debt is owed;  
3. Statement that the consumer has 30 days to dispute, after which the debt will be assumed valid;  
4. Statement that, upon written dispute within 30 days, the collector will obtain verification and mail it to the consumer;  
5. Statement that, upon written request within 30 days, the collector will provide the name and address of the original creditor if different from the current creditor.  
  
### 6.2 Reg F Safe Harbor  
Regulation F, 12 C.F.R. § 1006.34, provides a model validation notice. Use of the model notice creates a safe harbor; **non-use does not create a presumption of violation**, but a collector who deviates from the model accepts the risk of § 1692g challenges. § 1006.34(d)(2) — model form. The "itemization date" framework (current balance vs. itemization date balance) is mandatory.  
  
### 6.3 MSJ Posture  
- Compare the letter to § 1692g(a) requirements line-by-line.  
- Where one required disclosure is missing, MSJ is appropriate.  
- "Overshadowing" — language that contradicts or obscures the 30-day right — is more fact-intensive but still amenable to MSJ where the language is on its face inconsistent (e.g., "Pay within 10 days or we will sue" appearing alongside the 30-day notice). *Russell*, 74 F.3d at 35; *Bartlett v. Heibl*, 128 F.3d 497, 500 (7th Cir. 1997).

## 7. Defeating the Bona Fide Error Defense at MSJ  
  
### 7.1 Statutory Structure  
Section 1692k(c) creates an affirmative defense: no liability if the collector shows by a preponderance that the violation was "not intentional and resulted from a bona fide error notwithstanding the maintenance of procedures reasonably adapted to avoid any such error."  
  
### 7.2 *Jerman* — No Defense for Mistakes of Law  
*Jerman v. Carlisle, McNellie, Rini, Kramer & Ulrich LPA*, 559 U.S. 573 (2010), forecloses the defense for **legal errors**: a debt collector cannot rely on bona fide error to excuse a mistaken interpretation of the FDCPA itself. *Id.* at 604–05.  
  
### 7.3 MSJ Tactic  
On plaintiff's MSJ, the defense often raises § 1692k(c). Plaintiff defeats it by:  
1. **Characterizing the error as legal.** If the violation flows from how defendant interpreted §§ 1692c, e, f, or g — that is a *Jerman*-barred legal error.  
2. **Procedures shortfall.** Even if the error is factual (e.g., wrong amount due to a data feed error, name confusion, or duplicate file), defendant must show **specific, documented procedures** reasonably adapted to avoid that very type of error. Conclusory testimony fails. *Reichert v. Nat'l Credit Sys., Inc.*, 531 F.3d 1002, 1006 (9th Cir. 2008); *Edwards v. Niagara Credit Sols., Inc.*, 584 F.3d 1350, 1353 (11th Cir. 2009).  
3. **Burden.** Defendant bears the burden of production and persuasion. Vague procedures or post-hoc testimony will not survive plaintiff's prima facie showing.  
  
### 7.4 Discovery Targets  
- Written policies and procedures manuals.  
- Training records.  
- QA scripts and audit logs.  
- Compliance committee minutes.  
- Vendor service-level agreements.  
- Prior consumer complaints showing the same error.

## 8. Damages on MSJ  
  
Statutory damages under § 1692k(a)(2)(A) are capped at $1,000 per **action** in individual cases. *Wright v. Finance Serv. of Norwalk, Inc.*, 22 F.3d 647, 651 (6th Cir. 1994) (en banc). Even on liability MSJ, the **amount** of statutory damages (up to $1,000) is reserved for the trier of fact under § 1692k(b)(1) factors. *Savino v. Computer Credit, Inc.*, 164 F.3d 81, 86 (2d Cir. 1998).  
  
Actual damages (emotional distress, out-of-pocket) typically present fact issues unsuitable for MSJ. *Smith v. Law Offices of Mitchell N. Kay*, 124 F.3d 1009, 1015 (8th Cir. 1997).  
  
Attorney's fees and costs are mandatory upon a prevailing plaintiff. § 1692k(a)(3); *Tolentino v. Friedman*, 46 F.3d 645, 651 (7th Cir. 1995).

## 9. Sample Plaintiff MSJ Brief Structure (Template)  
  
```  
UNITED STATES DISTRICT COURT  
[DISTRICT]  
  
[PLAINTIFF],  
 Case No. ___  
 Plaintiff,  
v. PLAINTIFF'S MOTION FOR  
 PARTIAL SUMMARY JUDGMENT  
[DEFENDANT],  
 Defendant.  
  
I. INTRODUCTION  
 - One-paragraph theory: undisputed form letter, facial § 1692e/g violation,  
 no genuine fact dispute, *Jerman* forecloses defense.  
  
II. STATEMENT OF UNDISPUTED MATERIAL FACTS (FRCP 56(c)(1); LR ___)  
 1. Plaintiff is a consumer. [Exh. A ¶ __; admitted at RFA __]  
 2. The Debt arose from a [consumer transaction]. [Exh. B; Def. 30(b)(6) at __]  
 3. Defendant is a debt collector. [Exh. C; admitted in Answer ¶ __]  
 4. Defendant sent Plaintiff a letter dated [date]. [Exh. D]  
 5. The letter is a template used for [class of recipients]. [Def. 30(b)(6) at __]  
 6. The letter states: "[quoted language]." [Exh. D]  
 7. The letter omits / contains: [specific defect]. [Exh. D]  
  
III. LEGAL STANDARD  
 - Rule 56; FDCPA strict liability; least-sophisticated-consumer.  
  
IV. ARGUMENT  
 A. Defendant violated § 1692e by [misrepresenting / omitting].  
 1. The text is undisputed.  
 2. The least sophisticated consumer would be deceived as a matter of law.  
 3. Circuit authority directly on point: [string cite].  
 B. Defendant violated § 1692g by [omission / overshadowing].  
 C. The bona fide error defense fails as a matter of law.  
 1. Any error is legal — *Jerman*.  
 2. Defendant has produced no procedures evidence.  
  
V. CONCLUSION  
 - Grant partial MSJ on liability for Counts ___; reserve damages for trial.  
  
Respectfully submitted,  
[Counsel signature block]  
  
EXHIBITS  
A: Plaintiff declaration  
B: Origination / purpose evidence  
C: Defendant license / 30(b)(6)  
D: The collection letter  
E: Defendant's discovery responses / RFA admissions  
F: 30(b)(6) deposition excerpts  
```  
  
### 9.1 Local-Rule Statement of Facts  
Most districts (e.g., N.D. Ill. LR 56.1; D. Mass. LR 56.1; S.D.N.Y. LR 56.1) require a separately-numbered statement of undisputed material facts with pinpoint citations. Format strictly to local rules; sloppy statements can result in fact concessions or denial of the motion.  
  
### 9.2 Evidentiary Hooks  
- Authenticate the letter via declaration of the recipient (FRE 901(b)(1)) and/or business-records affidavit (FRE 803(6); 902(11)).  
- Use Rule 36 admissions to lock down identity, date, and form-letter status.  
- Move *in limine* concurrent with MSJ to exclude defense expert speculation on intent (not relevant to strict-liability violation).

## 11. Practice Pointers  
  
1. **Move early, but after Rule 30(b)(6).** Pre-discovery MSJ rarely succeeds; the 30(b)(6) deposition typically closes off the bona fide error defense.  
2. **Stagger partial MSJs.** Consider separate motions on liability and on the bona fide error defense to force the defense to disclose its proof.  
3. **Use Rule 56(g) creatively.** Even if full MSJ fails, ask the court to deem specific facts established (e.g., the letter language, the sender, the date, the recipient class).  
4. **Avoid Rule 56(d) ambush.** Confirm discovery is closed (or substantially complete on the discrete issue) before filing.  
5. **Local-rule discipline.** A meticulous LR 56.1 statement is the single biggest predictor of plaintiff-side MSJ success.  
6. **Settlement leverage.** A pending plaintiff MSJ frequently produces classwide settlement; ensure the demand letter accompanying or preceding the motion captures full statutory plus fees plus injunctive relief.
