## Template A — Repeated Harassing Calls / 7-in-7 Violation  
  
```  
UNITED STATES DISTRICT COURT  
[DISTRICT OF __________]  
  
[PLAINTIFF NAME], Case No. ____________  
 Plaintiff,  
v. COMPLAINT  
 (Jury Trial Demanded)  
[DEFENDANT NAME],  
 Defendant.  
  
 COMPLAINT  
  
 Plaintiff [Name], by and through undersigned counsel, alleges as follows:  
  
 I. INTRODUCTION  
  
 1. This is an action for damages arising under the Fair Debt Collection  
 Practices Act, 15 U.S.C. §§ 1692 et seq. ("FDCPA"), and implementing  
 Regulation F, 12 C.F.R. Part 1006.  
  
 2. Defendant placed dozens of telephone calls to Plaintiff over a short  
 period for the purpose of collecting an alleged consumer debt,  
 exceeding Regulation F's seven-calls-in-seven-days frequency  
 presumption, 12 C.F.R. § 1006.14(b)(2)(i), and otherwise engaging in  
 conduct prohibited by 15 U.S.C. § 1692d.  
  
 II. JURISDICTION AND VENUE  
  
 3. This Court has subject-matter jurisdiction under 28 U.S.C. § 1331  
 and 15 U.S.C. § 1692k(d).  
  
 4. Venue is proper under 28 U.S.C. § 1391(b) because a substantial  
 part of the events giving rise to the claim occurred in this district  
 and because Defendant transacts business in this district.  
  
 III. PARTIES  
  
 5. Plaintiff is a natural person residing in [County, State]. Plaintiff  
 is a "consumer" as defined by 15 U.S.C. § 1692a(3).  
  
 6. Defendant is a [state of organization] [entity type] with its  
 principal place of business in [city, state]. Defendant regularly  
 collects, or attempts to collect, debts asserted to be owed to others  
 and uses the mails and instrumentalities of interstate commerce in  
 such collection. Defendant is a "debt collector" as defined by  
 15 U.S.C. § 1692a(6).  
  
 IV. FACTUAL ALLEGATIONS  
  
 7. At some point prior to [date], Plaintiff allegedly incurred a financial  
 obligation to [original creditor] arising from charges incurred  
 primarily for personal, family, or household purposes (the "Debt").  
 The Debt is a "debt" within the meaning of 15 U.S.C. § 1692a(5).  
  
 8. The Debt was placed with or sold to Defendant for collection.  
  
 9. Between [start date] and [end date] — a period of seven consecutive  
 days — Defendant placed [number] telephone calls to Plaintiff's  
 cellular telephone, number ending [xxxx], for the purpose of  
 collecting the Debt. A summary of the calls is attached as Exhibit A.  
  
 10. Defendant's call frequency exceeded the seven-calls-in-seven-days  
 presumption established by 12 C.F.R. § 1006.14(b)(2)(i).  
  
 11. On [date], during a call from Defendant, Plaintiff said: "Please  
 stop calling me." Defendant continued calling Plaintiff [number]  
 more times during the following [period].  
  
 12. Several calls were placed before 8:00 a.m. local time at Plaintiff's  
 residence, including calls on [date] at [time] and on [date] at  
 [time]. Defendant's records reflect Plaintiff's address in  
 [Time Zone].  
  
 13. Defendant's calls disrupted Plaintiff's sleep, work, and household  
 routine. Plaintiff suffered anxiety, frustration, embarrassment in  
 front of family members, and lost approximately [hours] of work  
 responding to and recovering from Defendant's calls.  
  
 14. Defendant's conduct constitutes an unreasonable intrusion upon  
 Plaintiff's seclusion analogous to the common-law tort of intrusion  
 upon seclusion, and therefore inflicted concrete harm sufficient to  
 establish Article III standing. See *TransUnion LLC v. Ramirez*,  
 594 U.S. 413, 425 (2021).  
  
 V. COUNT ONE — 15 U.S.C. § 1692d  
  
 15. Plaintiff incorporates the foregoing paragraphs.  
  
 16. Defendant's repeated and continuous telephone calls were conduct the  
 natural consequence of which was to harass, oppress, or abuse  
 Plaintiff in violation of 15 U.S.C. § 1692d, including § 1692d(5).  
  
 17. Defendant's conduct exceeded the call-frequency presumption of  
 12 C.F.R. § 1006.14(b)(2)(i).  
  
 VI. COUNT TWO — 15 U.S.C. § 1692c(a)(1)  
  
 18. Plaintiff incorporates the foregoing paragraphs.  
  
 19. Defendant communicated with Plaintiff at times known or which should  
 have been known to be inconvenient — specifically, before 8:00 a.m.  
 local time — in violation of 15 U.S.C. § 1692c(a)(1).  
  
 VII. DAMAGES  
  
 20. As a direct and proximate result of Defendant's conduct, Plaintiff  
 has suffered actual damages including emotional distress, lost time,  
 lost wages, and invasion of privacy.  
  
 VIII. PRAYER FOR RELIEF  
  
 WHEREFORE, Plaintiff respectfully requests judgment against Defendant  
 for:  
  
 (a) Actual damages pursuant to 15 U.S.C. § 1692k(a)(1);  
 (b) Statutory damages of up to $1,000 pursuant to 15 U.S.C. § 1692k(a)(2)(A);  
 (c) Reasonable attorney's fees and costs pursuant to 15 U.S.C. § 1692k(a)(3);  
 (d) Such other and further relief as the Court deems just and proper.  
  
 IX. JURY DEMAND  
  
 Plaintiff demands a trial by jury on all claims so triable, pursuant to  
 Federal Rule of Civil Procedure 38(b).  
  
 Respectfully submitted,  
  
 /s/ ______________________  
 [Counsel name, bar number, address,  
 telephone, email]  
 Attorney for Plaintiff  
  
Dated: ______________  
```

## Template B — False or Misleading Letter (§ 1692e)  
  
```  
UNITED STATES DISTRICT COURT  
[DISTRICT OF __________]  
  
[PLAINTIFF NAME], Case No. ____________  
 Plaintiff,  
v. COMPLAINT  
 (Jury Trial Demanded)  
[DEFENDANT NAME],  
 Defendant.  
  
 COMPLAINT  
  
 I. INTRODUCTION  
  
 1. This is an action for damages under the Fair Debt Collection  
 Practices Act, 15 U.S.C. §§ 1692 et seq.  
  
 2. Defendant sent Plaintiff a collection letter containing false,  
 deceptive, and misleading representations in violation of 15 U.S.C.  
 § 1692e, including failing to disclose that the stated balance was  
 subject to ongoing accrual of interest and fees. See *Avila v.  
 Riexinger & Assocs., LLC*, 817 F.3d 72 (2d Cir. 2016).  
  
 II. JURISDICTION AND VENUE  
  
 3. This Court has subject-matter jurisdiction under 28 U.S.C. § 1331  
 and 15 U.S.C. § 1692k(d). Venue is proper in this district under  
 28 U.S.C. § 1391(b).  
  
 III. PARTIES  
  
 4. Plaintiff is a natural person residing in [County, State] and is a  
 "consumer" within 15 U.S.C. § 1692a(3).  
  
 5. Defendant is a [entity type] with its principal place of business  
 in [city, state]. Defendant's principal purpose is the collection of  
 debts owed to others, and Defendant regularly engages in such  
 collection. Defendant is a "debt collector" under 15 U.S.C. § 1692a(6).  
  
 IV. FACTUAL ALLEGATIONS  
  
 6. Plaintiff allegedly incurred a financial obligation to [original  
 creditor] for personal, family, or household purposes (the "Debt"),  
 a "debt" within 15 U.S.C. § 1692a(5).  
  
 7. On or about [date], Defendant mailed Plaintiff a collection letter  
 (the "Letter"), a true and correct copy of which is attached as  
 Exhibit A.  
  
 8. The Letter stated, in pertinent part: "Current Balance: $4,732.18."  
  
 9. The Letter did not disclose that the Current Balance was subject to  
 continuing accrual of interest, fees, or other charges; nor did the  
 Letter advise Plaintiff that paying the stated balance would not  
 satisfy the Debt in full.  
  
 10. Upon information and belief, the Debt did in fact continue to accrue  
 interest and fees after the date of the Letter.  
  
 11. The least-sophisticated consumer reading the Letter would understand  
 "Current Balance" to represent a static amount that, if paid, would  
 satisfy the Debt in full. See *Avila*, 817 F.3d at 76.  
  
 12. In reliance on the Letter, Plaintiff [paid $__ on [date] / disputed  
 the Debt by letter dated [date] / contacted [name] attorney  
 incurring fees of $___].  
  
 13. Plaintiff suffered out-of-pocket loss, lost time, and anxiety,  
 constituting concrete harm under *TransUnion LLC v. Ramirez*, 594  
 U.S. 413 (2021), closely analogous to the common-law tort of  
 misrepresentation.  
  
 V. COUNT ONE — 15 U.S.C. § 1692e  
  
 14. Plaintiff incorporates the foregoing paragraphs.  
  
 15. The Letter contained false, deceptive, or misleading representations  
 in connection with the collection of a debt, in violation of  
 15 U.S.C. § 1692e generally and 15 U.S.C. §§ 1692e(2)(A) (false  
 representation of amount or legal status) and 1692e(10) (use of  
 deceptive means) specifically.  
  
 VI. COUNT TWO — 15 U.S.C. § 1692f  
  
 16. Plaintiff incorporates the foregoing paragraphs.  
  
 17. Defendant's conduct constituted unfair or unconscionable means to  
 collect a debt in violation of 15 U.S.C. § 1692f.  
  
 VII. PRAYER FOR RELIEF  
  
 WHEREFORE, Plaintiff requests judgment for:  
 (a) Actual damages, 15 U.S.C. § 1692k(a)(1);  
 (b) Statutory damages up to $1,000, 15 U.S.C. § 1692k(a)(2)(A);  
 (c) Attorney's fees and costs, 15 U.S.C. § 1692k(a)(3);  
 (d) Such other relief as is just.  
  
 VIII. JURY DEMAND  
  
 Plaintiff demands trial by jury under Fed. R. Civ. P. 38(b).  
  
 /s/ ______________________  
 [Counsel signature block]  
```

## Template C — Defective Validation Notice (§ 1692g + Reg F § 1006.34)  
  
```  
UNITED STATES DISTRICT COURT  
[DISTRICT OF __________]  
  
[PLAINTIFF NAME], Case No. ____________  
 Plaintiff,  
v. COMPLAINT  
 (Jury Trial Demanded)  
[DEFENDANT NAME],  
 Defendant.  
  
 COMPLAINT  
  
 1. This action arises under the FDCPA, 15 U.S.C. § 1692g, and  
 Regulation F, 12 C.F.R. § 1006.34, governing the content and  
 delivery of validation notices.  
  
 2. Defendant's initial written communication to Plaintiff failed to  
 provide the validation information required by § 1692g(a) and the  
 itemization, consumer-protection, and consumer-response information  
 required by 12 C.F.R. § 1006.34(c).  
  
 [Jurisdiction, venue, parties as in Template B]  
  
 IV. FACTUAL ALLEGATIONS  
  
 6. On or about [date], Defendant sent Plaintiff Defendant's first  
 written communication regarding the Debt (the "Initial Letter"),  
 attached as Exhibit A.  
  
 7. The Initial Letter omitted [or misstated] the following information  
 required by 15 U.S.C. § 1692g(a) and 12 C.F.R. § 1006.34(c):  
  
 (a) The name of the creditor to whom the debt is owed  
 (§ 1692g(a)(2); § 1006.34(c)(2)(iv));  
  
 (b) An itemization of the current amount of the debt that reflects  
 interest, fees, payments, and credits since the itemization date  
 (§ 1006.34(c)(2)(vi)–(viii));  
  
 (c) The statement that, unless the consumer disputes the validity  
 of the debt within thirty days after receipt of the notice, the  
 debt will be assumed valid (§ 1692g(a)(3));  
  
 (d) The consumer-response prompts (the dispute and original-creditor  
 request options) (§ 1006.34(c)(4)).  
  
 8. Additionally, the Initial Letter overshadowed the validation period  
 by demanding payment "IMMEDIATELY" in bold red type and threatening  
 "FURTHER COLLECTION ACTION" within ten days, in violation of  
 15 U.S.C. § 1692g(b). See *Bartlett v. Heibl*, 128 F.3d 497 (7th  
 Cir. 1997).  
  
 9. As a result of the defective notice, Plaintiff was confused about  
 her dispute rights and the identity of the creditor. Plaintiff  
 failed to timely dispute the Debt, paid $[amount] toward the Debt  
 that she would have disputed had she received compliant notice, and  
 incurred attorney's fees of $[amount] to investigate the Debt.  
  
 10. Plaintiff suffered concrete harm sufficient to confer Article III  
 standing under *TransUnion LLC v. Ramirez*, 594 U.S. 413 (2021).  
 See *Pierre v. Midland Credit Mgmt., Inc.*, 29 F.4th 934 (7th Cir.  
 2022).  
  
 V. COUNT ONE — 15 U.S.C. § 1692g(a) and Reg F § 1006.34  
  
 11. Plaintiff incorporates the foregoing paragraphs.  
  
 12. The Initial Letter failed to include the validation information  
 required by 15 U.S.C. § 1692g(a) and 12 C.F.R. § 1006.34(c).  
  
 VI. COUNT TWO — 15 U.S.C. § 1692g(b)  
  
 13. The Initial Letter overshadowed and was inconsistent with Plaintiff's  
 30-day validation rights in violation of 15 U.S.C. § 1692g(b).  
  
 VII. COUNT THREE — 15 U.S.C. § 1692e  
  
 14. The Initial Letter contained false, deceptive, or misleading  
 representations regarding the character of the Debt and Plaintiff's  
 rights in violation of 15 U.S.C. § 1692e and § 1692e(10).  
  
 [Prayer and jury demand as in Template A]  
```

## Template D — Improper Third-Party Communication (§ 1692c(b))  
  
```  
UNITED STATES DISTRICT COURT  
[DISTRICT OF __________]  
  
[PLAINTIFF NAME], Case No. ____________  
 Plaintiff,  
v. COMPLAINT  
 (Jury Trial Demanded)  
[DEFENDANT NAME],  
 Defendant.  
  
 COMPLAINT  
  
 1. This action arises under 15 U.S.C. §§ 1692b and 1692c(b), which  
 prohibit, with limited exceptions, communication with third parties  
 in connection with the collection of a debt.  
  
 [Jurisdiction, venue, parties]  
  
 IV. FACTUAL ALLEGATIONS  
  
 6. Plaintiff allegedly incurred a financial obligation to [original  
 creditor] for personal, family, or household purposes.  
  
 7. On [date], Defendant's representative telephoned Plaintiff's  
 [neighbor / sister / employer / minor child], [Name].  
  
 8. During the call, Defendant's representative stated, in substance,  
 that Plaintiff "owes money," is "behind on her account," or "has  
 a serious legal matter to address."  
  
 9. [Third party] did not at any time consent to receive such  
 information from Defendant, and Plaintiff did not consent to  
 Defendant's communication with [third party]. No court order  
 authorized the disclosure.  
  
 10. Defendant's communication exceeded the location-information scope  
 permitted by 15 U.S.C. § 1692b. Even if construed as a § 1692b  
 communication, it violated § 1692b(2) (disclosure that consumer  
 owes a debt) and § 1692b(3) (more than one contact).  
  
 11. As a result of Defendant's disclosure, [third party] inquired of  
 Plaintiff about the Debt. Plaintiff suffered embarrassment,  
 reputational harm in the family/community, anxiety, and emotional  
 distress closely analogous to the common-law tort of public  
 disclosure of private facts. See *TransUnion LLC v. Ramirez*,  
 594 U.S. 413, 425 (2021).  
  
 V. COUNT ONE — 15 U.S.C. § 1692c(b)  
  
 12. Plaintiff incorporates the foregoing.  
  
 13. Defendant communicated with a third party in connection with the  
 collection of a debt without Plaintiff's prior consent, without  
 court order, and outside the limited exceptions of § 1692c(b).  
  
 VI. COUNT TWO — 15 U.S.C. § 1692b  
  
 14. To the extent Defendant claims the communication was for purposes  
 of acquiring location information, Defendant violated § 1692b(2)  
 and § 1692b(3).  
  
 VII. COUNT THREE — 15 U.S.C. § 1692c(b) /  
 § 1692f  
  
 15. Defendant's conduct was unfair within the meaning of 15 U.S.C.  
 § 1692f.  
  
 [Prayer and jury demand]  
```  
  
**Drafting note — *Hunstein*:** If the third-party communication is to a letter vendor for mailing, Eleventh Circuit law in *Hunstein v. Preferred Collection & Mgmt. Servs., Inc.*, 48 F.4th 1236 (11th Cir. 2022) (en banc), holds that such disclosure lacks Article III standing absent concrete harm. Counsel filing in or facing transfer to the Eleventh Circuit should consider state-court filing.

## Template E — Time-Barred Debt Collection (§ 1692e + Reg F § 1006.26)  
  
```  
UNITED STATES DISTRICT COURT  
[DISTRICT OF __________]  
  
[PLAINTIFF NAME], Case No. ____________  
 Plaintiff,  
v. COMPLAINT  
 (Jury Trial Demanded)  
[DEFENDANT NAME],  
 Defendant.  
  
 COMPLAINT  
  
 1. This action arises under 15 U.S.C. § 1692e and Regulation F's  
 time-barred-debt provision, 12 C.F.R. § 1006.26. Defendant sought  
 to collect, and filed suit on, a consumer debt for which the  
 applicable state statute of limitations had expired.  
  
 [Jurisdiction, venue, parties]  
  
 IV. FACTUAL ALLEGATIONS  
  
 6. Plaintiff allegedly incurred a financial obligation to [original  
 creditor] arising from a credit-card account opened on or about  
 [date] (the "Debt"). The Debt was charged off on or about [date].  
  
 7. Under [state] law, the statute of limitations applicable to the  
 Debt is [N] years. The limitations period expired no later than  
 [date].  
  
 8. On or about [date], more than [N] years after the limitations  
 period expired, Defendant sent Plaintiff a collection letter  
 (Exhibit A) demanding payment of the Debt.  
  
 9. The letter did not disclose that the Debt was time-barred or that  
 Defendant could not sue to enforce the Debt. The letter further  
 threatened that Defendant "may take legal action" if the Debt was  
 not paid.  
  
 10. On [date], Defendant filed a state-court collection action against  
 Plaintiff for the Debt (Exhibit B), in violation of 12 C.F.R.  
 § 1006.26(b), which prohibits a debt collector from suing or  
 threatening to sue on a time-barred debt.  
  
 11. The least-sophisticated consumer would understand Defendant's  
 letter and lawsuit as representations that the Debt was legally  
 enforceable. See *McMahon v. LVNV Funding, LLC*, 744 F.3d 1010  
 (7th Cir. 2014); *Phillips v. Asset Acceptance, LLC*, 736 F.3d  
 1076 (7th Cir. 2013).  
  
 12. As a result of Defendant's conduct, Plaintiff incurred attorney's  
 fees defending the state-court action, lost wages attending court,  
 and suffered anxiety and embarrassment. Plaintiff's concrete harm  
 satisfies *TransUnion LLC v. Ramirez*, 594 U.S. 413 (2021).  
  
 V. COUNT ONE — 15 U.S.C. § 1692e  
  
 13. Defendant's conduct constituted false, deceptive, or misleading  
 representations in violation of § 1692e, § 1692e(2)(A) (false  
 representation of legal status), § 1692e(5) (threat of action that  
 cannot legally be taken or is not intended), and § 1692e(10).  
  
 VI. COUNT TWO — 15 U.S.C. § 1692f  
  
 14. Defendant's collection of the time-barred Debt is unfair or  
 unconscionable within 15 U.S.C. § 1692f, including § 1692f(1)  
 (collection of amount not authorized by law).  
  
 VII. COUNT THREE — 12 C.F.R. § 1006.26  
 (via § 1692e and § 1692f)  
  
 15. Defendant violated 12 C.F.R. § 1006.26(b) by suing or threatening  
 to sue on a time-barred debt; such conduct is unlawful and supports  
 Plaintiff's claims under § 1692e and § 1692f.  
  
 [Prayer and jury demand]  
```

## Template F — Class Action Complaint: Defective Form Validation Notice  
  
```  
UNITED STATES DISTRICT COURT  
[DISTRICT OF __________]  
  
[PLAINTIFF NAME], individually and Case No. ____________  
on behalf of all others similarly  
situated, CLASS ACTION COMPLAINT  
 Plaintiff, (Jury Trial Demanded)  
v.  
  
[DEFENDANT NAME],  
 Defendant.  
  
 CLASS ACTION COMPLAINT  
  
 I. INTRODUCTION  
  
 1. Plaintiff brings this class action under the Fair Debt Collection  
 Practices Act, 15 U.S.C. §§ 1692 et seq., and Regulation F, 12  
 C.F.R. § 1006.34, on behalf of herself and all consumers in  
 [State] who received a form initial-validation letter from Defendant  
 that failed to comply with § 1692g and § 1006.34.  
  
 II. JURISDICTION AND VENUE  
  
 2. This Court has jurisdiction under 28 U.S.C. § 1331 and 15 U.S.C.  
 § 1692k(d).  
  
 3. Venue is proper under 28 U.S.C. § 1391(b).  
  
 III. PARTIES  
  
 4. Plaintiff is a natural person residing in [County, State] and is  
 a "consumer" under 15 U.S.C. § 1692a(3).  
  
 5. Defendant is a [entity type] with its principal place of business  
 in [city, state]. Defendant's principal purpose is the acquisition  
 and collection of consumer debts. Defendant is a "debt collector"  
 under 15 U.S.C. �� 1692a(6).  
  
 IV. FACTUAL ALLEGATIONS  
  
 6. In the ordinary course of its business, Defendant uses a standard  
 form letter as its initial written communication to consumers  
 regarding consumer debts (the "Form Letter"), a true and correct  
 template of which is attached as Exhibit A.  
  
 7. The Form Letter is mailed, or caused to be mailed, by Defendant  
 (or Defendant's vendor) to thousands of [State] consumers each  
 year. Only individualized fields — name, address, account number,  
 and balance — vary among recipients; the substantive content is  
 uniform.  
  
 8. The Form Letter fails to comply with 15 U.S.C. § 1692g(a) and  
 12 C.F.R. § 1006.34(c) in the following respects:  
  
 (a) [Failure to identify creditor to whom the debt is owed];  
 (b) [Failure to provide itemization of the current amount];  
 (c) [Failure to provide consumer-response prompts];  
 (d) [Overshadowing of validation rights by demand for immediate  
 payment].  
  
 9. On or about [date], Plaintiff received the Form Letter from  
 Defendant.  
  
 10. As a result of the defective notice, Plaintiff was confused about  
 her dispute rights, failed to dispute the Debt within 30 days,  
 paid $[amount] toward the Debt, and incurred costs in seeking  
 counsel.  
  
 11. Each Class member received a substantially identical letter and  
 thereby faced the same statutory deprivation and exposure to  
 identical real-world harm — confusion of dispute rights and  
 misrepresentation of the character and legal status of the debt,  
 closely analogous to the common-law tort of misrepresentation.  
 See *TransUnion LLC v. Ramirez*, 594 U.S. 413, 425 (2021).  
  
 V. CLASS ACTION ALLEGATIONS  
  
 12. Plaintiff brings this action on behalf of herself and the  
 following Class pursuant to Federal Rule of Civil Procedure 23(a)  
 and 23(b)(3):  
  
 All natural persons with addresses in [State] to whom  
 Defendant mailed, or caused to be mailed, a letter in the  
 form of Exhibit A in connection with the collection of a  
 consumer debt, during the period beginning one year prior  
 to the filing of this Complaint through the date of class  
 certification.  
  
 13. Excluded from the Class are: (a) Defendant and its officers,  
 directors, employees, agents, and counsel; (b) the Court, court  
 personnel, and members of their immediate families; (c) persons  
 who timely opt out; and (d) persons whose claims have been  
 resolved by prior settlement or judgment.  
  
 14. *Numerosity.* Upon information and belief, Defendant mailed the  
 Form Letter to hundreds, if not thousands, of consumers in  
 [State] during the Class Period. The exact number is presently  
 within Defendant's exclusive knowledge. Joinder is impracticable.  
  
 15. *Commonality.* Common questions of law and fact include:  
  
 (a) Whether Defendant is a "debt collector" under 15 U.S.C.  
 § 1692a(6);  
 (b) Whether the Form Letter qualifies as an initial communication  
 under 15 U.S.C. § 1692g and 12 C.F.R. § 1006.34(a)(1);  
 (c) Whether the Form Letter omits or misstates information  
 required by § 1692g(a) and § 1006.34(c);  
 (d) Whether the Form Letter overshadows the validation period  
 in violation of § 1692g(b);  
 (e) Whether the Form Letter contains false, deceptive, or  
 misleading representations in violation of § 1692e;  
 (f) The amount of statutory damages recoverable under  
 15 U.S.C. § 1692k(a)(2)(B); and  
 (g) Defendant's net worth for purposes of the § 1692k(a)(2)(B)  
 cap.  
  
 16. *Typicality.* Plaintiff received the same Form Letter as all  
 Class members. Her claims are typical.  
  
 17. *Adequacy.* Plaintiff will fairly and adequately protect the  
 Class. Plaintiff's counsel is experienced in FDCPA and consumer-  
 protection class litigation. Plaintiff has no interests  
 antagonistic to the Class.  
  
 18. *Predominance and Superiority.* Common questions predominate  
 over individual issues. Defendant's liability turns on the content  
 of a uniform Form Letter; statutory damages under  
 § 1692k(a)(2)(B) do not require individualized proof. A class  
 action is superior to other available methods given the small  
 per-member recovery and the costs of individual litigation.  
  
 19. *Ascertainability.* Class members are identifiable from  
 Defendant's mailing records, account-management system, and any  
 vendor mailing logs.  
  
 VI. COUNT ONE — 15 U.S.C. § 1692g  
  
 20. Plaintiff incorporates the foregoing paragraphs.  
  
 21. The Form Letter violated 15 U.S.C. § 1692g(a) and 12 C.F.R.  
 § 1006.34(c) by failing to provide the required validation  
 information, and violated 15 U.S.C. § 1692g(b) by overshadowing  
 Plaintiff's and the Class members' validation rights.  
  
 VII. COUNT TWO — 15 U.S.C. § 1692e  
  
 22. The Form Letter contained false, deceptive, or misleading  
 representations in violation of § 1692e, § 1692e(10).  
  
 VIII. PRAYER FOR RELIEF  
  
 WHEREFORE, Plaintiff, individually and on behalf of the Class, prays  
 for judgment as follows:  
  
 (a) Certification of the Class under Fed. R. Civ. P. 23(a) and 23(b)(3);  
 (b) Appointment of Plaintiff as Class representative and undersigned  
 as Class counsel under Rule 23(g);  
 (c) Statutory damages for Plaintiff up to $1,000 under  
 15 U.S.C. § 1692k(a)(2)(B)(i);  
 (d) Statutory damages for the Class in an amount equal to the lesser  
 of $500,000 or 1% of Defendant's net worth pursuant to  
 15 U.S.C. § 1692k(a)(2)(B)(ii);  
 (e) Actual damages for Plaintiff and the Class under  
 15 U.S.C. § 1692k(a)(1);
```  

## Universal Pre-Filing Checklist  
  
- [ ] Statute of limitations verified (15 U.S.C. § 1692k(d); *Rotkiske v. Klemm*, 589 U.S. 8 (2019))  
- [ ] Defendant's debt-collector status confirmed (post-*Henson*, post-*Obduskey*)  
- [ ] Plaintiff is "consumer"; obligation is "debt"  
- [ ] Concrete-harm allegations sufficient under *TransUnion*  
- [ ] Tort-analog harm identified (intrusion upon seclusion, defamation, misrepresentation, public disclosure)  
- [ ] Specific statutory subsections cited  
- [ ] Challenged communication attached as exhibit  
- [ ] Rule 11 inquiry documented  
- [ ] Jury demand  
- [ ] Counsel admitted in forum / pro hac vice as needed

## Drafting Reminders Across Templates  
  
1. **Always attach the offending document.** Fed. R. Civ. P. 10(c) makes the exhibit part of the complaint. Courts are far more receptive to motions and summary-judgment positions backed by the exhibit on file.  
  
2. **Plead the date of each violation separately.** Each communication is its own potential violation and its own limitations trigger.  
  
3. **Do not paraphrase.** Quote the challenged language verbatim or attach a clean copy.  
  
4. **Match concrete-harm allegations to tort analogs.** *TransUnion* requires the harm to be analogous to a traditional, harm-recognized basis for suit at common law. Be explicit about which analog applies.  
  
5. **Watch the *Hunstein* and letter-vendor problem.** If the theory depends on third-party disclosure to a letter vendor, anticipate motion practice and consider state court.  
  
6. **For class complaints, plead net worth.** § 1692k(a)(2)(B)'s 1% net-worth cap requires factual allegations about defendant's net worth — even if pleaded "upon information and belief."  
  
7. **Combine claims for maximum coverage.** § 1692e and § 1692f are commonly pleaded together; § 1692g and § 1692e together for letter cases.
